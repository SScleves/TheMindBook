# Ch 9 — Book I, Part II: The Chonyid Bardo — The Dawning of the Peaceful Deities, First to Seventh Day

## In one paragraph
This unit is the translated text itself, not commentary: the officiant's address to a person now three and a half days dead, read aloud over the corpse or its substitute, covering the first seven days of the Bardo of the Experiencing of Reality. Its structure is a strict repetition. On each of the first five days one of the Dhyani Buddhas dawns as "Father-Mother" with an entourage, one of the five aggregates resolves into its primal form as a coloured light of a particular Wisdom, and that dazzling light strikes the deceased at the same moment as a *dull, soft* light from one of the six lokas produced by one of his own poisons — stupidity, anger, egotism, attachment, jealousy. The text's instruction never varies: the bright light terrifies and the dull light attracts, and the deceased must do the opposite of what he wants — not flee the radiance, not be fond of the dull light, pray to the Buddha of the day, and merge into his heart "in halo of rainbow light" to obtain Buddhahood in the Sambhoga-Kaya. The Sixth Day gathers all five orders plus door-keepers and the Buddhas of the six lokas into a single mandala of forty-two deities, with all six loka-lights shining at once; the Seventh Day brings the Knowledge-Holding Deities and their dakinis, five-coloured and terrifying, against the dull blue light of the brute world. Throughout, the text repeats its own explanation of the visions: they are not from anywhere outside, they issue from within the deceased's own heart, they are his own thought-forms and tutelary deities, and recognizing this *is* liberation.

## Core claims
- The text claims there are six states of Bardo (womb; dream-state; ecstatic equilibrium in deep meditation; the moment of death; the experiencing of Reality; the inverse process of sangsaric existence), of which the deceased will experience three: the Bardo of the moment of death (already passed, "up to yesterday"), the Chonyid Bardo, and the Sidpa Bardo.
- It claims the deceased has already had the Clear Light of Reality dawn on him at death and failed to hold on, "and so thou hast to wander here" — the Chonyid Bardo is explicitly the fallback stage for those who missed the first opportunity.
- It claims the deceased can now see and hear his relatives weeping and calling but cannot be heard by them, and "so he goeth away displeased"; he also sees his share of food set aside, his body stripped of its garments, his sleeping-place swept.
- The central repeated claim: **the visions are the deceased's own**. "May I recognize whatever [visions] appear, as the reflections of mine own consciousness" (OCR-damaged — see Key quotes); "these apparitions are thine own thought-forms"; on the Sixth Day, the forty-two deities are "issuing from within thy heart, being the product of thine own pure love", "these realms are not come from somewhere outside [thyself]"; "they exist from eternity within the faculties of thine own intellect"; "They are thine own tutelary deities."
- It claims the deceased now has "the thought-body of propensities", not a body of flesh and blood, and therefore the sounds, lights and rays "are, all three, unable to harm thee; thou art incapable of dying."
- It claims that without this teaching the same phenomena become terror: "the lights will daunt thee, the sounds will awe thee, and the rays will terrify thee."
- The structural claim of the whole unit: each day a Wisdom-light and a loka-light strike simultaneously; fear of the bright and fondness for the dull is the mechanism of rebirth; the dull light is called "an interruption to obstruct thee on the Path of Liberation."
- It claims the bright light is felt as unbearable ("thou wilt scarcely be able to look at it") and the dull light as attractive — the aversion and the attraction are themselves produced by the deceased's karma.
- It claims each day's failure is caused by a *specific* passion: stupidity (Day 1), anger or obscuring karma (Day 2), pride/egotism and bad-karma obscurations (Day 3), covetousness and miserliness (Day 4), jealousy (Day 5), evil propensities generally (Day 6), intensified propensities and stupidity (Day 7).
- It claims that recognition at any single stage suffices: "where one has recognized in any of them it is impossible not to be liberated."
- It claims the Wisdom-lights are "hook-rays" of grace that pursue: "Even if thou fleest, it will follow thee inseparably [from thyself]."
- The Sixth Day claims the green light-path of the Wisdom of Perfected Actions will *not* shine, "because the Wisdom-faculty of thine intellect hath not been perfectly developed" — only four of the five light-paths appear.
- It claims a liberation route it calls the Inner or hollow pathway through Vajra-Sattva, opened by understanding the Four Wisdoms united; and that "even the most lowly, by the power of the pure prayer, can close the doors of the Six Lokas."
- The closing frame claims the whole detailed procedure still fails for many: "The worst of the worst, [those] of heavy evil karma, having not the least predilection for any religion — and some who have failed in their vows — through the power of karmic illusions, not recognizing, although set face to face [with Truth], will stray downwards."

## Concepts
- **Chonyid Bardo / Bardo of the Experiencing of Reality** — the third stage of the after-death state, entered when neither the Primary nor the secondary Clear Light was recognized; "In this third stage of the Bardo, the karmic illusions come to shine." The unit's running head calls it the "Bardo of Karmic Illusions."
- **Setting-face-to-face** — the ritual act the text prescribes: the officiant calls the deceased by name and reads out, "correctly and distinctly", the identification of what he is seeing, so he may recognize it. Every day's section is introduced by this formula.
- **The secret of recognition** — a short verse the deceased is to hold in mind: that all apparitional appearances be recognized as the reflections of his own consciousness, known to be of the nature of apparitions, and that he not fear "the bands of Peaceful and Wrathful [Deities], mine own thought-forms" (the verse is badly garbled in this scan — see the OCR flag in Key quotes).
- **Thought-body of propensities** — the body the deceased now has, without flesh and blood, on which the second and third stages of the Bardo dawn; because of it, sounds, lights and rays cannot harm him.
- **Sounds, lights, and rays** — the triad experienced in this Bardo, said to awe, frighten, terrify and "cause much fatigue"; the natural sound of Reality reverberates "like a thousand thunders simultaneously sounding" and is "the natural sound of thine own real self."
- **The dull (soft) light of a loka** — on each day, alongside the Wisdom-radiance, a dim light shines from one of the six worlds, "the path which openeth out to receive thee" because of accumulated karma; the deceased's fondness for it is what carries him into that world's rebirth.
- **Hook-rays of grace** — the text's image for the light of the Buddha of the day: rays that catch and draw the deceased to safety, which may fail to "catch hold of one" if he flees in terror.
- **Ambuscade / ambush of the Bardo** — the recurring phrase in the daily prayers, its wording shifting: "May [I] be led safely across the fearful ambush of the Bardo" (Days One to Three), "the dangerous ambush of the Bardo" (Days Four and Five), "the ambuscades of the dread Bardo" (Day Six), "the fearful ambuscades of the Bardo" (Day Seven). The Wrathful-Deities preamble looks back and calls the Peaceful Bardo "seven stages of ambuscade."
- **Father-Mother** — each chief deity dawns embracing a Divine Mother; the text says the chief deity "personifies in himself the female as well as the male principle of nature", and prayers ask the Buddha to lead and the Mother to be "[my] rear-guard."
- **The Four Wisdoms United** — the Sixth Day's combined radiance, "like the rays of the sun spun into threads", from which proceeds "the Inner Path through Vajra-Sattva."
- **Wisdom of the Simultaneously-Born** — the Seventh Day's Wisdom, whose vari-coloured radiance is that of the *purified* propensities.
- **Non-thought-condition** — the prescribed mental posture before the mixed radiances: "be not attracted towards them; be not weak; be not terrified; but abide in the mood of non-thought-formation", in which "all the forms and radiances will merge into thyself, and Buddhahood will be obtained."
- **Tutelary deities** — what all these divine conclaves are declared to be: the deceased's own, to be known as such.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(For this unit the "examples" are the seven days themselves — the text's own worked cases of the same procedure. Each is given in full.)*

**Editorial framing before Day One** (bracketed, by the editor, not the translated text): the deceased is assumed to pass through the forty-nine days despite frequent settings-face-to-face; the first day is "reckoned from the time in which normally he would be expected to wake up to the fact that he is dead", about three and one-half to four days after death.

- **THE FIRST DAY — Vairochana.** The deceased has been in a swoon three and a half days; on recovering he will think "What hath happened?" All the Sangsara is in revolution. "The whole heavens will appear deep blue." From the Central Realm, called the Spreading Forth of the Seed, the Bhagavan Vairochana, white in colour, seated on a **lion-throne**, bearing an **eight-spoked wheel**, embraced by the Mother of the Space of Heaven. *Aggregate:* matter resolved into its primordial state, which is the blue light. *Wisdom-light:* the Wisdom of the Dharma-Dhatu, blue, shining, transparent, glorious, dazzling, shooting from Vairochana's heart as Father-Mother, so radiant one can scarcely look at it. *Competing light:* "a dull white light from the devas", striking him in front. *Poison:* stupidity ("bad karma"). *Instruction:* be not awed or startled; put faith in the blue light as the Light of the Wisdom of the Dharma-Dhatu, proceeding from Vairochana's heart, coming to receive him "while in the dangerous ambuscade of the Bardo." *Avoid:* fondness for the dull white light — "If thou be attached to it, thou wilt wander into the abodes of the devas and be drawn into the whirl of the Six Lokas." *Prayer:* to be led on the radiant light-path of the Dharma-Dhatu Wisdom by Vairochana, with the Divine Mother of Infinite Space as rear-guard. *Outcome if successful:* merging in halo of rainbow light into Vairochana's heart, Buddhahood in the Sambhoga-Kaya "in the Central Realm of the Densely-Packed."
  - Textual variant recorded in the notes: our MS. makes the aggregate of matter the blue light here, while the Block-print reads "It is the aggregate of consciousness … resolved into its primordial state which is the blue light" — the aggregate of consciousness otherwise appearing as the white light with Vajra-Sattva on the Second Day.
- **THE SECOND DAY — Vajra-Sattva.** For one who, "through power of anger or obscuring karma", was startled and fled. "On the Second Day the pure form of water will shine as a white light." From the deep blue Eastern Realm of Pre-eminent Happiness, the Bhagavan Akshobhya as **Vajra-Sattva**, blue in colour, holding a **five-pronged dorje**, on an **elephant-throne**, embraced by the Mother Mamaki, attended by the Bodhisattvas Kshitigarbha and Maitreya and the female Bodhisattvas Lasyema and Pushpema — "These six deities will appear to thee." *Aggregate:* the principle of consciousness in its pure form. *Wisdom-light:* the Mirror-like Wisdom, bright radiant white, from Vajra-Sattva's heart as Father-Mother. *Competing light:* "a dull, smoke-coloured light from Hell." *Poison:* violent anger. *Instruction:* do not fear the white light, know it to be Wisdom, think "I will take refuge in it", trust the hook of the rays of grace. *Avoid:* the smoke-coloured light — "thou wilt fall into the Hell-Worlds; and, falling therein, thou wilt have to endure unbearable misery, whence there is no certain time of getting out" — "and avoid anger." *Prayer:* the light-path of the Mirror-like Wisdom, Vajra-Sattva leading, Mamaki as rear-guard. *Outcome:* Buddhahood in the Sambhoga-Kaya in the Eastern Realm called Pre-eminently Happy.
  - A second textual variant recorded in the notes: a footnote calls the Mother's name here "evidently in error", since the Tibetan of this manuscript contains Sangs-gyas-spyan-ma, "She of the Buddha Eyes", who "in other manuscript texts comes with Ratna-Sambhava on the Third Day" — where this text does in fact place her. The variant is recorded, not resolved, and Mamaki is left standing in the translation.
- **THE THIRD DAY — Ratna-Sambhava.** For those who flee from the hook-rays through obscurations from bad karma and from pride. "On the Third Day the primal form of the element earth will shine forth as a yellow light." From the Southern Realm Endowed with Glory, the Bhagavan Ratna-Sambhava, yellow, bearing a **jewel**, on a **horse-throne**, embraced by the Divine Mother Sangs-gyas-Chanma (glossed "She of the Buddha Eyes"), with the Bodhisattvas Akasha-Garbha and Samanta-Bhadra and the female Bodhisattvas Mahlaima and Dhupema — "in all, six [Bodhic] forms" — from amidst a rainbow halo. *Aggregate:* touch, in its primal form. *Wisdom-light:* the Wisdom of Equality, dazzlingly yellow, "glorified with orbs having satellite orbs of radiance." *Competing light:* "the dull bluish-yellow light from the human [world]", striking his heart alongside the Wisdom-light. *Poison:* egotism. *Instruction:* do not fear; know it to be Wisdom; keep the mind resigned; and notably — "If thou knowest it to be the radiance of thine own intellect — although thou exertest not thy humility and faith and prayer to it — the Divine Body and Light will merge into thee inseparably, and thou wilt obtain Buddhahood." Only *if* he fails to recognize it as his own intellect is he to fall back on faith and prayer to Ratna-Sambhava. *Avoid:* the bluish-yellow human light — "thou wilt be born in the human world and have to suffer birth, age, sickness, and death; and thou wilt have no chance of getting out of the quagmire of worldly existence"; "abandon egotism, abandon propensities." *Prayer:* the light-path of the Wisdom of Equality, Ratna-Sambhava leading, "She-of-the-Buddha-Eyes" as rear-guard. *Outcome:* Buddhahood in the Southern Realm Endowed with Glory.
- **THE FOURTH DAY — Amitabha.** For those whose obscurations and evil karma "from covetousness and miserliness produce awe of the sounds and radiances, and they flee." "On the Fourth Day the red light, which is the primal form of the element fire, will shine." From the Red Western Realm of Happiness, the Bhagavan Buddha Amitabha, red, bearing a **lotus**, on a **peacock-throne**, embraced by the Divine Mother Gokarmo ("She-of-White-Raiment"), with the Bodhisattvas Chenrazee and Jampal and the female Bodhisattvas Ghirdhima and Aloke — "The six bodies of Enlightenment will shine upon thee from amidst a halo of rainbow light." *Aggregate:* feelings. *Wisdom-light:* the All-Discriminating Wisdom, glitteringly red, "glorified with orbs and satellite orbs", from the heart of the Divine Father-Mother Amitabha, striking his heart. *Competing light:* "a dull red light from the Preta-loka." *Poison:* intense attachment. *Instruction:* recognize it as Wisdom and keep the intellect "in the state of resignation" and he will merge inseparably; failing that, take refuge in the hook-rays of Amitabha's grace — "flee not. Even if thou fleest, it will follow thee inseparably." *Avoid:* the dull red light — "thou wilt fall into the World of Unhappy Spirits and suffer unbearable misery from hunger and thirst. Thou wilt have no chance of gaining Liberation [therein]." *Prayer:* the light-path of the Discriminating Wisdom, Amitabha leading, "She-of-White-Raiment" as rear-guard. *Outcome:* merging into the heart of the Divine Father-Mother Amitabha in halo of rainbow-light and attaining "Buddhahood in the Sambhoga-Kaya, in the Western Realm named Happy." **[OCR: the concatenated running text of this scan breaks off mid-word here — "attain Buddhahood in the SatH^^" — and resumes on the following page. - **[THE FIFTH DAY] — Amogha-Siddhi.** For sentient beings "unable through long association with propensities to abandon propensities, and, through bad karma and jealousy", in whom the sounds and radiances produce awe and terror, "the hook-rays of grace failing to catch hold of them", so that they "wander down also to the Fifth Day"; along with Amogha-Siddhi, "A light proceeding from the Asura-loka, produced by the evil passion of jealousy, will also come to receive one." "On the Fifth Day, the green light of the primal form of the element air will shine upon thee." From "the Green Northern Realm of Successful Performance of Best Actions", the Bhagavan Buddha Amogha-Siddhi, green in colour, "bearing a crossed-dorje in [his] hand", fitted upon a **sky-traversing Harpy-throne**, embraced by the Divine Mother "the Faithful Dolma", with the two Bodhisattvas Chag-na-Dorje and Dibpanamsel and the two female Bodhisattvas Gandhema and Nidhema — "These six [Bodhic] forms, from amidst a halo of rainbow light, will come to shine." Abide in the state of great resignation of impartiality." Meditate on the dull light "with impartiality, — with neither repulsion nor attraction"; if of low mental capacity, simply be not fond of it. Know the green radiance to be Wisdom and let the intellect rest in resignation; or believe it to be the hook-rays of Amogha-Siddhi's grace. *Avoid:* the dull green light — "thou wilt fall into the Asura-loka and have to engage in unbearable miseries of quarrelling and warfare." *Prayer:* the light-path of the All-Performing Wisdom, Amogha-Siddhi leading, the Divine Mother as rear-guard — her name garbled in this scan at this point **[OCR: the prayer reads "May the Divine Mother* the FalthTul TStIIt be [my] jmr* guard"; the same Mother is printed legibly as "the Faithful Dolma" at the head of the day]**. *Outcome:* Buddhahood in the Sambhoga-Kaya "in the Northern Realm of Heaped-up Good Deeds" (a footnote prefers "of Perfected Good Deeds (or Actions) … and this is the more correct").
- **THE SIXTH DAY — the whole mandala at once.** For one "long habituated to strong propensities and lacking in familiarity with, and pure affection for, Wisdom", whom the hook-rays could not catch. Now "all the Divine Fathers-Mothers of the Five Orders [of Dhyani Buddhas] with their attendants will come to shine upon one simultaneously" — and simultaneously the lights of all Six Lokas. The text tells the deceased that until yesterday each of the Five Orders shone one by one and he was awed and terrified; had he recognized them "to be the emanations from thine own thought-forms", he would already have Buddhahood. Now come "the Lights of the Union of Four Wisdoms."
  - *The four elements together:* "the four colours of the primal states of the four elements [water, earth, fire, air] will shine upon thee simultaneously."
  - *The five centres:* Vairochana from the Central Realm of the Spreading Forth of Seed; Vajra-Sattva from the Eastern Realm of Pre-eminent Happiness; Ratna-Sambhava from the Southern Realm endowed with Glory; Amitabha from the Happy Western Realm of Heaped-up Lotuses; Amogha-Siddhi from the Northern Realm of Perfected Good Deeds — each as Divine Father-Mother with attendants.
  - *Outer circle — four wrathful male Door-Keepers:* the Victorious One (East), the Destroyer of the Lord of Death (South), the Horse-necked King (West), the Urn of Nectar (North); *four female Door-keepers:* the Goad-Bearer, the No[o]se-Bearer **[OCR: printed here as "No(se- Bearer"; the accompanying footnote glosses her Skt. name as "She holding the Noose"]**, the Chain-Bearer, the Bell-Bearer.
  - *The six Buddhas of the six lokas:* the Buddha of the Devas, "the One of Supreme Power"; the Buddha of the Asuras, "[He of] Strong Texture"; the Buddha of Mankind, "the Lion of the Shakyas"; the Buddha of the brute kingdom, "the Unshakable Lion"; the Buddha of the Pretas, "the One of Flaming Mouth"; the Buddha of the Lower World, "the King of Truth" — "the Eight Father-Mother Door-keepers and the Six Teachers, the Victorious Ones."
  - *And:* "The All-Good Father, and the All-Good Mother, the Great Ancestors of all the Buddhas: Samanta-Bhadra [and Samanta-Bhadra], the Divine Father and the Divine Mother — these two, also will come to shine."
  - *Total and origin:* "These forty-two perfectly endowed deities, issuing from within thy heart, being the product of thine own pure love, will come to shine. Know them." They come "from within the four divisions of thy heart, which, including its centre, make the five directions." Their size is "not large, not small, [but] proportionate"; they have ornaments, colours, sitting postures, thrones and emblems; they form groups of five pairs, each group surrounded by a fivefold circle of radiances, "the male Bodhisattvas partaking of the nature of the Divine Fathers, and the female Bodhisattvas partaking of the nature of the Divine Mothers." All come "in one complete conclave." "They are thine own tutelary deities."
  - *The light-paths:* from the hearts of the Divine Fathers and Mothers of the Five Orders, "the rays of light of the Four Wisdoms united, extremely clear and fine, like the rays of the sun spun into threads." The Dharma-Dhatu Wisdom's blue path bears orbs printed as "like an [averted turquoise tupi" **[OCR: garbled; the three parallel clauses that follow all read "inverted"]**; the Mirror-like Wisdom's white path from Vajra-Sattva, orbs "each like an inverted mirror"; the Wisdom of Equality's yellow path from Ratna-Sambhava, orbs "each like an inverted gold cup"; the Discriminating Wisdom's bright red path from Amitabha, orbs "like inverted coral cups." The blue and the red paths alone are given the full formula — orbs "each made more glorious with five yet smaller [satellite] orbs", "leaving neither the centre nor the borders … unglorified"; the white and yellow paths are described only as orbs surrounded by smaller orbs.
  - *The missing fifth path:* "The green light-path of the Wisdom of Perfected Actions will not shine upon thee, because the Wisdom-faculty of thine intellect hath not been perfectly developed."
  - *The six competing lights, listed together:* "a dull white light from the devas, a dull green light from the asuras, a dull yellow light from human beings, a dull blue light from the brutes, a dull reddish light from the pretas, and a dull smoke-coloured light from Hell." A note records that the Block-Print assigns these colours differently (white devas; red asuras; blue human beings; green brutes; yellow pretas; smoke-coloured Hell), that the translator held the colours should match the Buddha of each loka, and that "the necessary corrections have been made in the translation herein" — the MS. itself being wrong in assigning dull blue to the human world and smoke-colour to the animal world in one place while a later folio correctly gives yellow to the human light-path.
  - *Instruction:* "all those are the radiances of thine own intellectual faculties come to shine. They have not come from any other place" — abide in non-thought-formation and all forms and radiances merge into him. If frightened by the pure radiances and attracted by the impure, "thou wilt assume a body in any of the Six, and suffer sangsaric miseries", never emancipated from "the Ocean of Sangsara." One who never received "the select words of the guru" is specifically expected to fear them.
  - *Prayer:* against "the five virulent poisons", on "the bright radiance-path of the Four Wisdoms united", led by the Five Victorious Conquerors with the Five Orders of Divine Mothers as rear-guard, rescued from the impure light-paths of the Six Lokas and placed "within the five pure Divine Realms."
  - *Outcome and its reach:* one recognizes one's own inner light and, merging in at-one-ment, attains Buddhahood; "through humble faith the ordinary devotee cometh to know himself, and obtaineth Liberation; even the most lowly, by the power of the pure prayer, can close the doors of the Six Lokas", obtaining Buddhahood "by the hollow pathway through Vajra-Sattva."
- **THE SEVENTH DAY — the Knowledge-Holding Deities.** "On the Seventh Day the vari-coloured radiance of the purified propensities will come to shine. Simultaneously, the Knowledge-Holding Deities, from the holy paradise realms, will come to receive one." Against them, "the pathway to the brute world, produced by the obscuring passion, stupidity, also cometh to receive one."
  - *Centre:* "the Lotus Lord of Dance, the Supreme Knowledge-Holder Who Ripens Karmic Fruits", radiant with all five colours, embraced by the Red Dakini.
  - *East:* "the Earth-Abiding Knowledge-Holder", white, radiant smiling countenance, embraced by the White Dakini.
  - *South:* "[He] Having Power Over Duration of Life", yellow, smiling and radiant, embraced by the Yellow Dakini.
  - *West:* "the Knowledge-Holding Deity of the Great Symbol", red, smiling and radiant, embraced by the Red Dakini.
  - *North:* "the Self-Evolved Knowledge-Holder", green, "with a half-angry, half-smiling radiant countenance", embraced by the Green Dakini.
  - Every one of the five holds "a crescent knife and a skull [filled] with blood, dancing and making the mudra of fascination, [with his right hand held] aloft."
  - *Outer Circle:* innumerable bands of dakinis — "dakinis of the eight places of cremation, dakinis of the four classes, dakinis of the three abodes, dakinis of the thirty holy-places and of the twenty-four places of pilgrimage" — with heroes, heroines, celestial warriors and faith-protecting deities male and female, "each bedecked with the six bone-ornaments", carrying drums, thigh-bone trumpets, skull-timbrels, "banners of gigantic human[-like] hides, human-hide canopies, human-hide bannerettes, fumes of human-fat incense", and innumerable other instruments, filling the world-systems with music and "causing them to vibrate, to quake and tremble with sounds so mighty as to daze one's brain, and dancing various measures, will come to receive the faithful and punish the unfaithful."
  - *Wisdom-light:* five-coloured radiances of the Wisdom of the Simultaneously-Born — the purified propensities — "vibrating and dazzling like coloured threads", issuing from the hearts of the five chief Knowledge-Holding Deities and striking his heart, "so bright that the eye cannot bear to look upon them."
  - *The sound:* "Within those radiances, the natural sound of the Truth will reverberate like a thousand thunders", with a rolling reverberation, "[amidst which] will be heard 'Slay! Slay!' and awe-inspiring mantras."
  - *Competing light:* "a dull blue light from the brute world."
  - *Instruction:* be not afraid of the five-coloured radiance nor terrified; "know the Wisdom to be thine own"; know the sounds to be "[of] the intellectual faculties of thine own [inner] light"; direct the mind one-pointedly to the Knowledge-Holding Conquerors and supplicate them with the admission that the Five Orders of the Buddhas of the Three Times have already exerted their grace in vain — "Alas! for a being like me!" — asking to be held "with the hook of their compassion."
  - *Avoid:* the dull blue light — "thou wilt fall into the brute-world, wherein stupidity predominates, and suffer the illimitable miseries of slavery and dumbness and stupidness; and it will be a very long time ere thou canst get out."
  - *Prayer:* on the bright light-path of the Simultaneously-born Wisdom, led by "the bands of Heroes, the Knowledge-Holders", with "the bands of the Mothers, the Dakinis" as rear-guard, to be placed "in the pure Paradise Realms."
  - *Outcome:* "there is no doubt that one will be born within the pure Paradise Realms", merged in rainbow-light into the heart of the Knowledge-Holding Deities. "All the pandit classes, too, coming to recognize at this stage, obtain liberation; even those of evil propensities being sure to be liberated here."
- **The closing colophon of the unit:** "Here endeth the part of the Great Thödol concerned with the setting-face-to-face of the Peaceful [Deities] of the Chönyid Bardo and the setting-face-to-face of the Clear Light of the Chikhai Bardo."
- **The retrospective verdict, given at the head of the Wrathful section:** the Peaceful Bardo contained "seven stages of ambuscade"; multitudes are liberated by recognition at one or another; "although multitudes obtain liberation in that manner, the number of sentient beings being great, evil karma powerful, obscurations dense, propensities of too long standing, the Wheel of Ignorance and Illusion becometh neither exhausted nor accelerated", and "there is a vast preponderance of those who wander downwards unliberated."

## Thread
This is the argumentative core of Book I. Part I offered the single, simple opportunity — recognize the Clear Light at the moment of death — and this unit is what the text prescribes for everyone who failed it, which it assumes is nearly everyone. Its method is repetition with variation: seven times over, the same fork is presented — an unbearable radiance that is the deceased's own Wisdom, and a soft light that is his own poison offering him a world to be born into — so that a hearer who misses the point on Day One has six more chances to catch it. The Sixth Day is the structural climax, gathering the five separate encounters into one mandala of forty-two deities and all six loka-lights at once, and stating in the plainest terms the doctrine the earlier days imply: the realms and the deities issue from the four divisions of the deceased's heart and have never come from anywhere outside. The Seventh Day then shifts register — dancing Knowledge-Holders with crescent knives and skull-cups, dakinis with human-hide banners, cries of "Slay! Slay!" — preparing the reader for the Wrathful Deities, whom the following section declares to be "only the former Peaceful Deities in changed aspect." The unit also quietly rank-orders its own soteriology: recognition without prayer is best (Day Three), faith and prayer are the fallback, and the "hollow pathway through Vajra-Sattva" is the route left open to the ordinary devotee.

## Connections
- Directly continues Book I, Part I (the Chikhai Bardo): the opening pages tell the deceased he has already had, and missed, the Primary and secondary Clear Light, and the unit's closing colophon binds the two settings-face-to-face together as one operation.
- Directly precedes the Dawning of the Wrathful Deities, Eighth to Fourteenth Day, whose introduction reads backward over this unit — "seven stages of ambuscade" — and identifies its "fifty-eight flame-enhaloed, wrathful, blood-drinking deities" as the Peaceful Deities of this unit in changed aspect. That section also claims recognition is *easier* there, because terror keeps the intellect "undistractedly alert in one-pointedness."
- Sets up the Sidpa Bardo (the third of the three Bardos the deceased is told at the outset he will pass through), the state of seeking rebirth into precisely the six lokas whose dull lights are refused here.
- The list of six states of Bardo given in the opening address is the text's own frame for the whole work.
