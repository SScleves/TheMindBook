# Ch 14 — Addenda

## In one paragraph
This unit is **editorial apparatus, not translated scripture**: it is Evans-Wentz's own seven-part supplement, printed after the text and colophon, and announced in its first sentence as "complementary to our Introduction and Commentary." The seven sections are (I) Yoga, (II) Tantricism, (III) Mantras, or Words of Power, (IV) the Guru and Shishya (or Chela) and Initiations, (V) Reality, (VI) Northern and Southern Buddhism and Christianity, and (VII) The Medieval Christian Judgement. Sections I–IV build a physiological-occult substructure for the *Bardo Thödol* — vital-airs, psychic-nerves, chakras, the Serpent Power, mandala arithmetic, mantra-vibration, guru-lines and initiation — very largely out of **Hindu Tantric sources in English translation (Arthur Avalon / Sir John Woodroffe, Rama Prasad)**, with Western comparisons (Plato's daemons, the caduceus of Hermes, wireless telegraphy, the chemical laboratory, the Veil of Isis) supplied by the editor. Section V leaves Tibet altogether and quotes a Chinese Mahayana treatise ascribed to Ashvaghosha through two rival English translations, to argue that the *Bardo Thödol*'s metaphysics is philosophically sound. Sections VI–VII are comparative-religion polemic: Northern vs. Southern Buddhism, Buddhism vs. "Church-council" Christianity, and a late-medieval English Judgement dialogue — plus a Surrey parish wall-painting — set beside the Bardo Judgement. Three layers run through the whole and the edition does not separate them — (a) what the editor reports of Tibetan/lamaic practice, (b) what he imports from Hindu-Tantric and Western occult/Theosophical material to explain it, and (c) his own conclusions, which repeatedly run toward a single prehistoric source for Eastern and Western symbolism.

**Reading the three layers.** Throughout the note, claims are tagged: **[TIB]** = presented as Tibetan/lamaic tradition or as the *Bardo Thödol*'s own content; **[IMPORT]** = drawn from Hindu/Tantric texts, Chinese Mahayana, or Western occult/classical/scientific analogy; **[E-W]** = the editor's own inference or verdict. Where the printed text itself blurs the line, that is noted.

## Core claims
- **[E-W]** The Addenda are explicitly the editor's supplement, offered "in mere outline, and, therefore, more often than not undetailed and incomplete" — his own words for the Tantricism section.
- **[IMPORT/E-W]** Yoga is a *joining or yoking of the lower human nature to the higher or divine nature*, achieved by control of the mental process; ignorance is the occupation of the mind-field by thought-forms arising from the false concept that phenomena are real. The mirror thick with dust and the crystal vase of muddied water are his images for the ordinary mind.
- **[E-W]** Yoga is "a scientific method" whose results are as definite and certain psychically as a European or American chemical laboratory's are physically; the parenthetical footnote calls it a system of applied psychology "far more highly developed than any known in Western Science." This scientific framing is the editor's, not the text's.
- **[E-W]** After living among yogis of various schools, he concludes that despite widely differing technical terms and theory, "the goal for all yogis is, in the last analysis of esotericism, identical" — the Hindus naming it one thing [OCR: "Bnd"; the Sanskrit term is lost in the scan] and the Buddhists Nirvana. This is a unity-of-esotericism thesis, asserted, not demonstrated.
- **[TIB]** The lamas maintain that comprehension of the nature of the Clear Light is impossible for the unilluminated — i.e. for one who has not recognized it ecstatically while still in the human body.
- **[E-W]** The *Bardo Thödol* is "a Tantric work, although, strictly speaking, not a Tantra": a ritual based on the Tantras whose chief matter is the science of birth, death, and rebirth.
- **[IMPORT]** The whole psycho-physical apparatus of Section II — five sheaths, ten vital-airs, fourteen principal nadis, six chakras plus the Sahasrara, the Serpent Power — is Sanskrit/Hindu-Tantric, sourced in footnotes to Avalon/Woodroffe and Rama Prasad, and then applied to the Tibetan text.
- **[E-W]** The rational explanation behind the deities, mandalas, and psychic-centres is that "each deity, as it dawns from its appropriate psychic-centre, represents the coming into after-death karmic activity of some corresponding impulse or passion of the complex consciousness." This is the editor's psychologizing reading, offered as *the* rational explanation.
- **[IMPORT]** Mantra-power is explained by "the ancient Greek theory of music" and the law of vibration: know a thing's key-note or vibratory rate and it can be disintegrated; know a deity's mantra and one can set up communication "similar to, but transcending, wireless or telepathic communication with that deity."
- **[IMPORT/E-W]** Mantras therefore must be guarded, and lines of gurus exist as a "Brotherhood of Guardians of the Mysteries" — a Theosophical-sounding formulation applied to Indo-Tibetan practice.
- **[IMPORT]** Initiation consists in the guru's giving the secret mantra; the vital-force centred in the Thousand-petalled Lotus acts "as through a wireless receiving station" to receive the gift-waves of the Supreme Guru, and divine grace is "made to glow, as electricity is made to glow when conducted to the vacuum of an electric bulb."
- **[E-W]** The secret mantra is "like the Egyptian Word of Power, the Password necessary for a conscious passing from the embodied state into the disembodied state" — an Egyptological equation the editor supplies.
- **[E-W]** Reporting the position of "Buddhism of all Schools": it denies the soul hypothesis and holds personal immortality impossible, all personal existence being a flux of instability karmically dependent on the false concept that phenomena are real; individualized mind cannot realize Reality.
- **[IMPORT]** Ashvaghosha is quoted at length (Of Ignorance, Of Phenomena, Of Space, Of Mind and Matter, Of the Nature of the Primordial Mind, Of the Nature of Nirvana) from a treatise whose title the scan renders only as "Awakening of …". The sentence that names him is destroyed by the §V lacuna: the surviving text picks up at the words "initiates direct from the time of the Buddha", but what that phrase was predicated of cannot be read from this scan, so it is not treated here as a description of Ashvaghosha.
- **[E-W]** Verdict of Section V: "Thus does Ashvaghosha bear witness to the soundness of the supreme philosophy of the Mahayana School underlying the *Bardo Thödol*; and, as an independent commentator, confirms our own interpretations." A Chinese Mahayana treatise is thereby made an independent witness for a Tibetan ritual text — the editor's move, not the tradition's.
- **[E-W]** The Christian-apologist hypothesis that Northern Buddhism was shaped by early Christian missionaries "seems to be disproved," because the recovered writings of Ashvaghosha show Northern Buddhism fundamentally the same in the first century A.D. as now and as before the Christian era; Nestorian, St. Thomas, or later missionary influence "could only have been superficial at most."
- **[E-W]** His own view, which he calls "merely hypothetical": it is Christianity that was shaped by the Faiths preceding it — Christian monasticism related to Hindu, Buddhist, Jain and Taoist monastic systems; Trinity and Incarnation not unique but principal doctrines of the Osirian Faith of Egypt "at least six thousand years ago."
- **[E-W]** The Sermon on the Mount "might very well be regarded, as many Buddhist scholars do regard it, as a Christian restating of doctrines which the Buddha, too, formulated as an inheritance from prehistoric Buddhas." (Attributed to "many Buddhist scholars," none named.)
- **[E-W]** Three doctrines mark modern "Church-council" Christianity off from Buddhism: one life followed by never-ending paradise or eternal hell; forgiveness of sins through the blood sacrifice of a Saviour; and the uniqueness of the Divine Incarnation. Primitive or Gnostic Christianity, by contrast, held Rebirth and Karma.
- **[TIB]** Lamaism modifies the doctrine of self-dependence in practice: the devotee appeals directly to the Dhyani Buddhas and tutelary deities "very much as to Jesus and saints and angels by Christians" — the editor's own comparison, but the practice is reported as Tibetan.
- **[E-W]** The comparison that carries Section VII is not only textual: a wall-painting of the Judgement in Chaldon Church, Surrey, is read as a Christianized Bardo Judgement — St. Michael for Shinje holding the scales, souls weighed instead of karmic actions, the six karmic pathways collapsed into one ladder to heaven, Christ at its top where the Buddhas of the lokas stand, sun at his right hand and moon at his left "as though he were a Buddha," and the same cauldron and spikes in the hell-world below.
- **[E-W]** Closing thesis of the Addenda: parallels between the medieval Christian Judgement and the Bardo Judgement suggest "the greater part of the symbolism nowadays regarded as being peculiarly Christian or Jewish seems to be due to adaptations from Egyptian and Eastern religions," and further, that Orient and Occident are "mentally and spiritually one."

## Concepts
- **Yoga** — Derived from the Sanskrit root [OCR: "jw/"], meaning 'to join', which the editor calls closely allied with the English verb *to yoke*: a joining of the lower human nature to the higher or divine so that the higher directs the lower, brought about by control of the mental process. **[IMPORT]** definition; applied to the Tibetan text throughout.
- **Ignorance** — The state of mental obscuration in which the mind-field is occupied by thought-forms and thought-processes arising from the universally dominating false concept that phenomena and phenomenal appearances are real. Only when these are wholly inhibited does the primordial, unmodified condition of mind become realizable, and Illumination dawns — symbolized in the *Bardo Thödol* as the Primary Clear Light of the Dharma-Kaya.
- **Veil of Isis** — The editor's name for Maya/Illusion, hiding "the Unsullied and Unsulliable Reality" from man. **[IMPORT — Western occult vocabulary.]**
- **Tantra** — Per "the Tibetan term [OCR: "TtiMtm (TUl Hgjmd — pron. Gyüd)"]", literally a treatise or class of scriptures of a mystic nature, usually belonging to the [School reference garbled]. Two classes exist, Hindu and Buddhist; Hindu Tantras are generally cast as a dialogue between the goddess and Shiva, often in their wrathful forms as Bhairava and Bhairavi, while Buddhist Tantras replace the Hindu deities with Buddhas, their shaktis, or gods and goddesses. Both classes are usually based on the [Shakti] Philosophy.
- **Vital-force (Skt. prana) and the five sheaths** — The human principle of consciousness, the Knower, clothes itself when incarnate in five sheaths: (1) physical (Anna-maya-kosha); (2) vital (Prana-maya-kosha); (3) the sheath of ordinary human consciousness [its Sanskrit name is dropped by the scan]; (4) subconsciousness (Vijnana-maya-kosha); (5) the all-transcending blissful consciousness of Reality (Ananda-maya-kosha). **[IMPORT — Vedantic/Tantric.]**
- **Vayu / the ten vital-airs** — Prana divides into ten vital-airs. As Plato's daemons are said to control the operations of the Cosmic Body, so these control the human body. Five fundamental, five minor (see examples).
- **Psychic-nerves (Skt. nadi)** — Invisible channels for the flow of psychic forces, whose conducting agents are the vital-airs; fourteen principal and hundreds of thousands of minor. The editor is explicit that the nadis of the East and the nerves of the West, "although literally the same in name, are not synonymous."
- **Sushumna-, ida-, pingala-nadi** — The median nerve in the hollow of the spinal column (Skt. Brahma-danda), "the Mt. Meru of the human body, man being regarded as the microcosm of the macrocosm"; the left and right nerves coil round it "as the two serpents coil round the caduceus carried by the messenger-god Hermes." **[IMPORT, with an explicit Greek identification the editor advances hypothetically — "It is believed that this ancient herald's wand symbolizes the sushumna-nadi…"]**. The sentence's conclusion is missing from the text layer but present in the per-page markup: "If so, we see again how the esoteric symbol[-c]ode of the West corr[e]spends [corresponds] to that [of the] East." — i.e. the caduceus is made evidence for the single-prehistoric-source thesis that closes the Addenda. **[E-W]**
- **Psychic-centres (chakra)** — Dynamo-like centres ranged along the sushumna and interconnected by it, storing the vital-force on which all psycho-physical processes depend; the sushumna is "the great highway" for the psychic forces.
- **Serpent Power / Kundalini** — The mighty occult power lying coiled like a sleeping serpent in the Muladhara-chakra at the base of the spinal column; the initial aim of the practitioner of Yoga is to awaken it, drive it through the centres one by one until, "rising like mercury in a magic tube," it reaches the thousand-petalled lotus and falls back "as a shower of heavenly ambrosia," at which the yogi experiences Illumination.
- **Aperture of Brahma (Skt. Brahmarandhra)** — The exit of the sushumna through the Sahasrara Padma, through which the consciousness-principle normally goes out from the body at death; the editor marks it as "referred to in our text". It is not, however, the only item of the imported anatomy he claims the translated text carries: he also writes that "in the preliminary instructions, the *Bardo Thödol* makes reference to the vital-force or vital-airs", that "there are next mentioned in our text the Psychic Nerves", and that the median, left and right nerves are named "to follow our text". The Addenda therefore claim four points of contact, not one — and offer no citation for any of them beyond the phrase "our text".
- **Mandala** — Mystic groupings of deities. Three chief mandalas correspond to the three chief chakras, subdivided into fourteen subsidiary mandalas for the first fourteen (7 + 7) days of the Bardo.
- **Northern and Southern Centre** — The Brain-centre and the Heart-centre, "the two poles of the human organism," said to be the first centres to form in the embryo, their formation directed by terrestrial prana "derived from the central prana reservoir in the sun of our planetary system." **[IMPORT — the solar-prana claim is Tantric/occult cosmology, not the Tibetan text.]**
- **Mantra / Word of Power** — Sound formulated to the vibratory rate of a deity or force; unless properly intoned mantras are without effect, and printed for the uninitiated they "appear utterly meaningless." Correct pronunciation depends on bodily purity as well as intonation, so the devotee first purifies mouth and tongue by purificatory mantras, then "gives life to" or awakens the sleeping power of the mantra.
- **Siddhi** — Supernormal powers conferred by the occult ability to employ a mantra properly; usable as white or black magic. "The right- and left-hand paths [are] the same up to this point of practical application of the fruits obtained through psychic development. One path leads upward to Emancipation, the other downward to Enslavement."
- **The three lines of gurus** — Divyaugha, the purely superhuman or 'heavenly (divine) line'; siddhaugha, the most highly developed human beings possessed of siddhic powers; manavaugha, 'human line', of ordinary religious teachers. A footnote insists the three are so called not from any difference in their powers but from their different places of abode: in the [Tantra name garbled] (ch. I), the Divya Order always abide in Shiva's Heaven, the Siddha Order both in the Human World and the Heaven Worlds, the Manava Order on Earth only. **[IMPORT — Hindu Tantric.]**
- **Shishya (chela)** — The disciple. Women as well as men, if qualified, may be gurus. Probation is normally one year; on probation the shishya is merely commanded to perform exercises, and only at its end is told the *why* of them and the results certain to follow.
- **Gift-waves** — The editor's rendering of the spiritual influence transmitted from the divine line of gurus to the human guru and thence to the shishya; the term also carries the mantra's "psychic or gift-wave communication."
- **Supreme Guru** — In "the occult language of the Indian and Tibetan Mysteries," he sits enthroned in the pericarp of the Thousand-petalled Lotus, as the Divine Father; the shishya, led there by the awakened Serpent Power under the human guru's guidance, bows at his feet, the Veil is lifted, and the Clear Light shines into his heart. "As one Lamp is lit by the Flame of another Lamp, so the Divine Power is communicated."
- **Reality** — What individualized mind or consciousness cannot realize; equated in Section V with the Dharma-Kaya, "for the two terms are synonymous."
- **Higher Buddhism** — Northern Buddhism as contrasted with Southern; beneath the *Bardo Thödol*'s ritual symbolism its essential teachings "are to be discovered, by those that have eyes to see."
- **Church-council Christianity vs. primitive/Gnostic Christianity** — The editor's governing distinction in Section VI: the former (post-conciliar, dependent on an outside Saviour, denying rebirth and karma) is what Western readers mistake for Christianity; the latter propounded an elaborate esotericism and upheld Rebirth and Karma.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(This unit argues and compares rather than experiments; the "examples" are its concrete schemes, textual citations, numbers, and comparisons.)*
- **The seven-section programme** — Stated in the Addenda's first sentence: (1) Yoga, (2) Tantricism, (3) Mantras, or Words of Power, (4) the Guru and Shishya (or Chela) and Initiations, (5) Reality, (6) Northern and Southern Buddhism and Christianity, (7) the Medieval Christian Judgement. The running heads that follow confirm the sequence: YOGA, TANTRICISM, MANTRAS, INITIATIONS, REALITY, BUDDHISM AND CHRISTIANITY, THE CHRISTIAN JUDGEMENT. **[E-W's own architecture.]**
- **The dusty mirror and the muddied vase** — Two images for the nescience-darkened normal mind; yoga is "a scientific method of removing the dust from the mirror and the earthy particles from the [water]." **[IMPORT/E-W]**
- **The laboratory analogy** — Piercing the Veil of Isis is done "through methods as definite and certain in psychical results as those employed in a European or American chemical laboratory are in physical results. As gold can be separated from impurities by methods of chemistry, so can Truth be divorced from Error by methods of [yoga]." **[E-W]**
- **The Asanga footnote** — Yoga is said to have been introduced into Buddhism by Asanga, a fourth-century [Indian], held to have been inspired directly by Maitreya, the coming Buddha, and to have produced the Scriptures of the 'Contemplative' School [Tibetan school-name garbled], citing Waddell [the title prints only as "Tk4 p/ 7Vr" and no reading is proposed for it], at a page number the scan renders only as "p. laB" — no reading is proposed for it. The same footnote records that the editor possesses a number of important translations by the late Dawa-Samdup of Tibetan treatises, most of them of Indian origin, and hopes to publish them with the results of his own researches. **[Transmission claim reported by E-W from a Western scholarly source.]**
- **The Tantra source-footnote** — Hung on the opening sentence of Section II, and the documentary proof that the chakra/prana apparatus is imported from Hindu-Tantric literature in English translation rather than derived from the Tibetan text. The scan prints it as: "General rc[fere]nces (tlu far Sections III uid IV followltig): A. - **Dating the Tantras** — Which class is older is disputed; the older Tantras are "probably far older than European critics (who have placed their origin well within the Christian era) have thought," while some Tantras are "indisputably quite modern." Orthodox Hindus hold the Tantras of Vedic origin, designed to serve as the chief Scriptures for this age, the Kali Yuga; some Buddhists claim a purely Buddhist origin; "the Hindu view is, however, the commonly accepted view."
- **The Tantras as encyclopaedias** — Their subject-matter: the nature of the cosmos, its evolution and dissolution; classification of sentient beings and of the various heavens, hells, and worlds; divinely instituted rules governing human relations and conduct; forms of worship and spiritual training, ceremonial rites, meditation, japa; duties of kings, law, custom, medicine, [alchemy — OCR: "oltrDtocJV"], astronomy, magic — "the whole cycle of the sciences of the East."
- **The five fundamental vital-airs** — [name garbled], controlling inspiration; *udana*, the ascending vital-force; *apana*, the downward vital-force, which expels wind, excrement, urine, and semen; [name garbled], the collective force of the vayu, which kindles the fire of the body whereby food is digested and then distributed by the blood; and *vyana*, controlling division and diffusion in all metabolic processes. **The scan's numbering is corrupt (it prints "(1) … (3) … (3) … (4) … (5)").**
- **The five minor airs** — Producing, respectively, hiccuping, opening and closing of the eyes, assistance to digestion, yawning, and distension. **Five effects are listed but only four names survive legibly in the scan — [OCR: "the iiirmfna, krikara, difva-daiia, and dAananjaya"]; one name is lost.**
- **The six chakras plus the seventh** — (1) Muladhara, the Root-support, in the perineum, holding "the secret Fountain of Vital-Force, presided over by the Goddess Kundalini"; (2) Svadhishthana, centre of the sex-organs; (3) Mani-pura, the navel nerve-centre; (4) Anahata, the heart nerve-centre; (5) Vishuddha, in the throat; (6) Ajna, between the eyebrows, "as depicted by the 'third eye' on images of the Buddha and of Hindu deities," where sushumna, ida and pingala come together and then separate; and, "above all, in the causal region of psychic man, as the sun of the body," (7) the Sahasrara Padma, the thousand-petalled lotus. **Note the internal tension: the text says "six are of fundamental importance," then adds the Sahasrara as "the Supreme or Seventh."**
- **The three chakras the Bardo Thödol uses** — Of the psychic-centres, the *Bardo Thödol* is concerned chiefly with three: the Heart-centre (Anahata), the Throat-centre (Vishuddha), and the Brain-centre (Sahasrara Padma). Of these, the Brain-centre (sometimes called the Northern Centre) and the Heart-centre (Southern Centre) are of chief importance. **[E-W's correlation of the Tibetan text with the imported scheme.]**
- **The mandala arithmetic** — First chief mandala: **42** deities in six subordinate mandalas, corresponding to the first Six Days of the Chönyid Bardo, emanating from the Heart-centre. Second: **10** principal deities dawning on the Seventh Day, from the Throat-centre. Third: **58** principal deities in seven subordinate mandalas, the last Seven Days, from the Brain-centre. The 42 and the 58 comprise "the Great Mandala of the 100 superior deities," the 42 called peaceful and the 58 wrathful; the 10 of the Throat-centre are classed with the 42 peaceful. **The grand total is a RECONSTRUCTION, not a printed number.** The scan reads: "Thus, when united in the Greater Mandala of the whole of the Chönyid Bardo, there are no principal deities." The word "no" stands where a numeral must stand (the sentence is a total, and "no principal deities" contradicts the three counts just given); the three printed component figures are 42 + 10 + 58, so the note reads the lost numeral as **110**. That figure is nowhere printed in this scan and no page image exists to check it against.
- **Day-by-day composition of the 42** — First Day: Vairochana and his shakti alone. Second to Fifth Days: on each, one of the remaining four Dhyani Buddhas and his shakti, together with two Bodhisattvas and their shaktis. Sixth Day: all of these dawn in one mandala, joined by 16 additional deities — 8 Door-keepers, the 6 Buddhas of the Six Lokas, and the Adi-Buddha and shakti. **The arithmetic checks internally: 2 + (4 × 6) + 16 = 42.**
- **Day-by-day composition of the 58** — Eighth to Twelfth Days: one of the Herukas with his shakti each day, 10 deities in all. Thirteenth Day: the 8 Kerima and the 8 Htamenma. Fourteenth Day: 4 Door-keepers and the 28 animal-headed Deities. **10 + 16 + 32 = 58, again internally consistent.** The 10 Knowledge-Holding Deities of the Throat-centre (called, in the Obeisances at p. 85, the Lotus Deities) dawn on the intervening Seventh Day.
- **Orientation** — "There is definite orientation in all the mandalas"; the Five Dhyani Buddhas with their shaktis are the chief deities dawning on the first Five Days.
- **The initiatory mystery-play** — "As though in an initiatory mystery-play, the actors for each day of the Bardo come on the mind-stage of the deceased, who is their sole spectator; and their director is Karma." The higher elements dawn first in the full glory of the primal Clear Light, then in ever-diminishing glory: Peaceful Deities of the Heart-centre, then of the Throat-centre, merging into the Wrathful Deities of the Brain-centre; finally the purely human and brutish propensities appear as horror-producing spectral hallucinations, and the percipient flees "from them — his own thought-forms — to the refuge of the womb." **[E-W's psychological staging of the Tibetan sequence.]**
- **The after-death body's life-cycle** — As the earth-plane body matures, withers, and disintegrates, so the mental-body "grows from the heavenly days of its Bardo childhood to the less idealistic days of its Bardo maturity, then fades and dies in the Intermediate State, as the Knower, abandoning it, is reborn." **[E-W]**

## Thread
The Addenda are where the edition's editorial project becomes explicit. Everything before them presents itself as a translated Tibetan ritual text with annotation; here Evans-Wentz drops the apparatus of translation and argues in his own voice, retro-fitting the *Bardo Thödol* with a Hindu-Tantric psycho-physiology (Sections I–IV) it does not itself expound, defending its metaphysics with a Chinese Mahayana authority (Section V), placing it in a two-school taxonomy of Buddhism (VI), and finally using it as a lever on the history of Christian symbolism (VII), where an English parish wall-painting of the Judgement is read image-by-image as a Christianized Bardo. The direction of travel is consistent: from local Tibetan ritual outward to a claimed universal esotericism whose parts — Egyptian, Greek, Hindu, Gnostic, Tibetan — are held to be adaptations of one prehistoric stock. Sections III and IV are also where the editor's most concrete practical claims live (mantra as vibration, initiation as transmission), and Section II supplies the numbers (42 / 10 / 58 / 110) that make the Chönyid Bardo's fourteen days a systematic scheme rather than a list. A reader who wants the Tibetan book must subtract the imports; a reader who wants to understand why this edition was influential in the West must read the imports, because they are what made the text legible — and attractive — to its first English-language audience.

**Quality of the recovered text.** These recoveries are lossy in a specific, uneven way. Where the swallowing tag is a processing instruction (Section VII's opening page), the text survives with its capitalisation and punctuation intact and can be quoted almost as printed. Where it is a pseudo-element with pseudo-attributes (the page numbered 240, the shakti footnote, the Edmunds footnote), the parser lowercases every word and discards most punctuation and some tokens outright — so wording drawn from those pages is a *reading* of mangled evidence, not a transcript, and is marked as such at each point of use.
