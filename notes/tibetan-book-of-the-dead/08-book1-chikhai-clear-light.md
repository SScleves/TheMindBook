## In one paragraph
Book I opens with a title page declaring the work "The Setting-Face-to-Face to the Reality in the Intermediate State: The Great Deliverance by Hearing while on the After-Death Plane," drawn from "The Profound Doctrine of the Emancipating of the Consciousness by Meditation upon the Peaceful and Wrathful Deities," followed by an Obeisance to the Divine Body of Truth, the Divine Body of Perfect Endowment, Padma Sambhava, and the Gurus of the Three Bodies. The Introduction declares the doctrine to have three divisions — preliminaries, subject-matter, conclusion — and sets out a descending ladder of chances: the highest intellects are liberated by *The Guide*; failing that, devotees of ordinary wit apply the Transference of the Consciousness-Principle at the moments of death; failing that, they must persevere in listening to this Great Doctrine of Liberation by Hearing. The ritual sections then specify who reads (guru, then brother in the Faith, then a trusted friend, then any competent reader), where (lips at the ear of the corpse without touching it; or, absent a corpse, from the dead person's own bed or seat with the spirit summoned and imagined present), and under what conditions (no weeping or wailing by relatives; offerings and a fixed sequence of "Path of Good Wishes" prayers first; the Thödol itself read seven times or thrice). Part I instructs the officiant to name the symptoms of death aloud as they arrive, to lay the dying one in the "Lying Posture of a Lion" and press the throat arteries so the vital-force passes out through the Brahmanic aperture, and — at the instant the expiration ceases — to address the dying person as "O nobly-born" and set him face to face with the Primary Clear Light, which the text identifies as his own intellect and consciousness, void, shining and blissful, the Dharma-Kaya itself. Recognition at that instant is said to bring instantaneous Liberation without any Intermediate State; failure to hold it is followed by a Secondary Clear Light dawning somewhat more than a meal-time later, when the consciousness-principle, now outside the body in a "shining illusory-body," sees its relatives, hears their wailing, and cannot tell whether it is dead — the last quiet interval before the karmic illusions of Part II begin.

## Core claims
- The text claims this doctrine "confereth spiritual freedom on devotees of ordinary wit while in the Intermediate State," and that it has three divisions: the preliminaries, the subject-matter, and the conclusion.
- It claims a ranked sequence of opportunities: highest intellects are liberated by *The Guide*; if not, they practise the Transference during the Bardo of the Moments of Death, "which giveth automatic liberation by one's merely remembering it"; devotees of ordinary wit ought certainly to be freed thereby; if not, they must persevere in listening to the Great Doctrine of Liberation by Hearing while in the Bardo of Reality.
- It claims the reading is unnecessary if the Transference has been effectually employed — the Thödol is the fallback, not the first instrument.
- It claims the reading works on an absent body: seated on the dead person's accustomed bed or seat, the reader expounds "the power of the Truth," summons the spirit, imagines it present and listening, and reads.
- It instructs that no relative or "fond mate" be allowed to weep or wail, "as such is not good [for the deceased]."
- The text instructs the reader to put his lips close to the ear "without actually touching it"; a note generalizes this into a rule — "according to Tibetan belief, the body of a dying [person] should not be touched" — and gives the reason: so the normal departure of the consciousness-principle through the aperture of Brahma is not interfered with; departure "through some other bodily aperture" is said to lead to birth in one of a class of states the scan garbles. The one worked example survives in sense: if the departure is through the ear, the deceased "will be obliged — ere he can return to human birth — to go to the world of the [ ]," glossed in the same note as "fairy-like celestial musicians," where bliss and music are said to be the predominant quality of existence. (The Sanskrit name of that class is unreadable in the scan and is not supplied here.)
- It claims that when the expiration has ceased, the vital-force sinks into the nerve-centre of Wisdom (a note locates this in the heart) and "the Knower" experiences the Clear Light of the natural condition.
- It claims the window for the setting-face-to-face is the interval before the vital-force rushes into the left nerve, ordinarily "as long as the inspiration is still present, or about the time required for eating a meal."
- It claims that at the moment the vital-force passes out through the Brahmanic aperture, the first glimpsing of the Bardo of the Clear Light of Reality, "which is the Infallible Mind of the Dharma-Kaya, is experienced by all sentient beings" — universally, not only by adepts.
- It claims the duration of the ensuing swoon is uncertain and constitutional: for those of evil life or unsound nerves only "so long as would take to snap a finger"; for others a meal-time; and it reports that some texts say about three and one-half days and "most other [religious treatises] say for four days," during which the setting-face-to-face ought to be persevered in.
- It claims the repetition of the address is to be continued until "a yellowish liquid beginneth to appear from the various apertures of the bodily organs" — a physical terminus for the ritual.
- It claims the Primary Clear Light IS the hearer's own present intellect and consciousness: void, without characteristic or colour, yet "unobstructed, shining, thrilling, and blissful," the All-Good, the Dharma-Kaya, the Immutable Light identified with Buddha Amitabha — not an external light met, but the recognizing of what one already is.
- It claims that recognition is the whole of what is required: "Knowing this is sufficient."
- It claims three effects from repeating the address three or seven times: recalling the guru's former setting-face-to-face; causing "the naked consciousness to be recognized as the Clear Light"; and, by recognizing one's own self, becoming "permanently united with the Dharma-Kaya," whereupon "Liberation will be certain."
- It claims non-recognition of the Primary Clear Light is followed by a Secondary Clear Light dawning "somewhat more than a meal-time period" after the expiration has ceased — a second, lesser chance, not a closed door.
- It claims that at the secondary stage the vital-force flows down the right or left nerve "according to one's good or bad karma" and leaves by some aperture of the body, after which "cometh a lucid condition of the mind."
- It claims the disembodied consciousness-principle then asks itself "Am I dead, or am I not dead?" and cannot determine; it sees its relatives as before and hears their wailings, while the terrifying karmic illusions and the apparitions caused by the Lords of Death have not yet come.
- It claims that if the instructions land in this state, "by the meeting of the Mother-Reality and the Offspring-Reality, karma controlleth not," and "the Clear Light on the Path dispelleth the power of karma" as the sun's rays dispel darkness.
- It sorts the dead twice over, and the two sortings are separate. First by attainment, governing *which* words are read: the perfected stage (called thrice by name, given the Clear Light address again), the visualizing stage (given the tutelary-deity meditation), and "the common folk" (told simply to meditate upon the Great Compassionate Lord). Then, after that, it names three categories for whom the instruction is specifically necessary or indispensable: those set face to face while living but never made familiar with it; those familiar but, "because of the violence of the disease causing death," mentally unable to withstand illusions; and those previously familiar who "have become liable to pass into the miserable states of existence, owing to breach of vows or failure to perform essential obligations honestly."

## Concepts
- **Bardo Thödol / Great Doctrine of Liberation by Hearing** — The text titles itself "The Setting-Face-to-Face to the Reality in the Intermediate State: The Great Deliverance by Hearing while on the After-Death Plane," extracted from "The Profound Doctrine of the Emancipating of the Consciousness by Meditation upon the Peaceful and Wrathful Deities." Its operating premise is that hearing, not doing, is the vehicle of liberation for the dying and the dead.
- **Setting-face-to-face** — The text's name for the officiant's act: confronting the dying or dead person with a truth he is presently experiencing but failing to recognize. It has three applications, listed in the Practical Application section: to the symptoms of death; to Reality while in the Intermediate State ("the great vivid reminder"); and to the methods of closing the doors of the womb when seeking rebirth.
- **The three Bardos of this cycle** — A footnote to that list identifies them, but the scan loses the third name: "The first Bardo is the Chikhai Bardo; the second, the Chonyid Bardo; the third, [ ]" — followed by a cross-reference whose page number is also dropped. The name of the third Bardo is **not** recoverable in this unit and is not supplied here.
- **The Transference of the Consciousness-Principle** — The practice applied at the moments of death "which giveth automatic liberation by one's merely remembering it," to be applied once all the symptoms of death are complete. A note limits the sense of "Liberation" here: for the average devotee it means chiefly "a liberation of the 'life-flux' from the dying body" in a manner securing the greatest possible after-death consciousness and a happy rebirth; only for the exceptional saint can it prevent any break in the stream of consciousness between a conscious death and the next state.
- **Consciousness-principle** — The translator's chosen term. A note explains that "soul" was deliberately avoided because Buddhism as a whole "denies the existence of a permanent, unchanging personal-consciousness such as the Semitic faiths and animistic creeds in general understand thereby"; where such a term occurs it is to be taken as "Consciousness-principle" or "Compound of consciousness," akin to what Southern Buddhists call the "life-flux."
- **The Knower** — A note glosses the Tibetan as "Knower," "i.e. the mind in its knowing, or cognizing function." It is the Knower that experiences the Clear Light of the natural condition, and the Knower that later "hovereth within those places to which its activities had been limited."
- **Clear Light of the natural condition** — A note glosses the underlying term as "devoid of formative activity," i.e. the mind in its natural or primal state, quiescent, as opposed to the incarnate mind driven continuously into formative activity by the five senses. The note adds that the conscious recognition of the Clear Light "induceth an ecstatic condition of consciousness such as saints and mystics of the West have called Illumination."
- **Primary Clear Light** — *In the text:* the Clear Light seen at the moment of death, addressed in the first setting-face-to-face; the text itself does not use the word "primary" until the transition passage ("Thus the primary Clear Light is recognized and Liberation attained"). *In a footnote:* "immediately after the passing of the [vital-force] into the median-nerve, the person dying experiences the Clear Light in its primordial purity, the Dharma-Kaya unobscured."
- **Secondary Clear Light** — *In the text:* what dawns "in somewhat more than a meal-time period after that the expiration hath ceased," if it be feared the primary was not recognized; at that point, "according to one's good or bad karma, the vital-force floweth down into either the right or left nerve and goeth out through any of the apertures [of the body]," and "then cometh a lucid condition of the mind" (a note on that phrase: "With the departure of the consciousness-principle from the human body there is a psychic thrill which gives rise to a state of lucidity"). *In the same footnote as above:* one who cannot hold fast to the first experience "next experiences the secondary Clear Light, having fallen to a lower state of the [Bardo] wherein the Dharma-Kaya is dimmed by karmic obscurations." A separate translator's comment adds that the first stage's ecstasy is "of the greatest intensity" and "the succeeding stage is less intense," and gives the ball-simile: "A ball let bounding reacheth its greatest height at the first bound; the second bound is lower, and each succeeding bound is still lower until the ball comes to rest." *Inference, this note's, not the source's:* taken together the footnotes make the difference one of intensity and of vantage rather than of kind — the same Dharma-Kaya, dimmed. The scripture itself never says this; it simply prescribes a second address.
- **Dharma-Kaya / Unborn Dharma-Kaya / Great Perpendicular Path** — The "Body of Truth," which a note calls the purest and highest state of being, a state of supramundane consciousness devoid of the mental limitations arising from the contact of primordial consciousness with matter. Those set face to face with the fundamental Clear Light are said to obtain "the Unborn Dharma-Kaya, by the Great Perpendicular Path" — glossed in a note as the "Great Straight Upward Path," the Northern Buddhist doctrine that emancipation, even Buddhahood, may be won instantaneously, without entering the after-death plane and without further suffering along the pathway of normal evolution. The same note stages the ascent — "Faith is the first step on the Secret Pathway. Then cometh Illumination; and, with it, Certainty; and, when the Goal is won, Emancipation" — and immediately qualifies it: "here again such implies very unusual proficiency in yoga, as well as much accumulated merit of good karma on the part of the devotee." The condition is stated as a chain: if the disciple can be made to grasp the Truth as soon as the guru reveals it, "that is to say, if he hath the power to die consciously," and at the supreme moment of quitting the body can recognize the Clear Light and become one with it, "all sangsaric bonds of illusion are broken asunder immediately."
- **The All-Good** — The name given to the Reality that the hearer's own void intellect is said to be; a note gives the Sanskrit sense as "Universal (or Complete) Good," a state in which "the experiencer and the thing experienced are inseparably one and the same, as, for example, the yellowness of gold cannot be separated from gold, nor saltness from salt."
- **Buddha Amitabha / the Immutable Light** — Thine own consciousness, "shining, void, and inseparable from the Great Body of Radiance," is said to have no birth nor death and to be "the Immutable Light — Buddha Amitabha." A note treats the All-Good state and the Amitabha state as ultimately the same state seen from two viewpoints — the first emphasizing the mind of the All-Good, the second the enlightening power.
- **Father and Mother (Radiance and Voidness)** — A note explains the pairing used in the address: the Radiance (or, as here, the intellect) is the Father, and the Voidness perceiving it, the Mother; from the union of the two states of mind is born the Dharma-Kaya, the state of Perfect Enlightenment.
- **Vital-force, median-nerve, right and left nerves** — The physiological machinery of the death process. *In the text:* when the expiration has ceased the vital-force has sunk into the nerve-centre of Wisdom; "the interval between the cessation of the expiration and the cessation of the inspiration is the time during which the vital-force remaineth in the median-nerve"; then, "being thrown backwards and flying downwards through the right and left nerves, the Intermediate State momentarily dawns." *In notes:* the term is glossed "vital-air" or "vital-force" or "psychic-force"; the two channels are the right and left [psychic] nerves; and the residence in the median-nerve is said to last "so long as the heart continues to throb."
- **The meal-time measure** — The unit's only unit of elapsed time, used three times (the window for the setting-face-to-face, the swoon in some persons, and the delay before the Secondary Clear Light): "about the time required for eating a meal." A footnote treats it as a genuine measure rather than a figure of speech: when the text took form "the reckoning of time was, apparently, yet primitive," mechanical time-keepers being unknown — "as still prevails in many parts of Tibet, where the period [is] frequently mentioned in old religious books — a perio[d] …". **OCR flag:** the note breaks off there, mid-word, exactly as it is about to define the period; the definition is not recoverable.
- **Brahmanic aperture** — The aperture of Brahma at the crown, the correct exit for the consciousness-principle. The artery-pressing and the prohibition on touching the body both exist, in the text's account, to force or preserve this route.
- **Lying Posture of a Lion** — The posture in which the dying one is placed when the expiration is about to cease: turned over on the right side.
- **Nerve of sleep** — Pressed firmly after the expiration has completely ceased, immediately before the address to the Fundamental Clear Light.
- **Symptoms of death (the sinkings)** — The graded bodily signs the officiant is to name aloud as they arrive; the text prints only the first, "Now the symptoms of earth sinking into water are come." A note supplies the three chief ones with their symbolic names: (1) a bodily sensation of pressure, "earth sinking into water"; (2) a sensation of clammy coldness as though immersed in water, gradually merging into feverish heat, "water sinking into fire"; (3) a feeling as though the body were being blown to atoms, "fire sinking into air." Each is said to be accompanied by visible external changes — loss of control over facial muscles, loss of hearing, loss of sight.
- **The state of swoon** — What "the common people" call the interval between the cessation of the expiration and the cessation of the inspiration, "the state wherein the consciousness-principle hath fainted away." Its duration is said to depend on constitution, nerves, and previous practice in dhyana. Notes add that the consciousness-principle is "not necessarily resident in the body all the while" — in certain conditions of trance it can be induced to leave — but that it normally quits the body at the moment called death, holding "a subtle magnetic-like relationship" with the body until the time-limits given in the text run out; that only "for adepts in yoga" would the departure be accomplished without break in the continuity of the stream of consciousness; and that "the death process is the reverse of the birth process, birth being the incarnating and death the discarnating of the consciousness-principle," both alike "a passing from one state of consciousness into another" — "just as a babe … wakes up in this world and learns by experience the nature of this world, so likewise a person [after] death must wake up in the Bardo world and become familiar with its own peculiar conditions." The Bardo body, "formed of matter in an invisible or ethereal-like state," is called "an exact duplicate of the human body, from which it is separated in the process of death," retaining the consciousness-principle and the psychic nerve-system.
- **Thought-body / shining illusory-body** — The body one has in the second stage of the Bardo: a note glosses the terms as "mental-body," "desire body," "thought-body," and "pure (or shining) illusory body," the ethereal counterpart of the physical body of the earth-plane.
- **Mother-Reality and Offspring-Reality** — A note defines the pair: the Offspring-Truth is that realized in this world through deep meditation; the Mother-Truth is the Primal or Fundamental Truth, experienced only after death while the Knower is in the state of equilibrium. "What a photograph is compared to the object photographed, the Offspring-Reality is to the Mother-Reality." Their meeting is said to put karma out of control.
- **Perfected stage and visualizing stage** — The two grades of devotee for whom the second-stage instructions differ: the perfected are called thrice by name and given the Clear Light address again; the visualizing are read the introductory descriptions and the text of the Meditation on their tutelary deity.
- **Tutelary deity; the Great Compassionate Lord** — The visualizing-stage devotee is directed to his own named tutelary deity, meditated upon "as if he were the reflection of the moon in water, apparent yet inexistent [in itself]." Those "of the common folk" are simply told: "Meditate upon the Great Compassionate Lord."

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(For a scripture-as-instruction unit, the "cases" are the prescribed ritual procedures and the contingencies the text enumerates — recorded here with the same completeness the template demands of examples.)*
- **The Obeisances** — Four lines opening Book I: to the Divine Body of Truth, "the Incomprehensible, Boundless Light"; to the Divine Body of Perfect Endowment, "Who are the Lotus and the Peaceful and the Wrathful Deities"; to the Lotus-born Incarnation, Padma Sambhava, "Who is the Protector of all sentient beings"; and to "the Gurus, the Three Bodies," obeisance. A translator's note attached here insists on the interiority of the whole pantheon: "These Deities are in ourselves. They are not something apart from us. We are one with all that is, in every state of sentient existence, from the lowest worlds of suffering to the highest states of bliss and Perfect Enlightenment" — the Lotus Order representing the deified principles of the vocal functions, the Peaceful those of the heart or feeling, the Wrathful those of mentality (limiting or reasoning, imagination or memory) centred in the brain.
- **The order of precedence for the reader** — Stated twice with slightly different lists. In *The Reading of this Thödol*: if the body is present, just when the expiration has ceased, either a lama "who hath been as a [guru] to the deceased," or a brother in the Faith whom the deceased trusted, or a friend for whom the deceased had great affection. In *Part I*: best is the guru from whom the deceased received guiding instructions; failing that a brother of the Faith; failing that "a learned man of the same Faith"; and failing all these, "a person who can read correctly and distinctly ought to read this many times over." The claimed outcome of the fallback is not diminished: the deceased "will be put in mind of what he had [previously] heard of the setting-face-to-face and will at once come to recognise that Fundamental Light and undoubtedly obtain Liberation."
- **The reading over an absent body** — "If there be no corpse, then the bed or the seat to which the deceased had been accustomed should be occupied [by the reader], who ought to expound the power of the Truth. Then, summoning the spirit [of the deceased], imagine it to be present there listening, and read." A note supplies a formula of Truth to be used whether or not the corpse is there — the surviving OCR gives its skeleton as an appeal to the power of Truth ("Although no Trinity is …, by the power of that Truth I summon thee") but the clause is too damaged to reconstruct; see the OCR flags above. The same note explains why a corpse may be absent — death away from home, accidental death entailing loss or destruction of the body, or removal of the body in accordance with astrological rules immediately after death — and asserts that the deceased in the invisible Bardo body must attend.
- **The prohibition on weeping** — "During this time no relative or fond mate should be allowed to weep or to wail, as such is not good [for the deceased]; so restrain them."
- **The lips-at-the-ear rule** — The reader puts "the lips close to the ear [of the body] without actually touching it." It recurs at each address: the resolution passage is spoken "in a low tone of voice in the ear"; the reader "shall put his lips close to the ear, and shall repeat it distinctly, clearly impressing it upon the dying person so as to prevent his mind from wandering even for a moment"; and the death-moment address is to be repeated "many times in the ear of the person dying, even before the expiration hath ceased."
- **The offering and the prayer sequence (Practical Application by the Officiant)** — If a grand offering can be gathered, offer it in worship of the Trinity; if not, arrange whatever can be gathered, plus objects on which the officiant can concentrate and "mentally create as illimitable an offering as possible." Then recite the "Path of Good Wishes Invoking the Aid of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas" seven times or thrice. Then read distinctly and with proper intonation the "Path of Good Wishes Giving Protection from Fear in the [Bardo]," the "Path of Good Wishes for Safe Delivery from the Dangerous Pitfalls of the Bardo," and the "Root Words of the Bardo." Then the Great Thödol itself, "either seven times or thrice, according to the occasion."
- **The physical management of the dying body** — When the expiration is about to cease, turn the dying one over on the right side (the Lying Posture of a Lion). The throbbing arteries on the right and left of the throat are to be pressed. "If the person dying be disposed to sleep, or if the sleeping state advances, that should be arrested, and the arteries pressed gently but firmly." The stated purpose: "Thereby the vital-force will not be able to return from the median-nerve and will be sure to pass out through the Brahmanic aperture."
- **The address at the moment breathing ceases** — Delivered by name: "O nobly-born (so and so by name), the time hath now come for thee to seek the Path [in reality]. Thy breathing is about to cease. Thy guru hath set thee face to face before with the Clear Light; and now thou art about to experience it in its Reality in the Bardo state, wherein all things are like the void and cloudless sky, and the naked, spotless intellect is like unto a transparent vacuum without circumference or centre. At this moment, know thou thyself; and abide in that state. I, too, at this time, am setting thee face to face."
- **The naming of the symptoms** — If the dying person can diagnose the symptoms himself, "use [of the knowledge] should have been made ere this." If not, "either the [ ] or a [ ], or a brother in the Faith with whom the one [dying] was very intimate" is kept at hand — the first two offices are italicized terms the scan mangles beyond reading and are not supplied here — "who will vividly impress upon the one [dying] the symptoms [of death] as they appear in due order," beginning: "Now the symptoms of earth sinking into water are come."
- **The resolution enjoined as the symptoms complete** — "O nobly-born (or, if it be a priest, O Venerable Sir), let not thy mind be distracted." Then, by name: "O nobly-born, that which is called death being come to thee now, resolve thus: 'O this now is the hour of death. By taking advantage of this death, I will so act, for the good of all sentient beings, peopling the illimitable expanse of the heavens, as to obtain the Perfect Buddhahood, by resolving on love and compassion towards [them, and by directing my entire effort to] the Sole Perfection.'" The fallback resolution follows: "Even if I cannot realize it, yet will I know this Bardo, and, mastering the Great Body of Union in Bardo, will appear in whatever [shape] will benefit [all beings] whomsoever; I will serve all sentient beings, infinite in number as are the limits of the sky." He is then to remember whatever devotional practices he was accustomed to perform during his lifetime.
- **The Great Symbol** — The dying one is told that at this time, "when the Dharma-Kaya of Clear Light [in the state] after death can be realized for the benefit of all sentient beings," he should know that he is in that state and resolve to obtain "the best boon of the State of the Great Symbol." A note conditions the promise sharply: realization of the Ultimate Truth is possible here "providing [that] advance on the Path hath been made by the deceased before death. Otherwise he cannot benefit now, and must wander on into lower and lower conditions … as determined by [karma], until rebirth."
- **The forms in which the liberated one will reappear (note)** — Attached to the fallback resolution's "will appear in whatever [shape] will benefit [all beings] whomsoever," a note reports that the Tibetan is "here unusually literally rendered" and reads "will appear in whatever will subdue [for beneficial ends] whomsoever," then spells the principle out: "to appeal to a Shaivite devotee, the form of Shiva is assumed; to a Buddhist, the form of the Buddha Shakya Muni; to a Christian, the form of Jesus; to a Moslem, the form of the Prophet; and so on for other religious devotees" — and, beyond religions, a form appropriate to the occasion for children, for common people, for kings or rulers, and for kings' ministers of state.
- **The address to a lama or a superior** — After the expiration has completely ceased and the nerve of sleep is pressed, if the dead person was a lama "or a person higher or more learned than thyself," the words differ: "Reverend Sir, now that thou art experiencing the Fundamental Clear Light, try to abide in that state which now thou art experiencing."
- **The address to everyone else — the Primary Clear Light setting-face-to-face** — The central instruction of the unit, quoted in full under Key quotes: the hearer is told that what he is now experiencing is the Radiance of the Clear Light of Pure Reality; that his present intellect, void and colourless, "is the very Reality, the All-Good"; that this voidness is not the voidness of nothingness but the intellect itself, "unobstructed, shining, thrilling, and blissful," which is "the very consciousness, the All-good Buddha"; that consciousness and intellect are inseparable and their union is the Dharma-Kaya; and that his own consciousness, inseparable from the Great Body of Radiance, is the Immutable Light, Buddha Amitabha.
- **The three claimed effects of the repetition** — Repeat distinctly and clearly three or seven times. First, it recalls to the dying one's mind the former setting-face-to-face performed by his guru while living. Second, "it will cause the naked consciousness to be recognized as the Clear Light." Third, "recognizing one's own self [thus], one becometh permanently united with the Dharma-Kaya and Liberation will be certain."
- **The needle-on-a-thread simile (note)** — A long note explains why most fail: "so very rare" is the spiritual efficiency required that the normal mentality of the dying is unequal to the feat of holding on to the state in which the Clear Light shines. The lamas' simile is "a needle balanced and set rolling on a thread" — so long as it retains its balance it remains on the thread; eventually gravitation acts, and it falls. The mentality of the dying "momentarily enjoys a condition of balance, or perfect equilibrium, and of oneness"; being unfamiliar with such a state — an ecstatic state of non-ego, of subliminal consciousness — the consciousness-principle lacks the power to function in it, karmic propensities becloud it with thoughts of personality, individualized being and dualism, and it "falls away from the Clear Light." The note names the cause bluntly: "It is ideation of ego, self, which prevents the realization of Nirvana … and so the Wheel of Life continues to turn."
- **The second-stage sorting by attainment** — Read in this order in the text: devotees of the perfected stage are called thrice by name and given the Clear Light setting-face-to-face "over and over"; devotees of the visualizing stage are read the introductory descriptions and the text of the Meditation on their tutelary deity, then addressed "O thou of noble-birth, meditate upon thine own tutelary deity — [here the deity's name is to be mentioned by the reader]"; those "of the common folk" are told only, "Meditate upon the Great Compassionate Lord." The text then claims of the whole procedure: "By thus being set-face-to-face even those who would not be expected to recognise the Bardo [unaided] are undoubtedly certain to recognise it."
- **The three cases requiring the instruction** — Enumerated after that claim, as the reasons it is needed: (1) persons set face to face by a guru while living "yet who have not made [themselves] familiar with it" and cannot recognise it clearly by themselves — for them a guru or brother of the Faith will have to impress it vividly; (2) those who have made themselves familiar with the teachings "yet who, because of the violence of the disease causing death, may be mentally unable to withstand illusions" — for these "this instruction is absolutely necessary"; (3) those previously familiar who "have become liable to pass into the miserable states of existence, owing to breach of vows or failure to perform essential obligations honestly" — to these "this [instruction] is indispensable." The text then adds the ranking of the two chances: "If the first stage of the Bardo hath been taken by the forelock, that [is] best. But if not, by application of this distinct recalling [to the deceased], while in the second stage of the [Bardo], the intellect is awakened and attaineth liberation."
- **The state of not knowing one is dead** — "When the consciousness-principle getteth outside [the body, it sayeth to itself], 'Am I dead, or am I not dead?' It cannot determine. It seeth its relatives and connexions as it had been used to seeing them before. It even heareth the wailings. The terrifying karmic illusions have not yet dawned. Nor have the frightful apparitions or experiences caused by the Lords of Death yet come."
- **The closing claim of Part I** — "That which is called the second stage of the Bardo dawneth upon the thought-body. The Knower hovereth within those places to which its activities had been limited. If at this time this special teaching be applied efficiently, then the purpose will be fulfilled; for the karmic illusions will not have come yet, and, therefore, he [the deceased] cannot be turned hither and thither [from his aim of achieving Enlightenment]."
- **The comparative citations in the notes (editor's voice, not the scripture)** — Not confined to one section: they run alongside the ritual instructions from the Practical Application through the second-stage passages, setting the Tibetan procedure beside late-medieval Western *ars moriendi* material. *The Book of the Craft of Dying*, chap. VI, "in Bodleian MS. 423 (fifteenth century), Comper's ed.", is quoted at the prayer-sequence instruction: the prayers "may be conveniently said upon a sick man that laboureth to his end," and if he "be a religious person" then "there shall be said first the litany, with the psalms and orisons that he used therewith"; and if the sick man "may not, or can not, say the orisons and prayers aforesaid, some of the assistants [i.e. bystanders] ought to say them before him with a loud voice, changing the words there as they ought to be changed." *The Craft to Know Well to Die*, chap. IV, is quoted twice from Comper's edition, once for "After all these things he [the person dying] ought to say three times, if he [can], the words that follow." *The Book of the Craft of Dying*, chap. V, appears again at the lips-at-the-ear passage: "Also, if he that shall die have long time and space to bethink himself, and he not taken with hasty [death], then may be read afore him, of them that be about him …" (the clause breaks off in the scan). At the symptoms-of-death instruction a further note cites *Ars Moriendi* (fifteenth century, Comper's ed.): "When any of likelihood shall die [i.e. is likely to die], then it is most necessary to have a special friend, the which will heartily help and pray for him, and therewith counsel the sick for the weal [i.e. health] of his soul." The Eastern citations are two: at the Mother-Reality passage, the *Tantra of the Great Liberation* as edited by Arthur Avalon, London — "The man blinded by the darkness of ignorance, the fool caught in the meshes of his actions, and the illiterate man, by listening to this Great Tantra, are released from the bonds of [karma]"; and at the "divine mind of the Buddha" instruction, a work the scan gives as "The Diamond [-or Immutable] …" with its Chinese commentary (London, the date read as 1912) — "Every form or quality of phenomena is transient and illusive. When the mind realizes that the phenomena of life are not real phenomena, the Lord Buddha may then be clearly perceived," and "The spiritual Buddha must be realized within the mind, otherwise there can be no true perception of the Lord Buddha." (That work's title and its translator's surname are both garbled and are not reconstructed here.) A note signed "Atal Bihari Ghosh" is attached to the discussion of the consciousness in the process of death.
- **The translator's claim about the source of the death-science** — A note states: "The translator held that the science of death, as expounded in this treatise, has been arrived at through the actual experiencing of death on the part of learned lamas, who, while dying, have explained to their pupils the very process of death itself, in analytical and elaborate detail."

## Thread
This unit is the hinge on which the whole Bardo Thödol turns, and it is deliberately front-loaded: everything the text most values happens in its first pages and in the shortest possible time. The introductory sections build a descending ladder — *The Guide*, then the Transference, then the reading of this Thödol — so that the scripture presents itself as the *last* of several instruments, addressed to those who have already missed the earlier ones. Part I then makes the highest claim in the book: that at the instant of death every sentient being without exception glimpses the Clear Light of Reality, so that liberation depends not on merit accumulated but on recognition achieved in a window "about the time required for eating a meal." The two-stage structure (Primary, then Secondary Clear Light) establishes the book's characteristic rhythm of descending chances, which the remainder of Book I extends across days rather than minutes: what is missed here reappears in Part II as the Peaceful Deities, then the Wrathful, each dawning likewise as the deceased's own thought-forms and each likewise turning on recognition. The doctrinal key planted here — that what appears is one's own consciousness, and that recognizing it as such is liberation — is the single interpretive instruction the rest of the text repeats in ever more elaborate imagery. The unit also fixes the book's dual character: it is at once a metaphysical treatise on what death is and a working funerary script with postures, pressure points, prayer counts, and a physical signal (the yellowish liquid) for when to stop reading.

## Connections
- Forward, within Book I: this unit ends exactly where the Bardo of the Experiencing of Reality (the Chonyid Bardo) begins; the text there states that "even though the Primary Clear Light be not recognized, the Clear Light of the second Bardo being recognized, Liberation will be attained," and that failing that the third Bardo dawns and "the karmic illusions come to shine." The Lords of Death and the karmic illusions named here as *not yet arrived* are the subject matter of that next unit.
- Forward, within Book I: the six states of Bardo (womb, dream-state, ecstatic equilibrium in deep meditation, moment of death, experiencing of Reality, inverse process of sangsaric existence) are enumerated at the start of Part II, not here; this unit covers only the first of the three the deceased is told he will experience.
- Forward, to Book II: the third application listed in *The Practical Application of this Thödol by the Officiant* — "the methods of closing the doors of the womb while in the Intermediate State when seeking rebirth" — is the business of the third Bardo of this cycle, which a footnote here enumerates as third after the Chikhai and the Chonyid, though the scan has lost the name it gives.
- Backward, to the editorial front matter: the Introduction's numbered sections (among them "The Death Ceremonies," "The Bardo or After-Death State," "The Esoteric Significance of the Forty-Nine Days of the Bardo") stand outside this unit and are the editor's voice; the material here is the translated text and its footnotes, and the two should not be conflated when the two are read together.
