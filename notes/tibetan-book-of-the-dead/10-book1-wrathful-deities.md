# Ch 10 — The Dawning of the Wrathful Deities, from the Eighth to the Fourteenth Day; and the Conclusion, Showing the Fundamental Importance of the Bardo Teachings

## In one paragraph
This unit is the second half of the Chonyid Bardo liturgy: having failed at the seven "stages of ambuscade" during which the Peaceful and Knowledge-Holding Deities dawned, the deceased is now met by "the fifty-eight flame-enhaloed, wrathful, blood-drinking deities," whom the text states outright are "only the former Peaceful Deities in changed aspect." Days Eight through Twelve bring the five Herukas of the five Orders — Buddha, Vajra, Ratna, Padma and Karma — each three-faced, six-handed, four-footed, embraced by his Krotishaurima consort who puts a blood-filled shell to his mouth, each said by the text itself to issue from the hearer's own brain (Buddha-Heruka from the brain simply; the other four from the eastern, southern, western and northern quarters of it in turn) and each declared to be "in reality" the corresponding peaceful Bhagavan. The Thirteenth Day brings the eight Kerimas and the eight animal-headed Htamenmas; the Fourteenth the four Female Door-Keepers and the twenty-eight various-headed mighty goddesses, after which the text states the governing formula — the Peaceful Deities emanate from the Voidness of the Dharma-Kaya, the Wrathful from its Radiance — and warns that non-recognition collapses all of them into Maha-Kala and Dharma-Raja, the Lord of Death, with his record-board. The refrain never changes: fear not, these are thine own thought-forms, thine own tutelary deities; recognize and merge in at-one-ment and Buddhahood is won "in a moment." The Conclusion then makes the book's case for itself: this Thodol liberates by hearing alone, is remembered in the Bardo without a word omitted, saves even those who have committed the five boundless sins, and must be read in congregations, over the pillows of the sick, and beside all corpses.

## Core claims
- The text claims the wrathful deities are not other beings: "the fifty-eight flame-enhaloed, wrathful, blood-drinking deities come to dawn, who are only the former Peaceful Deities in changed aspect — according to the place [or psychic-centre of the …" **[OCR: the sentence is cut off at the foot of p. 131 in this scan; the clause explaining the psychic-centre correspondence is lost. The text layer of the following page ends with the stray, unplaceable fragment "Etc brun psychk" — probably the wreck of the same footnote, but nothing is proposed from it. This EPUB contains page images for only eleven plate/front-matter pages (none for the printed pp. 131 or 136), so the "render the page image" check could not be applied here — see FLAG note in Examples.]**
- Each Heruka is identified with a peaceful Buddha in the same breath as his terrifying description: Buddha-Heruka is "in reality … the Bhagavan Vairochana, the Father-Mother"; Vajra-Heruka is "in reality [they are] the Bhagavan Vajra-Sattva, the Father and Mother"; Ratna-Heruka is "the Father-Mother Bhagavan Ratna-Sambhava"; Padma-Heruka is "the Father-Mother Bhagavan Amitabha"; Karma-Heruka is "the Father-Mother Bhagavan Amogha-Siddhi."
- The stated origin of the visions is internal and anatomical, and it is stated in the translated liturgy itself (the words the officiant reads to the deceased), not in an editorial footnote: each Heruka "will come forth from within thine own brain" — Buddha-Heruka from the brain simply, Vajra-Heruka from "the eastern quarter of thy brain," Ratna from the southern, Padma from the western, Karma from the northern. The goddesses likewise issue "from within thine own brain."
- The text gives the general formula: "the Peaceful Deities emanate from the Voidness of the Dharma-Kaya … From the Radiance of the Dharma-Kaya emanate the Wrathful Deities."
- Recognition and liberation are claimed to be simultaneous: "Recognition [of them] and the obtaining of liberation will be simultaneous"; "Buddhahood will be obtained in a moment [of time]" is said to be the saying "which applieth now"; bearing this in mind, "one will obtain Buddhahood by merging, in at-one-ment, into the Radiances and the [—]" **[OCR: the sentence breaks off at the foot of the page; the final noun is lost and none is proposed.]**
- The text claims this Bardo is harder than the peaceful one because of fear — "recognition becometh more difficult; the intellect, gaining not in independence, passeth from one fainting state to a round of fainting states" — yet also easier, because "because of the dawning of the radiances — [which produce] fear, terror, and awe — the intellect is undistractedly alert in one-pointedness; that is why."
- It claims scholarship without esoteric practice fails here: "There are even discipline-holding abbots [or —] and doctors in metaphysical discourses who err at this stage, and, not recognizing, wander into the Sangsara. As for the common worldly folk, what need is there to mention them!" **[OCR: the bracketed gloss on "abbots" is printed "[or MiM&m]" and is unrecoverable; no reading proposed.]**
- It claims prior exposure suffices — meditating on the blood-drinking deities, worshipping or praising them, "or, at least, by having seen their painted likenesses and their images," produces recognition here: "In this lieth the art."
- It claims a physical, post-mortem test of esoteric attainment: abbots and metaphysicians who held the mystic doctrines only outwardly produce no rainbow-halo at the funeral pyre and no bone-relics from the ashes, "because when they lived the mystic [or esoteric] doctrines were never held within their heart."
- Conversely, it claims the least, most unrefined tantric devotee — inelegant in habits, not living according to his vows, perhaps unable to carry his practices to a successful issue — must not be disrespected; reverence to the doctrines he holds "alone" obtains liberation at this stage, and at his death "there will come at least one kind of sign," because "the esoteric [or mystic] doctrines possess great gift-waves."
- It claims ordinary competent mystics who have practised the visualizing and perfecting processes and the essence mantras "need not wander down this far on the Bardo" — at the cessation of breath they are led to the pure paradise realms by the Heroes, Heroines and Knowledge-Holders, with cloudless sky, rainbow radiance, sun-showers, incense scent, music in the skies, bone-relics and images as signs.
- It claims those who have meditated on the Great Perfection and the Great Symbol recognize the Clear Light at the moment of death and do not need this Thodol read at all.
- It claims the terrifying forms have no external existence: "Beyond the emanations of thine own intellectual faculties, externally, the Peaceful and the Wrathful Ones, the Blood-Drinking Ones, the Various-Headed Ones, the rainbow lights, the terrifying forms of the Lord of Death, exist not in reality: of this, there is no doubt."
- It claims the Bardo body cannot be harmed: "the body which now thou possessest being a mental-body of [karmic] propensities, though slain and chopped [to bits], cannot die … voidness cannot injure voidness."
- The Conclusion claims the doctrine "liberating by the hearing [of it] alone" reaches even the worst: "because even those who have committed the five boundless sins are sure to be liberated if they hear it by the path of the ear."
- It claims a single hearing suffices without comprehension: "Through having heard it once, even though one do not comprehend it, it will be remembered in the Intermediate State without a word being omitted, for the intellect becometh ninefold more lucid [there]."
- It claims mere non-disbelief is enough: "Liberation will be won through simply not disbelieving it upon hearing it."

## Concepts
- **The fifty-eight blood-drinking deities** — The wrathful assembly of the Chonyid Bardo. The text calls them "flame-enhaloed, wrathful, blood-drinking deities" and states they are "only the former Peaceful Deities in changed aspect." They comprise, in the text's own enumeration, the five Herukas with their consorts, the eight Kerimas, eight Htamenmas and four Door-Keepers (called collectively "these thirty wrathful deities, Herukas"), plus "the twenty-eight various-headed mighty goddesses" of the outer circle.
- **Heruka** — The wrathful form proper to each of the five Orders (Buddha, Vajra, Ratna/[Precious]-Gem, Padma/Lotus, Karma). Uniform iconography across all five: three faces, six hands, four feet firmly postured, embraced by a Mother in union, each holding a bell and a skull-bowl among the left hands.
- **Krotishaurima** — The name-element of each Heruka's consort ("the Mother"): Buddha-Krotishaurima, Vajra-Krotishaurima, Ratna-Krotishaurima, Padma-Krotishaurima, Karma-Krotishaurima. In every case "her right [hand] clinging to his neck, her left offering to his mouth a red shell [filled with blood]." An editor's footnote glosses the first as, in badly damaged OCR, the "[female] Buddha, Mother Wrathful …" **[OCR: footnote gloss garbled and only partly legible.]**
- **Tutelary deity** — The category under which the hearer is told to recognize the wrathful forms: "As it is thine own tutelary deity, be not terrified." Recognition converts terror into the meeting of acquaintances.
- **Thought-forms** — The text's repeated designation for every apparition: "Recognize whatever shineth to be the thought-forms of thine own intellectual faculties." Non-recognition is what makes them illusory and dangerous: "one's own thought-forms turn into illusory appearances, and one wandereth into the Sangsara."
- **Voidness of the Dharma-Kaya / Radiance of the Dharma-Kaya** — The two aspects from which the two orders of deities are said to emanate: the Peaceful from the Voidness, the Wrathful from the Radiance.
- **Mother Clear-Light and Offspring Clear-Light** — Upon the Mother Clear-Light "which one had been accustomed to formerly," a secondary Clear-Light, the Offspring Clear-Light, is produced; the two, "coming together like two intimate acquaintances, blend inseparably," and from this "a self-emancipating radiance dawneth."
- **Kerimas** — Eight goddesses of "the Abodes [or Eight Directions]" who emanate from the eastern quarter of the brain on the Thirteenth Day and surround the Five Blood-Drinking Fathers. Named individually (see Examples).
- **Htamenmas** — Eight animal-headed goddesses "of the [eight] regions [of the brain]" in the circle outside the Kerimas. An editor's footnote says the name, as pronounced from a Tibetan form, "is probably the name of an order of pre-Buddhistic deities belonging to the ancient [—] religion of Tibet." **[OCR: both the Tibetan form (printed "F^nr-jiMn^iifit") and the religion's name are destroyed in the text layer; no readings proposed.]**
- **Wang-chugma** — Named in the Twelfth Day passage as accompanying the Karmic Order's blood-drinkers, alongside "the Kerima" and "Htamenmas." An editor's footnote renders it from Tibetan as the Sanskrit for "Mighty Goddesses." **[OCR: the Sanskrit term itself is illegible in the scan.]**
- **The Four Female Door-Keepers** — Four animal-headed goddesses of the Fourteenth Day, each named for the implement she holds (goad, noose, iron chain, bell).
- **The Twenty-eight Mighty Goddesses** — Outer-circle goddesses: six Yoginis each of East, South, West and North, plus four Door-Keeping Yoginis. The text says they "emanate from the bodily powers of Ratna-Sambhava, [He] of the Six Heruka Deities"; a footnote records that the Block-Print instead reads that they "are emanations from the [?] of the self-produced Wrathful Deities." **[OCR: one word of the Block-Print variant is lost.]**
- **Maha-Kala and Dharma-Raja** — The forms all the deities take on for the non-recognizer: "all the Peaceful Deities will shine forth in the shape of Maha-Kala; and all the Wrathful Deities will shine [forth] in the form of Dharma-Raja, the Lord of Death."
- **The record-board** — The object the Lord of Death's forms carry in the hand; an editor's footnote glosses the Tibetan as a board (of wood, skin, or clay) "written over with … records of the deceased's life," and offers "wood register" and "record" or "inventory like a rent-roll" as renderings. **[OCR: the Tibetan term and part of the gloss are garbled.]**
- **Gift-waves** — The power ascribed to the esoteric doctrines by which reverence alone liberates; the editor's footnote explains that because the doctrines are based on Truth, one who follows or reveres them is "automatically brought into rapport with very definite psychic forces."
- **The five boundless sins** — Listed in a footnote to the Conclusion, but the list is largely destroyed in the scan. Only two items are legible: setting two religious bodies at war, and causing [something] to flow from the body of a Tathagata — the footnote itself glosses Tathagata as "(i.e. a Buddha)". **[OCR: the surviving fragment reads in full "Thttc tre : &u£ricld«. - **The Six Syllables** — The prayer asks that the thousand-thunder natural sound of Reality "be transmuted into the sounds of the Six Syllables"; a footnote identifies these as the essence mantra of Chenrazee **[OCR: the Sanskrit equivalent is printed "Aralofcrtcah" here and "Atfaiaktia" in the earlier footnote; no reading proposed]**, each syllable coloured to one of the six light-paths of the six states of existence.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(The unit is liturgy, not argument; its "examples" are the day-by-day apparitions, the similes, and the claimed physical signs.)*

**Introduction (pp. 131–136).** The text reviews the failure of the peaceful stage: there were "seven stages of ambuscade," and though multitudes are liberated by recognition at one or another, "the number of sentient beings being great, evil karma powerful, obscurations dense, propensities of too long standing, the Wheel of Ignorance and Illusion becometh neither exhausted nor accelerated"; "there is a vast preponderance of those who wander downwards unliberated." Hence the wrathful dawning. The introduction contains the unit's doctrinal hinge (the wrathful are the peaceful in changed aspect), the claim that fear both hinders and sharpens recognition, the failure of learning without initiation, the rainbow-halo/bone-relic test, and the defence of the unrefined tantric devotee.

**THE EIGHTH DAY — the Buddha (Heruka) Order.** "The Great Glorious Buddha-Heruka, dark-brown of colour; with three heads, six hands, and four feet firmly postured; the right [face] being white, the left, red, the central, dark-brown; the body emitting flames of radiance; the nine eyes widely opened, in terrifying gaze; the eyebrows quivering like lightning; the protruding teeth glistening and set over one another; giving vent to sonorous utterances of 'a-la-la' and 'ha-ha', and piercing whistling sounds; the hair of a reddish-yellow colour, standing on end, and emitting radiance; the heads adorned with dried [human] skulls, and the [symbols of the] sun and moon; black serpents and raw [human] heads forming a garland for the body." Right hands: wheel, sword, battle-axe. Left hands: bell, skull-bowl, ploughshare. Embraced by the Mother Buddha-Krotishaurima, her right hand at his neck, her left putting a red shell filled with blood to his mouth, "[making] a palatal sound like a crackling [and] a clashing sound, and a rumbling sound as loud as thunder." Radiant flames of wisdom blaze from every hair-pore, "each containing a flaming dorje"; the pair stand one leg bent and one straight and tense on a dais supported by "horned eagles" — an editor's footnote identifies these as a class of bird-beings "of Indian and Tibetan mythology," depicted with eagle head, human-bird body, two human-like arms, two eagle wings and two eagle feet, and says they symbolize energy and aspiration **[OCR: the footnote's name for them is destroyed (printed "£7^fnp^«") and the closing phrase is printed "tbey porsapii J oaofey and upiralion"; no name is proposed]**. They "come forth from within thine own brain." Instruction: "Fear that not. Be not awed. Know it to be the embodiment of thine own intellect. As it is thine own tutelary deity, be not terrified. Be not afraid, for in reality it is the Bhagavan Vairochana, the Father-Mother." Result: merging in at-one-ment, "Buddhahood in the [—] Kaya will be won" **[OCR: the Kaya is printed "Kitja" — unrecoverable; no reading proposed.]**

**THE NINTH DAY — the Vajra Order.** For those who flee "through awe and terror being begotten." Bhagavan Vajra-Heruka, dark-blue, three faces, six hands, four feet firmly postured. Right hands: dorje, skull-bowl, battle-axe. Left hands: bell, skull-bowl, ploughshare. Mother Vajra-Krotishaurima, right hand at his neck, left offering the blood-filled red shell. "Will issue from the eastern quarter of thy brain." Instruction: "Fear it not. Be not terrified. Be not awed. Know it to be the embodiment of thine own intellect… In reality [they are] the Bhagavan Vajra-Sattva, the Father and Mother. Believe in them. Recognizing them, liberation will be obtained at once."

**THE TENTH DAY — the [Precious]-Gem Order.** For those whose "obscurations of evil deeds" are too great. Ratna-Heruka, yellow; three faces (right white, left red, central darkish yellow), six hands, four feet firmly postured, "enhaloed in flames." Right hands: gem, trident-staff, baton. Left hands: bell, skull-bowl, trident-staff. Mother Ratna-Krotishaurima with the blood-filled red shell. Issues "from the southern quarter of thy brain." "In reality [they are] the Father-Mother Bhagavan Ratna-Sambhava."

**THE ELEVENTH DAY — the Lotus Order.** Bhagavan Padma-Heruka, "of reddish-black colour"; three faces (right white, left blue, central darkish red), six hands, four feet firmly postured. Right hands: lotus, trident-staff, club. Left hands: bell, skull-bowl filled with blood, small drum. Mother Padma-Krotishaurima, "the Father and Mother in union"; issues "from the western quarter of thy brain." Instruction adds a word absent from the others: "Fear that not. Be not terrified. Be not awed. **Rejoice.** Recognize [them] to be the product of thine own intellect." "In reality they are the Father-Mother Bhagavan Amitabha."

**THE TWELFTH DAY — the Karmic Order.** The blood-drinking deities of the Karmic Order come "accompanied by the Kerima, Htamenmas, and Wang-chugma." Karma-Heruka, dark green; three faces (right white, left red, middle dark green), "majestic [of appearance]," six hands, four feet firmly postured. Right hands: sword, trident-staff, club. Left hands: bell, skull-bowl, ploughshare. Mother Karma-Krotishaurima with the red shell; "the Father and Mother in union, issuing from the northern quarter of thy brain." "In reality they are the Father-Mother Bhagavan Amogha-Siddhi. Believe, and be humble; and be fond [of them]."

- **The lion-skin simile (Twelfth Day).** Introduced by "Through the guru's select teaching, one cometh to recognize them to be the thought-forms issuing from one's own intellectual faculties" — then the text's own explanation of how recognition works: "a person, upon recognizing a lion-skin [to be a lion-skin], is freed [from fear]; for though it be only a stuffed lion-skin, if one do not know it to be so actually, fear ariseth; but, upon being told by some person that it is a lion-skin only, one is freed from fear. Similarly here, too, when the bands of blood-drinking deities, huge of proportions, with very thick-set limbs, dawn as big as the skies, awe and terror are naturally produced in one. [But] as soon as the setting-face-to-face is heard [one] recognizeth them to be one's own tutelary deities and one's own thought-forms." This is followed immediately by the Mother/Offspring Clear-Light blending and self-emancipating radiance.

**The summary teaching after the Fourteenth Day.** "Since these Twenty-eight Mighty Goddesses emanate from the bodily powers of Ratna-Sambhava, [He] of the Six Heruka Deities, recognize them." ## Thread
This unit completes the Chonyid Bardo and, with it, Book I. Structurally it is the mirror-image of the peaceful sequence that precedes it: the same five Orders, the same five directions, the same five Bhagavans, now in terrifying form, each explicitly re-identified with its peaceful counterpart at the moment of its most frightening description. That identification is the argumentative payload of the whole Bardo Thödol as this text presents it — the deities are not two sets of beings but one set apprehended twice, and the second apprehension is generated by the hearer's own fear working on his own thought-forms. The unit therefore converts the preceding days' iconography from a pantheon into a psychological drama whose only real variable is recognition. Its escalating structure — Herukas, then Kerimas, then Htamenmas, then Door-Keepers, then twenty-eight goddesses, then the collapse into Dharma-Raja — is designed so that failure at every rung still leaves another rung, and the Conclusion then argues that the text itself is the last rung available to anyone: unpractised, unlearned, even guilty of the five boundless sins. Read as the text's own apologia, the Conclusion explains why the preceding hundred and fifty pages exist and how they are meant to be used — memorized, read thrice, proclaimed to the living, the sick and the dead. The final sentence hands off to the Sidpa Bardo, for those still unliberated.
