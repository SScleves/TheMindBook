# Ch 10 — Soul and Body, the Boats of Ra, and Sekhet-Hetepet

## In one paragraph
This unit is an unbroken run of fifteen chapters of primary translated scripture, and it turns the volume through ninety degrees: where the preceding transformation chapters gave the dead man new forms, these chapters give him back his old body, then rescue him, then open his tomb, then put him aboard a boat. It opens with SOUL AND BODY from the Papyrus of Ani — "The chapter of causing the soul to be united to its body in the underworld" — and closes with SEKHET-HETEPET from the Papyrus of Nebseni, the chapter of the Field of Peace itself, where the dead man ploughs, reaps, eats, drinks and makes love "even as a man doeth upon Earth." Between those two poles sit EVIL RECOLLECTIONS, RESCUE, OPENING THE TOMB, NOT SAILING TO THE EAST, THE INK-POT AND PALETTE, two chapters both titled BEING NIGH UNTO THOTH from different sheets of the same papyrus, five boat chapters (BRINGING A BOAT ALONG IN HEAVEN, BRINGING THE MAKHENT BOAT, ENTERING THE BOAT OF RA, PROTECTING THE BOAT OF RA, GOING INTO THE BOAT OF RA) and KNOWING THE SOULS OF THE EAST. Twelve of the fifteen come from the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477), two from the Papyrus of Nebseni (No. 9,900), one from the Papyrus of Ani (No. 10,470, sheet 17). Six capitalized rubrics occur; three of them are not promises but *instructions for making an object*: a soul of gold inlaid with precious stones laid on the breast, a design drawn on virgin papyrus in ink of green abut mixed with anti water, a bandlet of the fine linen of kings written with anti and laid on the neck at burial. **Everything below is translated Egyptian scripture; the volume's only framing in this range is the bracketed provenance line, the purpose-line, and the capitalized rubrics — plus modern photo captions, which are a fourth voice and are excluded from every quotation.**

## Core claims
- The soul is claimed to be separable and *recallable*: the whole premise of the opening chapter is that the ba may be anywhere at all and must be summoned back — "Grant thou that my soul may come unto me from wheresoever it may be. If [it] would tarry, then let my soul be brought unto me from wheresoever it may be."
- Reunion, not transcendence, is the stated goal of that chapter: the request is that the soul "look upon its material body" and "rest upon its spiritual body," and that the body itself "neither perish nor suffer corruption forever." The corpse is not written off.
- Annu is named as the specific place where the joining happens, and in bulk: "the land wherein souls are joined unto their bodies even in thousands."
- Memory is treated as a physical hazard with a demon attached to it. EVIL RECOLLECTIONS is addressed to a being "that cuttest off heads, and slittest brows," who puts "the memory of evil things from the mouth of the Khus," and the chapter's petition is that this memory "shall dart from my mouth."
- Captivity is the recurring danger across the middle of the unit: the soul may be held prisoner in the underworld (RESCUE), shut into the tomb (OPENING THE TOMB), or held at a door in Amentet — the RESCUE rubric promises he "SHALL NEVER BE HELD CAPTIVE AT ANY DOOR IN AMENTET, IN ENTERING IN OR IN COMING OUT." The chapters are claimed to work by opening ways, not by fighting.
- Literacy is claimed as a funerary asset in its own right. THE INK-POT AND PALETTE asks for a scribe's tools so the dead man may present himself "in the character of a scribe" carrying "the books of Thoth."
- Naming is claimed to be the mechanism of navigation: the Makhent boat will not carry the dead man until he can answer every one of its parts — rudder, mast, sail, keel, oars, planks, hull, the wind, the river, the river-banks and the ground itself — by name.
- Effect is claimed to depend on ritual manufacture as well as on knowledge. Three rubrics in this unit specify a material object (gold soul, inscribed papyrus, linen bandlet) and the exact circumstances of its placement, one of them insisting the papyrus "shall not enter in to (i.e., touch) his members."
- Sekhet-Hetepet is claimed to be a working agricultural country, not a state of mind: it has lakes, cities, walls of iron, measurable crops, and labour — and the wish list attached to it is emphatically physical.
- The Field's promise is stated as freedom from servitude and possession of authority: "may I never be in a state of servitude therein, but may I be in authority therein."

## Concepts
- **soul united to its body** — the volume's own chapter formula: "The chapter of causing the soul to be united to its body in the underworld." The operation is reunion of the ba with the corpse, performed in Annu.
- **ba (soul), khu, ka (double), khaibit (shade)** — the four are named and glossed in the text of this unit: the RESCUE chapter asks a way "for the ba (soul), and for the ka, and for the Khaibit (shade)"; SOUL AND BODY asks "possession of my ba (soul), and of my khu"; OPENING THE TOMB asks a way "for thy double (ka) along with thee and along with [thy] soul."
- **material body vs spiritual body** — distinguished within one sentence of the closing petition of SOUL AND BODY: "May it look upon its material body, may it rest upon its spiritual body."
- **perfect Khu** — the state the boat chapters confer; PROTECTING THE BOAT OF RA calls Nu "the perfect Khu" twice and "the perfect soul" once, its own rubric lays the amulet "UPON THE NECK OF THE PERFECT KHU," and the ENTERING rubric promises he shall go into the boat "REGULARLY AND CONTINUALLY, [INTO THE BOAT OF RA] AS A PERFECT KHU."
- **evil recollections** — memory of evil things, treated as a substance that can be cast into a mouth: Suti's agent "didst come to cast the recollection of evil things into the mouth of Osiris by the will of Suti his enemy."
- **abomination-formula** — recurs here as elsewhere in the volume: "that which is an abomination unto me, that which is an abomination unto me is filth and I will not eat thereof" (GOING INTO THE BOAT OF RA); and in EVIL RECOLLECTIONS as a mutual repulsion between the speaker and the demon.
- **Makhent boat** — the boat brought along in the underworld; the chapter of that name is the volume's longest interrogation-by-naming in this range.
- **Sekhet-Hetepet (Field of Peace / Field of Offerings)** — introduced by the text as "the mighty land, the Lady of Winds," containing the Lake of Hetep and the divine city Hetep; the god Hetep is its lord and namesake.
- **Sekhet-Aaru / Sekhet-Aarru** — printed both ways in this range; the destination of the Makhent rubric ("HE SHALL COME FORTH INTO SEKHET-AARRU") and the field whose crop-heights KNOWING THE SOULS OF THE EAST specifies.
- **Lake of double Fire** — in SEKHET-HETEPET it is the negative pole against which the Field is measured: "wherein there is none that rejoiceth, and wherein are all manner of evil things."
- **Sekhet-Sasa (Field of Fire) and its Pool of Flame** — glossed in the text: "I have come from the Pool of Flame which is in the Sekhet-Sasa (i.e., the Field of Fire)."
- **tchefau food** — the food placed before the nostrils of the great god Thekem in the Makhent recitation.
- **Mesget chamber** — the place of destruction the same recitation asks to avoid: "Let me not be destroyed at the Mesget chamber."
- **rubric** — printed in capitals. Six occur here, and this unit is where the rubric turns from promise into manufacturing instruction (gold soul, inscribed papyrus, linen bandlet).

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(For this unit: every chapter in the run, in printed order — title as printed, provenance line, purpose-line, speaker, substantial content, and every rubric verbatim.)*

1. **SOUL AND BODY** — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 17).] Purpose: "The chapter of causing the soul to be united to its body in the underworld." Speaker: "The Osiris Ani, triumphant."
   - Opens by hailing two functionary gods, each glossed: "Hail, thou god Anniu (i.e., Bringer)! Hail, thou god Pehrer (i.e., Runner), who dwellest in thy hall! [Hail,] great God!"
   - The summons: "Grant thou that my soul may come unto me from wheresoever it may be. If [it] would tarry, then let my soul be brought unto me from wheresoever it may be, for thou shalt find the Eye of Horus standing by thee like unto those beings who are like unto Osiris, and who never lie down in death."
   - Annu named as the joining-place: "Let not the Osiris Ani, triumphant, lie down in death among those who lie down in Annu, the land wherein souls are joined unto their bodies even in thousands." [A modern photo caption, "The Great Sphynx of Giza," is dropped into the middle of this sentence in the text as extracted; it is not part of the chapter.]
   - The possession-claim: "Let me have possession of my ba (soul), and of my khu, and let me triumph therewith in every place wheresoever it may be."
   - A bracketed aside the edition itself marks as doubtful: "[Observe these things which [I] speak, for it hath staves with it]; observe then, O ye divine guardians of heaven, my soul [wheresoever it may be]. If it would tarry, do thou make my soul to look upon my body."
   - A second address, to the tow-gods of the solar boat: "Hail, ye gods, who tow along the boat of the lord of millions of years, who bring [it] above the underworld and who make it to travel over Nut, who make souls to enter into [their] spiritual bodies, whose hands are filled with your ropes and who clutch your weapons tight, destroy ye the Enemy; thus shall the boat of the sun be glad and the great God shall set out on his journey in peace."
   - The closing petition, which is the whole point of the chapter: "And behold, grant ye that the soul of Osiris Ani, triumphant, may come forth before the gods and that it may be triumphant along with you in the eastern part of the sky to follow unto the place where it was yesterday; [and that it may have] peace, peace in Amentet. May it look upon its material body, may it rest upon its spiritual body; and may its body neither perish nor suffer corruption forever."
   - **Rubric** (printed in capitals): "[THESE] WORDS ARE TO BE SAID OVER A SOUL OF GOLD INLAID WITH PRECIOUS STONES AND PLACED ON THE BREAST OF OSIRIS." The first of this unit's object-rubrics: the spell is attached to a manufactured amulet in the shape of the thing it is about.

2. **EVIL RECOLLECTIONS** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 8).] Purpose: "The chapter of driving evil recollections from the mouth." Speaker, with his parentage given in full — the only father named in this unit: "The overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant, the son of the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Amen-hetep, triumphant."
   - The addressee is a mutilator: "Hail, thou that cuttest off heads, and slittest brows, thou being who puttest away the memory of evil things from the mouth of the Khus by means of the incantations which they have within them, look not upon me with the [same] eyes with which thou lookest upon them."
   - The counter-threat — the demon is told to turn round and see his own executioners: "Go thou round about on thy legs, and let thy face be [turned] behind thee so that thou mayest be able to see the divine slaughterers of the god Shu who are coming up behind thee to cut off thy head, and to slit thy brow by reason of the message of violence [sent] by thy lord, and to see(?) that which thou sayest."
   - The petition: "Work thou for me so that the memory of evil things shall dart from my mouth; let not my head be cut off; let not my brow be slit; and let not my mouth be shut fast by reason of the incantations which thou hast within thee, according to that which thou doest for the Khus through the incantations which they have within themselves." [OCR: "Khus" is printed "Ahus" here.]
   - The mythic precedent, with Isis's two speeches quoted inside the chapter: "Get thee back and depart at the [sound of] the two speeches which the goddess Isis uttered when thou didst come to cast the recollection of evil things into the mouth of Osiris by the will of Suti his enemy, saying, 'Let thy face be toward the privy parts, and look upon that face which cometh forth from the flame of the Eye of Horus against thee from within the Eye of Tem,' and the calamity of that night which shall consume thee."
   - The four-fold abomination chiasmus, the chapter's formal centrepiece: "And Osiris went back, for the abomination of thee was in him; and thou didst go back, for the abomination of him is in thee. I have gone back, for the abomination of thee is in me; and thou shalt go back, for the abomination of me is in thee."
   - A refusal of the demon's approach, and then the chapter breaks off in the middle of a quotation: "Thou wouldst come unto me, but I say that thou shalt not advance to me so that I come to an end, and [I] say then to the divine slaughterers of the god Shu, 'Depart." [OCR: a stray "299" is interleaved before "divine slaughterers," and the closing quotation mark after "Depart" is absent in this text; whether anything follows on the page cannot be determined.] No rubric printed.

3. **RESCUE** — [FROM THE PAPYRUS OF NU] (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 6).] [OCR: the provenance line is printed with its brackets scrambled exactly as reproduced here — the title-line and the bracketed source have run together.] Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the soul of Nu, triumphant, be captive in the underworld." Speaker: "He saith."
   - The whole chapter is short: "Hail, thou who art exalted! [Hail,] thou who art adored! O thou mighty one of Souls, thou divine Soul, thou possessor of terrible power, who dost put the fear of thyself into the gods, thou who art crowned upon thy throne of majesty, I pray thee to make a way for the ba (soul), and for the ka, and for the Khaibit (shade) of the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant [and let him be] provided therewith. I am a perfect khu, and I have made [my] way unto the place wherein dwell Ra and Hathor." [OCR: "ba (soul)" is printed "da (soul)" and "ka" is printed "kAu"; both are given here in the forms the same passage-type carries elsewhere in the volume.]
   - **Rubric** (printed in capitals, on the page following, after an intervening photograph): "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] HE SHALL BE ABLE TO TRANSFORM HIMSELF INTO A KHU PROVIDED [WITH HIS SOUL AND WITH HIS SHADE] IN THE UNDERWORLD, AND HE SHALL NEVER BE HELD CAPTIVE AT ANY DOOR IN AMENTET, IN ENTERING IN OR IN COMING OUT:" [The rubric ends with a colon as printed.] This rubric ties the soul-rescue chapters back to the transformation chapters: the reward for knowing it is a *transformation*, into a fully equipped Khu.

4. **OPENING THE TOMB** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 6).] [OCR: the closing bracket is printed as a solidus, "sheet 6)./".] Purpose, the longest purpose-line in the unit: "The chapter of opening the tomb to the soul [and] to the shade of Osiris the scribe Nebseni, the lord of reverence, born of the lady of the house Mut-Restha, triumphant, so that he may come forth by day and have dominion over his feet." [OCR: printed "dominion over his fleet"; the same chapter twice afterwards reads "dominion over his feet" and "dominion over thy legs".] Speaker: "[He saith:]" — Nebseni, whose mother is named, the only mother named in this unit.
   - The opening is a pair of reversals: "That which was shut fast hath been opened, that is to say, he that lay down in death [hath been opened]. That which was open hath been shut to my soul through the command of the Eye of Horus, which hath strengthened me and which maketh to stand fast the beauties which are upon the forehead of Ra, whose strides are long as [he] lifteth up [his] legs [in journeying]."
   - Self-identification with Horus as filial rescuer: "I have made for myself a way, my members are mighty and are strong. I am Horus the avenger of his divine father. I am he who bringeth along his divine father, and who bringeth along his mother by means of his sceptre(?)."
   - The soul-census in the solar boat: "he shall see the Great God in the Boat of Ra, [when] souls are counted therein at the bows, and when the years also are counted up."
   - The Eye of Horus asked to act as deliverer, against named opponents: "Grant that the Eye of Horus, which maketh the adornments of light to be firm upon the forehead of Ra, may deliver my soul for me, and let there be darkness upon your faces, O ye who would hold fast Osiris."
   - The core petition against the tomb-wardens: "Oh, keep not captive my soul, Oh, keep not ward over my shade, but let a way be opened for my soul [and] for my shade, and let [them] see the Great God in the shrine on the day of the judgment of souls."
   - The wardens named by function: "those who guard the members of Osiris, and who keep ward over the Khus, and who hold captive the shades of the dead who would work evil against me."
   - The chapter's close switches from first to second person, addressing the dead man himself: "Heaven shall [not] keep thee, the earth shall [not] hold thee captive, thou shalt not have thy being with the divine beings who make slaughter, but thou shalt have dominion over thy legs, and thou shalt advance to thy body straightway in the earth [and to] those who belong to the shrine and guard the members of Osiris." No rubric printed.

5. **NOT SAILING TO THE EAST** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 6).] Purpose: "The chapter of not sailing to the east in the underworld." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. The most violently worded chapter in the unit, and one of the very few anywhere in the volume that threatens the gods with cosmic damage if the request is refused.
   - Opening address: "Hail, phallus of Ra, who departest from thy calamity [which ariseth] through opposition(?), the cycles have been without movement for millions of years. I am stronger than the strong, I am mightier than the mighty."
   - The conditional threat, with the edition's own variant reading printed inline: "If I sail away or if I be snatched away to the east through the two horns," or (as others say), "if any evil and abominable thing be done unto me at the feast of the devils, the phallus of Ra shall be swallowed up, [along with] the head of Osiris."
   - The escalation: "now verily the two horns of the god Khepera shall be thrust aside; and verily pus shall spring into being in the eye of Tem along with corruption if I be kept in restraint, or if I have gone toward the east, or if the feast of devils be made in my presence, or if any malignant wound be inflicted upon me."
   - Between the two, a glimpse of the country he does want: "I journey along over the fields wherein the gods mow down those who make reply unto [their words]." No rubric printed.

6. **THE INK-POT AND PALETTE** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 12).] Purpose: "The chapter of praying for an ink-pot and for a palette." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Complete text: "Hail, aged god, who dost behold thy divine father and who art the guardian of the book of Thoth, [behold I have come; I am endowed with glory, I am endowed with strength, I am filled with might, and I am supplied with the books of Thoth], and I have brought [them to enable me] to pass through the god Aker who dwelleth in Set. I have brought the ink-pot and the palette as being the objects which are in the hands of Thoth; hidden is that which is in them. Behold me in the character of a scribe! I have brought the offal of Osiris, and I have written thereon. I have made (i.e., copied) the words of the great and beautiful god each day fairly. O Heru-khuti, thou didst order me and I have made (i.e., copied) what is right and true, and I do bring it unto thee each day." The dead man's qualification here is his trade: he is admitted as a copyist, and his daily output of true copy is the offering. No rubric printed.

7. **BEING NIGH UNTO THOTH** (first of two) — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 7).] Purpose: "The chapter of being nigh unto Thoth." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Complete text: "I am he who sendeth forth terror into the powers of rain and thunder, and I ward off from the great divine lady the attacks of violence. [I have smitten like the god Shat (i.e., the god of slaughter), and I have poured out libations of cool water like the god Ashu, and I have worked for the great divine lady [to ward off] the attacks of violence], I have made to flourish [my] knife along with the knife which is in the hand of Thoth in the powers of rain and thunder." Two sentences; the bracketed middle is the edition's own supplement, and it duplicates the surrounding clauses almost word for word. No rubric printed.

8. **BEING NIGH UNTO THOTH** (second of two, same title, different sheets) — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 19 and 20).] Purpose, extended: "The chapter of being nigh unto Thoth and of giving glory unto a man in the underworld." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. The volume prints the two identically titled chapters a page apart without comment, exactly as it printed the two lotus chapters earlier.
   - Opening identification, with the god's name glossed: "I am the god Her-ab-maat-f (i.e., 'he that is within his eye'), and I have come to give right and truth to Ra; I have made Suti to be at peace with me by means of offerings made to the god Aker and to the Tesheru deities, and by [making] reverence unto Seb."
   - A rubric-like stage direction, but printed in ordinary type rather than capitals: "[The following] words are to be recited in the Sektet boat."
   - The recitation itself: "[Hail,] sceptre of Anubis, I have made the four Khus who are in the train of the lord of the universe to be at peace with me, and I am the lord of the fields through their decree. I am the divine father Bah (i.e., the god of the water-flood), and I do away with the thirst of him that keepeth ward over the Lakes."
   - The claim of precedence over the great gods: "Behold ye me, then, O great gods of majesty who dwell among the Souls of Annu, for I am lifted up over you. I am the god Menkh (i.e., Gracious one) who dwelleth among you."
   - A plea against obstruction, and a claim of cleansing: "Verily I have cleansed my soul, O great god of majesty, set not before me the evil obstacles which issue from thy mouth, and let not destruction come round about me, or upon me."
   - Ritual bathing in three named waters: "I have made myself clean in the Lake of making to be at peace, [and in the Lake of] weighing in the balance, and I have bathed myself in Netert-utchat, which is under the holy sycamore tree of heaven." The bathing is then declared effective: "Behold [I am] bathed, [and I have] triumphed [over] all [mine enemies] straightway who come forth and rise up against right and truth. I am right and true in the earth."
   - Close: "I, even I, have spoken(?) with my mouth [which is] the power of the Lord, the Only one, Ra the mighty, who liveth upon right and truth. Let not injury be inflicted upon me, [but let me be] clothed on the day of those who go forward(?) to every [good] thing." No rubric printed.

9. **BRINGING A BOAT ALONG IN HEAVEN** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of bringing along a boat in heaven." Speaker: "The chancellor-in-chief, triumphant" — printed here without Nu's name.
   - Opens on a constellation: "Hail to thee, O thou Thigh which dwellest in the northern heaven in the Great Lake, which art seen and which diest not. I have stood up over thee when thou didst rise like a god. I have seen thee, and I have not lain down in death; I have stood over thee, and I have risen like a god."
   - Borrowed bird-forms for the ascent: "I have cackled like a goose, and I have alighted like the hawk by the divine clouds and by the great dew. I have journeyed from the earth to heaven."
   - The ladder and the stars: "The god Shu hath [made] me to stand up, the god of Light hath made me to be vigorous by the two sides of the ladder, and the stars which never rest set [me] on [my] way and bring [me] away from slaughter."
   - The god Pen, invoked twice in one sentence and nowhere else in the unit: "I bring along with me the things which drive back calamities as I advance over the passage of the god Pen; thou comest, how great art thou, O god Pen!"
   - The Field of Fire, and a paired residence claim: "I have come from the Pool of Flame which is in the Sekhet-Sasa (i.e., the Field of Fire). Thou livest in the Pool of Flame in Sekhet-Sasa, and I live upon the staff of the holy [god]."
   - A broken address: "Hail, thou god Kaa, who dost bring those things which are in the boats by the ..." [The text breaks off in ellipsis at this point in the edition.]
   - Steering, then the reward — landed property and a workforce: "I stand up in the boat and I guide myself [over] the water; I have stood up in the boat and the god hath guided me. I have stood up. I have spoken. [I am master of the] crops. I sail round about as I go forward, and the gates which are in Sekhem (Letopolis) are opened unto me, and fields are awarded unto me in the city of Unnu (Hermopolis), and laborers(?) are given unto me together with those of my own flesh and bone." No rubric printed.

10. **BRINGING THE MAKHENT BOAT** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 21 and 22).] Purpose: "The chapter of bringing along a boat in the underworld." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. The longest chapter in the unit and the one that carries the Sekhet-Aarru rubric.
    - Petition to the boatmen: "Hail, ye who bring along the boat over the evil back [of Apepi], grant that I may bring the boat along, and coil up [its] ropes in peace, in peace. Come, come, hasten, hasten, for I have come to see my father Osiris, the lord of the ansi garment, who hath gained the mastery with joy of heart."
    - Four hails to the boat's guardian, one of whom binds up severed heads, the last running straight into the petition: "Hail, lord of the rain-storm, thou Male, thou Sailor! Hail, thou who dost sail over the evil back of Apep! Hail, thou that dost bind up heads and dost stablish the bones of the neck when thou comest forth from the knives. Hail, thou who art in charge of the hidden boat, who dost fetter Apep, grant that I may bring along the boat, and that I may coil up the ropes and that I may sail forth therein."
    - A description of a capsized cosmos, one of the strangest passages in the volume: "This land is baleful, and the stars have overbalanced themselves and have fallen upon their faces therein, and they have not found anything which will help them to ascend again: their path is blocked by the tongue of Ra. Antebu [is] the guide of the two lands. Seb is stablished [through] their rudders."
    - Two unattached epithets stand between that passage and the next, with no sentence around them: "The power which openeth the Disk. The prince of the red beings," [Printed thus, the first as a complete stopped sentence and the second running without a verb into the clause that follows; the text gives neither a speaker nor a referent for either.]

12. **PROTECTING THE BOAT OF RA** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 27).] Purpose: "The chapter of protecting the boat of Ra." No speaker-formula is printed; the chapter opens directly in address to Ra and refers to Nu in the third person throughout.

    - After that opening the chapter is built of three conditional stanzas, each opening "Hail, Ra, in thy name of Ra"; all four paragraphs close on the same exchange of strength ("and let thy strength be his strength," once printed "and may thy strength be his strength").     - **Rubric** (printed in capitals), the third object-rubric and the longest promise in the unit: "[THIS CHAPTER] SHALL BE RECITED OVER A BANDLET OF THE FINE LINEN OF KINGS [UPON WHICH] IT HATH BEEN WRITTEN WITH ANTI, WHICH SHALL BE LAID UPON THE NECK OF THE PERFECT KHU ON THE DAY OF THE BURIAL. 13. **GOING INTO THE BOAT OF RA** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 28).] Purpose: "The chapter of going into the boat of Ra." Speaker: Nu, triumphant.

15. **SEKHET-HETEPET** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 17).] The destination-chapter of the whole book, and the second of the two Nebseni texts in this unit. Its heading is not a purpose-line but a *table of contents*, and it is the most complete single statement in the volume of what the blessed dead are promised:
    - "Here begin the chapters of Sekhet-Hetepet, and the chapters of coming forth by day; of going into and of coming out from the underworld; of coming to Sekhet-Aaru, of being in Sekhet-Hetepet, the mighty land, the Lady of Winds; of having power there; of becoming a Khu there; of ploughing there; of reaping there; of eating there; of drinking there; of making love there; and of doing everything even as a man doeth upon Earth."
    - Speaker: "Behold the scribe and artist of the Temple of Ptah, Nebseni, who saith" — his trade named, as with the ink-pot chapter, as part of his standing.
    - **The Horus-and-Set frame.** The chapter opens not in the Field but at a quarrel over it: "Set hath taken possession of Horus, who looked with the two eyes upon the building(?) round Sekhet-hetep, but I have unfettered Horus [and taken him from] Set, and Set hath opened the ways of the two eyes [which are] in heaven. Set hath cast(?) his moisture to the winds upon the soul [that hath] his day (or his eye) and who dwelleth in the city of Mert, and he hath delivered the interior of the body of Horus from the gods of Akert."
    - **The geography, first pass — boat, lakes, cities.** "Behold me now, for I make this mighty boat to travel over the Lake of Hetep, and I brought it away with might from the palace of Shu; the domain of his stars groweth young and reneweth its former strength. I have brought the boat into the lakes thereof so that I may come forth into the cities thereof, and I have sailed into their divine city Hetep." The Field is thus a lake-district with cities in it, entered by boat, and its capital bears the god's own name.
    - **Peace as the Field's defining property.** "And behold, it is because I, even I, am at peace with his seasons, and with his guidance, and with his territory, and with the company of the gods who are his first-born. He maketh the two divine fighters (i.e., Horus and Set) to be at peace with those who watch over the living ones whom he hath created in fair form, and he bringeth peace [with him]... He cutteth off the hair from the divine fighters, he driveth away storm from the helpless, and he keepeth away harm from the Khus."
    - **Knowledge as title-deed, and a warning about the neighbours.** "Let me gain dominion within that Field, for I know it, and I have sailed among its lakes so that I might come into its cities. My mouth is strong; and I am equipped [with weapons to use] against the Khus; let them not have dominion over me." The Field is not empty: other Khus are there, and they might take precedence.
    - **The great wish-list — the chapter's centre, and the book's destination stated as a series of ordinary verbs.** "Let me be rewarded with thy fields, O thou god Hetep; that which is thy wish, shalt thou do, O lord of the winds. May I become a khu therein, may I eat therein, may I drink therein, may I plough therein, may I reap therein, may I fight therein, may I make love therein, may my words be mighty therein, may I never be in a state of servitude therein, but may I be in authority therein."
    - **The god Hetep described.** "Thou hast made strong(?) the mouth and the throat(?) of the god Hetep; Qetetbu is its(?) name. He is stablished upon the watery supports(?) of the god Shu, and is linked unto the pleasant things of Ra. He is the divider of years, he is hidden of mouth, his mouth is silent, that which he uttereth is secret, he fulfilleth eternity and taketh possession of everlastingness of existence as Hetep, the lord Hetep."
    - **The measured Hawk.** "The god Horus maketh himself to be strong like unto the Hawk which is one thousand cubits in length and two thousand [cubits in width] in life; he hath equipments with him, and he journeyeth on and cometh where the seat of his heart wisheth in the Pools thereof and in the cities thereof."
    - **The birth-chamber cycle, printed twice.** "He was begotten in the birth-chamber of the god of the city, he hath offerings [made unto him] of the food of the god of the city, he performeth that which it is meet to do therein, and the union thereof, in the matter of everything of the birth-chamber of the divine city. When [he] setteth in life like crystal he performeth everything therein, and these things are like unto the things which are done in the Lake of double Fire, wherein there is none that rejoiceth, and wherein are all manner of evil things." The passage is then repeated with the subject changed to the god Hetep: "The god Hetep goeth in, and cometh out, and goeth backward [in] that Field which gathereth together all manner of things for the birth-chamber of the god of the city. When he setteth in life like crystal he performeth all manner of things therein which are like unto the things which are done in the Lake of double Fire, wherein there is none that rejoiceth, and wherein are no—" [The page ends here mid-clause and the text does not resume at the same point; see the lacuna flagged below.]
    - **The litany of entries.** The chapter's second half is a series of first-person entries into named places of the Field, each opening "O [name], I have entered in to thee." The surviving entries, in printed order:
      - **Tchefet** — "Tchefet, I have entered in to thee. I array myself in apparel, and I gird myself with the sa garment of Ra; now behold, [he is] in heaven, and those who dwell therein follow Ra, and [I] follow Ra in heaven."
      - **Unen-em-hetep** — "O Unen-em-hetep, lord of the two lands, I have entered in to thee, and I have plunged into the lakes of Tchesert; behold me, for all filth hath departed from me. The Great God groweth therein, and behold, I have found [food therein]; I have snared feathered fowl and I feed upon the finest [of them]."
      - **Qenqentet** — "O Qenqentet, I have entered into thee, and I have seen the Osiris [my father], and I have gazed upon my mother, and I have made love. I have caught the worms and serpents, and I am delivered. And I know the name of the god who is opposite to the goddess Tchesert, and who hath straight hair and is equipped with two horns; he reapeth, and I both plough and reap."
      - **Hast** — "O Hast, I have entered in to thee, I have driven back those who would come to the turquoise [sky], and I have followed the winds of the company of the gods. The Great God hath given my head unto me, and he who hath bound on me my head is the Mighty one who hath turquoise(?) eyes, namely, Ari-en-ab-f (i.e., he doeth as he pleaseth)."
      - **Usert** — "O Usert, I have come into thee at the head of the house wherein divine food is brought for me."
      - **Smam** — "O Smam, I have come into thee. My heart watcheth, my head is equipped with the white crown, I am led into celestial regions, and I make to flourish terrestrial objects, and there is joy of heart for the Bull, and for celestial beings, and for the company of the gods. I am the god who is the Bull, the lord of the gods, as he goeth forth from the turquoise [sky]."
      - **The divine nome of wheat and barley** — "O divine nome of wheat and barley, I have come unto thee, I have come forward to thee and I have taken up that which followeth me, namely, the best of the libations of the company of the gods."
    - **The close** — the dead man moors, and the chapter ends on liturgy correctly performed: "I have tied up my boat in the celestial lakes, I have lifted up the post at which to anchor, I have recited the prescribed words with my voice, and I have ascribed praises unto the gods who dwell in Sekhet-hetep." No rubric is printed at the chapter's end; its promises are all carried in the opening table-of-contents heading.

**Distribution of sources across the run**
- Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477): twelve of the fifteen chapters — sheet 6 (rescue; not sailing to the east), sheet 7 (being nigh unto Thoth, first), sheet 8 (evil recollections), sheet 9 (bringing a boat along in heaven), sheet 12 (ink-pot and palette; knowing the souls of the east), sheets 19 and 20 (being nigh unto Thoth, second), sheets 21 and 22 (Makhent boat), sheet 27 (protecting the boat of Ra), sheets 27 and 28 (entering the boat of Ra), sheet 28 (going into the boat of Ra). The sheet numbers do not run in order, unlike the near-consecutive run in the transformation chapters.
- Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900): two chapters — OPENING THE TOMB (sheet 6) and SEKHET-HETEPET (sheet 17). Nebseni is titled "Osiris the scribe Nebseni, the lord of reverence, born of the lady of the house Mut-Restha" in the first and "the scribe and artist of the Temple of Ptah" in the second.
- Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 17): one chapter — SOUL AND BODY.
- No Lepsius chapter numbers are printed for any text in this run; chapters are identified only by capitalized topical title plus bracketed provenance line. Two chapters carry the identical title BEING NIGH UNTO THOTH.

**The four voices in this range**
1. **Scripture** — the translated chapter text, in ordinary type, first person, attributed to a named dead man.
2. **The edition's apparatus** — the bracketed provenance lines, the purpose-lines, the square-bracketed supplements and glosses inside the text ("(i.e., Bringer)", "(i.e., Letopolis)"), the query marks the edition itself sets inside doubtful words, and the inline variant "or (as others say)".
3. **The rubrics** — printed in capitals, stating either an effect or a manufacturing instruction.
4. **Modern editorial photo captions**, which are neither Budge nor Wilson nor scripture and belong to the 2016 volume's picture editing. They are interleaved into the prose by the extraction and several split a sentence mid-way. In this range they are: "The Great Sphynx of Giza" (inside SOUL AND BODY, splitting the sentence about Annu); "English Egyptologist Howard Carter discovering the intact tomb of Tutankhamun" (immediately before the RESCUE rubric); "(Previous spread) A modern copy of Tutankhamun's wooden sarcophagus"; "(Following spread) A room inside an ancient Egyptian temple"; "(Opposite) The Mortuary Temple of Hatshepsut" (dropped into the middle of the Makhent naming-litany, between the Rudder and the Rope); "(Opposite) This XVIIIth-dynasty quartzite sarcophagus was originally made for Queen Hatshepsut, who later turned pharaoh. She later decided to transfer her father's mummy, Pharaoh Thutmose I, from his tomb to hers and had the inscriptions rewritten" (splitting the last sentence of the Makhent food-petition); "(Previous spread) A view of the chapel dedicated to the god Amun from the Temple of Osiris in Abydos, Egypt"; "(Opposite) An excerpt from the Book of the Dead of Pinedjem II, who was a high priest of Amun at Thebes from 990-969 B.C. and the de facto ruler of southern Egypt" (splitting the Sekhet-hetepet litany). All are excluded from every passage quoted above. Note that this fourth voice carries dates and factual claims found nowhere in Budge's or Wilson's text.

- "Annu, the land wherein souls are joined unto their bodies even in thousands." — SOUL AND BODY; the reunion has an address and a throughput.

- "[THESE] WORDS ARE TO BE SAID OVER A SOUL OF GOLD INLAID WITH PRECIOUS STONES AND PLACED ON THE BREAST OF OSIRIS." — the SOUL AND BODY rubric; the first rubric in the volume's Section II run that instructs the making of an object rather than promising an effect.

## Thread
This run is where the volume brings the dead man home. The chapters immediately before it had scattered him into fourteen borrowed shapes; the first chapter here calls his soul back out of "wheresoever it may be" and sets it down on the body it left, in a named town, among thousands of others doing the same thing on the same day. What follows is a sequence of removals — a demon of evil memory driven off the mouth, a soul let out of captivity, a tomb opened, an unwanted eastward voyage refused — and then a sequence of acquisitions: a scribe's ink-pot and palette, standing next to Thoth, and finally a boat. The boat chapters are the structural spine, five of them in a row, and they escalate: bringing a boat along in heaven, bringing the Makhent boat in the underworld, entering Ra's boat, protecting Ra's boat, going into Ra's boat. By the last of them the relation has inverted — the dead man is no longer a passenger asking to be taken aboard but a physician binding up the god's arm and making his leg to walk, and the rubric of PROTECTING THE BOAT OF RA states that Ra's own members are established by the chapter. The unit is also where the rubric changes character. Earlier rubrics in Section II promised outcomes to whoever *knew* the chapter; three of the six here specify an *object* — a gold soul-amulet, a design on virgin papyrus in green-abut ink, a linen bandlet written in anti and tied on the neck on the day of burial — and describe how it must be placed. Knowledge alone stops being sufficient; the funerary workshop is written into the scripture. And the run ends at Sekhet-Hetepet, which is the answer to the whole book. Its heading is not a prayer but an itinerary of activities, and every one of them is something a living Egyptian farmer does: plough, reap, eat, drink, make love, speak with authority, and not be anybody's servant. KNOWING THE SOULS OF THE EAST has already given the same country its dimensions — iron walls, wheat five cubits high, barley seven, reapers nine — so that the destination arrives measured before it arrives described. Read together, the two make the volume's eschatology unmistakable: the reward is not release from the world but the world again, larger, with better crops and no master.

## Connections
- The transformation chapters immediately preceding close on THE CROCODILE, the last of a run in which safety came from *not* remaining oneself. This unit opens by reversing that premise in its first purpose-line — the soul is to be reunited with its own body — so the two runs are printed as a pair: dispersal, then re-integration.
- The RESCUE rubric is the hinge between them: its promised effect is that the deceased "SHALL BE ABLE TO TRANSFORM HIMSELF INTO A KHU PROVIDED [WITH HIS SOUL AND WITH HIS SHADE]" — a transformation chapter's promise attached to a soul-rescue chapter, and the mechanism by which the volume moves from one theme to the other without a seam.
- The Makhent rubric's "IN ANY FORM WHATSOEVER HE PLEASETH" and the food-petition's "a coming forth by day in any form whatsoever in which it may please me to appear in Sekhet-Aarru" restate, inside a boat chapter, the same open-ended promise the transformation run's rubrics carry ("HE SHALL PERFORM WHATSOEVER TRANSFORMATIONS HIS HEART DESIRETH"). The volume repeats the formula across otherwise unrelated chapter-types.
- SEKHET-HETEPET and KNOWING THE SOULS OF THE EAST describe the same country from two angles — the first from inside, as a lake-district of cities entered by boat, the second from outside, as a walled field with measurable crops. Both name it Sekhet-Aaru / Sekhet-Aarru, and the Makhent rubric promises entry to it; three chapters of one unit therefore converge on a single destination.
- Sekhet-hetep was already named in the transformation run, where the hawk of gold says "the divine Sekhet-hetep is before me, I have eaten therein, I have become a khu therein, I have an abundance therein—as much as I desire." This unit is where that passing mention becomes a chapter of its own, with the same three verbs (become a khu, eat, have abundance) expanded into the full wish-list.
- The two chapters titled BEING NIGH UNTO THOTH, printed a page apart from different sheets of the same papyrus, repeat the volume's habit of setting variants side by side without comment — the same practice that put the two lotus chapters back to back in the transformation run.
- The Papyrus of Nebseni supplies both OPENING THE TOMB (sheet 6) and SEKHET-HETEPET (sheet 17); the Papyrus of Ani supplies SOUL AND BODY (sheet 17). Section II's Introduction, which catalogues these manuscripts by museum number, is the reference for what each papyrus is.
- Judgment vocabulary appears here in advance of the Hall of Maati material, as it did in the transformation run: OPENING THE TOMB asks that soul and shade see the Great God "on the day of the judgment of souls," and BEING NIGH UNTO THOTH bathes the speaker in "[the Lake of] weighing in the balance."
- Section III's list of Egyptian deities is the reference for the cast invoked here: Anniu, Pehrer, Osiris, Ra, Horus, Set/Suti, Isis, Nut, Seb, Shu, Thoth, Anubis/Anpu, Aker, Heru-khuti, Her-ab-maat-f, Bah, Menkh, Hetep, Ari-en-ab-f, Mesthi, Hapi, Tuamautef, Qebh-sennuf, Ap-uat, Menqet, Septet, Apep/Apepi, Khepera, Tem, Khenti-Amenti and Utu.
