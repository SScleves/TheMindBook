# Ch 12 — Section II close — Making Perfect the Khu, the boat chapters, and The Four Flames

## In one paragraph
This unit is the last stretch of translated scripture in the volume: Section II runs out here and the book turns to its Section III list of deities. Eleven chapters are printed, and after the closing lines of a HYMN OF PRAISE TO OSIRIS (whose opening falls in the previous unit) every one of them is drawn from the Papyrus of Nu or from Lepsius's *Todtenbuch*, with a single four-sentence Ani chapter set among them. The dominant form is the chapter "of making perfect the Khu" — four separate texts carry that title, all four of them from the Papyrus of Nu — and the dominant machinery is the model boat: the reader is told to paint a boat of Ra in colors, or to build one four cubits long out of green porcelain, to set images of the deceased and of the gods in it, to purify it with natron and incense, and to keep it hidden from every eye but his own, his father's and his son's. The rubrics are unusually explicit about payoff, promising that the Khu shall not die a second time, shall eat beside Osiris, shall be seen in the underworld "in the form of the radiance of Ra"; one carries a finding-legend dating the chapter's discovery to the reign of Hesepti. The travel chapters (LIVING NIGH UNTO RA, TRAVELLING IN THE BOAT OF RA, SAILING IN THE GREAT BOAT) give way at the end to fire: the section's final chapter, THE FOUR FLAMES, is a ritual of four clay troughs of incense and white cow's milk in which the Eye of Horus is set upon the brow of the dead. **Section II does not close with a colophon or a summary — it simply stops** at the end of THE FOUR FLAMES (body text ends line 927 of the source), followed only by untranscribed plate pages before the SECTION III half-title at line 939.

## Core claims
**Claims made by the scripture itself (recorded as the texts state them):**
- The dead man's victory is corporate and forensic: it must be won and registered before a bench, "victorious over his enemies in the heights of heaven, and in the depths thereof, and before the divine sovereign chiefs of every god and of every goddess."
- Horus's vengeance is the model for the deceased's own: "Rise up, then, O Horus, thou son of Isis, and avenge thy father Osiris," and "Rise up, then, O Osiris, for I have struck down for thee all thine enemies, and I have taken vengeance upon them for thee."
- Osiris's names are functionally interpreted — the ka rests in him "in thy name of Ka-Hetep," he is made glorious "in thy name of Khu," made like the Morning Star "in thy name of Pehu," and the ways are opened "in thy name of Ap-uat."
- The sunrise is a total opening of the cosmos: "The heavens are opened, the earth is opened, the West is opened, the East is opened, the southern half of heaven is opened, the northern half of heaven is opened, the doors are opened, and the gates are thrown wide open to Ra."
- Creation proceeds from Ra's breath: "he breatheth, and the god Shu [cometh into being], and he createth the goddess Tefnut."
- The deceased's requests in the Nu chapters are overwhelmingly negative-protective: not to be repulsed before Ra, not to walk in the valley of darkness, not to enter the Lake of the evil, not to have existence among the damned "even for a moment," not to be shipwrecked in the Great Boat — and the matching assertions that he has not been shipwrecked and "hath not journeyed upon the path of the crocodile—for he abominateth the crocodile."
- Secrecy is claimed to be part of the deceased's qualification: "He doth not tell that which he hath seen, and he doth not repeat that which he hath heard in the secret places."
- Rebirth is described as bodily reassembly performed daily by the god: "Thou takest count of thy bones, thou gatherest together thy members, thou settest thy face toward the beautiful Amentet, and thou comest, being renewed each day."
- Dying **a second time** is the thing the rubrics repeatedly promise escape from — "his soul shall have existence, and shall live forever, and shall not die a second time"; "he shall not die a second time in the underworld; and on the day of weighing of words no evil hap shall befall him."
- Efficacy is claimed for knowledge alone as well as for performance: "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN BY THE DECEASED HE SHALL BECOME A PERFECT KHU IN THE UNDERWORLD"; and known upon earth, "HE SHALL BE LIKE UNTO THOTH, AND HE SHALL BE ADORED BY THE LIVING ONES."
- The Nu chapters claim identity with Ra outright — "I am that god Ra who shineth in the night"; "I have not been shipwrecked, I have not been turned back in the horizon, for I am Ra-Osiris" — and elsewhere identity with Horus, "to whom his mother the goddess Isis hath given birth, and whom the goddess Nephthys hath nursed and dandled."
- Protection is transitive: "the force which protecteth Osiris is the force which protecteth thee"; "His strength which protecteth is my strength which protecteth, which is the strength which protecteth Ra."
- The four sons of Horus are addressed as guarantors — because they protected Osiris, they are bound to protect Nu: "inasmuch as ye have destroyed the opponent[s] of Osiris, the governor of Amenti ... even so do ye destroy the opponent[s] of the Osiris Nu."

**Editorial claims in the printed apparatus:**
- One rubric is attributed to a different recension: "The following is from the rubric to this chapter in the Saite Recension (see Lepsius, op. cit., Bl. 53)," which adds that the deceased "shall know] the hidden things of the underworld, he shall penetrate the hidden things in Neter-khertet (the underworld)."
- That same passage carries a **finding-legend**: the chapter "was found in the large hall(?) of the Temple under the reign of his Majesty Hesepti, triumphant, and it was found in the cavern of the mountain which Horus made for his father Osiris Un-nefer, triumphant."

## Concepts
- **making perfect the Khu** — the standing title of four chapters in this unit; the operation of rendering the deceased's shining/spirit form effective, "PERFECT IN THE HEART OF RA," so that gods, mankind and the dead recognize him as a divine being. Three of the four are keyed to an occasion (the birthday of Osiris; "the day of the month"; "the festival of Six"); the fourth is headed simply "Another chapter of making perfect the Khu."
- **dying a second time** — the fate the rubrics promise exemption from; dying again *in* the underworld, after the first death. The edition never uses the noun "the second death"; its phrase is always the verbal one, "shall not die a second time." Promised four times in this unit's rubric matter: the birthday-of-Osiris rubric, the Saite rubric appended to it, the NEW MOON rubric, and the rubric of OF MAKING PERFECT THE KHU.
- **the day of weighing of words** — the judgment occasion on which, if the chapter is performed, "no evil hap shall befall him."
- **the divine sovereign chiefs** (also "divine sovereign princes," "divine princes") — the tribunal before whom triumph is declared and among whom the deceased asks to be admitted: Nu "shall come in among thy divine sovereign chiefs" and "hath triumph among the divine sovereign chiefs," and the green-porcelain rubric has them painted on the boat as "THE DIVINE SOVEREIGN CHIEFS OF THE CITIES."
- **the beings of the Thigh** — a group addressed in MAKING PERFECT THE KHU: "ye divine beings of the Thigh, the knives of God [work] in secret"; they are asked to "do ye away with his sorrow, and his suffering, and his pain." The Thigh recurs in the boat chapters — "[he] cometh upon the flame of thy boat, [O Ra,] upon that mighty Thigh," and "Behold him whose face is in the god of the Thigh" — and as the Thigh of the goddess Nut, over which the water-flood is turned back.
- **sah (spiritual body)** — glossed in the text; the speaker claims to be "the spiritual body (sah) of the lord of divine right and truth made by the goddess Uatchit."
- **ureret / wreret crown** — the crown whose appearance on the deceased's head makes men, gods, Khus and the dead fall on their faces. [OCR: this edition prints the word both as "ureret" and as "wreret" within the same paragraph.]
- **Neter-khertet** — glossed in the text as "the underworld," in the Saite rubric.
- **Sekhet-hetep** — the place the speaker asks to circle with Ra: "grant ye that I may go round about with him in Sekhet-hetep."
- **Neb-er-tcher** — a god to whom praises are ascribed once right and truth have come to their lord.
- **the Four Flames** — four blazing flames made for the Khu, produced with four square clay troughs, incense, and the milk of a white cow; identified through the chapter with the Eye of Horus set upon the brow and breast of the deceased.
- **the children of Horus** — named in the final chapter as Mesthi, Hapi, Tuamautef and Qebhsennuf, the protectors of Osiris whose protection is petitioned for Nu.
- **the model-boat ritual** — the recurring rubric form: a boat is painted or manufactured, images are set in it, purification is performed, and the object is then handled, hidden, or made to travel. It appears in four distinct rubrics in this unit with different specifications (the painted boat of Ra, the green-porcelain boat, the hawk-and-plaque model, and the figure placed in a model of the boat of the sun).
- **the secrecy clause** — the instruction that the ritual object be seen by no one: "LET NOT THE EYE OF ANY MAN WHATSOEVER LOOK UPON IT WITH THE EXCEPTION OF THINE OWN SELF, OR THY FATHER, OR THY SON, AND GUARD [THIS] WITH GREAT CARE."
- **the chancellor-in-chief / overseer of the palace** — the titulary attached to Nu throughout, repeated in nearly every sentence of his chapters.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(For this unit: every translated text in the range, in printed order, with its title as printed, its bracketed provenance, its stated purpose, and its rubric.)*

1. **HYMN OF PRAISE TO OSIRIS** — [From Lepsius, Todtenbuch, Bl. 57.] Speaker: "The Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant." *(This text opens in the preceding unit's range and concludes at the start of this one; recorded here for its close.)* Purpose as stated: "A hymn of praise to Osiris." It opens by piling up titles — "Homage to thee, O Osiris Un-nefer, triumphant, thou son of Nut, thou first-born son of Seb, thou mighty one who comest forth from Nut, thou King in the city of Nifu-ur, thou Governor of Amentet, thou lord of Abtu (Abydos), thou lord of souls, thou mighty one of strength, thou lord of the atef crown in Suten-henen, thou lord of the divine form in the city of Nifu-ur, thou lord of the tomb, thou mighty one of souls in Tattu, thou lord of [sepulchral] offerings, thou whose festivals are many in Tattu." Horus exalts his father in every place and unites himself to Isis and Nephthys; "the god Thoth reciteth for him the mighty glorifyings which are within him, [and which] come forth from his mouth, and the heart of Horus is stronger than that of all the gods." The speaker takes Horus's part: "I am Horus and I have avenged thee, and I feed this day upon the sepulchral meals of oxen, and feathered fowl, and upon all the beautiful things [offered] unto Osiris." Then the name-play: "thy ka hath come unto thee and is with thee, and thou restest therein in thy name of Ka-Hetep. [It] maketh thee glorious in thy name of Khu, and it maketh thee like unto the Morning Star in thy name of Pehu, and it openeth for thee the ways in thy name of Ap-uat." [OCR: this clause is printed "I maketh thee glorious"; the parallel clauses in the same sentence read "it maketh" / "it openeth".] It closes on the god's restored office: "thou hast received thy sceptre and the place whereon thou art to rest, and thy steps are under thee. Thou bringest food to the gods, and thou bringest sepulchral meals unto those who dwell in their tombs. Thou hast given thy might unto the gods and thou hast created the Great God ... and thou hearest the word of right and truth on the day when offerings to this god are ordered on the festivals of Uka." No rubric.

2. **MAKING PERFECT THE KHU** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 17).] Stated purpose and occasion: "Another chapter of making perfect the Khu, which is [to be recited on] the birthday of Osiris, and of making to live the soul forever." Speaker: "The chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant." The longest text in the unit and structurally a triptych — three speeches, each opened with quotation marks in the printed text. (i) The opening of the cosmos at sunrise, the Sektet boat opening the double doors and the Matet boat bursting open the gates, Shu and Tefnut coming into being from Ra's breath, and Nu following in Ra's train: "He taketh his iron weapon and he forceth open the shrine even as doth Horus." A run of prohibitions follows: "Storms are the things which he abominateth. Let no water-flood be nigh unto him ... Let not the Osiris Nu, the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, triumphant, walk in the valley of darkness, let him not enter into the Lake of those who are evil, and let him have no existence among the damned, even for a moment." (ii) The address to the beings of the Thigh, with Thoth "within his hidden places" performing libations "unto the god who reckoneth millions of years," doing away with storms and whirlwinds, and putting light into the deceased's heart. (iii) "Hymns of praise unto thee, O Ra, in the horizon" — the iron firmament in Amentet which Apep has broken through with his storms before the double Lion-god, which Nu will set in order; Ra delivers him from Apep each day; Thoth provides his prepared things; he circles right and truth about the bows of the Great Boat and establishes it "for millions of years." Thoth "leadeth the Osiris Nu within his eye, and he sitteth [upon his] thigh[s] in the mighty boat of Khepera." Ends on the doubled formula "The Osiris Nu is at peace, the Osiris Nu is at peace" and the crocodile clause: "he hath not journeyed upon the path of the crocodile—for he abominateth the crocodile—and it hath not drawn nigh unto him."
   - **Rubric** (capitals): [THIS CHAPTER SHALL BE] RECITED OVER A BOAT OF THE GOD RA WHICH HATH BEEN PAINTED IN COLORS IN A PURE PLACE. AND BEHOLD THOU SHALT PLACE A FIGURE OF THE DECEASED IN THE BOWS THEREOF, AND THOU SHALT PAINT A SEKTET BOAT UPON THE RIGHT SIDE THEREOF, AND AN ATET BOAT UPON THE LEFT SIDE THEREOF, AND THERE SHALL BE MADE UNTO THEM OFFERINGS OF BREAD, AND CAKES, AND WINE, AND OIL, AND EVERY KIND OF FAIR OFFERING UPON THE BIRTHDAY OF OSIRIS. IF THESE CEREMONIES BE PERFORMED HIS SOUL SHALL HAVE EXISTENCE, AND SHALL LIVE FOREVER, AND SHALL NOT DIE A SECOND TIME.
   - **The Saite rubric and finding-legend appended to it**: "The following is from the rubric to this chapter in the Saite Recension (see Lepsius, op. cit., Bl. 53)" — the deceased "shall know] the hidden things of the underworld, he shall penetrate the hidden things in Neter-khertet (the underworld)." Then: "[This chapter] was found in the large hall(?) of the Temple under the reign of his Majesty Hesepti, triumphant, and it was found in the cavern of the mountain which Horus made for his father Osiris Un-nefer, triumphant. Now since Ra looketh upon this deceased in his own flesh, he shall look upon him as the company of the gods. The fear of him shall be great, and the awe of him shall be mighty in the heart of men, and gods, and Khus, and the damned. He shall be with his soul and shall live forever; he shall not die a second time in the underworld; and on the day of weighing of words no evil hap shall befall him. He shall be triumphant over his enemies, and his sepulchral meals shall be upon the altar of Ra in the course of each day, day by day."

3. **LIVING NIGH UNTO RA** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 17 and 18).] Stated purpose: "The chapter of having existence nigh unto Ra." Speaker: Nu. Opens with an identity claim — "I am that god Ra who shineth in the night. Every being who followeth in his train shall have life in the following of the god Thoth, and he shall give unto him the risings of Horus in the darkness." The speaker recounts what he has already done: "I am a follower of Ra, and [I have] received his iron weapon ... I have cried unto the mighty goddess, I have equipped the god Hu, and I alone have removed the Nebt god from the path of Ra. I am a Khu, and I have come to the divine prince at the bounds of the horizon." The petition is for embarkation and free action: "let me embark in thy boat, O Ra, let me open myself out in the form of a divine hawk, let me give my commands in words, let me do battle in my Sekhem(?), let me be master under my vine. Let me embark in thy boat, O Ra, in peace, and let me sail in peace to the beautiful Amentet." The speaker asks that Tem speak to him, "[saying], 'Wouldst [thou] enter therein?'" — and the text closes on immensity: "The lady, the goddess Mchen, is a million of years, yea, two million years in extent, and dwelleth in the House of Urt and Nif-urt [and in] the Lake of a million years ... 'On every road and among these millions of years is Ra the lord, and his path is in the fire, and they go round about behind him, and they go round about behind him.'" [OCR: the goddess's name is printed "Mchen" and cannot be confirmed; a stray "299" is intruded into the final sentence between "round about" and "behind him" — evidently a page number pulled into the line, since the clause is complete and doubled without it.] No rubric.

4. **BRINGING MEN BACK TO EARTH** — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 18).] Stated purpose: "The chapter of causing a man to come back to see his house upon Earth." Speaker: "The Osiris Ani." The whole text, four sentences: "I am the Lion-god coming forth with extended strides. I have shot arrows and I have wounded the prey; I have shot arrows and I have wounded the prey. I am the Eye of Horus, and I pass through the Eye of Horus at this season. I have arrived at the furrows; let the Osiris Ani advance in peace." The only Ani text in the unit, and the shortest chapter in it.

5. **MAKING PERFECT THE KHU** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 16).] Stated purpose and occasion: "The book of making perfect the Khu, which is to be recited on the day of the month." Speaker: Nu. A sunrise hymn: food falls from the eastern horizon at the word of Nut "who maketh plain the paths of Ra"; the god is told to "draw thou into thyself the winds, inhale the north wind, swallow thou the skin(?) of thy net on the day wherein thou breathest right and truth." The renewal refrain runs twice — "thou comest, being renewed each day" — around the image "thou art that Image of gold, and thou dost possess the splendors of the disks of heaven and art terrible." The gods in the heavens ascribe to Nu "praises which are like unto those ascribed unto Ra"; he "seeketh(?) the wreret crown of Ra"; he "shall not tarry, and he shall not lie without motion in this land forever. Being doubly beautiful [he] shall see with his two eyes, and he shall hear with his two ears; rightly and truly, rightly and truly." It closes on secrecy and on transformation: "He doth not tell that which he hath seen, and he doth not repeat that which he hath heard in the secret places ... The Osiris Nu, the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, is a hawk, the transformations of which are mighty (or manifold)."
   - **Rubric** (capitals, the most materially detailed in the unit): [THIS CHAPTER SHALL BE] RECITED OVER A BOAT FOUR CUBITS IN ITS LENGTH AND MADE OF GREEN PORCELAIN [ON WHICH HAVE BEEN PAINTED] THE DIVINE SOVEREIGN CHIEFS OF THE CITIES; AND A HEAVEN WITH ITS STARS SHALL [ALSO] BE MADE, AND THIS THOU SHALT HAVE MADE CEREMONIALLY PURE BY MEANS OF NATRON AND INCENSE. AND, BEHOLD, THOU SHALT MAKE AN IMAGE OF RA IN YELLOW(?) COLOR UPON A NEW PLAQUE AND SET IT AT THE BOWS OF THE BOAT. AND BEHOLD, THOU SHALT PLACE AN IMAGE OF THE KHU WHICH THOU DOST WISH TO MAKE PERFECT [AND PLACE IT] IN THIS BOAT, AND THOU SHALT MAKE IT TO TRAVEL ABOUT IN THE BOAT [WHICH SHALL BE MADE IN THE FORM OF THE BOAT] OF RA; AND HE SHALL SEE THE GOD RA HIMSELF THEREIN. LET NOT THE EYE OF ANY MAN WHATSOEVER LOOK UPON IT WITH THE EXCEPTION OF THINE OWN SELF, OR THY FATHER, OR THY SON, AND GUARD [THIS] WITH GREAT CARE. [NOW THESE THINGS] SHALL MAKE THE KHU PERFECT IN THE HEART OF RA, AND IT SHALL GIVE UNTO HIM POWER WITH THE COMPANY OF THE GODS; AND THE GODS SHALL LOOK UPON HIM AS A DIVINE BEING LIKE UNTO THEMSELVES; AND MANKIND AND THE DEAD SHALL LOOK UPON HIM AND SHALL FALL DOWN UPON THEIR FACES, AND HE SHALL BE SEEN IN THE UNDERWORLD IN THE FORM OF THE RADIANCE OF RA.

6. **MAKING PERFECT THE KHU** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 17).] Stated purpose: "Another chapter of making perfect the Khu." Speaker: Nu. Addressed to Khepera in his boat, "who decreest joy for millions of years unto those who love him, who givest their hearts' desire unto mankind ... who hast overthrown Apep." The petition is exterminatory and taxonomic — the children of Seb are to overthrow Nu's enemies and destroy them from the boat of Ra, "and the god Horus shall cut off their heads in heaven [where they are] in the form of feathered fowl, and their hind parts shall be on the earth in the form of animals and in the Lake in the form of fishes." Every male and every female fiend is to be destroyed "whether he descendeth from the heaven, or whether he cometh forth from the earth, or whether they come upon the waters, or whether they advance toward the stars," by "the god Thoth, the son of Aner, coming forth from the Anerti." Then the blood image: "cause ye this god, the mighty one of slaughter, the being greatly to be feared, to make himself clean in your blood and to bathe himself in your gore." The deceased is Horus "to whom his mother the goddess Isis hath given birth, and whom the goddess Nephthys hath nursed and dandled, even like Horus when [he] repulsed the fiends of the god Suti"; at the sight of the ureret crown on his head "they fall down upon their faces and they glorify [him]."
   - **Rubric** (capitals): [THIS CHAPTER] SHALL BE RECITED OVER A HAWK STANDING AND HAVING THE WHITE CROWN UPON HIS HEAD, [AND OVER FIGURES OF] TEM, SHU, TEFNUT, SEB, NUT, OSIRIS, ISIS, SUTI, AND NEPHTHYS PAINTED IN YELLOW COLOR UPON A NEW PLAQUE, WHICH SHALL BE PLACED IN [A MODEL OF] THE BOAT [OF THE SUN], ALONG WITH A FIGURE OF THE DECEASED WHOM THOU WOULDST MAKE PERFECT: THESE SHALT THOU ANOINT WITH CEDAR OIL, AND INCENSE SHALL BE OFFERED UP TO THEM ON THE FIRE, AND FEATHERED FOWL SHALL BE ROASTED. IT IS AN ACT OF PRAISE TO RA AS HE JOURNEYETH, AND IT SHALL CAUSE A MAN TO HAVE HIS BEING ALONG WITH RA DAY BY DAY, WHITHERSOEVER THE GOD VOYAGETH; AND IT SHALL DESTROY THE ENEMIES OF RA IN VERY TRUTH REGULARLY AND CONTINUALLY.

7. **THE NEW MOON** — [From Lepsius Todtenbuch, Bl. 55.] Stated purpose and occasion: "Another chapter to be recited when the moon reneweth itself on the day of the month." Speaker: "The Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant." Short, and carrying a variant reading inside itself: "'Osiris unfettereth,' or, as others say, 'openeth the storm cloud [in] the body of heaven, and is unfettered himself; Horus is made strong happily each day. He whose transformations are great (or many) hath offerings made unto him at the moment, and he hath made an end of the storm which is in the face of the Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant. Verily he cometh, and he is Ra in [his] journeying, and he is the four celestial gods in the heavens above. The Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant, cometh forth in his day, and he embarketh among the tackle of the boat."
   - **Rubric** (capitals; a two-branch rubric, one clause for the dead and one for the living): IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN BY THE DECEASED HE SHALL BECOME A PERFECT KHU IN THE UNDERWORLD, AND HE SHALL NOT DIE THEREIN A SECOND TIME, AND HE SHALL EAT HIS FOOD SIDE BY SIDE WITH OSIRIS. IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN BY HIM UPON EARTH HE SHALL BE LIKE UNTO THOTH, AND HE SHALL BE ADORED BY THE LIVING ONES; HE SHALL NOT FALL HEADLONG AT THE MOMENT OF ROYAL FLAME OF THE GODDESS BAST, AND THE MIGHTY PRINCESS SHALL MAKE HIM TO ADVANCE HAPPILY.

8. **TRAVELLING IN THE BOAT OF RA** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 28).] Stated purpose: "Another chapter of travelling in the great boat of Ra." Speaker: Nu. Whole text: "Behold now, O ye luminaries in Annu, ye people in Kher-aba, the god Kha(?) hath been born; his cordage hath been completed, and the instrument wherewith he maketh his way hath [he] grasped firmly. I have protected the implements of the gods, and I have delivered the boat Kha(?) for him. I have come forth into heaven, and I have travelled therein with Ra in the form of an ape, and have turned back the paths of Nut at the staircase of the god Sebek."

9. **OF MAKING PERFECT THE KHU** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 16).] Stated purpose and occasion: "Another chapter of making perfect the Khu. [It shall be recited] on the festival of Six." [OCR: the festival name is printed "Six" and cannot be confirmed.] Speaker: Nu. **This chapter opens with the same sentence as the preceding one and then continues far beyond it** — "Behold now, O ye luminaries in Annu (Heliopolis), ye people in Kher-aba, the god hath been born; his cordage(?) hath been completed, and the instrument wherewith he maketh his way he hath grasped firmly" — the two texts being, on the evidence of the printed page, a short and a long version of one composition (the shorter names the boat "Kha(?)" and the god "Sebek"; the longer omits the boat's name and prints "Sebaku," and travels "in the form of apes" rather than "of an ape"). The long version proceeds: "The hearts of Seb and Nut are glad and repeat the name which is new. Un-neferu reneweth [his] youth, Ra is in his splendors of light, Unti hath his speech, and lo, the god of the Inundation is Prince among the gods. The taste of sweetness hath forced a way into the heart of the destitute one, and the lord of thy outcries hath been done away with, and the oars(?) of the company of the gods are in vigorous motion." Then the petitions: that Nu be great in heaven "even as thou art great among the gods"; deliverance "from every evil and murderous thing which may be wrought upon him by the Fiend"; that he be "stronger than all the gods, all the Khus, and all the dead." Then the assertions of standing: "The Osiris Nu is strong and is the lord of powers. The Osiris Nu is the lord of right and truth which the goddess Uatchit worketh." The protection formula follows — "The strength which protects the Osiris Nu is the strength which protects the god Ra in heaven" — and then the crocodile: "The Osiris Nu driveth back the Crocodile from Ra day by day ... the gods rejoice greatly when the Osiris Nu repulseth the Crocodile." Two amuletic guarantees close that stretch — "The Osiris Nu hath the amulet(?) of the god, and the cloud of Nebt shall not come nigh unto him, and the divine guardians of the mansions of the sky shall not destroy him" — followed by the sentence the printed rubric cuts in half, "The Osiris Nu is a divine being whose face is hidden, and he dwelleth within the Great House [as] the chief of the Shrine of the god." The chapter's last lines make him a messenger: "The Osiris Nu carrieth the words of the gods to Ra, and he cometh and maketh supplication unto the divine lord with the words of his message. The Osiris Nu is strong of heart, and he maketh his offering at the moment among those who perform the ceremonies of sacrifice."
   - **Rubric** (capitals): [THIS CHAPTER] SHALL BE SAID OVER A FIGURE OF THE DECEASED WHICH SHALL BE PLACED IN [A MODEL OF] THE BOAT OF THE SUN, AND BEHOLD, [HE THAT RECITETH IT] SHALL BE WASHED, AND SHALL BE CEREMONIALLY PURE, AND HE SHALL HAVE BURNT INCENSE BEFORE RA, AND SHALL HAVE OFFERED WINE, AND CAKES, AND ROASTED FOWL FOR THE JOURNEY [OF THE DECEASED] IN THE BOAT OF RA. NOW, EVERY KHU FOR WHOM SUCH THINGS ARE DONE SHALL HAVE AN EXISTENCE AMONG THE LIVING ONES, AND HE SHALL NEVER PERISH, AND HE SHALL HAVE A BEING LIKE UNTO THAT OF THE HOLY GOD; NO EVIL THING WHATSOEVER SHALL ATTACK HIM. AND HE SHALL BE LIKE UNTO A HAPPY KHU IN AMENTET, AND HE SHALL NOT DIE A SECOND TIME. HE SHALL EAT AND HE SHALL DRINK IN THE PRESENCE OF OSIRIS EACH DAY; HE SHALL BE BORNE ALONG WITH THE KINGS OF THE NORTH AND OF THE SOUTH EACH AND EVERY DAY; HE SHALL QUAFF WATER AT THE FOUNTAIN-HEAD; HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY EVEN AS DOTH HORUS; HE SHALL LIVE AND SHALL BECOME LIKE UNTO GOD; AND HE SHALL BE HYMNED BY THE LIVING ONES, EVEN AS IS RA EACH AND EVERY DAY CONTINUALLY AND REGULARLY FOREVER. *(On the printed page this rubric is set as an inserted block that interrupts the body text mid-sentence and runs across a page turn — see the OCR section below for the reconstruction.)*

10. **SAILING IN THE GREAT BOAT** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 28).] Stated purpose: "The chapter of sailing in the great boat of Ra to pass over the circle of bright flame." Speaker: Nu. Addressed first to the flames themselves: "[Hail], ye bright and shining flames that keep your place behind Ra, and which slay behind him, the boat of Ra is in fear of the whirlwind and the storm; shine ye forth, then, and make [ye yourselves] visible." The speaker recites his credentials as a series of completed acts — he has come with the god Sek-hra "from the bight of his holy lake," seen the Maat goddesses pass with their lion-gods, made a way in front of the boat of Ra, "lifted myself up into his divine Disk," brought Tefnut to him, "brought unto him the jaws of the passages of the tomb," "brought unto him the bones which are in Annu (Heliopolis)," gathered together his manifold parts, driven back Apep, "spit upon his gashes for him." Then the passage itself: "I have gone forward over the circle of bright flame which is behind the lord of the lock of hair which moveth round about." It closes appealing to the worms: "Behold, ye who cry out over yourselves, ye worms in [your] hidden places, grant ye that I may pass onward, for I am the mighty one, the lord of divine strength, and I am the spiritual body (sah) of the lord of divine right and truth made by the goddess Uatchit ... [I am] a great god, and [I have been] judged by the company of his gods; grant that divine, sepulchral meals may be given unto me." No rubric.

11. **THE FOUR FLAMES** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 26).] **The last translated text in the book.** Stated purpose: "The chapter of the four blazing flames which are made for the Khu." Its ritual instruction is placed *before* the recitation rather than after it, and is not set in capitals: "Behold, thou shalt make four square troughs of clay, whereon thou shalt scatter incense, and thou shalt fill them with the milk of a white cow, and by means of these thou shalt extinguish the flame." Speaker: Nu. The text works by relentless doubling, each formula said first for "Osiris, governor of Amenti" and then for "Osiris Nu": "The fire cometh to thy KA, O Osiris, governor of Amenti; the fire cometh to thy KA, O Osiris Nu." The flame rises in Abtu (Abydos) and is brought to the Eye of Horus; "It is set in order upon thy brow ... and it is fixed within thy shrine and riseth upon thy brow; it is set in order upon thy breast, O Osiris Nu." The Eye of Horus then does the protective work in fourfold repetition — "it keepeth thee in safety; it casteth down headlong all thine enemies for thee and all thine enemies have fallen headlong before thee" — and is described in its own right: "The Eye of Horus cometh, it is sound and well, and it sendeth forth rays like unto Ra in the horizon; it covereth over with darkness the powers of Suti, it taketh possession thereof and it bringeth its flame against him upon [its] feet(?)." The four flames then enter the Ka. The children of Horus are named and their obligation argued from precedent, and the chapter — and Section II — ends on offerings and titles: "Hail, Osiris, governor of Amenti, grant thou light and fire to the happy soul which is in Suten-henen (Heracleopolis); and [O ye children of Horus] grant ye power unto the living soul of the Osiris Nu within his flame. Let him not be repulsed and let him not be driven back at the doors of Amentet; oh let his offerings of bread and of linen garments be brought unto him among [those of] the lords of funeral oblations, oh, offer ye praises as unto a god, to the Osiris Nu, destroyer of his opponent[s] in his form of right and truth and in his attributes of a god of right and truth."

**How Section II ends (the question the unit was asked to settle)**
- There is **no colophon, no summary, no editorial sign-off, and no "end of Section II" line**. The section simply stops: THE FOUR FLAMES runs to its final sentence, the printed page number 203 follows, and the next body text in the volume is the SECTION III half-title "SECTION III / List of Egyptian Deities."
- Between the two lie only plate pages: three of them — two carrying no extracted text at all and one surviving as character noise only. Two captions stand at the head of that last text page, "(Opposite) Scenes and texts ... from the sarcophagus of King Nekht-Herwhebt, B.C. 378" and "(Following spread) A hand-painted wooden sarcophagus," so the three plates are between them accounted for as pictures; which caption names which page cannot be settled from the text layer alone, and this edition has no page scans to check against.
- The last chapter also declines the pattern of the chapters around it — it has no closing rubric in capitals, its ritual instruction standing at the head instead. So the section ends on the deceased's own voice, mid-liturgy, rather than on a promise to the reciter.

## Key quotes
- "The heavens are opened, the earth is opened, the West is opened, the East is opened, the southern half of heaven is opened, the northern half of heaven is opened, the doors are opened, and the gates are thrown wide open to Ra [as] he cometh forth from the horizon." — MAKING PERFECT THE KHU (Nu, sheet 17), the sunrise as universal unbarring.
- "he breatheth, and the god Shu [cometh into being], and he createth the goddess Tefnut." — the same chapter; creation as an incident of the god's breathing.
- "Let not the Osiris Nu ... walk in the valley of darkness, let him not enter into the Lake of those who are evil, and let him have no existence among the damned, even for a moment." — the same chapter's central prohibition.
- "IF THESE CEREMONIES BE PERFORMED HIS SOUL SHALL HAVE EXISTENCE, AND SHALL LIVE FOREVER, AND SHALL NOT DIE A SECOND TIME." — the birthday-of-Osiris rubric, stating the payoff in its plainest form.

- "I am that god Ra who shineth in the night." — LIVING NIGH UNTO RA, the identity claim on which the chapter rests.
- "I am the Lion-god coming forth with extended strides. I have shot arrows and I have wounded the prey." — BRINGING MEN BACK TO EARTH, the whole force of the shortest chapter in the unit.
- "Thou takest count of thy bones, thou gatherest together thy members, thou settest thy face toward the beautiful Amentet, and thou comest, being renewed each day." — MAKING PERFECT THE KHU (Nu, sheet 16), daily resurrection as reassembly.

- "LET NOT THE EYE OF ANY MAN WHATSOEVER LOOK UPON IT WITH THE EXCEPTION OF THINE OWN SELF, OR THY FATHER, OR THY SON, AND GUARD [THIS] WITH GREAT CARE." — the green-porcelain-boat rubric's secrecy clause.

## Thread
Section II ends where its logic has been heading throughout: not in argument or judgment but in workshop instructions. The earlier translated units established the deceased's standing — adoration of the god, the declaration of innocence, the restoration of mouth and heart, passage through the halls. This closing stretch assumes all of that is settled and concentrates on the two things left: getting aboard Ra's boat, and being made *perfect* rather than merely acquitted. Hence the strange density of one title, "making perfect the Khu," across four chapters drawn from two sheets (16 and 17) of a single papyrus — a sign, on the printed evidence, that the compilers were gathering variants of one operation rather than composing a sequence. The rubrics carry the unit's real weight, and they are the most concrete in the book: a boat painted in colors, a boat four cubits long in green porcelain with a starred heaven, a hawk in the white crown on a new plaque with nine named gods in yellow, cedar oil, natron, roasted fowl, four clay troughs of milk. Where the earlier chapters bargained with words, these manufacture objects, and the objects are to be hidden. The recurring promise — not to die a second time — reveals what the whole apparatus is defending against: the discovery that survival of the first death is not final. The section's last text answers that with fire brought from Abtu and fixed on the brow, and with the four sons of Horus obliged, by what they already did for Osiris, to do the same for a palace overseer named Nu. Then the translations stop, without ceremony, and the volume becomes a reference work.

## Connections
- The papyri named in every provenance line here — Nu (British Museum No. 10,477), Ani (No. 10,470) — are catalogued at the opening of Section II, where Budge describes the Papyrus of Nu as bearing "upon every line of it the impress of care and knowledge"; this unit is where that papyrus supplies the overwhelming majority of the text.
- The convention that rubrics are printed in capital letters, stated once at the opening of Section II, is what makes the rubrics in this unit identifiable as such; the one exception here — the clay-trough instruction opening THE FOUR FLAMES, printed in ordinary type — is worth noting against that convention.
- Lepsius's edition of the Saite Recension, adopted at the opening of Section II as the source of the chapter numbering, reappears here in two roles: as the direct source of two texts (HYMN OF PRAISE TO OSIRIS and THE NEW MOON, both spoken by Auf-ankh) and as the source of an alternative rubric appended to a Nu chapter.
- The "triumphant / victorious" epithet, established in the earlier translated units, is attached to the speaker in every text here except the four-sentence Ani chapter BRINGING MEN BACK TO EARTH, which names "the Osiris Ani" without it. The "divine sovereign chiefs" before whom triumph must be declared recur in four of the eleven texts (the HYMN OF PRAISE TO OSIRIS, the birthday-of-Osiris MAKING PERFECT THE KHU, the green-porcelain rubric, and the hawk-rubric chapter); LIVING NIGH UNTO RA and SAILING IN THE GREAT BOAT use "divine princes" instead.
- The chapters immediately preceding this unit — PRAISE OF THE GODS (Tomb of Rameses IV) and ADORATION OF THE GODS OF THE QERTI (Papyrus of Ptah-mes) — share the doorkeeper-and-passage material that the boat chapters here replace with navigation.
- Section III, the List of Egyptian Deities, begins immediately after this unit and is the reference for the divine names crowded into these chapters (Khepera, Tem, Shu, Tefnut, Seb, Nut, Isis, Nephthys, Suti, Thoth, Sebek, Uatchit, Bast, Apep, and the four children of Horus).
