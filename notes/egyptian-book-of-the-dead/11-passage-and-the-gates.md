# Ch 11 — Passage and the Gates: Knowing the Souls, Re-stau, and the Hall of Double Maati

## In one paragraph
This stretch of Section II is primary translated scripture and it is the book's own account of how a dead man gets *through* things. Its chapters are not petitions for goods but demonstrations of knowledge: the deceased recites the divisions of the Field of Peace and says "I have entered in to thee" to each by name; he proves he knows the Souls of Pe, of Nekhen, of Khemennu, of Annu, telling the myths that explain why those cities belong to Horus; he receives paths in Re-stau and comes forth from it; and then he arrives at the Hall of Double Maati, where the volume prints the judgment liturgy in full — two Introductions to Maati (from Ani and from Nu), the forty-two-fold Negative Confession from Nebseni, and Nu's Address to the Gods of the Underworld. That Address contains the mechanism the whole book runs on: the deceased is stopped, asked who he is and what his name is, made to answer a catechism about a journey he has taken, and then challenged in turn by every physical component of the door — bolts, both lintels, threshold, fastening, socket of the fastening, guardian of the leaf, posts, the floor itself, and finally the doorkeeper — each of which refuses him "unless thou tellest [me] my name," and each of which lets him by the moment he supplies it. The unit closes with the chapters addressed to the collective gatekeepers: the four Hour Apes who undo his sin and open the hidden pylons of Amentet, the Praise of the Gods of the Qerti from the Tomb of Rameses IV, its near-duplicate Adoration from the Papyrus of Ptah-mes, and a Hymn of Praise to Osiris. Editorial voice appears here only in the bracketed provenance lines, the parenthetical "(i.e., …)" glosses inside the translations, and the one unattributed connective that introduces the fuller form of the Re-stau chapter; everything else is either translated text or a 2016 photo caption, and the note labels which is which.

## Core claims
- The texts claim that passage is granted for **knowledge, not merit alone** — the recurring formula is "I, even I, know," and the recurring reward is "Since thou knowest pass on." The Souls chapters end by naming their answer outright ("I, even I, know the Souls of Nekhen, namely, Horus, Tuamautef, and Qebhsennuf").
- They claim knowledge is a *secret* that must be withheld from the wrong audience even while it is proved to the right one: the Nebseni Khemennu chapter says "I will neither declare it unto men nor tell it unto the gods," and the Nu chapter of the same title prints the same refusal as "I will not declare it unto men, and I will not tell it unto the gods" — twice over, in immediate succession.
- The Hall of Double Maati texts claim the deceased knows the god's name, the names of "the two and forty gods who exist with thee in this Hall of double Maati," and the god's own compound name "Rekhti-merti-neb-Maati" — and that this knowledge is itself the argument against condemnation: "therefore let not evil befall me in this land and in this Hall of double Maati, because I, even I, know the name[s] of these gods who are therein."
- They claim the forty-two gods "live as warders of sinners and who feed upon their blood on the day when the lives of men are taken into account in the presence of the god Un-nefer."
- The Negative Confession claims innocence in the form of a denial paired to a named god and his city — forty-two hails, forty-two sins disavowed, one per god.
- Nu's Address claims positive virtue as well as absence of sin: "I have given bread to the hungry man, and water to the thirsty man, and apparel to the naked man, and a boat to the [shipwrecked] mariner."
- The door sequence claims that every *part* of a door has a name and a will of its own, and that each will independently obstruct: "'We will not let thee enter in through us,' say the bolts of the door, 'unless thou tellest [us] our names.'"
- The floor of the Hall claims a right to refuse on grounds of holiness and ignorance: "because I am silent and I am holy, and because I do not know the name[s] of thy two feet wherewith thou wouldst walk upon me."
- The Hour Apes chapter claims sins can be actively removed by intercessors rather than merely denied: the apes answer "we have done away with thy wickedness, and we have put away thy sin ... and we have destroyed [all] the evil which belonged to thee upon the earth."
- The Souls of Pe chapter claims the city of Pe was Ra's compensation to Horus for the injury to his eye, and that Suti caused the injury by transforming himself into a black pig — from which it derives the pig's status: "The pig is an abominable thing unto Horus."
- The Souls of Nekhen chapter claims Nekhen was given to Horus "for the habitation of his two arms and hands," after Sebek netted them out of the water.
- The Rameses IV text claims the king's passage is already accomplished: "he hath made his way through the gates of heaven, and of earth, and of the underworld, even as hath Ra."
- The rubrics claim measurable results: the Hall of Double Maati rubric promises that the deceased "shall not be turned back at any door in Amentet"; the Khemennu rubric promises that "offal shall be an abomination unto him, and he shall not drink filthy water."

## Concepts
- **Sekhet-hetepet / Sekhet-hetep** — the Field of Peace, entered division by division; the speaker addresses each by name ("O Qenqentet, I have entered into thee") and ends by ascribing "praises unto the gods who dwell in Sekhet-hetep."
- **"I have entered in to thee"** — the entry formula of the Sekhet-hetepet litany: the name of the place is vocative, the entry is asserted in the perfect tense, and a proof of what was done there follows.
- **knowing the Souls (of Pe, Nekhen, Khemennu, Annu, the East)** — a chapter type in which the deceased demonstrates possession of a city's hidden myth and then names its three tutelary Souls. Pe: "Horus, Mesthi, and Hapi." Nekhen: "Horus, Tuamautef, and Qebhsennuf." Annu: "Ra, Shu, and Tefnut." (The Nu chapter headed for Khemennu closes on a second triad, but names it as Annu's: "I, even I, know the Souls of Annu ... They are Thoth the Hidden one, and Sa, and Tem.")
- **Re-stau** — the funerary region whose paths must be "received" and from which one must "come forth"; three consecutive chapter headings in this unit are about receiving its paths, being born in it, and coming out of it, and the fuller form appended to the third is titled for "knowing the name of Osiris and of entering into and of going out from Re-Stau."
- **Ammehet** — the underworld through which a way is made; "the chapter of coming forth from Heaven, and of making a way through the Ammehet," and in the Hour Apes chapter "grant ye that I may make my way through the underworld (ammehet)."
- **the Hall of Double Maati** — the hall of judgment which the deceased enters "so that he may be separated from every sin which he hath done and may behold the faces of the gods." Its lord is named "Rekhti-merti-neb-Maati," glossed in the text as "twin-sisters with two eyes, ladies of double Maati."
- **the two and forty gods** — the assessors of the Hall, each addressed by title, each named with the place he "comest forth from," each matched to one denied sin.
- **naming the parts of the door** — the challenge mechanism: bolts, right lintel, left lintel, threshold, fastening, socket of the fastening, guardian of the leaf, posts, floor, and doorkeeper each demand their own name before yielding.
- **Maau-Taui** — glossed in the text as "he who keepeth the record of the two lands"; the name the deceased must produce for "the god that dwelleth in his hour," and identified two lines later: "He is Thoth."
- **Hra-f-ha-f** — glossed "he whose face is behind him"; the god before whom the deceased "has been made to give evidence" and who "hath given a decision [concerning me]."
- **Baba** — the god "who feedeth upon the entrails of the mighty ones upon the day of the great judgment," from whom the gods of the Hall are asked to deliver the speaker.
- **the Hour Apes** — "ye four apes who sit in the bows of the boat of Ra," who "convey right and truth to Neb-er-tcher" and who sit "in judgment on my misery and on my strength."
- **the hidden pylons of Amentet** — what the apes are asked to let the deceased pass through, paired with entry into Re-stau: "let me pass through the hidden pylons of Amentet."
- **the Qerti** — the region whose gods are "the guardians of the doors of the underworld, who keep ward over the god, who bear and proclaim [the names of those who come] into the presence of the god Osiris." The Negative Confession in this same unit glosses the name in passing: "Hail, Qerti, (i.e., the two sources of the Nile), who come forth from Amentet."
- **guardians of the doors / doorkeepers** — the collective gatekeepers addressed in the last three chapters: "Hail, ye doorkeepers, who guard your gates, who punish souls, who devour the bodies of the dead."
- **sahu (spiritual bodies)** — glossed inline; those "who dwell in their spiritual bodies (sau) in the habitation where libations are made unto Osiris," and the Sahu seen "in the city of Qem-ur."

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(Every text in the unit, in printed order: title as printed, bracketed provenance as printed, stated purpose, and content quoted.)*

**The four voices in this range, before the entries** — the page mixes them without warning, and the OCR splices captions into the middle of sentences, so each is labelled below: (i) the **translated scripture** itself, spoken by a named owner ("The overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant, saith"), including the speech put into the mouths of gods, apes and door-parts; (ii) the **translator's apparatus inside the translation** — the parenthetical "(i.e., …)" glosses, the "(?)" uncertainty marks, and the square brackets supplying words not in the papyrus; (iii) the **edition's editorial voice**, present here as the bracketed provenance line heading each chapter, the capitalized rubrics, and the one unattributed connective "The following is the chapter in a fuller form:"; (iv) the **2016 volume's photo captions** ("(Opposite)", "(Previous spread)", "(Following spread)", "(Top)/(Bottom)", "(Below)"), which are modern editorial matter about photographs and belong to no ancient text — these are collected separately below and are never quoted as scripture.

1. **SEKHET-HETEPET (conclusion)** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 17).] *The chapter's title, provenance and opening stand just before this unit; its closing litany falls inside it.* The surviving portion is a sequence of entries into named divisions of the Field, each opening with a vocative and the perfect "I have entered in to thee," each followed by proof of what was done or known there: "**Tchefet**, I have entered in to thee. I array myself in apparel, and I gird myself with the sa garment of Ra"; "O **Unen-em-hetep**, lord of the two lands, I have entered in to thee, and I have plunged into the lakes of Tchesert; behold me, for all filth hath departed from me ... I have snared feathered fowl and I feed upon the finest [of them]"; "O **Qenqentet**, I have entered into thee, and I have seen the Osiris [my father], and I have gazed upon my mother, and I have made love. I have caught the worms and serpents, and I am delivered. And I know the name of the god who is opposite to the goddess Tchesert, and who hath straight hair and is equipped with two horns; he reapeth, and I both plough and reap"; "O **Hast**, I have entered in to thee, I have driven back those who would come to the turquoise [sky] ... The Great God hath given my head unto me, and he who hath bound on me my head is the Mighty one who hath turquoise(?) eyes, namely, Ari-en-ab-f (i.e., he doeth as he pleaseth)"; "O **Usert**, I have come into thee at the head of the house wherein divine food is brought for me"; "O **Smam**, I have come into thee. My heart watcheth, my head is equipped with the white crown, I am led into celestial regions ... I am the god who is the Bull, the lord of the gods, as he goeth forth from the turquoise [sky]"; "O **divine nome of wheat and barley**, I have come unto thee ... and I have taken up that which followeth me, namely, the best of the libations of the company of the gods." It ends on mooring and recitation: "I have tied up my boat in the celestial lakes, I have lifted up the post at which to anchor, I have recited the prescribed words with my voice, and I have ascribed praises unto the gods who dwell in Sekhet-hetep."
   - **Plate caption on the same page** (photographic matter, not translated text): "(Opposite) An excerpt from the Book of the Dead of Pinedjem II, who was a high priest of Amun at Thebes from 990-969 B.C. and the de facto ruler of southern Egypt." The facing page is the plate itself; the volume prints no translation of it, and the page yields only hieroglyph-strip noise in this text layer.

2. **KNOWING THE SOULS OF PE** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 18).] Purpose: "Another chapter of knowing the souls of Pe." Opens with a challenge the speaker puts to the gods rather than the reverse: "[Hail,] ye goddesses of the chase who dwell in the city of Pe ... do ye know for what reason the city of Pe hath been given unto Horus? I, even I, know though ye know it not." The myth follows as reported speech throughout. Ra gave the city "in return for the injury in his eye," and the injury is narrated: Ra said to Horus, "Let me see what is coming to pass in thine eye," and then "Look at that black pig," "and he looked, and straightway an injury was done unto his eye, [namely,] a mighty storm [took place]." Horus's complaint: "Verily, my eye seems as if it were an eye upon which Suti had inflicted a blow;" — "[and thus saying] he ate his heart." Ra's remedy: "Place ye him in his chamber, and he shall do well." The identification: "Now the black pig was Suti who had transformed himself into a black pig, and he it was who had aimed the blow of fire which was in the eye of Horus." The aetiology of the pig: "The pig is an abominable thing unto Horus; oh, but he shall do well although the pig is an abomination unto him" — followed by the gods' resolution, "Let sacrifices be made [to the gods] of his bulls, and of his goats, and of his pigs." Parentage is stated flatly: "Now the father of Mesthi, Hapi, Tuamautef and Qebhsennuf is Horus, and their mother is Isis." Horus's request: "Give me two divine brethren in the city of Pe and two divine brethren in the city of Nekhen, who [have sprung] from my body and who shall be with me in the guise of everlasting judges, then shall the earth blossom and thunder-clouds and rain be blotted out." Close: "And the name of Horus became 'Her-uatch-f' (i.e., Prince of his emerald stone). I, even I, know the Souls of Pe, namely, Horus, Mesthi, and Hapi."

3. **KNOWING THE SOULS OF NEKHEN** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 18).] Purpose: "The chapter of knowing the souls of Nekhen." The hidden thing known is "what the mother of Horus did for him" — her cry over the waters, "Speak ye unto me concerning the judgment which is upon me, [and shew me] the path behind you, and let me discover [it]"; Ra's verdict, "This son of Isis hath perished"; and Isis's summons, "Sebek, the lord of the papyrus swamp, shall be brought to us." Sebek's own report of the netting: "I went and I found the place where they had passed with my fingers on the edge of the waters, and I enclosed them in [my] net: and strong was that net." Ra's comment turns the place into a name: "So then, there are fish with the god Sebek, and [he] hath found the hands and arms of Horus for him in the land of fish; and [that] land became the land of the city of Remu (i.e., Fish)," with the cry "A land of the pool, a land of the pool to this net." The grant: "I give the city of Nekhen to Horus for the habitation of his two arms and hands, and his face shall be uncovered before his two hands and arms in the city of Nekhen." Horus then asks for Tuamautef and Qebhsennuf "to watch over my body," and Ra extends the grant to "the city of Senket (i.e., Sati)." Close: "Grant to me [that I, even I, may pass on to the Souls of Nekhen, and that I may unloose the bonds of Horus]. I, even I, know the Souls of Nekhen, namely, Horus, Tuamautef, and Qebhsennuf."

4. **KNOWING THE SOULS OF KHEMENNU** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 7).] Purpose: "The chapter of knowing the souls of Khemennu (Hermopolis)." A judicial rather than narrative chapter: "The goddess Maat is carried by the arm at the shining of the goddess Neith in the city of Mentchat, and at the shining of the Eye when it is weighed." The secrecy clause: "I know what it bringeth from the city of Kesi, and I will neither declare it unto men nor tell it unto the gods." The speaker's commission: "I have come, being the envoy of Ra, to stablish Maat upon the arm at the shining of Neith in the city of Mentchat and to adjudge the eye to him that shall scrutinize it." His pleasure in judgment: "I know Maat, which hath germinated, and hath become strong, and hath been judged, and I have joy in passing judgment upon the things which are to be judged." Close: "I, even I, know the things which are unknown on the festivals of the month and half month. Ra knoweth the hidden things of the night, and know ye that it is Thoth who hath made me to have knowledge. Homage to you, O ye Souls of Khemennu, since I know you each day."

5. **COMING FORTH FROM HEAVEN** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 18).] Purpose: "The chapter of coming forth from Heaven, and of making a way through the Ammehet, and of knowing the souls of Annu (Heliopolis)." Opens with a claim of co-existence with the creator: "I have passed the day since yesterday among the great divine beings, and I have come into being along with the god Khepera. [My] face is uncovered before the Eye, the only One, and the orbit of the night hath been opened. I am a divine being among you. I know the Souls of Annu." Then a run of rhetorical questions ("Shall not the god Ur-ma pass over it as [he] journeyeth forward with vigor? Have I not overcome(?), and have I not spoken to the gods? Behold, he that is the heir of Annu hath been destroyed"), and the hidden thing itself: "I, even I, know for what reason was made the lock of hair of the Man." The aetiology involves an injury to the mouth of the god Ami-haf, Ra's command "O heir of men, receive [thy] harpoon" — "and the harpoon-house came into being" — and the coming into being of two brethren, "Senti-Ra came into being, and Setem-ansi-f came into being." Then the lock: "he made his form into that of a woman with a lock of hair which became the divine lock in Annu, and which became the strong and mighty one in this temple ... and it became the heir of the heir of Ur-maat-f (i.e., the mighty one of the two eyes), and it became before him the god Ur-ma of Annu." Close: "I know the Souls of Annu, namely, Ra, Shu, and Tefnut."

6. **KNOWING THE SOULS OF KHEMENNU** *(second version, same title)* — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 18).] Purpose: "Another chapter of knowing the souls of Khemennu (Hermopolis)." A variant of entry 4 with different wording: "The goddess Neith shineth in Matchat, and the goddess Maat is carried by the arm of him who eateth the Eye, and who is its divine judge, and the Sem priest carrieth me over upon it." The secrecy clause is doubled: "I will not declare it unto men, and I will not tell it unto the gods; I will not declare it unto men, and I will not tell it unto the gods." The paradox of initiation: "I have entered in being an ignorant man, and I have seen the hidden things." Close, with a scale of festivals: "I, even I, know the Souls of Annu; they are great at the festival of the month, and are little at the festival of the half month. They are Thoth the Hidden one, and Sa, and Tem." *(The chapter is titled for Khemennu but its closing formula names the Souls of Annu — printed so.)*
   - **Rubric** (printed in capitals): IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] OFFAL SHALL BE AN ABOMINATION UNTO HIM, AND HE SHALL NOT DRINK FILTHY WATER.

7. **RECEIVING PATHS** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of receiving paths [whereon to walk] in Re-Stau." Text: "The paths which are above me [lead] to Re-stau. I am he who is girt about with his girdle and who cometh forth from the [goddess of] the Ureret crown. I have come, and I have stablished things in Abtu (Abydos), and I have opened out paths in Re-stau. The god Osiris hath eased my pains. I am he who maketh the waters to come into being, and who setteth his throne [thereon], and who maketh his path through the funeral valley and through the Great Lake. I have made my path, and indeed I am [Osiris]." A bracketed passage naming a different owner follows: "[Osiris was victorious over his enemies, and the Osiris **Nebget** is victorious over his enemies. He hath become as one of yourselves, [O ye gods], his protector is the Lord of eternity, he walketh even as ye walk, he standeth even as ye stand, he sitteth even as ye sit, and he talketh even as ye talk in the presence of the Great God, the Lord of Amentet.]"

8. **COMING FORTH FROM RE-STAU** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of coming forth from Re-Stau." Whole text: "I was born in Re-stau, and splendor hath been given unto me by those who dwell in their spiritual bodies (sau) in the habitation where libations are made unto Osiris. The divine ministers who are in Re-stau shall receive [me] when Osiris is led into the twofold funeral region of Osiris; oh, let me be a divine being whom they shall lead into the twofold funeral region of Osiris."

9. **COMING FORTH FROM RE-STAU** *(second chapter, identical title and provenance)* — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of coming forth from Re-Stau." Text: "I am the Great God who maketh his light. I have come to thee, O Osiris, and I offer praise unto thee. [I am] pure from the issues which are carried away from thee. Thy name is made in Re-stau, and thy power is in Abtu (Abydos). Thou art raised up, then, O Osiris, and thou goest round about through heaven with Ra, and thou lookest upon the generations of men, O thou One who circlest, thou Ra. Behold, verily, I have said unto thee, O Osiris, 'I am the spiritual body of the God,' and I say, 'Let it come to pass that I shall never be repulsed before thee, O Osiris.'"
   - **The fuller form, printed immediately after** with an unattributed editorial connective: "The following is the chapter in a fuller form: The chapter of knowing the name of Osiris and of entering into and of going out from Re-Stau [in all the forms wherein he willeth to come forth]. The scribe **Mes-em-neter**, triumphant, saith:" — it opens "I am the Great **Name** who maketh his light" (where the shorter version has "the Great God"), adds "[Thy] name hath been made in Re-stau when it hath fallen therein" and a doubled exaltation, "Homage to thee, O Osiris, in thy strength and in thy power, thou hast obtained the mastery in Re-stau. Thou art raised up, O Osiris, in thy might and in thy power, thou art raised up, O Osiris, and thy might is in Re-stau, and thy power is in Abtu (Abydos)," and ends on the same refusal to be repulsed.

10. **GOING ABOUT IN THE UNDERWORLD** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of going in after coming forth [from the underworld]." **A challenge-and-answer text.** It opens with the demand and the interrogation run together, unattributed: "Open unto me? Who then art thou? Whither goest thou? What is thy name?" The answer is a claim of kinship followed by the naming of the parts of his boat, one by one: "I am one of you, '**Assembler of Souls**' is the name of my boat; '**Making the hair to stand on end**' is the name of the oars; '**Watchful one**' is the name of its bows; '**Evil is it**' is the name of the rudder; '**Steering straight for the middle**' is the name of the Matchabet; so likewise [the boat] is a type of my sailing onward to the pool." The requests follow: "Let there be given unto me vessels of milk, together with cakes, and loaves of bread, and cups of drink, and pieces of meat in the Temple of Anpu," or (as others say), "Grant thou me [these things] wholly. Let it be so done unto me that I may enter in like a hawk, and that I may come forth like the Bennu bird, [and like] the Morning Star. Let me make [my] path so that [I] may go in peace into the beautiful Amentet, and let the Lake of Osiris be mine. Let me make my path, and let me enter in, and let me adore Osiris, the Lord of life."

11. **ENTERING INTO THE GREAT HOUSE** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] Purpose: "The chapter of entering into the Great House." Whole text: "Homage to thee, O Thoth. I am Thoth, who have weighed the two divine Fighters (i.e., Horus and Set), I have destroyed their warfare and I have diminished their wailings. I have delivered the Atu fish in his turning back, and I have performed that which thou didst order concerning him, and afterward I lay down within my eye. [I am he who hath been without opposition. I have come; do thou look upon me in the Temple of Nem-hra (or Uhem-hra).] I give commands in the words of the divine aged ones, and, moreover, I guide for thee the lesser deities."

12. **ENTERING THE PRESENCE** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] Purpose: "The chapter of going into the presence of the divine sovereign princes of Osiris." Begins with settlement and husbandry — "My soul hath built for me a habitation in the city of Tattu; I sow seed in the city of Pe, and I plough my field with my laborers(?), and for this reason my palm tree is like Amsu" — then the doubled abomination formula, "That which is an abomination unto me, that which is an abomination unto me is filth. I shall not eat thereof," and the clean diet that replaces it: "my cakes are [made] of white grain, and my ale is [made] of red grain, and behold, the Sektet boat and the Matet boat bring them to me." A **doorkeeper is addressed directly**: "Hail, thou guardian of the divine doors of the god Sehetep-taui (i.e., 'he who maketh the world to be at peace'), bring [thou] to me that of which they make sepulchral meals; grant thou that I may lift up the branches(?)." The chapter then claims judicial protection against any god who opposes him — "If any god or any goddess shall come forth against me he shall be judged by the ancestors of the year who live upon hearts ... and Osiris shall devour him at [his] coming forth from Abtu" — and rights of speech with every order of being: "I shall speak with the followers of the gods. I shall speak with the Disk and I shall speak with the denizens of heaven." Its summit is equality with Osiris: "And behold, I shall be there with Osiris. My condition of completeness shall be his condition of completeness among the divine princes. I shall speak unto him [with] the words of men, and he shall repeat unto me the words of the gods." It ends with an equipment claim and a bracketed passage naming a different owner: "I am equipped [with the power] of all the khus, [being the form of the Sahu (i.e., spiritual bodies) of Annu, Tattu, Suten-henen, Abtu, Apu, and Sennu. The Osiris **Auf-ankh** is victorious over every god and every goddess who are hidden in Neter-khertet]."

13. **THE INTRODUCTION TO MAATI** — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 30).] Purpose: "The chapter of entering into the hall of Double Maati; a hymn of praise to Osiris, the governor of Amentet." The approach is described as a journey into sterility and hiddenness: "I came and I drew nigh unto [the place where] the acacia-tree groweth not, where the tree thick with leaves existeth not, and where the ground yieldeth neither herb nor grass. Then I entered into the hidden place, and I spake with the god Set, and my protector(?) advanced to me, and his face was clothed (or covered), and [he] fell upon the hidden things." The officials of the gate appear: "the sovereign chiefs of the pylons [were] in the form of khus. And the god Anpu spake [to those who were on] both sides of him with the speech of a man [as he] came from Ta-mera; he knoweth our paths and our cities." There follows a long itinerary of places already visited and things already seen — the borders of Ba-neb-Tettet (glossed "the 'Soul, the lord of Tattu,' or Osiris"), the Shentet tree of the divine children, the Temple of the goddess Satet, the Sahu in the city of Qem-ur, the city of Tattu, the Temple of Teptu-f (glossed "'he that is on his hill' or Anubis"), the Temple of Osiris "and I have arrayed myself in the apparel of him that is therein," Re-stau where "I have seen the hidden things which are therein. I was shrouded [therein], but I found a way for myself," and the city of An-aarret-f (glossed "the place where nothing groweth") where "I covered my nakedness with the garments which were therein."
    - **The door-naming challenge, quoted in full as printed:** "The Majesty of the god Anpu saith, 'Knowest thou the name of this door so as to declare it unto me?' And Osiris, the scribe Ani, triumphant in peace, triumphant! saith, '**Destroyer of the god Shu**' is the name of this door. The Majesty of the god Anpu saith, 'Knowest thou the name of the upper leaf and of the lower leaf?' '**Lord of Maat upon his two feet**' is the name of the upper leaf, and '**Lord of twofold strength, the subduer of cattle**,' [is the name of the lower leaf. The Majesty of the god Anpu saith], '**Since thou knowest pass on**, O Osiris the scribe, the teller of the divine offerings of all the gods of Thebes, Ani, triumphant, the lord of reverence.'"

14. **THE INTRODUCTION TO MAATI** *(second version, same title)* — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10477, sheet 22).] The chapter opens with its rubric rather than closing with one — **printed in capitals**: [THE FOLLOWING] SHALL BE SAID WHEN THE OVERSEER OF THE PALACE, THE CHANCELLOR-IN-CHIEF, NU, TRIUMPHANT, COMETH FORTH INTO THE HALL OF DOUBLE MAATI SO THAT HE MAY BE SEPARATED FROM EVERY SIN WHICH HE HATH DONE AND MAY BEHOLD THE FACES OF THE GODS. The declaration of knowledge comes first: "I know thee, and I know thy name, and I know the name[s] of the two and forty gods who exist with thee in this Hall of double Maati, who live as warders of sinners and who feed upon their blood on the day when the lives of men are taken into account in the presence of the god Un-nefer; in truth '**Rekhti-merti-neb-Maati**' (i.e., 'twin-sisters with two eyes, ladies of double Maati') is thy name."
    - **The declaration of innocence in continuous prose** (unnumbered, distinct in form from the Nebseni list that follows): "I have brought Maat (i.e., right and truth) to thee, and I have destroyed wickedness for thee. [I have not done evil to] mankind. I have not oppressed the members of my family, I have not wrought evil in the place of right and truth. I have had no knowledge of worthless men ... [I have] not brought forward my name for [exaltation] to honors. I have not ill-treated servants. [I have not thought scorn of God.] I have not defrauded the oppressed one of his property ... I have not caused harm to be done to the servant by his chief. I have not caused pain. I have made no man to suffer hunger. I have made no one to weep. I have done no murder. I have not given the order for murder to be done for me ... I have not defrauded the temples of their oblations. I have not purloined the cakes of the gods. I have not carried off the cakes offered to the khus. I have not committed fornication ... nor diminished from the bushel. I have neither added to nor filched away land. I have not encroached upon the fields [of others]. I have not added to the weights of the scales [to cheat the seller]. I have not misread the pointer of the scales [to cheat the buyer]. I have not carried away the milk from the mouths of children. I have not driven away the cattle which were upon their pastures. I have not snared the feathered fowl of the preserves of the gods. I have not caught fish [with bait made of] fish of their kind. I have not turned back the water at the time [when it should flow]. I have not cut a cutting in a canal of running water. I have not extinguished a fire (or light) when it should burn. I have not violated the ties [of offering] the chosen meat-offerings. I have not driven off the cattle from the property of the gods. I have not repulsed God in his manifestations."
    - **The purity claim and its date:** "I am pure. I am pure. I am pure. I am pure. My purity is the purity of that great Bennu which is in the city of Suten-henen (Heracleopolis), for, behold, I am the nose of the God of the winds, who maketh all mankind to live on the day when the Eye (Utchat) of Ra is full in Annu (Heliopolis) at the end of the second month of the season Pert (i.e., the season of growing)." The chapter's argument then closes on knowledge, not innocence: "therefore let not evil befall me in this land and in this Hall of double Maati, because I, even I, know the name[s] of these gods who are therein [and who are the followers of the great god]."

15. **THE NEGATIVE CONFESSION** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 30).] The forty-two-fold form: forty-two hails, each naming a god by epithet and by the place he comes forth from, each answered by one disavowed sin. A representative run, quoted as printed: "Hail, thou whose strides are long, who comest forth from Annu (Heliopolis), I have not done iniquity. / Hail, thou who art embraced by flame, who comest forth from Kher-aba, I have not robbed with violence. / Hail, thou divine Nose (Fenti), who comest forth from Khemennu (Hermopolis), I have not done violence [to any man]. / Hail, thou who eatest shades, who comest forth from the place where the Nile riseth, I have not committed theft. / Hail, Neha-hau, who comest forth from Re-stau, I have not slain man or woman. / Hail, thou double Lion-god, who comest forth from heaven, I have not made light the bushel. / Hail, thou whose two eyes are like flint, who comest forth from Sekhem (Letopolis), I have not acted deceitfully. / Hail, thou Flame, who comest forth as [thou] goest back, I have not purloined the things which belong unto God. / Hail, thou Crusher of bones, who comest forth from Suten-henen (Heracleopolis), I have not uttered falsehood. / Hail, thou who makest the flame to wax strong, who comest forth from Het-ka-Ptah (Memphis), I have not carried away food." Further gods and their sins include: "Qerti, (i.e., the two sources of the Nile), who come forth from Amentet, I have not uttered evil words"; "thou whose teeth shine, who comest forth from Tashe (i.e., the Fayyum), I have attacked no man"; "thou who dost consume blood, who comest forth from the house of slaughter, I have not killed the beasts [which are the property of God]"; "thou god of Right and Truth, who comest forth from the city of double Maati, I have not laid waste the lands which have been ploughed(?)"; "thou who goest backward, who comest forth from the city of Bast (Bubastis), I have never pried into matters [to make mischief]"; "Aati, who comest forth from Annu (Heliopolis), I have not set my mouth in motion [against any man]"; "thou serpent Uamemti, who comest forth from the house of slaughter, I have not defiled the wife of a man"; "Chief of the divine Princes, who comest forth from the city of Nehatu, I have not struck fear [into any man]"; "Khemiu (i.e., Destroyer), who comest forth from the Lake of Kaui, I have not encroached upon [sacred times and seasons]"; "thou Child, who comest forth from the Lake of Heq-at, I have not made myself deaf to the words of right and truth"; "Basti, who comest forth from the Secret city, I have made [no man] to weep"; "thou whose face is [turned] backward, who comest forth from the Dwelling, I have not committed acts of impurity, neither have I lain with men"; "Leg of fire, who comest forth from Akhekhu, I have not eaten my heart"; "Kenemti, who comest forth from [the city of] Kenemet, I have abused [no man]"; "thou god of faces, who comest forth from the city of Tchefet, I have not judged hastily"; "Tem-Sep, who comest forth from Tattu, I have not uttered curses [on the king]"; "Ahi of the water, who comest forth from Nu, I have not made haughty my voice"; "thou who givest commands to mankind ... I have not cursed the god"; "Neheb-nefert, who comest forth from the Lake of Nefer(?), I have not behaved with insolence"; "Neheb-kau, who comest forth from [thy] city, I have not sought for distinctions"; "thou whose head is holy, who comest forth from [thy] habitations, I have not increased my wealth, except with such things as are [justly] mine own possessions." The forty-second closes the series: "Hail, thou who bringest thine own arm, who comest forth from Aukert (underworld), I have not thought scorn of the god who is in my city." *(The hails number exactly forty-two in this text; see the OCR note on the numeral column.)*

16. **ADDRESS TO THE GODS OF THE UNDERWORLD** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 24).] Purpose, in a bracketed direction: "[Then shall the heart which is righteous and sinless say:]" **The longest and most fully articulated challenge text in the unit.** It opens as an appeal to the assessors on the strength of knowing them: "Homage to you, O ye gods who dwell in the Hall of double Maati, I, even I, know you, and I know your names. Let me not fall under your knives of slaughter, and bring ye not forward my wickedness unto the god in whose train ye are ... Oh, declare ye me right and true in the presence of Neb-er-tcher, because I have done that which is right and true in Ta-mera (Egypt)." It asks deliverance "from the god Baba who feedeth upon the entrails of the mighty ones upon the day of the great judgment," states the positive record of charity ("I have given bread to the hungry man, and water to the thirsty man, and apparel to the naked man, and a boat to the [shipwrecked] mariner"), and cites prior testimony: "I have been made to give evidence before the god Hra-f-ha-f (i.e., he whose face is behind him), and he hath given a decision [concerning me]." Purification is itemized down to the anatomy: "I have purified myself and my breast with libations, and my hinder parts with the things which make clean, and my inner parts have been in the Pool of Right and Truth. There is no single member of mine which lacketh right and truth."
    - **First challenge — identity.** "And they say unto me, 'Let thyself come forward.' And they say unto me, 'Who, then, art thou?' And they say unto me, 'What is thy name?' '**I am he who is equipped under the flowers [and I am] the dweller in his olive-tree**,' is my name. And they say unto me straightway, 'Pass thou on.'"
    - **Second challenge — a catechism about the journey**, question and answer alternating without attribution: "and I passed on by thy city to the north of the olive-tree. *What, then, didst thou see there?* The leg and the thigh. *What, then, didst thou say unto them?* Let me see rejoicings in those lands of the Tenkhu. *And what did they give unto thee?* A flame of fire and a tablet (or sceptre) of crystal. *What, then, didst thou do therewith?* I buried them by the furrow of Manaat as 'things for the night.' *What, then, didst thou find by the furrow of Manaat?* A sceptre of flint, the name of which is 'Giver of winds.' *What, then, didst thou do to the flame of fire and the tablet (or sceptre) of crystal after thou hadst buried them?* I uttered words over them in the furrow, [and I dug them out therefrom]; I extinguished the fire, and I broke the tablet (or sceptre), and I created a pool of water." Verdict: "'Come, then,' [they say,] 'and enter in through the door of this Hall of double Maati, for thou knowest us.'"
    - **Third challenge — the parts of the door, each speaking for itself.** Quoted in printed order:
      - "'We will not let thee enter in through us,' say **the bolts of the door**, 'unless thou tellest [us] our names;' '**Tongue [of the Balance] of the place of right and truth**' is your name."
      - "'I will not let thee enter in by me,' saith **the [right] lintel of the door**, 'unless thou tellest [me] my name;' '**Balance of the support of right and truth**' is thy name."
      - "'I will not let thee enter in by me,' saith **the [left] lintel of the door**, 'unless thou tellest [me] my name;' '**[Balance of] wine**' is thy name."
      - "'I will not let thee pass over me,' saith **the threshold of this door**, 'unless thou tellest [me] my name;' '**Ox of the god Seb**' is thy name."
      - "'I will not open unto thee,' saith **the fastening of this door**, 'unless thou tellest [me] my name;' '**Flesh of his mother**' is thy name."
      - "'I will not open unto thee,' saith **the socket of the fastening of the door**, 'unless thou tellest me my name;' '**Living eye of the god Sebek, the lord of Bakhau**,' is thy name."
      - "'I will not open unto thee [and I will not let thee enter in by me,' saith **the guardian of the leaf of] this door**, 'unless thou tellest [me] my name;' '**Elbow of the god Shu when he placeth himself to protect Osiris**' is thy name."
      - "'We will not let thee enter in by us,' say **the posts of this door**, 'unless thou tellest us our names;' '**Children of the urei-goddesses**' is your name. 'Thou knowest us,' [they say,] 'pass on, therefore, by us.'"
    - **Fourth challenge — the floor**, which refuses on its own grounds: "'I will not let thee tread upon me,' saith **the floor of the Hall of double Maati**, 'because I am silent and I am holy, and because I do not know the name[s] of thy two feet wherewith thou wouldst walk upon me; therefore tell them to me.' '**Traveller(?) of the god Khas**' is the name of my right foot, and '**Staff of the goddess Hathor**' is the name of my left foot. 'Thou knowest me,' [it saith,] 'pass on therefore over me.'"
    - **Fifth challenge — the doorkeeper and the god beyond him.** "'I will not make mention of thee,' saith **the guardian of the door of this Hall of double Maati**, 'unless thou tellest [me] my name;' '**Discerner of hearts and searcher of the reins**' is thy name. 'Now will I make mention of thee [to the god]. But who is the god that dwelleth in his hour? Speak thou it' (i.e., his name). **Maau-Taui** (i.e., he who keepeth the record of the two lands) [is his name]. 'Who then is Maau-Taui?' **He is Thoth.**"
    - **Sixth challenge — Thoth's own examination, and the final riddle.** "'Come,' saith Thoth. 'But why hast thou come?' I have come, and I press forward that I may be mentioned. 'What now is thy condition?' I, even I, am purified from evil things, and I am protected from the baleful deeds of those who live in their days; and I am not among them. 'Now will I make mention of thee [to the god].' '[Tell me now,] who is he whose heaven is of fire, whose walls [are surmounted by] living ure, and the floor of whose house is a stream of water? Who is he? I say.' **It is Osiris.** 'Come forward, then: verily thou shalt be mentioned [to him]. Thy cakes [shall come] from the Eye of Ra, and thine ale [shall come] from the Eye of Ra, and the sepulchral meals [which shall be brought to thee] upon earth [shall come] from the Eye of Ra. This hath been decreed for the Osiris the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant.'"
    - **Rubric**, with its own parenthetical heading (printed in capitals): (THE MAKING OF THE REPRESENTATION OF WHAT SHALL HAPPEN IN THIS HALL OF DOUBLE MAATI) THIS CHAPTER SHALL BE SAID [BY THE DECEASED] AFTER HE HATH BEEN CLEANSED AND PURIFIED, AND WHEN HE IS ARRAYED IN APPAREL, AND IS SHOD WITH WHITE LEATHER SANDALS, AND HIS EYES HAVE BEEN PAINTED WITH ANTIMONY, AND [HIS BODY] HATH BEEN ANOINTED WITH UNGUENT OF ANTI, AND WHEN HE OFFERETH OXEN, AND FEATHERED FOWL, AND INCENSE, AND CAKES, AND ALE, AND GARDEN HERBS. AND, BEHOLD, THOU SHALT DRAW A REPRESENTATION OF THIS IN COLOR UPON A NEW TILE MOULDED FROM EARTH UPON WHICH NEITHER A PIG NOR OTHER ANIMALS HAVE TRODDEN. AND IF [THOU] DOEST THIS BOOK UPON IT [IN WRITING, THE DECEASED] SHALL FLOURISH, AND HIS CHILDREN SHALL FLOURISH, AND [HIS NAME] SHALL NEVER FALL INTO OBLIVION, AND HE SHALL BE AS ONE WHO FILLETH (I.E., SATISFIETH) THE HEART OF THE KING AND OF HIS PRINCES, AND BREAD, AND CAKES, AND SWEETMEATS, AND WINE, AND PIECES OF FLESH SHALL BE GIVEN UNTO HIM UPON THE ALTAR OF THE GREAT GOD; AND HE SHALL NOT BE TURNED BACK AT ANY DOOR IN AMENTET, AND HE SHALL BE BROUGHT IN ALONG WITH THE KINGS OF UPPER AND LOWER EGYPT, AND HE SHALL BE IN THE TRAIN OF OSIRIS CONTINUALLY AND REGULARLY FOREVER.

17. **THE HOUR APES** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 24).] Speaker given with a genealogy: "The overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant, the son of the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Amen-hetep, triumphant." Address: "Hail, ye four apes who sit in the bows of the boat of Ra, who convey right and truth to Neb-er-tcher, who sit in judgment on my misery and on my strength, who make the gods to rest contented by means of the flame of your mouths, who offer holy offerings to the gods and sepulchral meals to the Khus, who live upon right and truth, and who feed upon right and truth of heart, who are without deceit and fraud, and to whom wickedness is an abomination, do ye away with my evil deeds, and put ye away my sin ... and let there be no obstacle whatsoever on my part toward you." The request is passage: "Oh, grant ye that I may make my way through the underworld (ammehet), let me enter into Re-stau, let me pass through the hidden pylons of Amentet."
    - **The apes' answer is printed as speech, a granted request rather than a challenge:** "[The four apes make answer, saying], 'Come, then, for we have done away with thy wickedness, and we have put away thy sin, along with the [sin deserving of] stripes which thou [didst commit] upon earth, and we have destroyed [all] the evil which belonged to thee upon the earth. Enter, therefore, into Re-stau, and pass thou through the hidden pylons of Amentet, and there shall be given unto thee cakes, and ale, and sweetmeats(?), and thou shalt come forth and thou shalt enter in at thy desire, even as do those khus who are favored [of the god], and thou shalt be proclaimed (or called) each day in the horizon.'"

18. **PRAISE OF THE GODS** — [From the Tomb of Rameses IV (see Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 141; Lefébure, "Tombeau de Ramses IV," Plate 13).] Purpose stated as an occasion of use: "The book of the praise of the gods of the Qerti which a man shall recite when he cometh forth before them to enter in to see the god in the Great Temple of the Underworld." Address to the gatekeepers: "Homage to you, O ye guardians of the doors of the underworld, who keep ward over the god, who bear and proclaim [the names of those who come] into the presence of the god Osiris, and who hold yourselves ready, and who praise [him], and who destroy the Enemies of Ra. Oh, send ye forth your light and scatter ye the darkness [which is about] you." The beneficiary is a named king, given in two cartouches throughout: "the King of the North and of the South, (Usr-Maat-Ra-setep-en-Amen), the son of the Sun, (Ra-meses-meri-Amen-Ra-heq-Maat)" — asked to be led "through your doors" so that "his divine soul enter into your hidden places, [for] he is one among you." A second address turns on the doorkeepers' violence: "Hail, ye doorkeepers(?), hail, ye doorkeepers, who guard your gates, who punish souls, who devour the bodies of the dead, who advance over them at their examination in the places of destruction, who give right and truth to the soul and to the divine khu ... unbolt ye for him the doors, and open [ye] the place of his Qerti for him." The claimed result: "he hath made his way through the gates of heaven, and of earth, and of the underworld, even as hath Ra." The king's own petition closes it: "Open unto me the gate[s] of heaven, and of earth, and of the underworld, for I am the divine soul of Osiris and I rest in him, and let me pass through their halls. Let [the gods] sing praises unto me [when] they see me; let me enter and let favor be shown unto me; let me come forth and let me be beloved; and let me go forward, for no defect or failure hath been found clinging unto me."

19. **ADORATION OF THE GODS OF THE QERTI** — [From the Papyrus of Ptah-mes (Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 142).] Purpose: "A chapter to be recited on coming before the divine sovereign chiefs of Osiris to offer praise unto the gods who are the guides of the underworld." Speaker: "Osiris, the chief scribe and draughtsman, Ptah-mes, triumphant." A close variant of entry 18 with a private owner in the king's place: "Homage to you, O ye gods who dwell in the Qerti, ye gods who dwell in Amentet, who keep ward over the gates of the underworld and are the guardians [thereof], who bear and proclaim [the names of those who come] into the presence of Osiris, who praise him and who destroy the enemies of Ra." Where the royal text asks to be led through, this one asks admission on the speaker's own standing: "Let me come forth and let me enter in through your secret places, for I am a mighty prince among you, for I have done away with evil there, and I have beaten down the obstacles(?) [which have been set up] in Amentet." The gatekeepers are characterized as accountants and executioners at once: "ye [gods] who keep ward over their habitations(?), who keep the reckoning and who commit [souls] to destruction, who grant right and truth to the divine soul which is stablished, who are without evil in the abode of Akert." The petition: "open ye unto him the gates of the underworld, and the uppermost part of his estate and his Qert." The acclamation: "The great and mighty gods cry out, 'He hath gotten the victory,' and they rejoice in him, and they ascribe praise unto him with their hands, and they turn unto him their faces." Close: "Osiris triumphed over his enemies, and Osiris, the chief scribe and draughtsman, Ptah-mes, triumphant, hath gained the victory over his enemies in the presence of the great divine sovereign chiefs who dwell in heaven, and in the presence of the great divine sovereign chiefs who dwell upon the earth."

20. **HYMN OF PRAISE TO OSIRIS** — [From Lepsius, *Todtenbuch*, Bl. 57.] Speaker: "The Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant." A litany of titles: "Homage to thee, O Osiris Un-nefer, triumphant, thou son of Nut, thou first-born son of Seb, thou mighty one who comest forth from Nut, thou King in the city of Nifu-ur, thou Governor of Amentet, thou lord of Abtu (Abydos), thou lord of souls, thou mighty one of strength, thou lord of the atef crown in Suten-henen, thou lord of the divine form in the city of Nifu-ur, thou lord of the tomb, thou mighty one of souls in Tattu, thou lord of [sepulchral] offerings, thou whose festivals are many in Tattu." Then the family: Horus "exalteth his father in every place (or shrine)," unites himself to Isis and Nephthys, "and the god Thoth reciteth for him the mighty glorifyings which are within him ... and the heart of Horus is stronger than that of all the gods. Rise up, then, O Horus, thou son of Isis, and avenge thy father Osiris." The speaker then takes Horus's part in the first person: "I am Horus and I have avenged thee, and I feed this day upon the sepulchral meals of oxen, and feathered fowl, and upon all the beautiful things [offered] unto Osiris. Rise up, then, O Osiris, for I have struck down for thee all thine enemies, and I have taken vengeance upon them for thee." A name-etymology sequence follows: "thy ka hath come unto thee and is with thee, and thou restest therein in thy name of **Ka-Hetep**. [It] maketh thee glorious in thy name of **Khu**, and it maketh thee like unto the Morning Star in thy name of **Pehu**, and it openeth for thee the ways in thy name of **Ap-uat**." The close: "Hail, O Osiris, thou hast received thy sceptre and the place whereon thou art to rest, and thy steps are under thee. Thou bringest food to the gods, and thou bringest sepulchral meals unto those who dwell in their tombs. Thou hast given thy might unto the gods and thou hast created the Great God; thou hast thy existence with them in their spiritual bodies, thou gatherest thyself unto all the gods, and thou hearest the word of right and truth on the day when offerings to this god are ordered on the festivals of Uka."

## Thread
This is the unit where the volume's governing mechanism becomes explicit. Everything earlier in Section II equips the dead man — a mouth, a heart, charms, defenses against named predators, a boat; here that equipment is put to its use, and the use turns out to be *examination*. The chapters arrange themselves as an ascending series of thresholds: the divisions of the Field of Peace, which he enters by addressing each by name; the cult-cities, which he enters by knowing the myth that explains them and then naming their three Souls; Re-stau, whose paths he receives and from which he comes forth; the boat, whose every fitting has a name; and finally the Hall of Double Maati, where the examination is total — the god, the forty-two assessors, the door, both its leaves, its bolts, both lintels, threshold, fastening, socket, posts, floor, its keeper, the god the keeper reports to, and the god beyond that god all have names, and each must be produced on demand. The Negative Confession sits inside this structure rather than replacing it: it supplies the *content* of innocence, but the argument that actually opens the door is "because I, even I, know the name[s] of these gods who are therein." The volume then prints, side by side, the same door-opening request made in three registers — a private official's (Nu, before the apes and the Hall), a king's (Rameses IV, before the gods of the Qerti), and another scribe's (Ptah-mes, in a near-duplicate of the royal text) — which lets the reader see that gate-passage was a single liturgical technology available at every social level, with only the name in the cartouche changing. The closing Hymn of Praise to Osiris returns the sequence to its object: the god at whose hall all of this is aimed, addressed by every title he holds in every city, and finally by the etymologies of his own names.

## Connections
- The judgment material treated in Section I — the weighing of the heart, Osiris as judge, the assessors — is here supplied in its primary form: this unit prints the actual liturgy of the Hall of Double Maati, both Introductions, the forty-two-fold Negative Confession, and the door-challenge that precedes the weighing. The Section I chapters describe the scene; these chapters are what is said in it.
- The Section I discussion of the doors and divisions of the kingdom of Osiris is the explanatory counterpart to the pylons, gates, Qerti and hidden places named here; the pylon-passage machinery this unit shows in operation at one hall is the same machinery those chapters describe as governing the whole realm.
- The Litany to Osiris earlier in Section II carries the refrain "for I am just and true; I have not spoken lies wittingly, nor have I done aught with deceit" after each of nine homages. That refrain is the seed form of what this unit prints in full: the same claim itemized once per god, forty-two times over.
- The equipment chapters immediately preceding — giving and opening the mouth, the chapters of charms and of memory ("let me be able to proclaim his name forthwith") — are the precondition of everything here. A dead man who cannot speak and cannot remember a name fails at the first bolt.
- The rubric convention established at the opening of Section II (rubrics printed in capital letters) governs the three rubrics recorded in this unit; the papyri named in the bracketed provenance lines throughout — Nu (Brit. Mus. 10,477), Nebseni (9,900), Ani (10,470) — are the ones catalogued in Budge's Introduction, along with the further sources listed there (Mes-em-neter, Ptah-mes, the Tomb of Ramses IV) which all appear as provenances here.
- The chapters that follow continue with Nu's making-perfect-the-Khu material and its Saite rubric, keeping the same owners and the same promise of not dying "a second time in the underworld."
- Section III's list of Egyptian deities is the reference for the unusually dense divine onomastics of this unit — Baba, Hra-f-ha-f, Maau-Taui, Neheb-kau, Uamemti, Kenemti, Sebek, Sa, and the four sons of Horus named here as Mesthi, Hapi, Tuamautef and Qebhsennuf.
