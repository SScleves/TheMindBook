# Ch 9 — The Chapters of the Transformations

## In one paragraph
This unit is a single, self-contained block of primary translated scripture: fourteen consecutive chapters, each one a spell by which the dead man takes on a chosen form. The volume prints them in an unbroken run — a general chapter "of a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth," then a hawk of gold, a divine hawk, the governor of the sovereign princes, the god who gives light in the darkness, a lotus (twice, from two different papyri), Ptah, a Bennu bird, a heron, a living soul, a swallow, the serpent Sata, and a crocodile. Twelve of the fourteen come from the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477), sheets 8 to 14, one from the Papyrus of Ani (No. 10,470, sheet 28), and one from the Papyrus of Paqrer by way of Naville; the two lotus chapters are printed back to back so that the divergence between the two manuscripts is visible on the page. Two capitalized rubrics interrupt the run: both promise coming forth by day, but their further promises differ — the first (after the Bennu chapter) that the possessor "shall perform whatsoever transformations his heart desireth," the second (after the swallow chapter) that he "shall make his transformation into a swallow regularly and continually." The texts themselves are not petitions but assertions of identity: "I am the pure lotus which springeth up from the divine splendor that belongeth to the nostrils of Ra"; "I am the serpent Sata whose years are many. I die and I am born again each day." **Four voices are on the page in this range and are kept apart below: (1) the translated Egyptian text, first-person throughout; (2) the translator's apparatus — the bracketed provenance line, the square-bracketed restorations, the parenthetical glosses "(i.e., …)" and the query marks; (3) the capitalized rubrics as the volume prints them; (4) the 2016 edition's photo captions, which the text extraction splices into the prose and which are excluded from every quotation below.**

## Core claims
- The claim that organizes the whole run is stated in the first chapter's own title: this is "the chapter of a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth." The individual chapters that follow are worked instances of that general capacity, not alternatives to it.
- The transformations are claimed to be *effective*, not descriptive: each chapter is a set of words spoken by a named dead man, and the rubrics attached to two of them state that knowing the chapter is what produces the result.
- The claimed benefits are consistently three: coming forth by day, freedom of movement (no gate turns him back, his legs and footsteps are his own), and exemption from a specified doom — corruption, the Chamber of Torture, death itself.
- Identity with a god is asserted flatly and in the first person, without hedging: "I am the god Tem, the maker of heaven"; "I am the divine Soul of Ra"; "I am the god Khensu, who driveth back all that oppose him"; "I am Hem-Nu(?), and I will not let you hear concerning the matter."
- The transformed dead man claims to become an object of *fear* to the gods of the underworld, and claims their fear as protection: "O grant thou that I may be feared, and make thou me to be a terror. Let the gods of the underworld be afraid of me, and may they fight for me in their habitations" — this petition is printed twice in the divine-hawk chapter, and the clause "fight for me in their habitations" four times there (a fifth time in the second person, "fight for thee in their habitations").
- Several chapters claim the *sah* (spiritual body) as the thing actually achieved by transformation: Horus "made for me a spiritual body containing his own soul," and the hawk-form is what qualifies the speaker as one.
- The chapters claim purity as the entry condition, expressed through an abomination-formula: "That which is an abomination unto me, that which is an abomination unto me, I have not eaten; filth is an abomination unto me and I have not eaten thereof" (Ptah); "that which is an abomination unto me is sin whereon I look not... that which is an abomination unto me is death" (Living Soul); "I am the pure one who cometh forth out of the Field" (Lotus).
- The Serpent Sata chapter claims a cyclical rather than a once-for-all survival: "I die and I am born again each day... I renew myself, and I grow young each day."
- The Bennu chapter claims priority to form altogether: "I came into being from unformed matter. I came into existence like the god Khepera."
- *Appetite satisfied* is an explicit part of the reward, not an aside: cakes and ale in Annu under Hathor's palm (Ptah), an abundance in Sekhet-hetep "as much as I desire" (hawk of gold); the divine hawk is provisioned "both of the fowl of the air and of the fish of the sea," and the Bennu rubric promises that the deceased "SHALL BE SATISFIED WITH THE FOOD OF OSIRIS AND WITH SEPULCHRAL MEALS."

## Concepts
- **transformation ("making the transformation into…")** — the volume's own recurring chapter formula. Each such chapter names a form, names the speaker, and gives the words by which the form is taken; the general chapter defines the class as "a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth."
- **sah (spiritual body)** — glossed in the text itself as "spiritual body (sah)." It is what Horus makes for the speaker "through his own soul," and what the double Lion-god says he has become "through thy form of Horus."
- **khu** — the shining form; the hawk-of-gold speaker says "I have become a khu therein" in Sekhet-hetep, the divine hawk is "one of those Khus who dwell with the divine Khu," and the heron chapter hails "[Hail], Aged ones [hail,] Khus, who are provided with the opportunity."
- **nemmes crown** — the crown at issue in the divine-hawk chapter: the double Lion-god first refuses it ("the nemmes crown is not for thee") and afterward binds it upon the speaker's head "by the decree of the double Lion-god on my behalf."
- **the double Lion-god** — "the governor of the things which belong to the Temple of the nemmes crown, who dwelleth in his secret abode"; the authority who both rebuffs and then invests the transformed speaker.
- **Ureret crown** — taken, not given, in the god-of-light chapter: "I have taken possession of the Ureret crown; Maat (i.e., right and truth) is in my body."
- **Chamber of Torture** — the place the Living Soul chapter exists to avoid; its title is "the chapter of making the transformation into a living soul, and of not entering into the Chamber of Torture," and the text prays "let me not go into the chamber of torture which is in the Tuat (underworld)."
- **Sekhet-hetep** — the field the hawk of gold reaches: "the divine Sekhet-hetep is before me, I have eaten therein, I have become a khu therein, I have an abundance therein—as much as I desire."
- **Sekhet-Aaru** — the destination whose "hidden ways and the doors" the swallow claims to know.
- **Sekhet-neheh** — glossed in the text as "Field of illimitable time"; the divine hawk passes to its border, "where lieth the shipwrecked mariner."
- **Pool of Double Fire** — where the swallow asks to pass its days: "stretch out unto me thy hand so that I may be able to pass my days in the Pool of Double Fire."
- **mantis (abit)** — the creature that conducts the speaker in the very first transformation chapter: "I have come into the House of the King by means of the mantis (abit) which led me hither."
- **Bennu bird** — the form taken by the chapter of that name; the hawk of gold is also said to have "the head of a Bennu bird."
- **Smam bull** — the heron's self-identification: "I am the Smam bull [for slaughter] which is written down in the books."
- **Nefer-Temu** — the god of whom the lotus is called the type in the Paqrer version: "Hail, thou lotus, thou type of the god Nefer-Temu!"
- **rubric** — as elsewhere in the volume, printed in capital letters and stating the chapter's effect. Two occur in this run, attached to the Bennu chapter and to the swallow chapter.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(For this unit: every transformation chapter in the run, in printed order — form, provenance line exactly as printed, purpose-line, speaker, the words spoken, and any rubric.)*

1. **TRANSFORMATION** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth." Speaker: "The chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant." The whole text: "I have come into the House of the King by means of the mantis (abit) which led me hither. Homage to thee, O thou who fliest into heaven, and dost shine upon the son of the white crown, and dost protect the white crown, let me have my existence with thee! I have gathered together the great god[s], I am mighty, I have made my way and I have travelled along thereon." No rubric printed. This is the chapter that makes the whole class explicit — the general power, of which the thirteen following chapters are named instances.

2. **PERFORMING TRANSFORMATIONS (hawk of gold)** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] [OCR: the museum number is printed "No. t10,477".] Purpose: "The chapter of performing the transformation into a hawk of gold." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. The bird is described with measurements: "I have risen, I have risen like the mighty hawk [of gold] that cometh forth from his egg; I fly and I alight like the hawk which hath a back four cubits wide, and the wings of which are like unto the mother-of-emerald of the south." Route and reception: "I have come forth from the interior of the Sektet boat, and my heart hath been brought unto me from the mountain of the east. I have alighted upon the Atet boat, and those who were dwelling in their companies have been brought unto me, and they bowed low in paying homage unto me and in saluting me with cries of joy." The composite form: "I have risen, and I have gathered myself together like the beautiful hawk of gold, which hath the head of a Bennu bird, and Ra entereth in day by day to hearken unto my words; I have taken my seat among those first-born gods of Nut." Closes on food and the throat: "I am stablished, and the divine Sekhet-hetep is before me, I have eaten therein, I have become a khu therein, I have an abundance therein—as much as I desire—the god Nepra hath given to me my throat, and I have gained the mastery over that which guardeth (or belongeth to) my head."

3. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A HAWK** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 13 and 14).] Purpose: "The chapter of making the transformation into a divine hawk." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. By far the longest chapter in the unit, and the only one with a narrative episode inside it.
   - Opening petition, at Tattu: "Hail, Great God, come now to Tattu! Make thou smooth for me the ways and let me go round about [to visit] my thrones; I have renewed(?) myself, and I have raised myself up."
   - The terror-refrain, printed twice in this exact form (and echoed in four further "fight for me / for thee in their habitations" clauses): "O grant thou that I may be feared, and make thou me to be a terror. Let the gods of the underworld be afraid of me, and may they fight for me in their habitations which are therein."
   - Protection against the anonymous harmer "in the House of Darkness, that is, he that clotheth and covereth the feeble one, and whose [name] is hidden."
   - A demand for silence in the divine assembly, and for Osiris's advocacy: "Be ye therefore silent, O ye gods, when one god speaketh unto another, for he hearkeneth unto right and truth; and what I speak unto [him] do thou also speak for me then, O Osiris." He asks to see Osiris's "own Form (or forms), and the dispositions of thy Souls," to have "power over my legs," to be joined to Isis, and that no one "come to see the divine one naked and helpless."
   - His food is provided "both of the fowl of the air and of the fish of the sea"; he is "one of those Khus who dwell with the divine Khu," a "spiritual body (sah)" possessing his soul.
   - A cosmogonic aside: the divine Khu was created by Tem "from the blossom (i.e., the eyelashes) of his eye"; "he is the only One in Nu."
   - The germination claim, set before Horus's birth: "I am one of those worms(?) which the eye of the Lord, the only One, hath created. And behold, when as yet Isis had not given birth to Horus, I had germinated, and had flourished, and I had become aged, and I had become greater than those who dwelt with the divine Khu... And I had risen up like the divine hawk, and Horus made for me a spiritual body containing his own soul, so that I might take possession of all that belonged unto Osiris in the underworld."
   - **The nemmes-crown episode.** The double Lion-god, "the governor of the things which belong to the Temple of the nemmes crown, who dwelleth in his secret abode," says: "Get thee back to the uttermost parts of heaven, for behold, inasmuch as through thy form of Horus thou hast become a spiritual body, (sah) the nemmes crown is not for thee; but behold, thou hast the power of speech even to the uttermost parts of heaven." The speaker nonetheless takes possession, and Horus tells him aloud "the things which his divine father Osiris spake unto him in years [gone by] on the day of his own burial." The crown is then granted after all: "I have given unto thee the nemmes crown through the double Lion-god that thou mayest pass onward and mayest come to the heavenly path." And later, in the speaker's own voice: "I, even I, am exalted in my tomb, and the double Lion-god hath bound the nemmes crown upon my [head], and he hath also given unto me the double hairy covering of my head. He hath stablished my heart through his own backbone... and I shall not fall through Shu."
   - The god Auhet appears twice in the crown episode and nowhere else in the unit: "And of them is the Auhet," and "the god Auhet hath prepared a way for me."
   - The volume marks a restored passage inside the chapter with square brackets: "Horus hath made me to be a spiritual body through his soul, [and I see what is therein. If I speak near the mighty ones of Shu they repulse my opportunity. I am the guardian and I] take possession of the things which Horus had from Osiris in the underworld."
   - Passage claims: "I, even I, am he who knoweth the roads through the sky, and the wind thereof is in my body. The bull which striketh terror [into men] shall not drive me back, and I shall pass on to the place where lieth the shipwrecked mariner on the border of the Sekhet-neheh (i.e., Field of illimitable time)." He enters "the House of the double Lion-god" daily and comes forth "into the House of Isis, the divine lady," to "behold sacred things which are hidden" and to "see the birth of the Great God."
   - Full identification with Horus: "I, even I, am Horus who dwelleth in the divine Khu. [I] have gained power over his crown, I have gained power over his radiance... My face is like unto that of the divine hawk, my strength is like unto that of the divine hawk."
   - Command over the resisting gods: "[Down] upon your faces, ye gods of the Tuat (underworld), who would resist me with your faces and oppose me with your powers... Horus hath commanded that ye lift up your faces so that I may look upon you."
   - A variant recorded inline: "I have bound up and I have gathered together the powers of Kesemu-enenet," or (as others say), "Kesemiu-enenet."
   - The chapter closes not on the speaker but on Osiris and Horus: "Exalted [be thou] upon thy throne, O Osiris! ... Thy head is fastened unto thee, O Osiris. Thy brow is stablished, O Osiris. Thy heart is glad, [O Osiris]... Thou art stablished like the Bull of Amentet. Thy son Horus hath risen like the sun upon thy throne, and all life is with him. Millions of years minister unto him, and millions of years hold him in fear." Final lines: "Horus is both the divine food and the sacrifice... Horus is [his] deliverer, Horus is [his] deliverer. Horus hath sprung from the water of his divine father and [from his] decay. He hath become the Governor of Egypt. The gods labor for him, and they toil for him for millions of years; and he hath made to live millions of years through his Eye, the only One of its Lord (or Neb-s), Nebt-er-tcher."

4. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A GOVERNOR** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 8 and 9).] Purpose: "The chapter of being transformed into the governor of the sovereign princes." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Opens with the fullest creator-identification in the unit: "I am the god Tem, the maker of heaven, the creator of things which are, who cometh forth from the earth, who maketh to come into being the seed which is sown, the lord of things which shall be, who gave birth to the gods; [I am] the great god who made himself, the lord of life, who maketh to flourish the company of the gods."
   - Then a six-fold homage, each clause to a class of gods: "Homage to you, O ye lords of divine things (or of creation), ye pure beings whose abodes are hidden! Homage to you, O ye everlasting lords, whose forms are hidden and whose shrines are hidden in places which are unknown! Homage to you, O ye gods, who dwell in the Tenait(?)! Homage to you, O ye gods of the circuit of the flooded lands of Qebhu! Homage to you, O ye gods who live in Amentet! Homage to you, O ye company of the gods who dwell in Nut!"
   - The purity petition that follows: "Grant ye that I may come unto you, for I am pure, I am divine, I am a kAu, I am strong, I am endowed with a soul (or [am mighty), and I have brought unto you incense, and sweet-smelling gums, and natron." [OCR: "I am a kAu" and the unclosed bracket in "(or [am mighty)" are garbled in this text; no reading is recoverable from it.]
   - The offering is framed as a settlement of grievances in both directions: "I have made an end of the spittle which floweth from your mouth upon me. I have come, and I have made an end of the evil things which are in your hearts, and I have removed the faults which ye kept [laid up against me]. I have brought to you the things which are good, and I make to come into your presence Right and Truth."
   - Knowledge as the ground of entry: "I, even I, know you, and I know your names, and I know your forms, which are unknown, and I come into being along with you."
   - A startling self-description: "My coming is like unto that of the god who eateth men and who liveth upon the gods."
   - The reception: "the gods come to me in gladness, and goddesses make supplication unto me when they see me... I have taken my seat in the horizon, and I receive my offerings upon my tables, and I drink drink-offerings at eventide." Closes on birth from Nut: "I am exalted like the holy god who dwelleth in the Great Temple, and the gods rejoice when they see me in my beautiful coming forth from the body of Nut, when my mother Nut giveth birth unto me."

5. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A GOD** — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 28).] The only chapter in the unit from Ani's papyrus. Purpose: "[The chapter of] making the transformation into the god who giveth light [in] the darkness." Speaker: "Osiris, the scribe Ani, triumphant." The form taken is not an animal but an article of divine clothing: "I am the girdle of the robe of the god Nu, which shineth and sheddeth light upon that which belongeth to his breast, which sendeth forth light into the darkness, which uniteth the two fighting deities who dwell in my body through the mighty spell of the words of my mouth, which raiseth up him that hath fallen—for he who was with him in the valley of Abtu (Abydos) hath fallen—and I rest. I have remembered him."
   - Calendrical acts: "I have rescued the Eye [of the Sun] when it waned at the coming of the festival of the fifteenth day, and I have weighed Sut in the celestial houses against the Aged one who is with him. I have endowed Thoth [with what is needful] in the Temple of the Moon-god for the coming of the fifteenth day of the festival."
   - "I have taken possession of the Ureret crown; Maat (i.e., right and truth) is in my body; its mouths are of turquoise and rock-crystal. My homestead is among the furrows which are [of the color of] lapis-lazuli."
   - The name-refrain: "I am Hem-Nu(?) who sheddeth light in the darkness. I have come to give light in the darkness, which is made light and bright [by me]. I have given light in the darkness, and I have overthrown the destroying crocodiles."
   - Consolation of the dead: "I have sung praises unto those who dwell in the darkness, I have raised up those who wept, and who had hidden their faces and had sunk down in wretchedness; and they did not look then upon me."
   - Close: "[Hail, then,] ye beings, I am Hem-Nu(?), and I will not let you hear concerning the matter. [I] have opened [the way], I am Hem-Nu(?), [I] have made light the darkness, I have come, having made an end of the darkness, which hath become light indeed." [The name is printed "Hem-Nu(?)" with the volume's own query mark each time.]

6. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A LOTUS (Nu)** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 11).] Purpose: "The chapter of making a transformation into a lotus." Speaker: "The overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu" — printed here without the "triumphant" epithet the other Nu chapters carry. The entire chapter is three sentences: "I am the pure lotus which springeth up from the divine splendor that belongeth to the nostrils of Ra. I have made [my way], and I follow on seeking for him who is Horus. I am the pure one who cometh forth out of the Field." This is the shortest and the most concentrated of the transformations: the form is asserted, its origin is the god's own breath, and the errand is the search for Horus.

7. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A LOTUS (Paqrer)** — [From the Papyrus of Paqrer (see Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 93).] The same title, printed immediately after the Nu version and wholly different in content — the volume's habitual practice of setting variants side by side rather than choosing between them. Speaker: "Osiris Paqrer." Text: "Hail, thou lotus, thou type of the god Nefer-Temu! I am the man that knoweth you, and I know your names among [those of] the gods, the lords of the underworld, and I am one of you. Grant ye that [I] may see the gods who are the divine guide in the Tuat (underworld), and grant ye unto me a place in the underworld near unto the lords of Amentet. Let me arrive at a habitation in the land of Tchesert, and receive me, O all ye gods, in the presence of the lords of eternity. Grant that my soul may come forth whithersoever it pleaseth, and let it not be driven away from the presence of the great company of the gods." Where Nu's version *is* the lotus, Paqrer's version *addresses* the lotus and petitions it — the same chapter title carrying opposite grammar.

8. **TRANSFORMATION INTO PTAH** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 9 and 10).] Purpose, unusually detailed: "The chapter of making the transformation into Ptah, of eating cakes, and of drinking ale, and of unfettering the steps, and of becoming a living being in Annu (Heliopolis)." Speaker: Nu, triumphant.
   - Opens with two other forms borrowed en route: "I fly like a hawk, I cackle like the smen goose, and I perch upon that abode of the underworld (aat) on the festival of the great Being."
   - The abomination-formula, doubled: "That which is an abomination unto me, that which is an abomination unto me, I have not eaten; filth is an abomination unto me and I have not eaten thereof, and that which is an abomination unto my ka hath not entered into my belly."
   - The food petition, which is the chapter's centre: "let me live and let me have power over cakes; let me eat them before the gods and the Khus [who have a favor] unto me; let me have power over [these cakes] and let me eat of them under the [shade of the] leaves of the palm tree of the goddess Hathor, who is my divine Lady. Let the offering of the sacrifice, and the offering of cakes, and vessels of libations be made in Annu; let me clothe myself in the taau garment [which I shall receive] from the hand of the goddess Tait; let me stand up and let me sit down wheresoever I please."
   - Limb-by-limb divinity, in miniature: "My head is like unto that of Ra, and [when my members are] gathered together [I am] like unto Tem; the four [sides of the domain] of Ra, and the width of the earth four times. I come forth. My tongue is like unto that of Ptah and my throne is like unto that of the goddess Hathor, and I make mention of the words of Tem, my father, with my mouth."
   - Inheritance and dominion: "I am decreed to be the divine Heir of Seb, the lord of the earth and to be the protector therein. The god Seb refresheth me, and he maketh his risings to be mine. Those who dwell in Annu bow down their heads unto me, for I am their lord and I am their bull."
   - The closing boast is one of the boldest in the volume: "I am more powerful than the lord of time, and I shall enjoy the pleasures of love, and shall gain the mastery over millions of years."

9. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A BENNU BIRD** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] Purpose: "The chapter of making the transformation into a Bennu bird." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Complete text: "I came into being from unformed matter. I came into existence like the god Khepera, I have germinated like the things which germinate (i.e., plants), and I have dressed myself like the Tortoise. I am [of] the germs of every god. I am Yesterday of the four [quarters of the world] and of those seven Urei which came into existence in Amentet, that is to say, [Horus, who emitteth light from his divine body. He is] the god [who] fought against Suti, but the god Thoth cometh between them through the judgment of him that dwelleth in Sekhem, and of the Souls who are in Annu, and there is a stream between them. I have come by day, and I have risen in the footsteps of the gods. I am the god Khensu, who driveth back all that oppose him."
   - **Rubric** (printed in capitals, following this chapter and preceding the heron): "[LF] THIS CHAPTER [BE KNOWN BY THE DECEASED] HE SHALL COME FORTH PURE BY DAY AFTER HIS DEATH, AND HE SHALL PERFORM WHATSOEVER TRANSFORMATIONS HIS HEART DESIRETH. HE SHALL BE IN THE FOLLOWING OF UNNEFER, AND HE SHALL BE SATISFIED WITH THE FOOD OF OSIRIS AND WITH SEPULCHRAL MEALS. [HE] SHALL SEE THE DISK, [HE] SHALL BE IN GOOD CASE UPON EARTH BEFORE RA, AND HE SHALL BE TRIUMPHANT BEFORE OSIRIS, AND NO EVIL THING WHATSOEVER SHALL HAVE DOMINION OVER HIM FOREVER AND EVER." [OCR: the rubric's first word is printed "[LF]"; the same conditional formula is printed elsewhere in this volume as "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED]", including in the swallow rubric a few pages later.] This rubric is the single most important sentence in the unit: it makes the *general* power of transformation the reward for knowing *one particular* chapter.

10. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A HERON** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] Purpose: "The chapter of making the transformation into a heron." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Opens on sacrificial knives: "[I] have gotten dominion over the beasts that are brought for sacrifice, with the knives which are [held] at their heads, and at their hair, and at their ..." [The text breaks off in ellipsis at this point in the edition.]
   - The address to the dead, and the earth/heaven split: "[Hail], Aged ones [hail,] Khus, who are provided with the opportunity, the chancellor-in-chief, the overseer of the palace, Nu, triumphant, is upon the earth, and what he hath slaughtered is in heaven; and what he hath slaughtered is in heaven and he is upon the earth."
   - Journey, prefaced by "Behold, I am strong, and I work mighty deeds to the very heights of heaven": "I have made myself pure, and [I] make the breadth of heaven [a place for] my footsteps [as I go] into the cities of Aukert; I advance, and I go forward into the city of Unnu (Hermopolis). I have set the gods upon their paths, and I have roused up the exalted ones who dwell in their shrines."
   - A four-fold rhetorical catechism of knowledge: "Do I not know Nu? Do I not know Tatunen? Do I not know the beings of the color of fire who thrust forward their horns? Do I not know [every being having] incantations unto whose words I listen?"
   - Self-identification as the sacrificial animal: "I am the Smam bull [for slaughter] which is written down in the books."
   - Quoted divine speech and a claim about light: "The gods crying out say: 'Let your faces be gracious to him that cometh onward.' The light is beyond your knowledge, and ye cannot fetter it; and times and seasons are in my body."
   - Closes on truthfulness and a night festival: "I do not utter words to the god Hu, [I do not utter words of] wickedness instead of [words of] right and truth, and each day right and truth come upon my eyebrows. At night taketh place the festival of him that is dead, the Aged One, who is in ward [in] the earth."

11. **THE LIVING SOUL** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] Purpose, with its own embedded promise: "The chapter of making the transformation into a living soul, and of not entering into the Chamber of Torture. Whosoever knoweth [it] shall not see corruption." Speaker: Nu, triumphant.
   - "I am the divine Soul of Ra proceeding from the god Nu; that divine soul which is God, [I am] the creator of the divine food, and that which is an abomination unto me is sin whereon I look not. I proclaim right and truth, and I live therein."
   - Self-generation: "I am the divine food, which is not corrupted in my name of Soul: I gave birth unto myself together with Nu in my name of Khepera in whom I come into being day by day."
   - The chapter's operative petition: "I am the lord of light, and that which is an abomination unto me is death; let me not go into the chamber of torture which is in the Tuat (underworld)."
   - Fear again as protection: "They cause the fear of me [to abound], and they create awe of me in those beings who dwell in their divine territories."
   - Primacy and eternity: "I am the first-born god of primeval matter, that is to say, the divine Soul, even the Souls of the gods of everlastingness, and my body is eternity. My Form is everlastingness, and is the lord of years and the prince of eternity. [I am] the creator of the darkness who maketh his habitation in the uttermost parts of the sky."
   - "My soul is the Souls of the souls of everlastingness, and my body is eternity. I am the divine exalted being who is the lord of the land of Tebu."
   - Two names given in full: "'I am the Boy in the city and the Young man in the plain' is my name; 'he that never suffereth corruption' is my name."
   - The unhatched-egg image: "I am the Soul, the creator of the god Nu who maketh his habitation in the underworld: my place of incubation is unseen and my egg is not cracked."
   - Close: "I have done away with all my iniquity, and I shall see my divine Father, the lord of eventide, whose body dwelleth in Annu. I travel(?) to the god of night(?), who dwelleth with the god of light, by the western region of the Ibis (i.e., Thoth)."

12. **THE SWALLOW** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] Purpose: "The chapter of making the transformation into a swallow." Speaker: Nu, triumphant.
   - Doubled opening and an unexpected second identity: "I am a swallow, I am a swallow. I am the Scorpion, the daughter of Ra."
   - Doubled address: "Hail, ye gods, whose scent is sweet; hail, ye gods, whose scent is sweet! [Hail,] Flame, which cometh forth from the horizon! Hail, thou who art in the city, I have brought the Warden of his Bight therein." [OCR: "the Warden of his Bight" is garbled; no reading is recoverable from this text.]
   - The swallow's business is *message-carrying* — the form is chosen for errand, not for power: "Oh, stretch out unto me thy hand so that I may be able to pass my days in the Pool of Double Fire, and let me advance with my message, for I have come with words to tell. Oh, open [thou] the doors to me and I will declare the things which have been seen by me."
   - The news it brings is the succession: "Horus hath become the divine prince of the Boat of the Sun, and unto him hath been given the throne of his divine father Osiris, and Set, that son of Nut, [lieth] under the fetters which he had made for me."
   - Judgment passed and reported, opening on a reckoning at Sekhem — the city the crocodile chapter also names: "I have made a computation of what is in the city of Sekhem, I have stretched out both my hands and arms at the word(?) of Osiris, I have passed on to judgment, and I have come that [I] may speak; grant that I may pass on and declare my tidings. I enter in, [I am] judged, and [I] come forth worthy at the gate of Neb-er-tcher. I am pure at the great place of the passage of souls, I have done away with my sins, I have put away mine offences, and I have destroyed the evil which appertained unto my members upon earth."
   - Fellowship with the door-keepers: "Hail, ye divine beings who guard the doors, make ye for me a way, for, behold, I am like unto you."
   - The unit's sharpest single line, setting achieved freedom against the body left behind: "I know the hidden ways and the doors of the Sekhet-Aaru, verily I, even I, have come, I have overthrown mine enemies upon earth, and yet my perishable body is in the grave!"
   - **Rubric** (printed in capitals): "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED], HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY, HE SHALL NOT BE TURNED BACK AT ANY GATE IN THE UNDERWORLD, AND HE SHALL MAKE HIS TRANSFORMATION INTO A SWALLOW REGULARLY AND CONTINUALLY."

13. **THE SERPENT SATA** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 11).] Purpose: "The chapter of making the transformation into the serpent Sata." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Complete text: "I am the serpent Sata whose years are many. I die and I am born again each day. I am the serpent Sata which dwelleth in the uttermost parts of the earth. I die, and I am born again, and I renew myself, and I grow young each day." Four sentences, built entirely on repetition; the only chapter in the unit that offers renewal rather than escape as the mode of survival.

14. **THE CROCODILE** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 11).] Purpose: "The chapter of making the transformation into a crocodile." Speaker: Nu, triumphant. Complete text: "I am the divine crocodile which dwelleth in his terror, I am the divine crocodile, and I seize [my prey] like a ravening beast. I am the great and Mighty Fish which is in the city of Qem-ur. I am the lord to whom bowing and prostrations [are made] in the city of Sekhem." This closes the run of transformations; the next chapter in the volume turns from taking new forms to reuniting the soul with the old body.

**Distribution of sources across the run**
- Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477): twelve of the fourteen chapters — sheets 8-9 (governor), 9 (general transformation; Ptah, with sheet 10; living soul), 10 (hawk of gold; Bennu; heron; swallow), 11 (lotus; serpent Sata; crocodile), 13-14 (divine hawk). Nu's title is given throughout as "the chancellor-in-chief," twice expanded with "the overseer of the palace" — before the title in the lotus chapter's speaker-line ("The overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Nu"), after it inside the heron chapter ("the chancellor-in-chief, the overseer of the palace, Nu, triumphant").
- Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 28): one chapter — the god who gives light in the darkness.
- Papyrus of Paqrer (Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 93): one chapter — the second lotus.
- No Lepsius chapter numbers are printed for any text in this run; the chapters are identified only by the volume's capitalized topical titles and the bracketed provenance lines.

## Key quotes
- "The chapter of a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth." — the title of the first chapter in the run, and the definition of the whole class.
- "I have come into the House of the King by means of the mantis (abit) which led me hither." — the same chapter's opening; the dead man is conducted by an insect.
- "I fly and I alight like the hawk which hath a back four cubits wide, and the wings of which are like unto the mother-of-emerald of the south." — the hawk of gold, given exact dimensions and colour.
- "Get thee back to the uttermost parts of heaven, for behold, inasmuch as through thy form of Horus thou hast become a spiritual body, (sah) the nemmes crown is not for thee; but behold, thou hast the power of speech even to the uttermost parts of heaven." — the double Lion-god's refusal in the divine-hawk chapter, the only quoted divine rebuff in the unit.
- "O grant thou that I may be feared, and make thou me to be a terror. Let the gods of the underworld be afraid of me, and may they fight for me in their habitations." — the divine-hawk refrain, printed twice in this form; fear of the transformed dead is what recruits the gods to his side.
- "My coming is like unto that of the god who eateth men and who liveth upon the gods." — Transformation into a Governor.
- "I, even I, know you, and I know your names, and I know your forms, which are unknown, and I come into being along with you." — the same chapter; knowledge of hidden forms is the ground of admission.
- "I am the girdle of the robe of the god Nu, which shineth and sheddeth light upon that which belongeth to his breast." — Transformation into a God (Ani), where the form taken is a garment.
- "I am the pure lotus which springeth up from the divine splendor that belongeth to the nostrils of Ra." — Transformation into a Lotus, Papyrus of Nu; the whole chapter is three sentences long.
- "Hail, thou lotus, thou type of the god Nefer-Temu! I am the man that knoweth you, and I know your names among [those of] the gods." — the Paqrer lotus, which addresses the lotus instead of being it.
- "I am more powerful than the lord of time, and I shall enjoy the pleasures of love, and shall gain the mastery over millions of years." — the close of Transformation into Ptah.

## Thread
This is the point at which the volume's most famous promise stops being a description and becomes a set of usable texts. An earlier stretch of Section II worked defensively — a mouth restored, a heart guarded, a crocodile beaten back, air and water secured — and every one of those chapters assumed a dead man who remains himself and must be protected as himself. The transformation chapters invert that assumption: the dead man's safety now comes from *not* remaining himself. He becomes a bird that no gate can hold, a lotus that grows from the god's own breath, a serpent that dies daily and is not diminished by it, a crocodile that other things fear. The forms are chosen with visible logic — the hawk for altitude and for Horus, the swallow for message-carrying, Ptah for cakes and ale and the unfettering of the steps, the Bennu for self-origination, the lotus for purity, the Sata serpent for renewal, the crocodile for terror. And the two rubrics show how the run is meant to be used: knowing one chapter is claimed to yield the whole capacity ("HE SHALL PERFORM WHATSOEVER TRANSFORMATIONS HIS HEART DESIRETH"), which is why the general chapter can stand at the head of the series without making the specific ones redundant. Structurally, the run is also an unusually long unbroken demonstration of what a single papyrus contains: twelve of these fourteen chapters come from Nu's manuscript, sheets 8 through 14, printed consecutively — so the reader is for once looking at a continuous stretch of one man's book rather than a composite. And the two lotus chapters, printed back to back from Nu and from Paqrer with almost nothing in common but the title, put the divergence between manuscripts in front of the reader without a word of commentary.

## Connections
- The Section I chapter "The Book Per-t em Hru, Commonly Called the Book of the Dead" records the volume's claim that possession of the work would allow its owner "to go in and to go out, and to take at any time any form he pleased." This unit is where that promise is cashed: the general chapter's title states the same power in the scripture's own words ("transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth"), and the Bennu rubric grants it as an effect ("HE SHALL PERFORM WHATSOEVER TRANSFORMATIONS HIS HEART DESIRETH"). The claim stands in Section I; the texts that are said to deliver it stand here in Section II, in the same volume.
- The same promise has already been echoed twice in Section II before this unit: in the rubric printed with Victory over Enemies (the chapter text is from the Papyrus of Nebseni, British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 12; the rubric is given from the Saite Recension — "HE SHALL PERFORM ALL THE TRANSFORMATIONS WHICH HIS HEART SHALL DICTATE"), and in Another Hymn to the Setting Sun, where every Khu for whom the words are said "shall come forth by day in any form which he is pleased to take" — the chapter that carries the hymn of "the lady Mut-hetep, victorious." Those are the forward-pointers; this unit is what they point at.
- An earlier run of Section II chapters — mouth, charms, memory, heart, crocodiles, serpents, air and water, drinking water — supplies the defensive counterpart to these offensive spells, and shares their machinery exactly: capitalized topical title, bracketed papyrus-and-sheet provenance, a purpose-line, first-person speech, and a capitalized rubric naming the effect. The chapters standing immediately before this run are a cluster of Coming Forth by Day texts (plus Lifting up the Feet and Journeying to Annu), which is the same benefit both transformation rubrics promise.
- The unit ends on the crocodile; the volume's next chapter reverses direction, asking that the soul be brought back to the body it left ("The chapter of causing the soul to be united to its body in the underworld," Papyrus of Ani, sheet 17) — so the transformations are bracketed on the far side by a re-integration chapter.
- The judgment vocabulary that dominates later Section II material appears here in passing and pre-emptively: the swallow says "I enter in, [I am] judged, and [I] come forth worthy at the gate of Neb-er-tcher," and the god-of-light chapter has already "weighed Sut in the celestial houses" — the transformed dead man arrives at the Hall of Maati material with the verdict treated as settled.
- The Introduction to Section II catalogues the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477) and states that in that manuscript "only a few of the Chapters—i.e., those of the Transformations, Arits, Pylons, Aats, and one or two others—have vignettes" — the Transformations named there are the chapters printed here, which is why so many of them carry Nu's name and consecutive sheet numbers. The same description adds that in Nu's papyrus "the text is in black throughout, and only the titles of the Chapters and the rubrics are given in red," the two elements this run prints in capitals — the volume itself does not say that its capitals stand for the papyrus's red, so the correspondence is an observation, not a statement of the text.
- Section III ("Selected List of Egyptian Deities") covers much of this unit's cast, but in the modern editorial voice and modern spellings rather than the translation's: Atum for Tem, Geb for Seb, Khepri for Khepera, Khonsu for Khensu, Nefertum for Nefer-Temu, Neper for Nepra, Tatenen for Tatunen, alongside Nu, Nut, Shu, Hu, Ra, Horus, Isis, Osiris, Set, Thoth, Ptah, Hathor and Bennu. The list does not carry Tait, Unnefer, Neb-er-tcher, Auhet, Suti or the double Lion-god, so the more obscure names in this unit have no entry there.
