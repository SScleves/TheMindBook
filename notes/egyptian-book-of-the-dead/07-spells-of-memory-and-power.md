# Ch 7 — Spells of Memory and Power: Mouth, Charms, Heart, Crocodiles, Serpents, Air and Water

## In one paragraph
This stretch of Section II is primary translated scripture, not Budge's explanatory prose: thirty short texts, each printed with a capitalized topical title supplied by the volume, a bracketed provenance line naming the papyrus and its British Museum or plate number, a one-sentence statement of what the chapter is *for*, and then the dead man's own first-person speech. The sequence runs through a coherent inventory of things a corpse can lose and must magically re-secure: a mouth that can speak, the *hekau* or charms that another power might steal, and above all the heart — ten of the thirty items concern it (a chapter of giving a heart, seven printed under the single repeated title PRESERVING THE HEART, a heart of carnelian, and a free-standing RUBRIC for the heart-scarab), the aim being to keep the heart in the body and unimpeachable at the judgment. It then turns outward to the attackers of the underworld road (crocodiles from all four quarters, the serpents Rerek and Seksek and Apep, the gnawing Apshait, the two Merti goddesses, the Eater of the Ass) and ends on the elemental needs of a body in a tomb: air to breathe and water to drink and not be scalded by. One printed title can cover many separate texts: PRESERVING THE HEART appears seven times from four sources (Ani three times, Nu twice, Amen-hetep, and Lepsius Bl. 76), DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS three times, and BEATING BACK THE CROCODILE, LIVING BY AIR and ABOLISHING THE SLAUGHTERINGS twice each — different owners, divergent wording, and the volume prints the variants side by side rather than harmonizing them, so the recension history is visible in the text itself. The defensive method is uniform throughout: the speaker does not petition, he identifies himself with a god ("I am Ra," "I am Tem," "I am the Child," "I am Horus who lives for millions of years") until, in the great limb-litany of the second Abolishing the Slaughterings, "There is no member of my body which is not the member of some god."

## Core claims
- The texts claim, as their working premise, that the dead retain what they can *name* and *say*: the mouth is asked for so that speech is possible ("May my mouth [be given] to me that I may speak therewith"), and memory is asked for so that a name can be pronounced on demand.
- The chapter of Memory claims that the danger is anonymity in two directions — forgetting one's own name in the House of Fire, and being unable to name an approaching god: "If any god whatsoever should advance unto me, let me be able to proclaim his name forthwith."
- The heart chapters claim the heart is both the organ of self-command and the witness at the trial: it can be carried off by heart-stealers, made to "go through its transformations according to his deeds," and made to testify against its owner before "him that keepeth the Balance."
- They claim the desired outcome is mastery, enumerated part by part: "I have gained the mastery over my heart, I have gained the mastery over my two hands, I have gained the mastery over my legs, I have gained the power to do whatsoever my ka (double) pleaseth."
- They claim the soul must not be "fettered to my body at the gates of the underworld," but must "enter in peace" and "come forth in peace" — free passage in both directions is the recurring definition of success.
- The crocodile and serpent chapters claim that magical words are portable property that fiends come specifically to steal: the crocodile is "beating back" because it "cometh to carry away the charm," and the speaker's defense is that he lives *by* those words — "I live by reason of the magical words which I have by me."
- They claim the fiend is repelled by the speaker's knowledge of its identity and diet: "I know them by their names and [what] they live upon"; against the Eater of the Ass, "I know thee, I know thee, I know thee, I know thee."
- The Apep text claims a cosmic-legal joint action: Ra, the gods of the four quarters, Maat, the Lynx, the Scorpion and the god Aker each perform a named act of overthrowing, and the speaker claims to have executed Ra's will — "I have performed thy will, O Ra... I have labored for the peace of Ra."
- The limb-deification litany claims total identity with the gods member by member, and derives immunity from it: "And shall do me hurt neither men, nor gods, nor the sainted dead, nor those who have perished, nor any one of those of ancient times, nor any mortal, nor any human being."
- The air-and-water chapters claim power over the elements by precedent-argument — grant me power over water "even as the goddess Sekhet had power over Osiris on the night of the storms and floods" (Nu), "even as Set had power over his enemies on the day when there were storms and rain upon the earth" (Auf-ankh).
- The rubrics claim specific, stated returns for recitation or knowledge of a chapter: coming forth by day, walking on earth among the living, never coming to an end, going into and coming forth from the underworld.
- The rubric of the heart-scarab claims a documentary origin for the chapter itself: found at Khemennu under the feet of the god's statue, inscribed on a slab of iron of the south in the god's own writing, discovered by a named royal son in the reign of a named king.

## Concepts
- **memory in the underworld** — The subject of the chapter "The chapter of making a man to possess memory in the underworld": possession of one's own name in the Great House and the House of Fire "on the night of counting the years and of telling the number of the months," plus the ability to name any god who approaches.
- **opening the mouth** — Printed as its own chapter (distinct from the neighboring "giving a mouth" chapters, which fall just before this unit): it asks Ptah, Thoth and Shu to loose the mummy's swathings and the "bandages of Set which fetter my mouth," Shu opening it "with his iron knife wherewith he opened the mouths of the gods."
- **charm / magical words (rendered "the charm," "magical words")** — A transferable power that can be gathered from wherever and whomever it resides with, "swifter than greyhounds and quicker than light," and which "either createth the gods or maketh them silent, and which giveth the heat of fire unto the gods." Crocodile-fiends come to carry it away.
- **ab and hati** — Two words for heart, kept distinct in the translation and glossed inline: the petition "May my heart (ab) be with me in the House of Hearts! May my heart (hat) be with me in the House of Hearts!" and, in the Nu text, "Let not this my heart (hati) be carried away from me by the fighting gods in Annu."
- **the House of Hearts / the heart-stealers** — The place where the heart must remain present, and the class of beings addressed as "ye who carry away hearts... ye who steal [hearts, and who make the heart of a man to go through its transformations according to his deeds]."
- **the heart as mother** — The recurring vocative that opens three of the heart chapters: "My heart, my mother; my heart, my mother! My heart of my existence upon earth" — the heart addressed as the source of the speaker's coming into being ("My heart whereby I came into being!").
- **the Shenit** — Glossed in the text as "the divine officers of the court of Osiris, who form the conditions of the lives of men"; the deceased asks that they "not cause my name to stink."
- **weighing of words** — The judicial moment the heart chapters are written for, named in the bracketed passage of the Ani text: "let there be joy of heart unto us at the weighing of words. Let not that which is false be uttered against me before the great god, the lord of Amentet."
- **the heart-scarab rubrics** — Two distinct object-instructions. Lepsius Bl. 76: a basalt scarab in a gold setting, placed inside the heart of a man for whom "opening the mouth" and anointing have been performed. Papyrus of Amen-hetep: "a scarab of green stone encircled with band of refined copper and [having] a ring of silver, which shall be placed on the neck of the Khu."
- **Khu** — The state the deceased asks to attain and remain in ("let me be a khu therein forever and ever"; "I have become glorious (or a Khu)"); also used for the deceased as an object of ritual ("placed on the neck of the Khu").
- **ka (double)** — Glossed in the text as "double"; the heart chapter ends with power "to do whatsoever my ka (double) pleaseth," and the Ani heart text addresses the heart as "my ka, the dweller in my body."
- **the eight crocodiles** — The set the speaker claims to know: "the Mighty One delivereth it from these eight crocodiles. I know them by their names and [what] they live upon" — two each for the West, East, South and North, each with a named diet and a countering divine identity (Set, Osiris, Sept, Tem, Uatch-Maati).
- **deification of the members** — The head-to-foot litany assigning each body part to a god ("My hair is the hair of Nu... My feet are the feet of Ptah"), concluding "There is no member of my body which is not the member of some god."
- **the Utchat and the Egg** — The dwelling the speaker claims in the second Abolishing the Slaughterings: "I am he who dwelleth in the Utchat [and in the Egg]... I am he that dwelleth in the Utchat when it closeth, and I exist by the strength thereof"; later, "Hail, O Egg! Hail, O Egg! I am Horus who lives for millions of years."
- **the Great Cackler (Nekek-ur)** — In the Ani air chapter, the egg the deceased watches and guards, in a growth-parallel formula: "It groweth, I grow; it liveth, I live; it snuffeth the air, I snuff the air."
- **rubric (as promised effect)** — The capitalized instruction/promise appended to certain chapters, stating what recitation or knowledge accomplishes; in this range, coming forth by day and never ending (crocodile chapter), and entering and leaving the beautiful Amentet (Ani's dominion chapter).
- **variant recensions printed in parallel** — The editorial habit visible throughout: the same chapter is given from Nu, Ani, Nebseni, Amen-hetep, Auf-ankh, Mes-em-neter or Lepsius/Naville plates, and divergences are noted rather than merged (e.g. "In the Papyrus of Nefer-uben-f... this chapter ends quite differently, and reads:").

- **[OPENING THE MOUTH OF OSIRIS — conclusion]** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 15). The chapter's title, provenance and opening fall just before this unit; its closing lines fall inside it. After Shu opens the mouths "of the gods," the speaker shifts to divine self-identification: "I am the goddess Sekhet, and I sit upon [my] place in the great wind(?) of heaven. I am the great goddess Sah who dwelleth among the Souls of Annu (Heliopolis). Now as concerning every charm and all the words which may be spoken against me, may the gods resist them, and may each and every one of the company of the gods withstand them." (The "(?)" after "wind" is the volume's own uncertainty mark.)
- **BRINGING CHARMS TO OSIRIS** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 15). Purpose: "The chapter of bringing charms unto Osiris Ani [in the underworld]." He saith: "I am Tem-Khepera, who brought himself into being upon the thigh of his divine mother. Those who are in Nu (i.e., the sky) are made wolves, and those who are among the sovereign princes are become hyenas. Behold, I gather together the charm [from every place where] it is, and from every man with whom it is, swifter than greyhounds and quicker than light." He then hails the towing of "the Makhent boat of Ra," whose "stays of thy sails and of thy rudder are taut in the wind as thou sailest up the Pool of Fire in the underworld," and repeats the gathering formula of the addressee, of "[the charm] which created the forms of being from the ... mother, and which either createth the gods or maketh them silent, and which giveth the heat of fire unto the gods." Closes with a recorded variant: "swifter than greyhounds and quicker than light," or (as others say) "quicker than a shadow." [OCR: an ellipsis stands in the printed text before "mother" — the qualifier is missing in this edition.]
- **MEMORY** — Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 5). Purpose: "The chapter of making a man to possess memory in the underworld." Speaker named in full: "The chancellor-in-chief, Nu, triumphant, the overseer of the palace, the son of the chief chancellor Amenhetep." Entire text: "May my name be given to me in the Great House, and may I remember my name in the House of Fire on the night of counting the years and of telling the number of the months. I am with the Divine One, and I sit on the eastern side of heaven. If any god whatsoever should advance unto me, let me be able to proclaim his name forthwith." No rubric is printed with it.
- **GIVING A HEART TO OSIRIS** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 15). Purpose: "The chapter of giving a heart to Osiris Ani in the underworld." Opens with the doubled petition in both heart-words ("ab," "hat") and states the penalty for failure: "May my heart be with me, and may it rest there, [or] I shall not eat of the cakes of Osiris on the eastern side of the Lake of Flowers, neither shall I have a boat wherein to go down the Nile, nor another wherein to go up." Then the inventory of restored faculties — mouth to speak, "my two legs to walk therewith, and my two hands and arms to overthrow my foe" — and the named divine services: Seb to "open wide his two jaws unto me" and to "open my two eyes which are blindfolded" and unbind the legs; "may Anpu (Anubis) make my thighs firm so that I may stand upon them. May the goddess Sekhet make me to rise so that I may ascend unto heaven." Ends on mastery and free passage: "I understand with my heart. I have gained the mastery over my heart... My soul shall not be fettered to my body at the gates of the underworld; but I shall enter in peace and I shall come forth in peace."
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (1)** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheets 15 and 16). Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the heart (Hati) of a man be taken from him in the underworld." Addresses the thieves directly — "Hail, ye who carry away hearts! [Hail,] ye who steal [hearts, and who make the heart of a man to go through its transformations according to his deeds, let not what he hath done harm him before you]" — then "ye lords of eternity, ye possessors of everlastingness," asking that they not take the heart "into your grasp" nor "cause ye not words of evil to spring up against it," because it belongs to "him of many names (i.e., Thoth), the mighty one whose words are his limbs, and who sendeth forth his heart to dwell in his body." The verdict is stated as already achieved: "it is made new before the gods, he hath gained power over it, he hath not been spoken to [according to] what he hath done... His heart obeyeth him, he is the lord thereof, it is in his body, and it shall never fall away therefrom." Closes with a command: "I, Osiris, the scribe Ani, victorious in peace, and triumphant in the beautiful Amenta and on the mountain of eternity, bid thee to be obedient unto me in the underworld."
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (2)** — Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 5). Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the heart of the overseer of the palace, the Chancellor-in-Chief, Nu, triumphant, be carried away from him in the underworld." Distinctive imagery: "Hail, thou Lion-god! I am the Flower Bush (Unb). That which is an abomination unto me is the divine block. Let not this my heart (hati) be carried away from me by the fighting gods in Annu." Addresses the bandager of Osiris "who hast seen Set," and describes the heart as a supplicant in its own right: "This my heart (a) [sitteth] and weepeth for itself before Osiris; it hath made supplication for me." [OCR: the volume prints the gloss as "(a)"; the two heart-words used elsewhere in this range are "ab" and "hati", but this edition gives no evidence for which was intended, so it is quoted as printed.] Offerings recorded: "I have given unto him and I have decreed unto him the thoughts of the heart in the House of the god Usekh-hra, and I have brought to him sand (sic) at the entry to Khemennu (Hermopolis Magna)" — the "(sic)" is the volume's own. Ends with the heart devoted "to the decrees of the god Tem who leadeth me into the dens of Suti," a plea that it not be surrendered, and the obscure closing line "When they find the leg and the swathings they bury them." [OCR/text: the bracketed insertion "[in Sekhet-hetep (with) years]" and the syntax of the "joinest together hearts (hatu)" passage are broken in this edition; quoted as printed rather than repaired.]
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (3)** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 15). Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the heart of a man be taken away from him in the underworld." The shortest of the heart texts, and a straight repulsion: "Turn thou back, O messenger of every god! Is it that thou art come [to carry away] this my heart which liveth? But my heart which liveth shall not be given unto thee. [As I] advance, the gods hearken unto my offerings, and they all fall down upon their faces in their own places."
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (4)** — Papyrus of Amen-hetep (Naville, Todtenbuch, Bd. [?] Bl. 40). Purpose: "The chapter of not allowing the heart of Amen-Hetep, triumphant, to be carried away dead in the underworld." Unlike the petitionary versions, this one asserts possession from the first line: "My heart is with me, and it shall never come to pass that it shall be carried away. I am the lord of hearts, the slayer of the heart. I live in right and truth (Maat) and I have my being therein. I am Horus, the dweller in hearts, who is within the dweller in the body. I live in my word, and my heart hath being." Then the plea against mutilation and the request to have being "in the body of [my] father Seb, [and in the body of my] mother Nut," ending on the moral claim: "I have not done that which is held in abomination by the gods; let me not suffer defeat there, [but let me be] triumphant." [OCR flag: the volume number in the bracketed provenance is garbled ("Bd. £."); the plate number, Bl. 40, is legible.]
- **THE HEART OF CARNELIAN** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 33). Purpose: "The chapter of a heart of Carnelian" — i.e. attached to an amulet, though the text prints no instruction for it. Complete: "I am the Bennu, the soul of Ra, and the guide of the gods in the Tuat (underworld). Their divine souls come forth upon earth to do the will of their kas; let, therefore, the soul of Osiris Ani come forth to do the will of his ka."
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (5)** — From Lepsius, Todtenbuch, Bl. 76. Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the heart of a man be driven away from him in the underworld." Owner named with matronymic: "Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant, born of Sheret-Amsu, triumphant." Text: "My heart, my mother; my heart, my mother! My heart of my existence upon earth. May naught stand up to oppose me in judgment; may there be no opposition to me in the presence of the sovereign princes; may [no evil] be wrought against me in the presence of the gods; may there be no parting [of thee] from me in the presence of the great god, the lord of Amentet." Then homage to "thou heart of Osiris-khent-Amentet," to "my reins," and to "ye gods who dwell in the divine clouds, and who are exalted (or holy) by reason of your sceptres!" — asked to "Speak ye fair words for the Osiris Auf-ankh, and make ye him to prosper before Nehebka." Ends: "though I be joined unto the earth, and am in the mighty innermost part of heaven, let me remain on the earth and not die in Amentet, and let me be a khu therein forever and ever." **Rubric, quoted in full as printed:** "THIS [CHAPTER] SHALL BE RECITED OVER A BASALT SCARAB, WHICH SHALL BE SET IN A GOLD SETTING, AND IT SHALL BE PLACED INSIDE THE HEART OF THE MAN FOR WHOM THE CEREMONIES OF 'OPENING THE MOUTH' AND OF ANOINTING WITH UNGUENT HAVE BEEN PERFORMED. AND THERE SHALL BE RECITED BY WAY OF A MAGICAL CHARM THE WORDS: 'MY HEART, MY MOTHER; MY HEART; MY MOTHER! MY HEART OF TRANSFORMATIONS.'"
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (6)** — Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 5). Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the heart of the overseer of the palace, the Chancellor-in-Chief, Nu, triumphant, be driven away from him in the underworld." Nearly the Auf-ankh text, with instructive divergences: the opposition is feared "in the presence of the lords of the trial," and the specific charge to be blocked is quoted — "let it not be said of me and of that which I have done, 'He hath done deeds against that which is right and true.'" The homage is doubled to the heart itself and then to the reins ("Homage to thee, O my heart! Homage to thee, O my heart! Homage to you, O my reins!") where the Auf-ankh text addresses "thou heart of Osiris-khent-Amentet"; the gods are asked to "Speak ye [for me] fair things to Ra" where Auf-ankh's ask them to "Speak ye fair words for the Osiris Auf-ankh" (both texts then "make ye him/me to prosper before Nehebka"); and the close is "let me remain upon the earth and let me not die in Amentet, but become a Khu therein."
- **PRESERVING THE HEART (7)** — Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 15). Purpose: "The chapter of not letting the heart of Osiris, the scribe of the holy offerings of all the gods, Ani, triumphant, be driven from him in the underworld." Opens "My heart, my mother; my heart, my mother! My heart whereby I came into being!" and names the court: no opposition "in the presence of the sovereign princes (Tchatcha); may there be no parting of thee from me in the presence of him that keepeth the Balance!" Identifies the heart with the ka and with Khnemu: "Thou art my ka, the dweller in my body; the god Khnemu who knitteth and strengtheneth my limbs." Asks that the Shenit "not cause my name to stink," and closes with the bracketed passage on the weighing of words and the exclamation "Verily how great shalt thou be when thou risest in triumph!"
- **RUBRIC** — Papyrus of Amen-hetep (Naville, Todtenbuch, Bd. II p. 99). Printed as its own titled item after the heart chapters. Object instruction: "These words are to be said over a scarab of green stone encircled with band of refined copper and [having] a ring of silver, which shall be placed on the neck of the Khu." Then the capitalized provenance legend: "THIS CHAPTER WAS FOUND IN THE CITY OF KHEMENNU (HERMOPOLIS MAGNA) UNDER THE FEET OF [THE STATUE OF] THIS GOD. [IT WAS INSCRIBED] UPON A SLAB OF IRON OF THE SOUTH, IN THE WRITING OF THE GOD HIMSELF, IN THE TIME OF THE MAJESTY OF THE KING OF THE NORTH AND OF THE SOUTH, MEN-KAU-RA, TRIUMPHANT, BY THE ROYAL SON HERU-TA-TA-F, WHO DISCOVERED IT WHILE HE WAS ON HIS JOURNEY TO MAKE AN INSPECTION OF THE TEMPLES AND OF THEIR ESTATES."
- **BEATING BACK THE CROCODILE (1)** — Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 3). Purpose: "The chapter of beating back the crocodile that cometh to carry away the charm from Nu, the overseer of the palace." Text: "Get thee back, return, get thee back, thou crocodile-fiend Sui; thou shalt not advance to me, for I live by reason of the magical words which I have by me." The speaker withholds the crocodile's name from "the great god who will cause thee to come to the two divine envoys; the name of the one is Betti, and the name of the other is 'Hra-k-en-Maat.'" The power-argument is formal: "Heaven hath power over its seasons, and the magical word hath power over that which is in its possession, let therefore my mouth have power over the magical word which is therein." Bodily threat: "My front teeth are like unto flint knives, and my jaw-teeth are like unto the Nome of Tutef." Closes: "Thou shalt not carry them away, O thou crocodile that livest by means of magical words!" **Variant noted by the volume:** "[In the Turin Papyrus (Lepsius, op. cit., Bl. 16) the following lines are added to this chapter:]" — a long self-identification ("I, even I, am Osiris, who hath shut in his father Seb together with his mother Nut on the day of the great slaughter... I am Horus, the first-born of Ra, who is crowned. I am Anpu (Anubis) on the day of reckoning"), a birth-and-renewal claim ("I have sat in the birth-chamber of Osiris, and I was born with him, and I renew my youth along with him"), a scribal-office claim in which he writes down the offerings — "thousands of loaves of bread, thousands of vases of beer, which are upon the altars of his father Osiris, [numbers of] jackals, wolves, oxen, red fowl, geese and ducks" — the remark "Horus hath done away with the sacrifices of Thoth," and a wind-gathering passage: "I snuff the wind of the East by his head, and I lay hold upon the breezes of the West thereby.... I go round about heaven in the four quarters thereof." **Rubric, quoted exactly:** "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN BY [THE DECEASED] HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY, HE SHALL RISE UP TO WALK UPON THE EARTH AMONG THE LIVING, AND HE SHALL NEVER FAIL AND COME TO AN END, NEVER, NEVER, NEVER."
- **BEATING BACK THE CROCODILE (2)** — From Lepsius, Todtenbuch, Bll. 16 and 17. Purpose: "The chapter of beating back the crocodile that cometh to carry away the magical words from the Khu in the underworld." Speaker: Osiris Auf-ankh. ## Thread
Within Section II — the volume's translations from the Theban Recension — this run of chapters supplies the practical middle of the funerary equipment, between the great hymns and the judgment scenes. Its logic is inventory: each chapter names one loss the dead can suffer (speech, charms, heart, breath, water, safe passage past a specific named predator) and supplies the exact words that undo it. Two structural features of the book as a whole are unusually visible here. First, the *rubric* — the capitalized instruction naming the effect ("HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY"), the object it is recited over (basalt scarab in gold; green-stone scarab with copper band and silver ring), and in one case the chapter's own claimed archaeological pedigree — which is the reason the chapter exists at all and the point at which scripture becomes ritual technology. Second, the *variant apparatus*: the volume prints Preserving the Heart seven times and Dominion over Elements three times from different owners' papyri, notes where the Turin Papyrus adds lines and where Nefer-uben-f "ends quite differently," and thereby lets the reader see that this is a tradition of copies rather than a single fixed book — the claim made in Section I about the "Book of the Dead" being neither a connected work nor of one period is here demonstrated on the page. That apparatus has a consequence worth stating plainly, because the capitalized headers are the volume's own topical labels rather than the translation's chapter-names: the translation's own naming survives only in the "The chapter of..." line printed beneath each header, and those lines differ wherever the headers repeat (the seven Preserving the Heart texts are variously "not letting the heart... be taken from him," "be carried away from him," "be taken away from him," "to be carried away dead," and "be driven away from him"). Any index built on the printed titles would therefore collapse seven separate texts into one entry and three Dominion over Elements into one. They have to be counted by their provenance lines, not by their titles.

## Connections
- Section I (unit 00) argues that the collection is miscellaneous, not a connected work of one period; the repeated, divergent versions of the same chapter in this unit are that argument's evidence in translated form, as is the volume's practice of naming a different papyrus and sheet for each.
- Section I's account of the mummified body and its protection against fiends of the road to the Kingdom of Osiris is the practical setting for the crocodile, serpent, Apshait and Eater-of-the-Ass chapters gathered here; the "opening of the mouth" ceremony named in the basalt-scarab rubric is the ritual these texts assume has already been performed.
- The immediately preceding material of Section II supplies the Chaplet of Victory and the mouth-giving chapters whose closing lines open this unit; the immediately following material continues the elemental sequence with preservation of the soul, drinking water and preservation from scalding — the same problem-and-remedy format, extended.
- The judgment vocabulary used defensively here (the Balance, the weighing of words, the Shenit, the sovereign princes/Tchatcha, "let not that which is false be uttered against me") anticipates the fuller judgment material of Section II; the heart chapters are the pre-emptive counterpart to that scene.
- Section III (List of Egyptian Deities) does *not* index the cast invoked here, and the mismatch is itself informative: it is a modern editorial "SELECTED LIST", explicitly "a select list, with most of the more prominent gods mentioned," written in modern spellings (Atum, Geb, Khepri, Anubis, Sekhmet, Khnum, Wepwawet) rather than the transliterations these translations use (Tem, Seb, Khepera, Anpu, Sekhet, Khnemu, Apuat). Of the gods named in this unit only Ra, Nu, Osiris, Isis, Horus, Set, Thoth, Hathor, Nut, Shu, Ptah, Maat, Neith, Apep, Hapi and the Bennu carry an entry under the same spelling, and only one entry bridges the two systems at all ("Khnum—(also Khnemu or Khemmu)"). The limb-litany's minor gods (Khenti-khas, Ba-neb-Tattu, Serqet, Suti, Nehebka, Maftet, Atch-ur, Abu-ur, Sept) and every fiend of this unit (Sui, Rerek, Seksek, Apshait, Hai, Haas) are absent from it — Rerek is reachable only through the volume's back-of-book index, not through Section III.
