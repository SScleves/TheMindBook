# Ch 8 — Section II continued: Dominion over the Elements, Preservation of the Soul, the Coming Forth by Day chapters, and the first Transformations

## In one paragraph
This unit is unbroken translated scripture — twenty-three short-to-long chapters printed one after another, each under a title in capitals and a bracketed provenance line: eighteen name a papyrus with its British Museum number and sheet (Nu 10,477; Ani 10,470; Nebseni 9,900), three name a papyrus by Naville's plate instead (Amen-em-heb Bl. 78; Mes-em-neter Bll. 81 and 82), and two cite Lepsius, *Todtenbuch* by plate alone (Bll. 23 and 25). The run opens on the three element-chapters (breathing and snuffing air, dominion over water) — **which Ch 7 already prints in full; see the overlap note at the head of the examples section** — passes through the one-sentence PRESERVATION OF THE SOUL, two DRINKING WATER chapters and PRESERVATION FROM SCALDING, then enters the long central block of COMING FORTH BY DAY texts — the Nebseni recital of "I am Yesterday, To-day, and To-morrow," its near-twin the CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE from the Papyrus of Nu, the two enemy-chapters, four further Coming Forth by Day chapters from Nu, Amen-em-heb and Mes-em-neter, and two from Nebseni, with OPENING THE UNDERWORLD, LIFTING UP THE FEET and JOURNEYING TO ANNU set among them — and closes on the first of the transformation chapters: into any form one pleases, into a hawk of gold, and into a divine hawk. Budge's own voice appears in only two notes (one on the Papyrus of Nefer-uben-f's grouping and order of the chapters, one on the variant rubric in the Papyrus of Mes-em-neter), plus the bracketed provenance lines themselves; everything else on these pages is either scripture or a photo caption. **The captions are the editorial hazard here: in exactly six places a picture caption is dropped into the middle of a sentence, and two of those six fall inside a rubric — all six are identified and excluded below.** The mechanism that recurs through the whole unit is the name: a doorkeeper demands "…is thy name?", the speaker answers by naming himself, names his boat and its every fitting, tells the gods "I know you, and I know your names," and is rewarded with passage.

## Core claims
**Claims made by the scripture itself (recorded as the texts state them):**
- Efficacy is repeatedly made conditional on *knowing* the chapter, not on merit: "IF HE (I.E., THE DECEASED) KNOWETH THIS CHAPTER, HE SHALL GO INTO, AFTER COMING FORTH FROM, THE UNDERWORLD OF THE [BEAUTIFUL AMENTET]." The formula "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED]" governs four separate rubrics in this range, and only four: the rubric of COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheets 23–24); both rubrics of the CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE, where the first prints it with the whole phrase in brackets as a restoration, "[IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN] BY A MAN"; and the rubric of COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheet 3). Four sibling formulas do the same work in different words elsewhere in the range and are not counted among them — "IF HE (I.E., THE DECEASED) KNOWETH THIS CHAPTER" (DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS, Ani), "IF THIS COMPOSITION BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED]" (twice: COMING FORTH BY DAY, Nu sheet 7, and COMING FORTH BY DAY, Mes-em-neter Bl. 82), and "IF THIS CHAPTER BE RECITED FOR THE DECEASED" (COMING FORTH BY DAY, Nebseni sheet 16), this last shifting the condition from knowing to reciting. A fifth variant, "AND MOREOVER, IF [HE] KNOW THIS CHAPTER," continues the CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE's first rubric rather than opening a new one.
- Knowing the chapter is claimed to confer the powers of the living: the knower "SHALL BE VICTORIOUS BOTH UPON EARTH AND IN THE UNDERWORLD," "SHALL DO WHATSOEVER A MAN DOETH WHO IS UPON THE EARTH," and "SHALL PERFORM ALL THE DEEDS WHICH THOSE DO WHO ARE [ALIVE]."
- The chapters claim a discovery-history for themselves, and two competing ones are printed side by side: found at Khemennu on a block of iron inlaid with lapis-lazuli under the feet of the god, in the reign of Men-kau-Ra (Mycerinus), by the royal son Heru-ta-ta-f while inspecting temples; or found in the foundations of the shrine of the Hennu boat by the chief mason in the reign of Hesepti.
- Recitation carries ritual-purity conditions stated as requirements: the chapter "SHALL BE RECITED BY A MAN WHO IS CEREMONIALLY CLEAN AND PURE, WHO HATH NOT EATEN THE FLESH OF ANIMALS OR FISH, AND WHO HATH NOT HAD INTERCOURSE WITH WOMEN."
- A material instrument is prescribed alongside the words: a scarab of green stone with a rim plated with gold, placed in the heart of a man, is claimed to "PERFORM FOR HIM THE 'OPENING OF THE MOUTH'" when anointed with anti unguent and recited over.
- Naming is treated as a working technology, not an ornament. Interrogated at a gate, the deceased answers questions about his identity and then names every part of his boat ("'Assembler of Souls' is the name of my boat; 'Making the hair to stand on end' is the name of the oars"). Elsewhere he claims mastery by name — "in my name of 'Opener'"; "'I know the abysses' is thy name"; "'Hehi, the lord of millions of years [and of] the earth'… [I am] the maker of my name"; "'Qem-ur-she' is my name."
- Reciprocal knowledge of names is claimed as the basis of a bargain with the executioner-gods: "I know you and I know your names, therefore know ye me even as I know your names. I come forth to you, therefore come ye forth to me, for ye live in me and I would live in you."
- The name is also the thing preserved: the Mes-em-neter rubric promises that the knower "SHALL HAVE THE FACULTY OF TRAVELLING ABOUT AMONG THE LIVING, AND HIS NAME SHALL NEVER PERISH."
- Power over the elements is claimed by analogy to a god's precedent: the Lepsius element-chapter asks power over the water "even as Set had power over his enemies on the day when there were storms and rain upon the earth," and power over the mighty-armed beings "even as the god who is apparelled in splendor and whose name is unknown had power over them."
- Immunity from fire and scalding water is claimed on the ground of being an object Ra used: "I am the oar of Ra wherewith he ferried over the divine aged ones; let me neither be burnt up nor destroyed by fire."
- The Coming Forth by Day chapters claim total identity with time and with the creator: "I am Yesterday, To-day, and To-morrow, [and I have] the power to be born a second time; [I am] the divine hidden Soul who createth the gods."
- Mastery is claimed as an itemized inventory of body and estate — heart, breast, hands, feet, mouth, whole body, sepulchral offerings, waters, air, canal, river, land, furrows, male and female workers — and repeated a second time in shortened form in the same chapter.
- The transformation chapters claim the dead man may take any shape at will: "The chapter of a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth."
- The hawk chapters claim the transformed man takes possession of Osiris's own property in the underworld: "Horus made for me a spiritual body containing his own soul, so that I might take possession of all that belonged unto Osiris in the underworld."
- The same chapter records a limit set on the transformed man by the double Lion-god: the nemmes crown is refused him — "inasmuch as through thy form of Horus thou hast become a spiritual body, (sah) the nemmes crown is not for thee; but behold, thou hast the power of speech even to the uttermost parts of heaven" — and then, later in the same text, granted to him after all.
- An oath-formula binds the cosmos to the petitioner's cause: if he is not allowed to come forth against his Enemy, then "may Hapi—who liveth upon law and order—not come forth into heaven… and may Ra—who feedeth upon fish—not descend into the waters."

**Budge's framing prose in this range (two notes only, plus the provenance lines; kept separate from the scripture):**
- On the drinking-water group: "The last three chapters, with a single vignette, are grouped in one in the Papyrus of Nefer-uben-f (see Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 72); but the order of them as there given is 61, 60, 62." He adds that in the Turin Papyrus (Lepsius, op. cit., Bl. 23) the vignette of each is the same — "the deceased holding a sail in his left hand."
- On the Nebseni Coming Forth by Day rubric: in the Papyrus of Mes-em-neter the chapter is said to have been "discovered in the foundations of the shrine of the divine Hennu boat by the chief mason in the time of the King of the North and of the South, Hesepti, triumphant," and it is there directed to be recited by one "who is ceremonially pure and clean, and who hath not touched women, and who hath not eaten flesh of animals or fish."
- The bracketed provenance lines throughout are his, not the papyri's.

## Concepts
- **"coming forth by day"** — the standing name of the largest group of chapters in this range; the thing asked for is the dead man's ability to leave the tomb into daylight and move among the living, and the rubrics measure success by exactly that.
- **knowing the chapter** — the operative condition in the rubrics. Not belief and not innocence: the text says "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED]," and the benefit follows from the knowing.
- **knowing the name** — the recurring mechanism of the unit. To name a gate-keeper, a boat-part, an executioner-god or oneself is to gain passage or power; the Nebseni chapter makes the exchange explicit and mutual. It is not an occasional figure but the unit's spine: it works in **nine** of the twenty-three texts, and in a tenth by inversion. The nine — DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS (Ani, breathing: "…is thy name?" and the four boat-parts named); DRINKING WATER (Nebseni: "in my name of 'Opener'"); DRINKING WATER (Nu: "I have made my name to germinate"); COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheets 23–24: "'I know the abysses' is thy name," "'Qemur is my name," "…is my name"); CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE (Nu: "in my name of 'Hehi…'," "[I am] the maker of my name," "[my name is his name]," "'Qem-ur-she' is my name"); COMING FORTH BY DAY (Mes-em-neter Bl. 82, in its rubric: "HIS NAME SHALL NEVER PERISH"); COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheet 16: "I know you and I know your names"); COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheet 3: "I know your names… his name is 'Tekem'"); and TRANSFORMATION INTO A HAWK (the adversary "whose [name] is hidden"). The tenth is DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS (Lepsius Bl. 23), where the power invoked belongs to "the god who is apparelled in splendor and whose name is unknown" — the same mechanism running the other way, potency held in a name withheld.
- **Khu** — the shining/spirit form: "I have become a Khu (or a shining being)"; the deceased is "one of those Khus who dwell with the divine Khu." In one chapter the khu is protective: "my khu shall be as an amulet for my body and as one who watcheth [to protect] my soul."
- **sah (spiritual body)** — glossed in the text as "spiritual body"; the hawk chapter's central acquisition — "Horus hath made me to be a spiritual body through his soul."
- **nemmes crown** — the headdress at issue in TRANSFORMATION INTO A HAWK, first refused to the transformed man by the double Lion-god and later bound upon his head "by the decree of the double Lion-god on my behalf."
- **Ammehet** — the region through which the Nebseni chapter opens a way: "the chapter of coming forth by day and of opening up a way through the Ammehet."
- **Aukert** — glossed in the text as "underworld"; addressed directly ("Hail, thou Aukert… which hidest thy companion who is in thee") and named as a goddess in the Nu variant.
- **Re-stau** — the place of which the speaker claims chieftainship in answer to the challenge "Who art thou, then, who consumest in its hidden place?"
- **Sekhet-Aarru / Sekhet-hetep** — the fields the deceased asks to sail in and reach; "grant me the power to float down and to sail up the stream in Sekhet-Aarru [and may I reach Sekhet-hetep!]."
- **the sovereign princes** — the tribunal before which victory over enemies must be won; "I have come against my Enemy… and I have gotten possession [of him] before the sovereign princes."
- **the double Lion-god** — a recurring figure: nurse of the deceased ("[my] nurse is the divine double Lion-god himself"), governor of the nemmes crown, and an identity the deceased claims outright ("I am the double Lion-god").
- **the seven beings who make decrees** — in the Nebseni chapter, those "who support the Scales on the night of the judgment of the Utchat, who cut off heads, who hack necks in pieces… who make slaughterings in the Lake of Fire," and with whom the naming-bargain is struck.
- **the green stone scarab** — the amulet prescribed at the end of the CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE rubric, gold-rimmed, placed in the heart, anointed with anti unguent, and claimed to perform the "opening of the mouth."

## Experiments, examples & case studies
*(For this unit: each translated text in printed order, with its provenance line, stated purpose, and content. Photo captions interleaved into the prose are identified at the end and are excluded from all quotations here.)*

**Overlap with Ch 7 — true duplication, flagged for synthesis.** Items 1, 2 and 3 below (the three chapters printed under the heading DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS) are also entered in full in Ch 7's examples section, there numbered DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS (1), (2) and (3). The duplication is real, not a near-match: the same three chapters, the same provenance lines, the same quoted text, and item 1's rubric quoted in both. Only item 1 genuinely straddles the page-boundary (its opening — the provenance line, the purpose-line, "Open to me," and the first three challenge-questions — is printed before this range begins, and only "is thy name?" onward falls inside it); items 2 and 3 are printed wholly inside this range and wholly inside Ch 7's coverage. A fourth chapter treated with them in Ch 7, AIR AND WATER [Papyrus of Nu, sheet 12] — the one that asks power over water "even as the goddess Sekhet had power over Osiris on the night of the storms and floods" — is printed **before** this range and belongs to that unit alone; nothing from it is quoted here. Synthesis should count these three chapters once, and take Ch 7 as their primary entry.

1. **DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS** *(also in Ch 7)* — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 16).] "The chapter of breathing the air and of having dominion over the water in the underworld." *(This chapter's opening lies in Ch 7's page range; only its close falls in this one.)* It is a dialogue at a barrier. Osiris Ani says "Open to me," and is challenged: "Who art thou? Whither goest thou? What is thy name?" — answer: "I am one of you." "Who are those with thee?" — answer: "The two serpent goddesses Merti. Separate thou from him, head from head, when [thou] goest into the divine Mesgen chamber. He letteth me set out for the temple of the gods who have found their faces." He then names his vessel part by part: "'Assembler of Souls' is the name of my boat; 'Making the hair to stand on end' is the name of the oars; 'Goad' is the name of the hold; 'Making straight for the middle' is the name of the rudder; likewise [the boat] is a type of my being borne onward in the pool." Closing petition: "Let there be given unto me vessels of milk, together with cakes, and loaves of bread, and cups of drink, and flesh in the Temple of Anpu."
   - **Rubric**: IF HE (I.E., THE DECEASED) KNOWETH THIS CHAPTER, HE SHALL GO INTO, AFTER COMING FORTH FROM, THE UNDERWORLD OF THE [BEAUTIFUL AMENTET].

2. **DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS** *(also in Ch 7)* — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 16).] "The chapter of snuffing the air, and of having dominion over the waters in the underworld." Whole text: "Hail, thou sycamore tree of the goddess Nut! Grant thou to me of [the water and of] the air which dwell in thee. I embrace the throne which is in Unnu (Hermopolis), and I watch and guard the egg of Nekek-ur (i.e., the Great Cackler). It groweth, I grow; it liveth, I live; it snuffeth the air, I snuff the air, I the Osiris Ani, in triumph."

3. **DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS** *(also in Ch 7)* — [From Lepsius, *Todtenbuch*, Bl. 23.] "Another chapter." Osiris Auf-ankh, triumphant: "Let the gates of heaven be opened for me by the god [Thoth] and by Hapi, and let me pass through the doors of Taqebh into the great heaven," with the edition's variant readings printed inline — or (as others say), "at the time," [or (as others say)], "with the strength(?) of Ra." The petition follows the analogy-pattern: power over the water "even as Set had power over his enemies on the day when there were storms and rain upon the earth," and power over "the divine beings who have mighty arms in their shoulders, even as the god who is apparelled in splendor and whose name is unknown had power over them."

4. **PRESERVATION OF THE SOUL** — [From the Papyrus of Ani (British Museum No. 10,470, sheet 15).] "The chapter of not letting the soul of a man be taken from him in the underworld." The first chapter in the printed sequence that belongs to this range alone. The whole text is a single sentence, quoted here entire — Osiris, the Scribe Ani, saith: "I, even I, am he who came forth from the water-flood which I make to overflow, and which becometh mighty as the river [Nile]." Nothing else is printed between the purpose-line and the next chapter's title: no second sentence, no petition, no rubric, no caption. (The printed text reads "T, even I" — the recurring OCR substitution for initial "I", corrected here.) The title states the danger (the soul can be *taken* from a man after death) and the text answers it purely by self-identification with the inundation — no petition, no rubric.

5. **DRINKING WATER** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 4).] "The chapter of drinking water in the underworld." The scribe Nebseni: "May be opened [to me] the mighty flood by Osiris, and may the abyss of water be opened [to me] by Tehuti-Hapi, the lord of the horizon, in my name of 'Opener.' May there be granted [to me] mastery over the water-courses as over the members of Set. I go forth into heaven. I am the Lion-god Ra. I am the Bull. [I] have eaten the Thigh, and I have divided the carcass. I have gone round about among the islands (or lakes) of Sekhet-Aaru. Indefinite time, without beginning and without end, hath been given to me; I inherit eternity, and everlastingness hath been bestowed upon me." *(The scribe's name is followed by an ellipsis in the printed text: "The scribe Nebseni … saith".)*
   - **Budge's note attached here** (framing prose, not scripture): the three chapters are grouped as one with a single vignette in the Papyrus of Nefer-uben-f, in the order 61, 60, 62; the Turin Papyrus gives each the same vignette, the deceased holding a sail in his left hand.

6. **DRINKING WATER** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 7).] "The chapter of drinking water and of not being burnt by fire [in the underworld]." Nu, the overseer of the palace and chancellor-in-chief: "Hail, Bull of Amentet! I am brought unto thee, I am the oar of Ra wherewith he ferried over the divine aged ones; let me neither be burnt up nor destroyed by fire. I am Bet, the first-born son of Osiris, who doth meet every god within his Eye in Annu. I am the divine Heir, the exalted one(?), the Mighty One, the Resting One. I have made my name to germinate, I have delivered [it], and thou shalt live through me day by day." — the name treated as a living thing that can be made to sprout.

7. **PRESERVATION FROM SCALDING** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 12).] "The chapter of not being scalded with water." Nu: "I am the oar made ready for rowing, wherewith Ra transported the boat containing the divine ancestors, and lifted up the moist emanations of Osiris from the Lake of Fire, and he was not burned. I lie down like a divine Khu, [and like] Khnemu who dwelleth among lions. Come, break away the restraints from him that passeth by the side of this path, and let me come forth by it."

8. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheets 23 and 24).] "The chapter of coming forth by day in the underworld." The longest of the Coming Forth by Day chapters, running across three printed pages (only TRANSFORMATION INTO A HAWK, item 23, is longer). It opens on the time-identity: "I am Yesterday, To-day, and To-morrow, [and I have] the power to be born a second time; [I am] the divine hidden Soul who createth the gods, and who giveth sepulchral meals unto the denizens of the Tuat (underworld), Amentet, and heaven. [I am] the rudder of the east, the possessor of two divine faces wherein his beams are seen. I am the lord of the men who are raised up; [the lord] who cometh forth from out of the darkness." Successive movements: an address to the two hawks perched on their resting-places who "guide the bier to the hidden place"; the identity-collapse "He is I, and I am he, and Ptah hath covered his sky with crystal"; a plea for roads — "Make thou thy roads glad for me, and make broad for me thy paths when I shall set out from earth for the life in the celestial regions"; a specific plea against inherited guilt, "let no defects of my mother be [imputed] unto me." Then the enumerated Khus, given as a broken-out number: "I work for you, O ye Khus—4,000,000, 600,000, 1,000, and 200 are they—concerning the things which are there," with the speaker "setting straight the shoulders of the twelve Sah gods, and joining the hands of their company, each to each; the sixth who is at the head of the abyss is the hour of the defeat of the Fiends." A challenge-and-answer follows the same pattern as the gate-chapters: "Who art thou, then, who consumest in its hidden place? I am the Chief in Re-stau, and 'He that goeth in in his own name and cometh forth in that of Hehi(?), the lord of millions of years, and of the earth,' is my name." The close is a series of self-identifications and gestures — "I clasp the sycamore tree, I myself am joined unto the sycamore tree, and its arm[s] are opened unto me graciously. I have come and I have clasped the Utchat, and I have caused it to be seated in peace upon its throne" — ending "I have gathered together [all my members], I have gathered together [all my members]. I soar like a bird and I descend upon the earth… I am the child of yesterday, and the Akeru gods of the earth have made me to come into being."
   - **Rubric**: IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] HE SHALL BE VICTORIOUS BOTH UPON EARTH AND IN THE UNDERWORLD. HE SHALL DO WHATSOEVER A MAN DOETH WHO IS UPON THE EARTH, AND HE SHALL PERFORM ALL THE DEEDS WHICH THOSE DO WHO ARE [ALIVE]. NOW IT IS A GREAT PROTECTION [GIVEN] BY THE GOD. THIS CHAPTER WAS FOUND IN THE CITY OF KHE-MENNU INSCRIBED UPON THE BLOCK OF IRON IN LETTERS OF LAPIS-LAZULI WHICH WAS UNDER THE FEET OF THIS GOD.

9. **CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 13).] "The chapter of knowing the 'chapters of coming forth by day' in a single chapter." Spoken by "the overseer of the palace, the chancellor-in-chief, Osiris Nu, triumphant, begotten of the overseer of the palace, Amen-hetep, triumphant." This is a parallel recension of the preceding Nebseni chapter, printed immediately after it, and the differences between the two as printed are these: the time-formula drops a term ("I am Yesterday and To-morrow"); the Khus' number is bracketed as a reconstruction, "who are in number [four] millions, [six] hundred, and 1,000, and 200, and they are [in height] twelve cubits"; the sixth hour is glossed as "which belongeth at the head of the Tuat (underworld)"; and the Aukert of the Nebseni text becomes a goddess — "The Eye shall not eat (or absorb) the tears of the goddess Aukert." Its own petitions: "Hail, goddess Aukert, open thou unto me the enclosed place, and grant thou unto me pleasant roads whereupon I may travel." Its self-naming is doubled — "[I] go in and come forth in my name of 'Hehi, the lord of millions of years [and of] the earth'; [I am] the maker of my name" and later "I am the god of the Inundation (Bah), and 'Qem-ur-she' is my name." It ends on a claim of transformed understanding: "I have entered in as a man of no understanding, and I shall come forth in the form of a strong Khu, and I shall look upon my form which shall be that of men and women forever and forever."
   - *The chapter carries two rubrics, numbered "I." and "II." in the edition's own text and printed one after the other in capitals. They give two different and incompatible discovery-legends for the same chapter — Hesepti's chief mason in the foundations of the Hennu shrine, and Heru-ta-ta-f under the feet of the god at Khemennu in the reign of Men-kau-Ra. Nothing on the page reconciles them, ranks them, or comments on the contradiction: no "or", no "but others say", no note from Budge. Both are simply recorded, and both are recorded here the same way. (The Hesepti version also appears a second time in this range, in Budge's note on the Papyrus of Mes-em-neter attached to the preceding chapter's rubric; the Khemennu version likewise closes that preceding chapter's own rubric. So each legend is printed twice in these pages, and the two are never set against each other.)*
   - **Rubric I** (printed in capitals): [IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN] BY A MAN HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY, AND HE SHALL NOT BE REPULSED AT ANY GATE OF THE TUAT (UNDERWORLD), EITHER IN GOING IN OR IN COMING OUT. HE SHALL PERFORM [ALL] THE TRANSFORMATIONS WHICH HIS HEART SHALL DESIRE FOR HIM AND HE SHALL NOT DIE; BEHOLD, THE SOUL OF [THIS] MAN SHALL FLOURISH… THIS CHAPTER WAS FOUND IN THE FOUNDATIONS OF THE SHRINE OF HENNU BY THE CHIEF MASON DURING THE REIGN OF HIS MAJESTY THE KING OF THE NORTH AND OF THE SOUTH, HESEPTI, TRIUMPHANT, WHO CARRIED [IT] AWAY AS A MYSTERIOUS OBJECT WHICH HAD NEVER [BEFORE] BEEN SEEN OR LOOKED UPON. THIS CHAPTER SHALL BE RECITED BY A MAN WHO IS CEREMONIALLY CLEAN AND PURE, WHO HATH NOT EATEN THE FLESH OF ANIMALS OR FISH, AND WHO HATH NOT HAD INTERCOURSE WITH WOMEN.
   - **Rubric II** (printed immediately after, giving the rival account of the same chapter's discovery; a photo caption splits this rubric mid-sentence between "WHO WAS DILIGENT IN MAKING HIM" and "TO UNDERSTAND(?) IT" — see the caption section): IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] HE SHALL BE VICTORIOUS BOTH UPON EARTH AND IN THE UNDERWORLD… THIS CHAPTER WAS FOUND IN THE CITY OF KHEMENNU, UPON A BLOCK OF IRON OF THE SOUTH, WHICH HAD BEEN INLAID [WITH LETTERS] OF REAL LAPIS-LAZULI, UNDER THE FEET OF THE GOD DURING THE REIGN OF HIS MAJESTY, THE KING OF THE NORTH AND OF THE SOUTH, MEN-KAU-RA (MYCERINUS) TRIUMPHANT, BY THE ROYAL SON HERU-TA-TA-F, TRIUMPHANT; HE FOUND IT WHEN HE WAS JOURNEYING ABOUT TO MAKE AN INSPECTION OF THE TEMPLES. ONE NEKHT(?) WAS WITH HIM WHO WAS DILIGENT IN MAKING HIM TO UNDERSTAND(?) IT, AND HE BROUGHT IT TO THE KING AS A WONDERFUL OBJECT… AND BEHOLD, THOU SHALT MAKE A SCARAB OF GREEN STONE, WITH A RIM PLATED(?) WITH GOLD, WHICH SHALL BE PLACED IN THE HEART OF A MAN, AND IT SHALL PERFORM FOR HIM THE "OPENING OF THE MOUTH." AND THOU SHALT ANOINT IT WITH ANTI UNGUENT, AND THOU SHALT RECITE OVER IT [THESE] ENCHANTMENTS.

10. **GAINING MASTERY OVER ENEMIES** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 15).] "The chapter of coming forth by day and of gaining the mastery over enemies." Opens on Ra enthroned "in his habitation of millions of years," having gathered the company of the gods "whose faces are hidden, who dwell in the Temple of Khepera, who eat the god Bah, and who drink the drink-offerings which are brought into the celestial regions of light; and conversely." Nu's petitions: "Grant that I may take possession of the captives of Osiris, and never let me have my being among the fiends of Suti!"; "Grant thou that I may sit upon the throne of Ra, and let me have possession of my body before the god Seb." The enemy is described as a snarer — the night-watchers of Suti and of the Crocodile, "whose snares are like unto everlastingness and whose cords are like unto eternity" — and the speaker reports seeing "the god Abet-ka placing the cord; the child is laid in fetters." The chapter's most striking move is a conditional oath that puts the order of nature in the balance: if he is not allowed to come forth against his Enemy and be victorious before the sovereign princes, "then may Hapi—who liveth upon law and order—not come forth into heaven… and may Ra—who feedeth upon fish—not descend into the waters"; and the terms are then inverted and affirmed, "and then, verily, the great day upon the earth shall not be in its season." It closes with the outcome asserted as accomplished: "I have come against my Enemy, he hath been given unto me, he hath come to an end, and I have gotten possession [of him] before the sovereign princes."

11. **VICTORY OVER ENEMIES** — [From Lepsius, *Todtenbuch*, Bl. 25.] "The chapter of coming forth by day and of gaining the master over enemies." A moon-chapter: "Hail, [thou] who shinest from the Moon and who sendest forth light therefrom, thou comest forth among thy multitudes, and thou goest round about, let me rise," with the printed variant "let me be brought in among the Khus, and let the underworld be opened [unto me]." The reward is a weapon: "I shall stand upon my feet and have a staff of gold," or (as others say), "a rod of gold in my hand, wherewith I shall inflict cuts on the limbs [of mine enemy] and shall live." It ends on a birth-image: "The legs of Sothis are stablished, and I am born in their state of rest." Also notable: the enemies are to be delivered up in public — "they shall cause my enemies to be brought to me in a state of misery in the presence of the divine sovereign princes" — and the mother's ka is named as beneficiary, "The divine ka (double) of my mother shall rest in peace because of this."

12. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Amen-em-heb (Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 78).] "The chapter of coming forth by day." Spoken by "the scribe Mahu," and short enough to quote whole: "I have knowledge. I was conceived by the goddess Sekhet, and the goddess Neith gave birth to me; I am Horus, and [I have] come forth from the Eye of Horus. I am Uatchit who came forth from Horus. I am Horus and I fly up and perch myself upon the forehead of Ra in the bows of his boat which is in heaven." *(The papyrus is named for Amen-em-heb; the speaker in the text is Mahu.)*

13. **OPENING THE UNDERWORLD** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 15).] "The chapter of opening the underworld." Nu: "The chamber of those who dwell in Nu is opened, and the footsteps of those who dwell with the god of Light are set free. The chamber of Shu is opened, and he cometh forth; and I shall come forth outside, and I shall advance from my territory(?), I shall receive … and I shall lay firm hold upon the tribute in the House of the Chief of his dead. I shall advance to my throne which is in the boat of Ra. I shall not be molested, and I shall not suffer shipwreck from my throne which is in the boat of Ra, the mighty one. Hail thou that shinest and givest light from Hent-she!"

14. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 7).] "The chapter of coming forth by day." The mastery-inventory chapter. It opens on doors: "The doors of heaven are opened for me, the doors of earth are opened for me, the bars and bolts of Seb are opened for me, and the first temple hath been unfastened for me by the god Petra." The turn from captivity: "Behold, I was guarded and watched, [but now] I am released; behold, his hand had tied cords round me and his hand had darted upon me in the earth." Then the litany, item by item: "I have gained the mastery over my heart; I have gained the mastery over my breast(?); I have gained the mastery over my two hands; I have gained the mastery over my two feet; I have gained the mastery over my mouth; I have gained the mastery over my whole body; I have gained the mastery over sepulchral offerings; I have gained the mastery over the waters; I have gained the mastery over the air; I have gained the mastery over the canal; I have gained the mastery over the river and over the land; I have gained the mastery over the furrows; I have gained the mastery over the male workers for me; I have gained the mastery over the female workers for me in the underworld; I have gained the mastery over [all] the things which were ordered to be done for me upon the earth." A diet clause follows: "That which is an abomination unto me, I shall not eat; [nay] I shall live upon cakes [made] of white grain, and my ale shall be [made] of the red grain of Hapi (i.e., the Nile)," and a resting-place — "In a clean place shall I sit on the ground beneath the foliage of the date-palm of the goddess Hathor… having the books of the divine words of the writings of the god Thoth." The whole mastery-litany is then repeated in shortened form, and the chapter ends on the simplest possible claim of bodily autonomy: "I shall lift myself up on my left side, and I shall place myself on my right side; I shall lift myself up on my right side, and I shall place myself [on my left side]. I shall sit down, I shall stand up, and I shall place myself in [the path of] the wind like a guide who is well prepared."
    - **Rubric**: IF THIS COMPOSITION BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY, AND HE SHALL BE IN A POSITION TO JOURNEY ABOUT OVER THE EARTH AMONG THE LIVING. AND HE SHALL NEVER SUFFER DIMINUTION, NEVER, NEVER.

15. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Mes-em-neter (Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 81).] "Another chapter." A chain of divine identifications, each asserted flatly: "I am the Fire-god, the divine brother of the Fire-god, and [I am] Osiris the brother of Isis"; "I am Sah (Orion) who travelleth over his domain and who journeyeth along before the stars of heaven, [which is] the belly of my mother Nut; she conceived me through her love, and she gave birth to me because it was her will so to do"; "I am Anpu (Anubis) on the day of the god Sepa"; "I am the Bull at the head of the meadow"; "I, even I, am the lord Tem. I am Osiris." The vengeance clause: "My divine son, together with his mother Isis, hath avenged me on mine enemies. My enemies have wrought every [kind of] evil, therefore their arms, and hands, and feet, have been fettered by reason of their wickedness which they have wrought upon me." The rejuvenation clause: "I have become mighty, and I have become a divine being by the side of the birth-chamber of Osiris, I am brought forth with him, I renew my youth, I renew my youth." It ends in an offering-inventory — cakes on the altars of the divine father, beasts, cattle, "red feathered fowl," oxen, geese, ducks, and sacrifices to Thoth and to An-heri-ertaitsa.

16. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Mes-em-neter (Naville, op. cit., Bd. I. Bl. 82).] "Another chapter." Short, and built on seizing the four winds by the body-part: "I sniff the wind of the east by its hair; I lay hold upon the north wind by its hair, I seize and hold fast to the west wind by its body, and I go round about heaven on its four sides; I lay hold upon the south wind by its eye, and I bestow air upon the venerable beings [who are in the underworld] along with the eating of cakes." It opens by naming the sacrifice already made — "I have sacrificed unto An-heri-ertaitsa, and I am decreed to be strengthened in heart, for I have made offerings at the altars of my divine father Osiris; I rule in Tattu."
    - **Rubric**: IF THIS COMPOSITION BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] UPON EARTH HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY, AND HE SHALL HAVE THE FACULTY OF TRAVELLING ABOUT AMONG THE LIVING, AND HIS NAME SHALL NEVER PERISH.

17. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 16).] "The chapter of coming forth by day," spoken by "the libationer, the lord of reverence, Nebseni." Structurally the most formal text in the unit: a responsion in seven paired stanzas. Each pair runs — an invocation ending "Strengthen thou me according as thou hast strengthened thyself, and show thyself upon the earth, O thou that returnest and withdrawest thyself, and let thy will be done," answered by "Behold the god of One Face is with me," followed by a fresh self-identification. The gods addressed in turn: the hawk who riseth in heaven, lord of the goddess Meh-urt; Horus the son of Isis; Thoth; Osiris; "thou who [standest] upon thy legs, in thine hour" (variant: "thou who art victorious upon thy legs in thine hour, thou lord of the two divine Tchafi"); "thou Nekhen who art in thine egg, thou lord of the goddess Meh-urt"; and the pair Sebek "within his ground" and Neith "within her plantation." The self-identifications between them include "[I am] the hawk which is within the shrine"; "[I am] the hawk in the southern heaven, and [I am] Thoth in the northern heaven; I make peace with the raging fire and I bring Maat to him that loveth her"; "I am the Plant of the region where nothing sprouteth, and the Blossom of the hidden horizon." The chapter then turns to the seven executioners — "ye seven beings who make decrees, who support the Scales on the night of the judgment of the Utchat, who cut off heads, who hack necks in pieces, who take possession of hearts by violence and rend the places where hearts are fixed, who make slaughterings in the Lake of Fire" — and strikes the naming-bargain with them: "I know you and I know your names, therefore know ye me even as I know your names. I come forth to you, therefore come ye forth to me, for ye live in me and I would live in you." The petition is for surplus time in every unit at once: "give me multitudes of years over and above my years of life, and multitudes of months over and above my months of life, and multitudes of days over and above my days of life, and multitudes of nights over and above my nights of life."
    - **Rubric**: IF THIS CHAPTER BE RECITED FOR THE DECEASED HE SHALL BE STRONG UPON EARTH BEFORE RA, AND HE SHALL HAVE A COMFORTABLE BURIAL (OR TOMB) WITH OSIRIS, AND IT SHALL BE OF GREAT BENEFIT TO A MAN IN THE UNDERWORLD. SEPULCHRAL BREAD SHALL BE GIVEN UNTO HIM, AND HE SHALL COME FORTH INTO THE PRESENCE [OF RA] DAY BY DAY, AND EVERY DAY, REGULARLY, AND CONTINUALLY.

18. **COMING FORTH BY DAY** — [From the Papyrus of Nebseni (British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 3).] "The chapter of coming forth by day and of opening up a way through the Ammehet." Addressed to "ye lords of Kas, ye who are without sin and who live for the limitless and infinite eons of time which make up eternity." The claim of qualification is again knowledge of names, and here the name is supplied: "I know you, and I know your names, and I know also the name of the mighty god, before whose nose ye set your tchefau food; and his name is 'Tekem.'" Petitions: deliverance "from the crocodile [which liveth in] this country of right and truth"; restoration of speech, "Grant ye to me my mouth that I may speak therewith"; and safe carriage with the sun, "[When] he openeth up his path in the eastern horizon of heaven, and [when] he fluttereth down in the western horizon of heaven, may he carry me along with him and may I be safe and sound!" The fear is stated as a door: "let me not be turned back at your portals, and let not your doors be shut in my face, because my cakes are in the city of Pe and my ale is in the city of Tep." It ends "I am the double Lion-god."
    - **Rubric**: IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] UPON EARTH, [OR IF IT BE DONE] IN WRITING UPON [HIS] COFFIN, HE SHALL COME FORTH BY DAY IN ALL THE FORMS WHICH HE IS PLEASED [TO TAKE], AND HE SHALL ENTER IN TO [HIS] PLACE AND SHALL NOT BE DRIVEN BACK. AND CAKES, AND ALE, AND JOINTS OF MEAT UPON THE ALTAR OF OSIRIS SHALL BE GIVEN UNTO HIM; AND HE SHALL ENTER IN PEACE INTO SEKHET-AARRU TO KNOW THE DECREE OF HIM WHO DWELLETH IN TATTU; THERE SHALL WHEAT AND BARLEY BE GIVEN UNTO HIM; THERE SHALL HE FLOURISH AS HE DID UPON EARTH; AND HE SHALL DO WHATSOEVER IT PLEASETH HIM TO DO, EVEN AS THE COMPANY OF THE GODS WHICH IS IN THE UNDERWORLD, CONTINUALLY, AND REGULARLY, FOR MILLIONS OF TIMES. *(This rubric is the one interrupted mid-sentence by a photo caption — see the OCR/layout section.)*

19. **LIFTING UP THE FEET** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 6).] "The chapter of lifting up the feet and of coming forth upon the earth." Whole text: "Perform thy work, O Seker, perform thy work, O Seker, O thou [who dwellest in thy house], and who [standest] on [thy] feet in the underworld! I am the god who sendeth forth rays of light over the Thigh of heaven, and I come forth to heaven and I sit myself down by the God of Light (Khu). Hail, I have become helpless! Hail, I have become helpless! but I go forward. I have become helpless, I have become helpless in the regions of those who plunder in the underworld." — a rare admission of weakness inside a genre of power-claims, with the assertion of motion set against it.

20. **JOURNEYING TO ANNU** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 13).] "The chapter of journeying to Annu (Heliopolis) and of receiving a throne therein." An itinerary through named shrines, opening on an arrival from the edge of the world — "I have come forth from the uttermost parts of the earth, and [I have] received my apparel(?) at the will(?) of the Ape" — and continuing: "I penetrate into the holy habitations of those who are in [their] shrines (or coffins), I force my way through the habitations of the god Remren, and [I arrive in the habitations of the god Akhsesef, I travel on through the holy chambers, and I pass into the Temple of the god Kemken." The Buckle is given to him, lays hands on him, and assigns him "its sister Khebent, and its mother Kehkehet"; it sets him "in [the eastern part of heaven wherein Ra riseth and is exalted every day; and I rise therein and travel onward, and I become a spiritual body (sah) like the god, and they set me] on that holy way on which Thoth journeyeth when he goeth to make peace between the two Fighting-gods (i.e., Horus and Set)." It closes on Thoth's own journey rather than the speaker's: "He journeyeth, he journeyeth to the city of Pe, and he cometh to the city of Tepu." *(Square brackets in this chapter mark two long restorations supplied by the editor; the first of the two is printed with no closing bracket.)*

21. **TRANSFORMATION** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 9).] "The chapter of a man transforming himself into whatever form he pleaseth." Whole text: "I have come into the House of the King by means of the mantis (abit) which led me hither. Homage to thee, O thou who fliest into heaven, and dost shine upon the son of the white crown, and dost protect the white crown, let me have my existence with thee! I have gathered together the great god[s], I am mighty, I have made my way and I have travelled along thereon." The general transformation-charter is thus the shortest of the transformation texts, and the specific ones follow it.

22. **PERFORMING TRANSFORMATIONS** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheet 10).] "The chapter of performing the transformation into a hawk of gold." Nu: "I have risen, I have risen like the mighty hawk [of gold] that cometh forth from his egg; I fly and I alight like the hawk which hath a back four cubits wide, and the wings of which are like unto the mother-of-emerald of the south. I have come forth from the interior of the Sektet boat, and my heart hath been brought unto me from the mountain of the east. I have alighted upon the Atet boat, and those who were dwelling in their companies have been brought unto me, and they bowed low in paying homage unto me and in saluting me with cries of joy. I have risen, and I have gathered myself together like the beautiful hawk of gold, which hath the head of a Bennu bird, and Ra entereth in day by day to hearken unto my words; I have taken my seat among those first-born gods of Nut. I am stablished, and the divine Sekhet-hetep is before me, I have eaten therein, I have become a khu therein, I have an abundance therein—as much as I desire—the god Nepra hath given to me my throat, and I have gained the mastery over that which guardeth (or belongeth to) my head." *(The provenance line's museum number is printed with a stray character: "No. t10,477".)*

23. **TRANSFORMATION INTO A HAWK** — [From the Papyrus of Nu (British Museum No. 10,477, sheets 13 and 14).] "The chapter of making the transformation into a divine hawk." The longest text in the unit, running across three printed pages, and the last complete text in this range. Its opening request is for *fear*, not for peace: "O grant thou that I may be feared, and make thou me to be a terror. Let the gods of the underworld be afraid of me, and may they fight for me in their habitations which are therein." That sentence is printed twice, verbatim; the second clause of it — the gods of the underworld fearing him and fighting for him in their habitations — recurs four times in all, twice inside the doubled refrain and twice on its own ("May the gods of the underworld fear me, and may they fight for me in their habitations"; "the gods of the underworld have fear of me, and they fight for me in their habitations when they see that thou hast provided me with food"). The protection sought is against an unnamed dweller in the House of Darkness — "he that clotheth and covereth the feeble one, and whose [name] is hidden." The transformation itself is credited to Horus and stated as a transaction: "I had risen up like the divine hawk, and Horus made for me a spiritual body containing his own soul, so that I might take possession of all that belonged unto Osiris in the underworld." The double Lion-god then refuses him the nemmes crown — "'Get thee back to the uttermost parts of heaven, for behold, inasmuch as through thy form of Horus thou hast become a spiritual body, (sah) the nemmes crown is not for thee; but behold, thou hast the power of speech even to the uttermost parts of heaven'" — but later in the same chapter the crown is bound upon him: "he hath bound [upon my head] the nemmes crown, by the decree of the double Lion-god on my behalf, and the god Auhet hath prepared a way for me." He claims a strange pre-natal seniority: "I am one of those worms(?) which the eye of the Lord, the only One, hath created. And behold, when as yet Isis had not given birth to Horus, I had germinated, and had flourished, and I had become aged." Road-knowledge is claimed as an internal possession: "I, even I, am he who knoweth the roads through the sky, and the wind thereof is in my body." The chapter's imperative to the gods of the Tuat is a command to be looked at: "[Down] upon your faces, ye gods of the Tuat (underworld), who would resist me with your faces and oppose me with your powers… Horus hath commanded that ye lift up your faces so that I may look upon you." It closes not on the speaker but on Osiris, in a rising sequence of restorations — "Exalted [be thou] upon thy throne, O Osiris! Thou hast heard fair things, O Osiris! Thy strength is vigorous, O Osiris. Thy head is fastened unto thee, O Osiris. Thy brow is stablished, O Osiris. Thy heart is glad, [O Osiris]" — and on the succession: "Thy son Horus hath risen like the sun upon thy throne, and all life is with him… Horus is [his] deliverer, Horus is [his] deliverer. Horus hath sprung from the water of his divine father and [from his] decay. He hath become the Governor of Egypt."

## Key quotes
- "The chapter of not letting the soul of a man be taken from him in the underworld." — the title of PRESERVATION OF THE SOUL; the danger it names is that the soul is confiscable after death.
- "I, even I, am he who came forth from the water-flood which I make to overflow, and which becometh mighty as the river [Nile]." — the entire body of that chapter: the soul is preserved by an assertion of identity, not by a plea.
- "…is thy name? 'I am one of you.'" — the gate-challenge and answer in DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS (Ani), the unit's clearest statement of the naming mechanism.
- "'Assembler of Souls' is the name of my boat; 'Making the hair to stand on end' is the name of the oars; 'Goad' is the name of the hold; 'Making straight for the middle' is the name of the rudder." — the same chapter; passage is bought by naming every fitting of the vessel.
- "I have made my name to germinate, I have delivered [it], and thou shalt live through me day by day." — DRINKING WATER (Nu); the name as a growing thing.
- "I am Yesterday, To-day, and To-morrow, [and I have] the power to be born a second time." — COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni), the opening self-identification with time itself.
- "Who art thou, then, who consumest in its hidden place? I am the Chief in Re-stau." — the same chapter, challenge and answer again.
- "I have gained the mastery over my heart; … I have gained the mastery over my whole body; … I have gained the mastery over the female workers for me in the underworld." — COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nu, sheet 7); the afterlife described as an estate inventory.
- "I know you and I know your names, therefore know ye me even as I know your names. I come forth to you, therefore come ye forth to me, for ye live in me and I would live in you." — COMING FORTH BY DAY [Papyrus of Nebseni, British Museum No. 9,900, sheet 16], spoken by "the libationer, the lord of reverence, Nebseni," and addressed to the seven who "make slaughterings in the Lake of Fire." **This is translated scripture — the body of a numbered chapter under its own title and provenance line — not a photo caption and not Budge's framing prose.** (Budge is the translator of the volume's texts; his own editorial voice in this range is confined to the two notes recorded under Core claims and to the bracketed provenance lines, and none of it touches this passage.) The sentence is printed unbroken; the caption interruption on this page falls earlier, inside the description of the seven beings, and does not touch these words.
- "I know also the name of the mighty god, before whose nose ye set your tchefau food; and his name is 'Tekem.'" — COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheet 3); the withheld name supplied.
- "IF THIS CHAPTER BE KNOWN [BY THE DECEASED] HE SHALL BE VICTORIOUS BOTH UPON EARTH AND IN THE UNDERWORLD. HE SHALL DO WHATSOEVER A MAN DOETH WHO IS UPON THE EARTH." — the rubric that states the promise most plainly.
- "…AND HIS NAME SHALL NEVER PERISH." — the Mes-em-neter rubric's promised effect; the name is both the tool and the prize.
- "THIS CHAPTER SHALL BE RECITED BY A MAN WHO IS CEREMONIALLY CLEAN AND PURE, WHO HATH NOT EATEN THE FLESH OF ANIMALS OR FISH, AND WHO HATH NOT HAD INTERCOURSE WITH WOMEN." — the recitation conditions attached to the CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE.
- "AND BEHOLD, THOU SHALT MAKE A SCARAB OF GREEN STONE, WITH A RIM PLATED(?) WITH GOLD, WHICH SHALL BE PLACED IN THE HEART OF A MAN, AND IT SHALL PERFORM FOR HIM THE 'OPENING OF THE MOUTH.'" — the same rubric; word plus object.
- "Hail, I have become helpless! Hail, I have become helpless! but I go forward." — LIFTING UP THE FEET; weakness admitted and motion asserted against it.
- "O grant thou that I may be feared, and make thou me to be a terror." — TRANSFORMATION INTO A HAWK, printed twice verbatim.
- "I, even I, am he who knoweth the roads through the sky, and the wind thereof is in my body." — the same chapter; knowledge carried in the body.

## Thread
This unit is where the Book of the Dead's operating principle becomes unmistakable: what saves the dead man is *knowing* — knowing the chapter, and knowing names. The mouth-and-heart chapters of Ch 6 and Ch 7 restored the organs of speech; this one puts that speech to work. Every barrier in these pages is a checkpoint at which something must be said correctly: a doorkeeper asks a name and gets one; a hidden consumer is challenged and answers with his own title; seven executioners are addressed by name and offered a reciprocal deal in which their lives and the speaker's become mutually dependent. The rubrics make the same point from the editorial side, and they escalate: earlier chapters promised triumph over enemies, but here the promise is that the knower will do everything a living man does, will never be repulsed at any gate, and — most striking — that his name shall never perish. Beside that, the two rival discovery-legends printed back-to-back for one chapter, as numbered rubrics I and II with nothing to choose between them (Hesepti's chief mason in the foundations of the Hennu shrine; Heru-ta-ta-f under the god's feet at Khemennu in the reign of Men-kau-Ra), show the tradition asserting the chapters' antiquity as part of their power — and show the volume content to print two incompatible antiquities without picking one, and the green-stone scarab shows the words needing a physical carrier. The unit ends by opening the transformation series — first the general charter to become "whatever form he pleaseth," then the hawk of gold, then the divine hawk — which converts all this knowledge into shape-changing, and sets up the long run of transformation chapters that follows.

## Connections
- **Direct overlap with Ch 7.** The three DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS chapters opening this unit are entered in full in Ch 7 as DOMINION OVER ELEMENTS (1), (2) and (3), with the same provenance lines and the same quoted text; Ch 7 also carries the AIR AND WATER chapter (Papyrus of Nu, sheet 12) that stands immediately before them, which this unit does not reach. This is duplication, not a parallel: see the overlap note at the head of the examples section.
- **Ch 6** supplies the conventions this unit runs on without restating them, and the catalogue of papyri behind the bracketed provenance lines here (Mut-hetep, Hu-nefer, Pagrer, Amen-hetep, Mes-em-neter are all named there). Ch 6 also prints the three short "mouth" chapters — giving a mouth to Nu, giving a mouth to Ani — which are the precondition for everything in this unit: the petition "Grant ye to me my mouth that I may speak therewith" recurs inside COMING FORTH BY DAY (Nebseni, sheet 3), and the CHAPTER OF KNOWLEDGE rubric here prescribes a green-stone scarab that performs the "opening of the mouth" as a physical substitute for it. Ch 7 carries OPENING THE MOUTH as a chapter in its own right.
- **Ch 6's** innocence-refrain from the Litany ("I have not spoken lies wittingly") and **Ch 11's** itemized declarations in the Hall of Double Maati bracket this unit on either side: here the qualification for passage is not innocence but knowledge, which is the alternative currency the book runs on.
- **Ch 9** continues the transformation series begun at the end of this unit: the governor of the sovereign princes (the chapter printed immediately after this range ends), the god who giveth light in the darkness, the lotus, Ptah, the Bennu bird, the heron — all under the same "TRANSFORMATION" heading and mostly from the same Papyrus of Nu.
- The two enemy-chapters here (GAINING MASTERY OVER ENEMIES; VICTORY OVER ENEMIES) belong with the Chaplet of Victory and the VICTORY OVER ENEMIES chapter printed in **Ch 6**: the same tribunal of "sovereign princes," the same demand that the enemy be produced and destroyed in their presence. The same tribunal is invoked defensively rather than offensively in **Ch 7's** heart and serpent chapters — "may there be no opposition to me in the presence of the sovereign princes."
- **Ch 13's** list of Egyptian deities is the reference for part of the dense divine onomasticon of these pages; the names from this unit that are entered there are Seker, Khnemu, Sebek, Neith, Uatchit and Meh-urt. Others that recur here — Nepra, Nekhen, An-heri-ertaitsa, Tekem, Auhet, Neb-er-tcher and the double Lion-god — do not appear in that list, so for those names these chapters' own words are all the volume gives.
