# Ch 4 — The Function of OEMII

## In one paragraph
Vallee devotes this chapter to the UMMO affair, the Spanish contact case he treats as the clearest working example of his fourth element — a "sophisticated group" already exploiting the belief patterns the earlier chapters described. He narrates the June 1, 1967 sighting of a large lens-shaped craft over San José de Valderas, a Madrid suburb, bearing an insignia the witness Carlos saw as a capital H with a vertical bar through the middle; the anonymous photographs delivered to the press; the landing at Santa Mónica the same evening; the small nickel capsules containing green plastic strips marked with the same symbol, which laboratory analysis at INTA identified as du Pont's restricted TEDLAR; and the courteous, untraceable letters offering 18,000 pesetas apiece for more capsules. He then traces the paper trail backwards to Fernando Sesma Manzano, the Madrid UFO-club founder telephoned in January 1965 by a foreign-accented stranger and thereafter flooded with hundreds of pages of typewritten documents from the planet UMMO, orbiting the star IUMMA (Wolf 424) — documents with mass figures, gravitational constants, a telepathy mechanism, a krypton-based soul, a moneyless economy, a doctrine of non-interference, condolences on the death of Bertrand Russell, and a stern warning against turning UMMO into a cult. Vallee reports that by 1970–71 Spanish officials, French space-agency scientists, Argentine astronomers, and a Paris biologist planning to hunt krypton in the human hypothalamus had all been drawn in, and that one meeting produced an "Ode to UMMO." He notes the sinister structural facts — a military technical school and airfield at the focus of all three sightings, industrially restricted plastic in the capsules — and entertains a military-intelligence origin, yet refuses the easy verdict of hoax, because French police confirmed obscure 1950 details in the documents, including a farmer's old complaint about a stolen electrical meter.

## Core claims
- Vallee claims the UMMO material cannot be dismissed as a simple hoax: "It would be comforting to discover that the claims in the UMMO stories can be disposed of as mere hoaxes, but such is not the case." His stated ground is that French police, checking old archives for months, confirmed details of the alleged 1950 landing that the documents had mentioned casually years before.
- He argues UMMO is more than an intelligence exercise, while conceding that a military intelligence group "could have a number of reasons either to sponsor or stage the UMMO process" — one possibility being a training project for intelligence agents. He poses it as a question: "Was UMMO an exercise in camouflage that got out of hand?"
- He claims that if the purpose was "the creation of a small nucleus of fanatics, the 'exercise' certainly succeeded!"
- Vallee reports as a structurally suspicious fact that "a military technical school and airfield lies at the focus of the three sightings that provide UMMO with all its 'evidence'," alongside the capsules and the strips of TEDLAR.
- He claims the two men who supplied the San José de Valderas photographs (an anonymous caller and "Antonio Pardo") both mysteriously vanished — telephone contact in each case, but neither could be met; of Pardo specifically he says he "was never interviewed in person by any of the investigators," and notes drily that "the name Antonio Pardo can be translated as Anthony Brown."
- He claims the letter-writers' names were untraceable: "no one by the name of Henri Dagousset, and no one by the name of Antoine Nancey could be located in Madrid."
- He claims the UMMO documents are exceptional in exactly two respects: they supply a wealth of technical data the reader is explicitly invited to check, and the contacted groups are asked NOT to disseminate the material and "in particular, not to reveal such communication to their governments."
- He reports that belief in UMMO penetrated institutions, not just hobbyist circles: "In Spain, high officials in the government, the law-enforcement agencies, and the military had become firm believers in the reality of a planet that circled Wolf 424."
- He claims the release of the Condon study's negative conclusions, prominently displayed in the Paris press, "was not what the U.S. Air Force had expected," because French military personnel and technical experts — some in high positions in the French Space Agency, "working alone, secretly" — already knew "what the real situation was" from quietly accumulated national data unavailable to most scientists.
- He notes a possible link between UMMO and the AFFA case of the previous chapter: the AFFA contactee described a "Universal Association of Planets" called OEEU, "a term which has a certain morphological similarity with UMMO," and in both cases messages allegedly originate with an extraterrestrial society. He says one "might be tempted to infer" a link — he does not assert it.
- Via the Socorro digression, he claims one "cannot understand the UFO phenomenon without taking into consideration all of its possible symbolic ramifications, no matter how seemingly remote from our technological reality."
- His summary claim: "the beliefs of humanity can be manipulated both by physical and symbolic means," and this is "certainly . . . one of the objectives of UMMO, whether it is a prankish game, a psycho-sociological test or a sinister plot." He leaves all three options open.
- He frames UMMO as a fragment, not the answer: "UMMO is only a small part of the total phenomenon, and the time has clearly come to assess the full impact of our strange, bewildering confrontation with it."

## Concepts
- **OEMII** — The chapter's title term. [FLAG: the word OEMII occurs nowhere in the chapter body — only in the chapter title, the running heads, the table of contents, and the book's index, where the single entry "OEMII, 95-112" simply points at this chapter entire. Vallee never defines it; the note deliberately does not import an outside definition.] From context the chapter is about the *function* served by whoever or whatever the UMMO correspondence is addressed to and acting upon.
- **UMMO** — In the documents' own account, a planet or "solidified celestial body" whose inhabitants have been visiting Earth since 1950 and are responsible for flying-saucer activity; in Vallee's account, an organization whose reputation is "rapidly spreading in Spain" and which is "attempting to influence human affairs" through a stream of technical and philosophical documents [that characterization is Ch. 3's forward reference to this chapter, not a phrase used here].
- **IUMMA / Wolf 424** — The star UMMO orbits, said in the documents to be "recorded on your earth under the denomination Wolf 424." The stated orbit is elliptical with IUMMA at a focus.
- **the UMMO insignia** — Described by the documents' letterhead as the symbol of "the UMMO General Government": three vertical bars crossed horizontally. Carlos saw it under the craft as "a capital H with a vertical bar in the center" — not quite the astrological symbol for Uranus, and passable for a Cyrillic letter had the vertical sides been slightly curved. "Others would later describe it as an M, or as an H, but Carlos clearly saw the middle bar."
- **OAWOLEA OUEWA** — The UMMO term, glossed in the documents themselves, for "lenticular spacecraft."
- **the capsules** — Small aluminum-looking cylinders reportedly found near the Santa Mónica landing site: length of tube 129.8 mm, caliber 8.8 mm, diameter of the central metallic disk 24 mm, rounded ends. One examined specimen contained a piece of green plastic engraved with the UMMO symbol.
- **TEDLAR** — Du Pont de Nemours' brand of polyvinyl fluoride, the plastic identified inside the capsule. Per Vallee: at the time of the events made exclusively in the United States, its use restricted, not sold commercially, with extraordinary resistance to ultraviolet radiation, weathering, solvents, chemical agents and abrasion; used by NASA at Cape Kennedy to cover rockets on the tower before launch; also noted for "some military applications"; unobtainable on the open Spanish market.
- **BUAE BIEE** — The documents' name for the telepathic impulses UMMO adults use: one holds in consciousness the mental image of one's correspondent and sends impulses of approximately 0.14 milliseconds' duration. A special bilingual code permits "double simultaneous conversations."
- **WOA** — The Creator or God of UMMO's "deeply religious" society. The documents claim to possess "scientific arguments in favor of the existence of a factor that you would call 'soul'."
- **the krypton link** — A claimed third factor binding soul to body, "constituted by krypton atoms embedded within the cephalic mass." Some of the UMMO documents place the krypton specifically in the human hypothalamus — the claim the Paris biologist announced he would go home and test.
- **IBOZOO UU** — The subject of a UMMO document containing "an expose of the errors of modern physics," analyzed at the 1971 meeting and said there to compare favorably with advanced texts by Minkowski and Eddington.
- **"true distance"** — UMMO's answer when Sesma objected that the stated 3.68502 light-years from Wolf 424 to the solar system looked wrong: the figure is the true distance, which "varied greatly from day to day according to the advanced physical concepts of UMMO." Vallee's gloss: coming to the solar system by such a route "would be like going to Australia by a direct tunnel through the earth" — his footnote reads this as travel through higher dimensions.
- **cosmic morality / non-interference** — The documents' stated reason for staying hidden: "A cosmic morality forbids any paternalistic attitude on planetary social networks that must be individually created by each of them." Second reason: any official presentation would produce "grave alterations, incalculable social perturbations," destroying the possibility of studying human society "in its current virgin conditions."
- **negative entropy** — The direction in which UMMO says its embedded "expeditionary group" — Russell, Gandhi, Che Guevara and the rest — has oriented the societies into which they were "inserted": toward "forms more in conformity with the ethical norms of collective coexistence."
- **the warning against cultism** — UMMO's explicit instruction not to swap one's religious, scientific and politico-economic ideas for UMMO's, on the grounds that the information is "purely descriptive," offered "void of positive arguments, rationales, and proofs," and that adopting it would "alter dangerously the normal rhythm of social existence and the future Culture on Earth."
- **the non-disclosure request** — The documents' instruction that contacted groups not disseminate the information and specifically not reveal the communication to their governments; Vallee counts this, with the checkable technical data, as what makes the corpus exceptional.
- **INTA** — The Spanish National Institute for Space Research, whose laboratories analyzed the capsule. Vallee notes that Farriols' uncle was INTA's president.
- **GEPA** — French research group whose physicists, per the chapter, agreed with the Spanish engineer that UMMO's physical concepts were "absolutely consistent."
- **Sociedad de Amigos del Espacio** — The UFO group Fernando Sesma Manzano founded in 1954, the institutional channel through which the UMMO material entered the world.
- **SPECTRA** — From the third epigraph: the name of a spacecraft its communicators say they "use as you use a planet," claimed to have been stationed over the earth for the past eight hundred years, with Israeli territory as its first landing site.
- **OEEU / Universal Association of Planets** — From the preceding chapter's AFFA case; Vallee flags the morphological similarity of the name to UMMO as grounds for suspecting a link between the two contact series.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **San José de Valderas, June 1, 1967 (the sighting)** — Vallee reconstructs the scene novelistically through a witness he names only as Carlos, reading the sports page on the grass in a quiet Madrid suburb just after sunset when a child's cry made him look up. A lens-shaped craft "no less than forty meters in diameter" rose behind a line of trees to the northeast and followed a curved trajectory toward the power lines; it flipped on one side (losing Carlos the view of its brilliant dome), exposing the H-with-a-middle-bar insignia on its underside. It swung right, stabilized, reached a point to the southeast, veered again — exposing the insignia a second time — turned north, hung stationary near the castle for several minutes, then shot up "at an unbelievable speed." Its color went from bright yellow through orange to red. Vallee stresses the crowd: dozens of people still staring at the sky, entire families resting near the pines, workers returning from Madrid with their wives, children playing.
- **The anonymous negatives at *Informaciones*** — The next morning Antonio San Antonio, graphics editor of the Madrid evening paper *Informaciones*, took a phone call from a man offering five "extraordinary photographic documents" — negatives of shots taken at San José de Valderas the previous evening — who declined to give his name and said the negatives could be picked up at a photographic laboratory on Calle General Ricardos. The paper published all five, "admittedly very bad ones." Vallee flags the oddity that the negatives were not in sequence, with the assumption that the photographer kept some of the sensational shots for himself. Excitement in Madrid followed; private investigators rushed to the scene and issued calls for more details.
- **Antonio Pardo's second series, August 26, 1967** — A second witness produced two photographs "as poor as the first series," plus several additional items. Telephone contact was again established, but investigators could never meet the man. Vallee's evidential verdict: plotted on a map against the object's flight, both photo sets agreed with the witnesses' descriptions of its motion — "But the two photographers had mysteriously vanished."
- **Santa Mónica, the same evening (multiple independent witnesses)** — (a) A man in his early thirties driving toward the suburb between 8:30 and 9:00 p.m. saw an oval glow, white-centered with diffused yellow edges, descending rapidly toward the restaurant La Ponderosa, then rising and vanishing. (b) Mr Rivero, a shopowner at No. 33 Calle de Sedano, stepping out of his door, saw a "conical thing" giving off a fiery light, flying over the trees and rising rapidly. (c) Doña Eugenia Arbiol Alonso, in Apartment 27-B of the Colonia Santa Mónica overlooking the Ponderosa area, watched with her mother from the window. Vallee reproduces a long verbatim exchange from Ribera and Farriols' *Un Caso Perfecto* in which she pinpoints the spot between the pine trees and the trucks, insists "No, no. I saw it land," describes portholes or airplane lights on a round thing "like a gasoline tank," her own dismissive first thought ("The kids must be doing something over there"), and the ending — "Later it rose and stopped a few meters above ground and then it was — now you see it, now you don't . . . and I cannot say where it went."
- **The La Ponderosa restaurant sequence** — Antonio Muñoz, atop a ladder stringing colored bulbs for the evening customers, was interrupted by a man rushing in from the road: "I think the craft or whatever it was has landed right near La Ponderosa." Muñoz thought him mad, sent him away and did not investigate. Five or ten minutes later a couple arrived by car, the woman weeping; the fairly calm middle-aged man said they had seen a lighted object land two hundred meters away and drew a sketch showing the H-like symbol on its underside. Two young couples arrived next, the girls holding each other and crying, reporting a huge sphere of fire that had come toward them diagonally and landed near their car.
- **The landing traces** — The middle-aged man returned, went to the landing site that night, and the next morning found three marks forming an equilateral triangle "six meters in size," each trace a fairly deep rectangle of thirty by fifteen centimeters. At the center of the triangle the grass was burned and a metallic powder was found.
- **The men who "had received a signal"** — Also that evening, several men came to the restaurant and questioned Muñoz; when he relayed the witnesses' description they got excited, saying it corresponded to a signal they had received "which was from another planet."
- **The Henri Dagousset letter** — In the following days several local shopowners received an undated letter in Spanish, in a "peculiarly formal and ornate style," signed with the French name Henri Dagousset. Vallee quotes it at length: it cites the June 1 incident on land near km. 3 of the Boadilla del Monte road (Madrid), "classified under the heading 'Property of the National Estate'"; reports that a steel worker and a young girl resident in Santa Mónica found metallic cylinders with a central disk (129.8 mm long, 8.8 mm caliber, 24 mm disk, aluminum-looking with rounded ends); states both capsules "are now in our hands" with photograph and drawing enclosed; adds that "according to our information, an unknown number of these small cylinders have been found by other residents of the area"; asks the recipient to publicize the appeal to patrons and neighbors; offers up to 18,000 pesetas ("exactly $300") per conforming cylinder, with a new offer negotiable for deteriorated or broken capsules whose contents survive; and offers the shopowner 7,000 pesetas for each capsule secured through his assistance, noting copies went to five other establishments. The closing paragraph declares the purpose: "Our interest is purely scientific. The capsules in question do not contain any device of military interest, but are, we repeat, genuinely technical. Our activities are restricted to the study of the so-called U.F.O. . . . popularly named in this Nation 'Platillos Volantes'." Recipients named: Francisco Arroyo, manager of the bar Santa Mónica; Muñoz of La Ponderosa; and, per Muñoz, Rivero. An accompanying note directed all correspondence before June 28th to "Mr Antoine NANCEY, Lista de Correos - Madrid."
- **The chain of custody on the analyzed capsule** — Vallee traces it deliberately: the capsule was allegedly found by a 12-year-old boy who broke it open with a pair of pliers; it reached Farriols not directly but from another UFO amateur, who had received it from Antonio Pardo — the same man who produced one of the photo sets and who was never interviewed in person by any investigator. The boy said there had been two pieces inside the cylinder and that a peculiar liquid escaped and evaporated when he broke it open. The green plastic bore the engraved UFO symbol: three vertical bars crossed horizontally.
- **The INTA laboratory analysis** — Farriols' uncle being INTA's president, the objects went into the Spanish National Institute for Space Research's laboratories. The extensive technical study reported: (1) the capsule was nickel of very high purity (99%) with traces of magnesium, iron, titanium, cobalt, silicon and aluminum — microphotographs published in Ribera and Farriols' book; (2) spectral analysis identified the plastic as polyvinyl fluoride, entry No. 5.29 in *Identification and Analysis of Plastics* by J. Haslam and H. A. Willis — i.e. du Pont's TEDLAR. Vallee's point: a restricted American aerospace material, unobtainable on the open Spanish market, turns up inside the "alien" capsules — and, "to confuse matters even further," neither of the two names on the letters that sought to buy the capsules back, Henri Dagousset and Antoine Nancey, could be located in Madrid.
- **Fernando Sesma Manzano and the January 1965 phone call** — A Spanish government employee and minor Madrid literary figure who founded the Sociedad de Amigos del Espacio in 1954, read the saucer literature avidly for years and hoped to be contacted; Vallee notes he was "therefore, well prepared" when in the early days of January 1965 a man with a strong foreign accent telephoned and uttered a sequence of strange words that Sesma carefully wrote down. Speaking Spanish, the stranger said he would forward items of an "extraterrestrial order." Sesma asked why not meet in person: "This is not possible at the moment. I am under strict orders."
- **The document flood** — Over the following months Sesma received typewritten material and telephone calls, plus letters from as far away as Australia referring to events that had occurred in Madrid only some forty-eight hours before, and some communications containing "wonderful three-dimensional photographs." Across hundreds of pages of technical detail and philosophical argument he saw unfolding the complete thought-system of an alien life form. Whatever their point of origin, the documents were always stamped with the UMMO General Government symbol.
- **The Aluche landing, February 6, 1966** — Between 8:00 and 9:00 p.m. in Aluche, a Madrid suburb not far from San José de Valderas and Santa Mónica, a large circular object was seen to land by a group of soldiers at a nearby ammunition dump; also seen by Vicente Ortuño and by José Luis Jordán, driving toward Madrid. Jordán's account (published in the Barcelona magazine *Porque*, February 16, 1966, and in later articles by Ribera): a whitish disk approaching, changing to yellow then orange; he got out of his car and watched it come down near an airfield, drove closer, and saw the disk — about thirty feet in diameter, amazingly luminous, emitting a steady muted vibration — rise quickly from the ground and then simply "go out." Three deep rectangular marks were later found at the site. As it rose he clearly saw the UMMO insignia on its underside. Vallee's point: for Sesma and the very few Madrid people already holding documents stamped with that symbol, "the coincidence was indeed fascinating."
- **The astronomical parameters document** — Quoted directly: "We come from UMMO, a planet that revolves around the star IUMMA, recorded on your earth under the denomination Wolf 424"; instructions on the orientation and limits of the information were received "on 12 January, 1965 (Earth Christian Era)"; the orbit is elliptical with Wolf 424 as focus; "Distance from focus Wolf 424 to the focus of the solar system on January 4, 1953: 3.68502 light-years." Sesma telephoned to request a correction of the distance, which struck him as erroneous, and was told it was the "true distance," varying greatly day to day under UMMO's advanced physics.
- **The physiology of the Ummites** — Mass of UMMO given as 514 × 1,021 tons [FLAG: as printed; almost certainly a superscript lost in setting/OCR, i.e. 514 × 10²¹ tons — the note records the printed figure rather than guessing]; acceleration of gravity 11.88 m/sec²; atmospheric conditions close to Earth's, so the inhabitants "look very much like us, except that they have no tonsils." Between ages 14 and 16 children's vocal cords become sclerosed, so adults cannot speak and must use telepathy (the BUAE BIEE impulses). Vallee flags the convenient exception: "some adults on UMMO keep their normal, earth-like phonetic organ in exception to the rule. This was the case for the messengers who spoke to Sesma by telephone."
- **The French-language document from West Germany** — The most complete exposition of UMMO's nature Vallee says he has seen: concise, in French, originating in West Germany, "written in an awkward style that might betray a Spanish author, although other elements seemed to indicate that the writer found difficulty in using not only French but any human language." Its striking feature is pre-emptive self-suspicion: it concedes that "a statement of this nature is usually formulated by a hoaxer, a mentally deranged person with fantastic ideas or, perhaps, by some journalist, some advertising man, or some agent of a political, esoteric or religious organization"; invites the reader to be extremely critical; admits its statements rest solely on witnesses' accounts whose real origin is unknown to the reader; concedes that press coverage of UFOs has often been "fraudulent, rarely authentic," creating "an understandable atmosphere of mistrust"; and disclaims its own sufficiency — the aim in sending "this document, typed by one of our assistants, is not to be believed without any evidence more extensive than these few paragraphs." It claims groups of educated persons who know of UMMO's existence, keeping "a discreet silence" at UMMO's suggestion, exist in Canada, Austria, Spain and Jugoslavia [printed spelling].
- **The account of the first landing** — "At 05 hours 17 minutes CMT of Earth day March 1950" [FLAG: the day-of-month is missing in the printed text, and "CMT" is likely GMT], an OAWOLEA OUEWA established contact with the Earth's lithosphere for the first time in UMMO's history, descending near the village of La Javie in the Basses-Alpes, France.
- **UMMO's self-described society** — Older and more advanced than Earth's; governed by four members "selected by psychophysiological evaluation," with laws "regulated as a function of socio-metric constants measured in time"; no money, transactions in the few valuable items handled through "a network of what you would call electronic computers," normal consumer goods essentially unpriceable because abundant production greatly exceeds demand; deeply religious, believing in a Creator (WOA), claiming scientific arguments for a soul and a krypton-atom link binding it to the body.
- **The 1970–71 diffusion** — The affair became "the most important subject in the minds and conversations of European UFO groups," and belief reached high officials in the Spanish government, law-enforcement agencies and military. In France, the Paris press's prominent display of the Condon study's negative conclusions backfired, because French military and technical experts — some in high positions at the French Space Agency, working alone and secretly — already knew the national data. These scientists obtained copies of the UMMO documents through private channels, had them carefully translated, and were intrigued by their "rational" approach to cosmic contact and by the Aluche / San José de Valderas / Santa Mónica background.
- **The La Plata Observatory check (late 1969, Argentina)** — A prominent journalist supplied the observatory staff near Buenos Aires with UMMO's astronomical parameters. Per the information relayed back to Spain, after spending "entire days and nights" on the figures the astronomers concluded that the planet UMMO must exist. At the Twentieth Congress of Astronautics in Mar del Plata, a small group concluded that "in order to shoot down the UMMO theory one needs, astronomically speaking, very strong arguments."
- **The June 1971 three-day meeting in Spain** — Four people from Madrid, all close associates of Sesma; half a dozen from Barcelona; and a professional biologist from Paris, who showed extreme interest in UMMO's concept of "biogenetic bases" and announced spectacularly that on returning to Paris he would test the theory that krypton could be found in the human hypothalamus. Several believers made "nearly fanatical statements . . . displaying something that closely resembled religious conviction" — one read an "Ode to UMMO." A Spanish engineer analyzed the IBOZOO UU document, said to compare favorably with Minkowski and Eddington, and agreed with GEPA's physicists that UMMO's physical concepts were absolutely consistent — with the corollary Vallee lets stand: if not extraterrestrial, they "must have originated with people who knew perfectly the 'ultimate advances of modern physics', and had extrapolated beyond them."
- **The Bertrand Russell condolence document** — Addressed "Men of the Earth!", it conveys sincere condolences on the death of "your brother, thinker and mathematician Bertrand Russell" and claims the expeditionary group from UMMO is found among the citizens of various nations, naming Russell "and others among his brothers": Mohandas K. Gandhi [printed "Mirandas K. Gandhi" — OCR/typesetting garble], Ernesto (Che) Guevara, Helder Camara, John XXIII, Martin Luther King, Karl Marx, Emmanuel Mounier, Albert Schweitzer, Tolstoi, "and others" — all said to have devoted their lives to reorienting their societies "in the direction of negative entropy."
- **The anti-cult document** — A separate document on philosophical, moral and logical questions in which UMMO warns its contacts not to substitute UMMO's ideas for their own, on grounds that the information is purely descriptive and unproven, that adopting it inside "the ideological constellation formed by your teachers on Earth" would be a mistake, and that doing so "would modify the normal technological process, damaging the current geo-social balance." Its formula: "A revolution of your structures must take place under the sign of the proper social network."
- **The French police archive check** — Vallee's strongest anti-hoax datum: many UMMO documents giving details of local conditions at the alleged 1950 first landing "have now been checked and confirmed by the French police," whose investigators found the tiny farmhouse named as the target of a burglary raid by the alleged "invaders." In the late sixties UMMO had casually claimed several objects, among them an electrical meter, were "taken for study" in 1950; the police searched their old archives for months and found a complaint filed by the farmer for the disappearance of his electrical meter. They did not, however, find the cave where other artifacts are supposedly hidden.
- **Vallee's own August 1974 field trip to La Javie** — Driving a Renault up a narrow, forlorn road along a precipitous mountain-creek valley in the Alps with Aimé Michel and Fernand Lagarde, "two of the best-informed researchers of paranormal phenomena in Europe." They drove as far as the road went, to a tiny village where Michel questioned a local farmer in patois; the villagers assured them there was no cave in the vicinity, so they moved on. After two days of inquiries in a small section of the mountains around La Javie they had located the area of the main events and compared notes with a friend who had searched both on land and by air. Vallee adds that the French Air Force had also photographed the area and that suspicious markings had been carefully investigated by search parties.
- **Socorro, New Mexico, 1964 (the Zamora insignia)** — Introduced as the "another celebrated case that had never been solved, another case of a UFO with an insignia" that Vallee was thinking about on the drive back. Officer Lonnie Zamora saw an egg-shaped object in the desert with two rather small men near it, and on its side a peculiar red sign, "a sort of vertical arrow with a horizontal bar underneath"; no explanation was ever found. Later, raising the Socorro symbol in a conversation about folklore and mythology with a friend at Stanford, the friend recognized it, searched among his many books, and produced a medieval Arabic text listing symbols for the major planets — where, "unmistakably, was the insignia Zamora had seen — the Arabic sign for Venus!" Vallee offers this as his demonstration that symbolic ramifications, however remote from technological reality, cannot be left out of the analysis.
- **The three epigraphs (contact by three different media)** — Vallee opens the chapter with three contact messages, each tagged by its transmission channel: the Guardians to Mrs Keech, "received by automatic writing," quoted from Festinger et al.'s *When Prophecy Fails*, promising a craft "which is to land upon the planet in the cast of the day of August first"; UMMO to a Spanish writer in 1968, "received in the mail," giving the March 1950 La Javie landing and "We are a people older than yours"; and SPECTRA to Uri Geller and Andrija Puharich in 1972, "received on magnetic tape. The tape disappeared after transcription," claiming an eight-hundred-year station over the earth, Israeli territory as the first landing site, and a future entry "through [?] transformation" [the bracketed query mark is in the printed text, marking an unrecovered word].

## Key quotes

- "Was UMMO an exercise in camouflage that got out of hand? If the purpose was the creation of a small nucleus of fanatics, the 'exercise' certainly succeeded!" — his intelligence-operation hypothesis, posed and left open.
- "there was, in the first place, the fact that a military technical school and airfield lies at the focus of the three sightings that provide UMMO with all its 'evidence'." — the structural fact he treats as sinister.
- "As someone was later to remark, the name Antonio Pardo can be translated as Anthony Brown." — on the untraceable second photographer.

## Thread
This chapter supplies the case study for the fourth of the four elements Vallee lays out in the Introduction — "the sophisticated groups which are already exploiting these patterns, such as the UMMO organization in Spain" — and the book had promised in Chapter Three that Chapter Four would analyze it in detail. Where Chapter Three's memorandum material (AFFA, OEEU, the automatic-writing cults, John Dee) showed how belief is born inside small groups, UMMO shows the same machinery running at industrial scale, with physical props: photographs, landing traces, insignia on three separate sightings, and laboratory-analyzable hardware made of a restricted American aerospace plastic. The chapter is thus the book's pivot from "contactees produce texts" to "someone produces contactees," and it is where Vallee installs the disjunction he will not resolve — "a prankish game, a psycho-sociological test or a sinister plot" — while insisting the phenomenon is not exhausted by any of the three. The Socorro/Venus digression plants the symbolic-manipulation thesis that Chapter Six ("The Winged Disk," Phoenician amulets) will develop, and the closing sentence explicitly hands off to Chapter Five ("The Confrontation"): "the time has clearly come to assess the full impact of our strange, bewildering confrontation with it."

- Chapter Nine ("The Control System") is where the manipulation-of-belief thesis stated in this chapter's last paragraph — that human belief "can be manipulated both by physical and symbolic means" — becomes the book's general model.
