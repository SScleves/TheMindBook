# Ch 3 — Memorandum for Archives

## In one paragraph
Under an epigraph taken from a Project Blue Book memorandum ("Are Catholics the Chosen People? / No. Signed AFFA."), Vallee lays several "contact" cases side by side in order to argue that none of them requires an alien agency to explain it. He opens with the SPECTRA/Hoova contacts of Andrija Puharich and Uri Geller — begun on November 30, 1971, when Puharich first hypnotized Geller — and reads their content as pseudoscientific jargon that mirrors the two men's own preoccupations, most damningly in a confusion of light-years with units of time that Geller makes in exactly the way SPECTRA does. He then declassifies-by-quotation the 1959 AFFA affair, in which an intelligence officer he calls Commander Curtis produced automatic writing from an entity "living on the planet Uranus" and three officers reported seeing a disk over Washington at 2 P.M. on July 6, 1959 after being told to "Go to the window" — a case gone over three days later before Colonel Friend of Project Blue Book, six men from the CIA and a representative of Naval Intelligence, and written up in the archived memorandum that gives the chapter its title. Against these he sets his own 1972 automatic-writing experiments with a tested psychic subject and an entity calling itself "7171," and refuses to draw Puharich's conclusion: he regards such exchanges as contact with "a level of consciousness, possibly (but not necessarily) nonhuman" whose content is borrowed from the sitters, illustrated by an unpublished Flammarion report of a table at Victor Hugo's house tapping out an eight-line poem worthy of Hugo himself. The chapter's last section takes up the Elizabethan magus John Dee, whose Angel Uriel, crystal, forgotten conversations and revealed "Enochian" language Vallee treats as the same phenomenon four centuries earlier — with the sting that Donald Hanlon found AFFA to be an Enochian word meaning "empty." Two American cases of received writing close it out: the Order of the Initiates of Tibet, a Washington cult with five thousand followers by 1909, and Nell Heberling's 1966 volume of automatic writing, whose verses Vallee finds "much more relevant to our present time than the statements of AFFA."

## Core claims
- Vallee claims the SPECTRA messages "do not explain UFOs": they fail to give a consistent interpretation of the characteristics of sightings over the last twenty-five years, and the cult building around Puharich and Geller is "very similar to a number of other belief structures that have developed in the U.S. and other countries in recent years."
- He argues the alleged cosmic intelligence expresses "the same concern that was in the minds of the people gathered in that apartment in Israel" — that is, its content tracks the sitters' preoccupations rather than transcending them.
- He claims the absurdity is structural, not incidental: a power able to stop automobile engines could "simply freeze the motion of all the tanks in the Middle East" rather than merely announcing that Egypt has planned a war; and a cosmic intelligence able to manipulate time, space and human minds would not need "such a primitive device as a computer" to sort out the abilities of the earth's population.
- He claims that with such messages "we are not asked to comprehend here; we are asked simply to believe" — to suspend rational judgment and trust a "higher" power expressing itself through a few chosen people — and notes drily that "there is a precedent, too, for a young man from Israel who believes he will save the world."
- He claims Puharich and Geller have positioned themselves as initiates and modern-day prophets, quoting Puharich's own statement that a Prophet like Geller "is specifically created to serve as an intermediary between a 'divine' intelligence and man."
- He states as a general historical claim that throughout history "the minds of men have been manipulated by sources apparently external to their environment," always with the same two features: a promise of enlightenment/happiness/salvation, and a claim of origin with "higher" beings superior in technology and wisdom — usually of trivial historical importance, occasionally profound, and often producing small groups of priests or initiates.
- He claims the historical importance of such communications lies in their social effect: they "make possible the appearance and development of mass religious movements, inspiring entire civilizations to war and conquest, submitting them to the will of leaders who may not hesitate to sacrifice millions of lives."
- He claims that automatic writing is nothing new — "as old as the Old Testament, which was, after all, written through communication with such a 'higher source'" — and that the contents of that communication "still rule over the lives and conduct of hundreds of millions of human beings."
- He asserts the governing principle of the chapter: "nothing that is within the power of any one of the participants is beyond the power of the alleged entity," and that messages produced by automatic writing "could tap the knowledge, conscious or unconscious, of the participants."
- He claims such messages "have never revealed anything that was not already known or within the intellectual grasp of man" — which is precisely where the danger lies, because the phenomena serve as "a means for convincing both scientists and nonscientists of the opportunity for contact with a 'higher' source of information and power."
- He concedes that Geller and other "silent contactees" can produce phenomena "inadequately explained in conventional terms" — he does not dismiss the effects, only the interpretation.
- He states his own reading of his 7171 dialogues: not a higher entity, but "an instance of communication with a level of consciousness, possibly (but not necessarily) nonhuman," whose nature "may be understandable only in terms of a space-time structure more complex than what current physics places at our disposal."
- He argues on statistical grounds against human exceptionalism: of billions of thinking entities in the universe, "about half [would] be lower than man and half to be higher," so humanity — "after all, the only form of evolved intelligence we know (we still have much to learn about dolphins, however)" — should expect to be about average among galactic civilizations: "Why should there be anything exceptional about us?"
- He claims exposure to such phenomena "is hazardous to psychic health!" because the entity's words most often merely borrow expressions existing, possibly unconsciously, in the minds of those present.
- He warns fellow scientists explicitly: in the wake of Geller's fame a number of people, some of them scientists, are receiving SPECTRA-like messages and are "tempted to drop everything else to devote all their time to such a study" — he hopes the book's data "will serve as a note of caution."
- He claims his own approach is neither acceptance nor dismissal: rejecting the Geller phenomenon "as an aberration or a hoax" will be, he supposes, "the reaction of many scientists," whereas his own is to recognize "precisely in what respects it does not resemble what we already know of the UFO phenomenon," then place it alongside other possibly similar long-standing problems, periodically revising his own prejudices.
- On John Dee, he claims the episode belongs with the UFO material on four grounds: the entity appeared in dazzling brightness; it delivered a physical object; the trained magician was struck speechless instead of challenging the intruder with formulas; and the messages arrived in an unintelligible language requiring a translation device — a "bit of folklore" he says will recur with the Mormon prophets.
- He claims genuine psychic phenomena may nonetheless occur in automatic writing and even with the Ouija board, with communications coming from "minds of other people perhaps or (who knows?) a higher level of awareness that is attached to no human head" — entities "known to masquerade as departed souls, as great minds of antiquity, as denizens of other planets."
- He concedes that the psychic literature contains cases where entities guiding a hand or using a voice "seem to exhibit a level of knowledge beyond that of the medium" — but adds that "such a fact can seldom be proved," and that beautiful or superior automatic output proves nothing, since "it is often sufficient simply to unlock the unconscious mind to release a veritable stream of artistic energy"; using it productively is another matter, "as the adepts of the psychedelic movement have painfully discovered."
- He states of the AFFA memorandum that he believes "the facts to be authentic," while apologizing that he must disguise the names of the principals.

## Concepts
- **SPECTRA / Hoova** — The name of the "Intelligence in the Sky" said to communicate with Puharich and Geller; in the chapter-four epigraph SPECTRA is described by itself as "the name of a spacecraft which we use as you use a planet," stationed over the earth for the past eight hundred years. Messages arrived chiefly on magnetic tape, which is said to have disappeared after transcription.
- **Rhombus 4-D** — A second alleged source in the Puharich material, described as a computer; Vallee cites it saying "That was planned hundreds, hundreds of light-years ago, Andrija" as further evidence of the units confusion.
- **Automatic writing** — The technique common to Mrs Swan, Commander Curtis, Vallee's 7171 subject, Geller, and (in the seance form) the tapping table: the sitter relaxes the arm completely, holds a pencil, poses questions, and an "unknown force" or "outside force" takes control of the arm and writes the reply. Vallee says the technique is "as old as the Old Testament."
- **Tensor equations** — The form in which Geller's automatic writing arrives, "a kind of higher mathematics of which Uri has no concept." Vallee's counter-explanation: Puharich is familiar with tensors, and the writing taps participants' knowledge.
- **The light-year confusion** — Vallee's diagnostic test: SPECTRA, Rhombus 4-D and Geller all use "light-year" as a unit of *time*. A light-year is a unit of space — the distance a light signal covers in a year at 186,000 miles per second — so "Time obviously cannot be measured in light-years, Einstein or no Einstein."
- **AFFA** — The entity contacted by Mrs Swan and then by Commander Curtis, said to live on the planet Uranus; the signature appended to the 1959 answers ("No. Signed AFFA"). Also, per Donald Hanlon, an existing word in the Enochian language, meaning "empty."
- **CRILL, ALOMAR, PONNAR, ANKAR** — The other entities in Mrs Swan's file: CRILL from Jupiter, ALOMAR from Mercury, PONNAR also from Mercury, ANKAR from the constellation Centaurus.
- **OEEU — "Universal Association of Planets"** — The organization named in the contact file as responsible for the entities, running a project called EU or EREVZA, meaning "Earth," whose aim "was never specified." Vallee's footnote records that Emenegger's version of the document spells these OEEV and EUENZA.
- **7171** — The entity encountered in Vallee's own 1972 automatic-writing investigation, claiming connection with UFOs; described by the communicating source as "only one of the infinite number of entities through which I may speak," chosen at a particular time for contact with that specific medium.
- **The Invisible College** — The book's network of researchers; here Vallee notes that its members "have become interested in these patterns, but they have carefully reserved judgment on their nature."
- **"Silent contactees"** — Vallee's category for Geller and others who can produce phenomena inadequately explained in conventional terms; his point is that the phenomena function as a means of persuasion toward belief in a higher source, not as an end.
- **Borrowed content / "nothing beyond the participants"** — Vallee's operating rule for judging channelled material: the alleged entity never exceeds what is within the power or knowledge (conscious or unconscious) of someone present. The Hugo table and Geller's tensors are his two demonstrations.
- **Level of consciousness (possibly nonhuman)** — Vallee's cautious label for what his own dialogues contacted: not a "higher entity," but something whose manifestations in our language "may be childish or absurd," whose elements "may be borrowed entirely from our own brains and reflected upon us," and which "may be systematically misleading."
- **The uncanny power of superhuman words** — Vallee's name for the social force at stake: "There is an uncanny power in words that appear to issue from a superhuman source, and it would be dangerous to assume that this power cannot still move the world today" — visible in the charismatic movement, hundreds of occult groups, and UFO-related writings such as those inspired by SPECTRA and AFFA. Such material, he notes, "is variously received as a manifestation from God, as a sign of lunacy, or as a dangerous form of social deviance."
- **"Aunt Martha"** — Vallee's shorthand for the ordinary spiritualist case: whether the entity claims space origin or claims to be a dead relative, it usually does "nothing more than borrow words and expressions that exist as thoughts, possibly unconsciously, in the minds of the persons who attend the meeting."
- **Enochian language** — The complete language revealed to Dee and Kelly by the entities, "still regarded by contemporary occultists as a tool of the greatest power"; Crowley expressed his greatest magical invocations in it, and they must be pronounced in a certain way to be effective.
- **The convex crystal** — The object Angel Uriel handed Dee, to be used whenever he wished to converse with "the beings of another sphere," who would appear in it and "unveil to him all the secrets of futurity." Vallee notes that per Granger's *Biographical History* it was in fact a black stone or piece of polished coal.
- **The linguistic translation device** — Vallee's flagged folklore motif: messages delivered in a language not understandable at first, requiring a device to translate — which he says will recur "when we discuss the phenomena surrounding the Mormon prophets in later chapters."
- **Order of the Initiates of Tibet** — A Washington, D.C. cult of the early twentieth century that Vallee offers as "an interesting precursor to the various movements and cults that are active today"; its stated mission was to draw men from the study of material effects to "the study of cause, force, vibration and the unseen," an advancement that would "transmute the scientist into a Magus" and lead to contact with the inhabitants of other planets.
- **UMMO** — Named here only as a forward reference: a similar organization "whose reputation is rapidly spreading in Spain" and which "is attempting to influence human affairs," to be analyzed in Chapter Four.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **Epigraph — the Blue Book memorandum exchange (July 6, 1959, Washington, D.C.).** Printed verbatim at the chapter head: "*Are Catholics the Chosen People? / No. Signed AFFA. / Can we see a spaceship or flying saucer? / When would you like to see it? / Can we see it now? / Go to the window!*" — attributed to an exchange between three intelligence officers and the alleged space intelligence AFFA, from a Project Blue Book memorandum.
- **The first SPECTRA contact (November 30, 1971).** The series of contacts between Andrija Puharich — a physician, a graduate of Northwestern University — and the "Intelligence in the Sky" called SPECTRA or Hoova began when Puharich hypnotized Uri Geller for the first time. Under hypnosis Geller said he found himself in a cave in Cyprus and "came there for learning": "*I learn and learn, but I don't know who is doing the teaching.*" / "*What are you learning?*" / "*It is . . . about people who come from space. But I am not to talk about these things yet.*" (Vallee's source note: Andrija Puharich, *Uri*, New York: Doubleday, 1974, p. 94.)
- **Geller's childhood garden vision (Tel Aviv, December 25, 1949).** During the same hypnotic session Geller recalled that at the age of three, playing in a garden in Tel Aviv, he saw a large shining bowl-shaped light in the sky above him, and a huge, very bright shining figure in the garden. Quoted from Puharich: "The shining figure had no face that could be seen, only a radiant countenance. Uri gazed at this radiance in total hypnotism. Then he became aware of arms slowly moving out from the side of the body of the radiance. The arms were raised over the 'head' of the radiance, and then Uri saw that held between the hands was the sun. It was so blazing in its brightness that Uri passed out from the power of its rays, with the pain of blindness."
- **The voice in the room and the Egypt message.** At that point in the hypnotic session a voice was heard in the room, identifying itself as the source that had found Uri in the garden and had "programmed" him. Its message: "*We reveal ourselves because . . . plans for war have been made by Egypt.*" Vallee's analysis: the message reproduced exactly the concern already in the minds of the people in that apartment in Israel, and nobody present noticed that a power capable of stopping automobile engines could as easily "freeze the motion of all the tanks in the Middle East."
- **The pseudoscientific messages.** Vallee's specimens, with page cites to *Uri*: "There is a dematerialized aspect to your atoms, that we can use" (p. 166); "We need that mass landing like you people on earth call refueling, and charging up, like a huge plane of yours charges up with electricity. We do that through your barometric and cosmic layer around earth" (p. 179); and the longer block: "We have passed our souls, bodies and minds into computors [sic] and moved several of millions of light-years backward toward your time and dimension. In due time we shall receive all material coming back to our main center which is zoned into a different dimension than yours. This different dimension lies beyond the so-called star, and so-called god, so-called planet that you call the sun. It is millions of light-years backward into the dieshold [?] of the ages." [The "[?]" after *dieshold* is Vallee's own, printed in the source — the word is unrecovered in the book itself, not an OCR artifact.]
- **The Keech connection.** Vallee reports that Puharich's first contact with alleged UFO entities was "through the same man who was a leader in Mrs Keech's sect!" — the man the sociologists who wrote *When Prophecy Fails* called Dr Armstrong. This closes the forward-link planted in chapter 2.
- **Vallee's personal position on the principals.** "I have met Dr Puharich, whom I regard as an intensely sincere man, and I think highly of Geller's talents." Their experiences cannot be brushed aside "with simple rejections"; what should be done is to sort out the implications of "the extremely confusing set of events they claim to have observed."
- **SPECTRA's flattery of Puharich.** "It is a shame that for such a brilliant mind we cannot contact you directly. Maybe in time we shall be able to contact you directly," and earlier, "Our computers studied everyone on earth. You were noticed for your abilities as the perfect man for this mission." Vallee's objection is the computer itself: why would an intelligence capable of manipulating time and space at will use so primitive a device to sort the earth's population?
- **The mass-landing predictions.** Uri and Puharich receive messages predicting mass landings — "perhaps visible only to themselves," Vallee adds. In discussions he has had with them, they ignored that similar predictions have been made to others all over the world, allegedly from other cosmic sources, "each one presenting a consistent philosophy, each one telling obvious falsehoods and uttering sheer jargon most of the time."
- **Puharich as designated prophet.** Three quotations Vallee stacks to show the initiate structure: Puharich (p. 127) — "I believe that a Prophet, a Uri Geller . . . is specifically created to serve as an intermediary between a 'divine' intelligence and man"; the communication to Puharich — "In the last twenty-four hours you have passed the test successfully. This is the last time ever in your life that you will have to be tested . . . You must tell the world everything about us . . ."; and Puharich's own summary — "The relationship . . . between these superior beings and Uri and me continues to grow deeper and mutually more meaningful." Vallee's conclusion: "In other words, Puharich and Uri are initiates, modern-day prophets."
- **THE AFFA CASE — the approach (July 1959).** A gentleman Vallee calls **Mr Talman** had an experience on July 6, 1959 that caused him to contact **Colonel Friend**, then head of Project Blue Book, about his personal experiences with psychic phenomena in connection with UFOs. Vallee: "I believe the facts to be authentic, and I am sorry that I must disguise the names of the principals." Footnote: the document has been quoted extensively in Robert Emenegger's *UFOs, Past, Present and Future*, with the permission of the institutions involved, "thus the facts contained in the memorandum can now be discussed publicly."
- **The meeting (three days later).** Talman and Friend met in the presence of **six men from the Central Intelligence Agency and a representative of the office of Naval Intelligence** and went over the entire case. It opened with a briefing on official government involvement in the study of UFOs; several local cases were reviewed, plus highly publicized American sightings with photographic evidence — specifically **the Mariana case of August 1950 and the Tremonton sighting of July 1952**. Several members of the group had had contact with the unit that examined the pictures. Vallee notes this evidence "was not the main point of the meeting."
- **Mrs Swan and her method.** For the last five years Talman's organization had been studying a woman, **Mrs Swan**, who claimed psychic contact with space entities. Her technique: ask specific questions, relax the body completely, hold a pencil and await an answer, whereupon an "unknown force" takes control of her arm and writes the reply. Her abilities "had been studied in depth by the Canadians," and the U.S. armed forces also had a complete file on her. Crucially, one of the investigators, an intelligence officer named **Curtis**, had learned her technique and acquired her ability.
- **Curtis's visit to Mrs Swan (second half of June 1959).** Curtis flew with another officer to visit her, to observe the contact and put questions through the "channel." At the end of the session she suggested the commander try to make contact himself; he attempted it and was unsuccessful.
- **The successful contact in Washington.** Back in Washington, Curtis discussed the case with Talman and another colleague and, at their repeated suggestion, tried again — this time apparently succeeding, with the source identified as **AFFA, living on the planet Uranus**. Curtis wrote down the questions as they were posed by Talman and another individual, relaxing his arm completely and allowing it to be controlled by the "outside force" that wrote the answers; he observed that he was "the object of a very intense physical tension during the message transmission."
- **The AFFA question-and-answer session.** "'Do you favor a particular Government, religion, group or race?' 'No. Signed AFFA.' / 'Will there be a third world war?' 'No. Signed AFFA.' / 'Are Catholics the Chosen People?' 'No. Signed AFFA.' / 'Can we see a spaceship or flying saucer?' 'When would you like to see it?' / 'Can we see it now?' 'Go to the window.'"
- **The window and the verbal "Yes!"** The three men rushed to the window and saw nothing. Coming back to the table, Curtis asked, "Are we looking in the right direction?" The response did not come through his arm but as a word Curtis pronounced verbally: "Yes!"
- **The Washington disk (2 P.M., July 6, 1959).** The three men then saw a disk-shaped object fly over Washington, "described as having clear edges and a dark center." They called the radar center and were told that, **for an unknown reason, the radar return was blocked in the sector they indicated**. Vallee notes that one of the earlier questions to AFFA had elicited a response dealing with the saucers' difficulty in penetrating the earth's radar network undetected.
- **The re-attempt before Colonel Friend.** Asked by Friend to repeat the contact as he had done three days earlier, Curtis tried and failed. He got a few replies, but they indicated that "the moment was not favorable." AFFA did state that there was no objection to the members of the group attending the meeting.
- **The character testimony.** Talman and all present stated for the record that they had known Curtis for many years and that he had always been "competent, quiet, calm, and very conservative." All thought the experience extremely significant — not only because of his background, but because of the testimony of **two other senior officers who had been with him when the flying saucer had appeared over the building**.
- **The contact file (the next day).** Curtis and Friend went to the office where the file was kept. It showed Mrs Swan had communicated with AFFA from Uranus, CRILL from Jupiter, ALOMAR from Mercury, PONNAR also from Mercury, and ANKAR from the constellation Centaurus. What impressed the investigators was that "the woman had been able to answer questions that appeared to be beyond her education and technical knowledge." What disappointed them: only vague descriptions of the propulsion mechanism, some allusions to the construction material, "but nothing specific was revealed." Curtis indicated the Canadians had pursued this particular aspect quite deeply.
- **OEEU and project EREVZA.** The file described an organization called OEEU, "Universal Association of Planets," running a project called EU or EREVZA, meaning "Earth," whose aim "was never specified." Vallee's footnote: his text is a copy of the original memorandum, and spellings differ from Emenegger's book — OEEV for OEEU, EUENZA for EREVZA. He immediately forward-links to UMMO in Chapter Four.
- **The memorandum's own verdict.** The memo closes with the writer's evaluation of personal attitudes: the leader of the interaction was "a man with a fine analytic mind," motivated only by his respect for Curtis; Curtis was "a very stable man" who "occupies — or occupied! — a responsible position" (Vallee notes the tongue-in-cheek), embarrassed at first at being the center of attention but later relaxed; Talman was "a man with both feet firmly on the ground." Final line: "*There is no question that the object seen over Washington on that day of July, 1959, was a flying saucer.*"
- **VALLEE'S OWN EXPERIMENT — the 7171 contacts (from 1972).** Vallee began his own investigation into automatic writing in 1972 and observed several lengthy exchanges between one of his subjects and an entity calling itself "7171," which claimed to be connected with UFOs.
- **The subject's tested psychic abilities.** The subject was a man whose abilities Vallee "personally tested": in successive experiments involving **small objects hidden in plastic boxes**, he produced drawings of the targets "with considerable accuracy"; other tests involved **the use of a computer in the prediction of the state of a random number generator**. For several years the man had occasionally felt the urge to write what at first seemed meaningless messages; when questions were asked of the "entity," the messages became more focused and soon centered on the UFO phenomenon. He then approached Vallee and agreed that more systematic testing was in order; together they tried to refine the technique and "to identify those components of the interaction that might be coming from the channel's own subconscious mind."
- **The 7171 dialogue (verbatim, medium's replies in capitals in the source).** *Can you experience the future?* — YES. *Why are you communicating?* — "THE ANSWER TO THIS I DO NOT FULLY KNOW MYSELF. I KNOW ONLY THAT IT IS CONSISTENT WITH THOSE HIGHER THAN I." *What are UFOs a symbol of?* — "THEY ARE A SYMBOL OF NOTHING IN THE SENSE THAT YOU MEAN. THEY HAVE A VALIDITY INDEPENDENT OF SYMBOLISM." *Why are there men like us in UFOs?* — "BECAUSE, FOR VERY DEEP REASONS, THE FORM OF MAN IS A UNIVERSAL CONSTANT." *Why do they come here?* — "TO HARMONIZE THIS WORLD WITH THE REST OF THE UNIVERSE." *Who is 7171?* — "HE IS ONLY ONE OF THE INFINITE NUMBER OF ENTITIES THROUGH WHICH I MAY SPEAK." *Does he have special significance?* — "ONLY IN THE SENSE THAT HE WAS CHOSEN AT A PARTICULAR TIME FOR CONTACT WITH XXX" (the medium's name, withheld). *Can you serve as a channel to an entity that is higher than yourself?* — "THERE IS NONE HIGHER THAN MYSELF." Vallee then catches the contradiction — "You lied when you said that you were the supreme source of life, then. Earlier you mentioned 'those higher than I.'" — and gets: "YES. I AM BOTH. THE APPLE IS BORN OF THE TREE, BUT IS ALSO THE SOURCE OF ALL APPLE TREES." *Can you produce physical effects that we can recognize (light, sound)?* — "WHEN THE TIME IS RIGHT SUCH THINGS WILL OCCUR." Vallee's stated point: not that such communications contain new knowledge, but that "taken in the context of the 'contact' with AFFA, they throw some light on the type of phenomena the men in Washington were playing with."
- **Vallee's refusal to become Puharich.** He poses the test on himself — should he now conclude he is in contact with a source of immense wisdom, and assume "the responsibility to reveal to the world the existence of this higher power"? He answers no, while explicitly not doubting the truthfulness of the man who acted as channel, "and whom I am happy to have as a friend."
- **Footnote 4 — the French table-tapping panic.** Catholic priests at the end of the nineteenth century discouraged many French families from experimenting with tables that tipped and tapped uncanny messages from Beyond. Vallee's comment: "The facts were undeniable, and the evidence of the phenomena was as good, if not better, than the evidence for flying saucers. In many cases the effects were the same."
- **The units-of-measurement audit.** Vallee's demonstration that SPECTRA's mind is Geller's and Puharich's: SPECTRA says "millions of light-years backwards into the dieshold [?] of the ages" (p. 185); Rhombus 4-D says "That was planned hundreds, hundreds of light-years ago, Andrija"; SPECTRA also speaks of "many billions of light-years ahead of time." Against this he sets Geller's own words **at his first interview with scientists from the Stanford Research Institute**: "You see, they are computers, which were fed millions of light-years in the future." His jibe: measuring time in light-years "would be like saying that Uri Geller weighs twenty inches and that it took Puharich 50.7 kilogauss to write his book." Conclusion: "we should consider the possibility that we are dealing with a phenomenon that uses, or emanates from, the brains of Geller and Puharich."
- **The ventriloquist hypothesis and its limit.** The unit confusion raises the thought that Uri "may be a clever ventriloquist who has been fooling Puharich for years, simulating the voice of SPECTRA." Vallee blocks it: Puharich sometimes receives the messages when Uri is away, "and it is difficult to believe that even a clever magician would be able to plant loudspeakers in the walls wherever Dr Puharich goes."
- **THE FLAMMARION / VICTOR HUGO SEANCE (reported 1899) — "previously unpublished."** Vallee reports that in 1899 the great French astronomer **Camille Flammarion**, also a keen psychic investigator, described having once attended a spirit seance at the home of the poet **Victor Hugo**, with Hugo's wife and brother and three other distinguished guests, **Messrs Vacquerie, Guerin, and Allix**. Seated around a table, they evoked a spirit who identified himself as **the ghost of Molière**. Everything went fine until someone asked the entity about the problem of death — at which point Molière was replaced by another entity, who took over the tapping and answered "in a magnificent poem of eight lines," signing itself "**The Shadow of the Sepulchre**." Flammarion "was so shaken that he did not publish this fact, reporting it only in a manuscript letter to **Paul Souday**, a journalist friend whose advice he was asking."
- **The poem (Vallee's own English rendering).** "Spirit, eager to know the secret of the night / Holding between your hands the terrestrial light, / You would come, stealthily, among shadows of gloom, / Running a searching hook through this enormous tomb! / Go back to your silence, and extinguish your flame, / Go back into this night out of which you came; / No living eye eternal books has read / Over the shoulders of the Dead!" - **What the seance proves, for Vallee.** "The good faith of the experimenters can hardly be doubted." The intriguing point is that the table generated, "through what must have been an excruciatingly long series of taps, one of the finest pieces of poetry that Victor Hugo could have written!" After such a feat, Vallee writes, he does not doubt that "through a similar mechanism Uri Geller can sit down in Puharich's presence and cover page after page with Hamiltonian tensors." Hence: "If a simple table can use the mind of Victor Hugo, and tease him by generating such a poem, why couldn't Geller 'borrow' some mathematical symbols from the mind of Puharich? I see nothing here that requires the intervention of an alien agency." He attributes the table phenomena to "unconscious and mental process among the attending 'spiritualists'" and applies the same explanation to Puharich and Geller: "Whatever force they are contacting in this fashion shows no evidence of being higher than man."
- **THE JOHN DEE CASE — the man.** Opening with Charles Mackay (*Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds*, New York: Noonday Press, 1967, p. 170): "John Dee and Edward Kelly claim to be mentioned together, having so long been associated in the same pursuits, and undergone so many strange vicissitudes in each other's society" — on which Vallee remarks, "One might wonder whether a future historian will not have the same thing to say about Dr Puharich and Uri Geller." Dee: born in London in **1527**, entered Cambridge at fifteen, developed a keen interest in the occult and found himself under pressure to leave; went to **Louvain** in the Low Countries and studied with the disciples of **Cornelius Agrippa**; returned to England in **1551** and obtained through friends a pension from **King Edward VI**; became an astrologer, enjoyed the favor of **Queen Elizabeth**, sought the elixir of life and the philosopher's stone; served as an intelligence agent to Elizabeth and **signed his reports from abroad with the now-famous number 007**.
- **The visitation (November 1581).** Dee was working in his museum, "whose window faced west," when a dazzling light appeared. In the middle of the light was the form of an entity that introduced itself as **Angel Uriel**. Dee was speechless. The angel smiled and gave him an object — a convex crystal — telling him to use it whenever he wished to converse with the beings of another sphere; he had only to gaze into it and they would appear and "unveil to him all the secrets of futurity."
- **Two discoveries in using the crystal.** First, Dee had to concentrate all of his awareness into the crystal — "which was in fact a black stone, or piece of polished coal, according to an account published in Granger's *Biographical History*" — in order to see the beings. Second, **he could never remember his conversations with them**, "a fact which is not unlike the mysterious disappearance of Dr Puharich's tapes when he records the voice of SPECTRA."
- **Kelly and the Enochian sessions.** To record the communications Dee confided the secret of the stone to his assistant **Edward Kelly**. On **December 2, 1581** the first of many sessions took place; "the complete text of these communications is now kept among the **Harleian manuscripts at the British Museum**." Among the revelations was a complete language, **Enochian**, still regarded by contemporary occultists as a tool of the greatest power — **Aleister Crowley** expressed his greatest magical invocations in it, and they are still used today by his many disciples.
- **Donald Hanlon's discovery — AFFA means "empty."** Vallee calls this "the most interesting discovery in the entire episode": his friend **Donald Hanlon** pointed out that the expression AFFA exists as a word in the Enochian language, meaning "empty." Vallee's punchline: "When the three intelligence officers were being told in 1959 that World War III would not take place, and when they were shown a flying saucer over the capital of their country, they were in direct communication with . . . emptiness!"
- **The Order of the Initiates of Tibet (Washington, D.C., c. 1900–1909).** A cult "similar to that of Mrs Keech," presided over by **Miss Marsland**, daughter of **George Marsland**, founder of the American Bankers' Association. By **1909 it had five thousand followers**, among them prominent members of the social and diplomatic set. Miss Marsland received her teachings from a mysterious source in Paris — allegedly emanating from Tibet, written in Sanskrit, translated into French by "the high initiate in Paris." Its stated mission: to "draw men from the study of material effects which has so far occupied the exclusive attention of scientists, and to direct them to the study of cause, force, vibration and the unseen," an advancement that would "transmute the scientist into a Magus" and lead to contact with the inhabitants of other planets. Vallee's source note: "Washington's Most Curious Cult — Under the Leadership of a Woman," *Washington Post*, October 31, 1909.
- **Nell Heberling, *The Golden Message from Crellritus* (1966).** A volume of automatic writing that Vallee finds "much more relevant to our present time than the statements of AFFA." ## Thread
Chapter 2 ended by naming absurdity as "the third coverup"; chapter 3 is the demonstration, and it is the book's most deflationary chapter. Vallee takes the chapter's three headline contact dossiers — a celebrity psychic's cosmic intelligence, an archived Blue Book memorandum whose facts he says he believes "to be authentic" (a contact and a same-day disk sighting, gone over three days later before CIA and Naval Intelligence men), and his own controlled experiment with a subject who passes hidden-target tests — and shows that in each case the "higher source" says nothing that the humans in the room did not already know, and repeats their errors when they make them. The light-year audit is the chapter's forensic centerpiece and its method in miniature: not "is this real?" but "whose mind is this?" The Flammarion/Hugo seance supplies the historical control (a table producing Hugo-grade verse in Hugo's own house), and John Dee supplies the deep-time control (dazzling light, gifted crystal, forgotten conversations, an untranslatable revealed language) — establishing the book's recurring argument that the UFO contact experience is a modern instance of a very old form, not a novel encounter with extraterrestrials. Crucially, Vallee does not conclude that nothing is happening: he keeps the phenomena ("inadequately explained in conventional terms") while stripping the interpretation, and reframes the object of study as *how belief is born* — the manufacture of prophets, sects, and mass movements out of words that appear to come from above. That reframing is what makes the chapter load-bearing: the material AFFA and SPECTRA can no longer explain is handed forward to the UMMO analysis, to the religious apparitions, and ultimately to the control-system hypothesis.
