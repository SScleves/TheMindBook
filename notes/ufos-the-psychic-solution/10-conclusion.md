# Conclusion

## In one paragraph
Vallee closes the book not with a theory of what UFOs are but with a claim about what belief in them is doing: civilizations periodically throw up "some wonderful untruth around which magnificent energy crystallizes," and the expectation of visitors from outer space is the current one. He takes his interpretive key from a definition of myth given to him by the British scholar Gordon Creighton during a drive through London in the strike-and-energy-crisis winter of 1973 — a myth is not an untruth but "that which is TRUER THAN TRUTH" — and argues that the extraterrestrial reading of flying saucers may be false and still become "truer than true" long enough to change certain things irreversibly. He points to Washington rumors, a lecturer's Bicentennial disclosure prediction, Puharich's predicted mass landing, and the UMMO, AFFA and Mrs Keech episodes as signs that responsible, sincere people are now participating in the myth-making while skepticism erodes and the evidence keeps arriving "so constructed as to elude classical analysis." The chapter then delivers two renunciations: Vallee says he no longer believes the security organizations were quietly running a competent investigation (the documents he has since seen show "incoherent restlessness in every country"), and he no longer believes science will seize paranormal phenomena as the chance to redefine human dignity. The last paragraph relocates the answer: what is threatened is not only freedom but "a certain concept of humanity," and the key to the psychic crisis lies neither in science nor in a secret Washington file but "within ourselves."

## Core claims
- Vallee claims that "time and again in the history of civilizations, there arises some wonderful untruth around which magnificent energy crystallizes, and great deeds are done," and that such a time has come again: "It has become very important for large numbers of people to expect visitors from outer space."
- He adopts Gordon Creighton's definition of myth — not an untruth, but "that which is TRUER THAN TRUTH" — as the thing that clarifies "the confusion of many approaches to the contemporary problems of UFOs."
- He argues the extraterrestrial interpretation can be factually false and socially decisive at once: "It may not be true that flying saucers represent visits from outer space. But if large enough numbers believe it, then in some sense it will become truer than true, long enough for certain things to change irreversibly."
- He reports — explicitly as gossip and rumor, not as established fact — that well-informed Washington sources expect UFOs to be increasingly prominent in coming years, and that persistent rumors hold that highly placed U.S. government officials have long had evidence that another form of intelligence was contacting us. His comment: "The stage is set for another UMMO."
- He claims a climate has slowly been created in which a much larger number of people participate in the myth-making; belief is reinforced by successive waves of sightings and skepticism is eroded. Statements that would not have been taken seriously ten years ago are today "eagerly listened to, evoking fear or passion in their audiences," and tomorrow, he says, some higher officials may join the ranks of the believers.
- He notes that the UMMO affair, the case of AFFA and the predictions of Mrs Keech "have involved sincere people, holding responsible positions" — i.e., that these belief episodes are not confined to the marginal or the credulous.
- He claims the cases are giving more and more evidence of the reality of the UFOs, "but this evidence is so constructed as to elude classical analysis by scientists," and offers three speculative reasons in the form of "perhaps": the UFOs may not behave according to our laws of causality; their time may flow differently from ours; their logic may be "a meta-logic."
- He argues that "the observable change is an increasing willingness to believe in extraterrestrial life," and that attitudes on this subject among scientists, the media and the public "have indeed totally changed in twenty years."
- He sets two readings of that change against each other and prefers the second: it can be rationalized as the product of radio astronomy's progress and "the pioneering work of a few biologists," or it can be recognized as "the result of a shifting of our mythological structure, the human learning curve bending toward a new cosmic behavior."
- He predicts that when this irreversible learning is achieved the UFO phenomenon "may go away entirely," or may "assume some suitable representation on a human scale" — "The angels may land downtown."
- He renounces a long-held private belief: that the organizations responsible for collective security were secretly conducting "a large-scale, quiet, and competent investigation" into the paranormal phenomena manifesting around us. He makes the book's own publication the proof of the change: "If I still believed it today I would not be publishing this book."
- He recounts having rationalized three disappointments into that belief — Blue Book's blunders as a deliberate front, the missing files and photographs as evidence someone better was doing the real work, worldwide Air Force reporting as confirmation of the coverup theory — and says he no longer defers: "Today I am no longer young, and I have lost my unquestioning trust of official wisdom in matters of advanced research."
- He claims that the documents he has recently examined, obtained through several members of the Invisible College and friends in Europe, show the opposite of quiet official research: "a picture of incoherent restlessness in every country," with scientists and military men meeting behind closed doors to "swap scary stories, while the real phenomena go on, unstudied, unconcerned, UNIDENTIFIED!"
- He renounces a second long-held belief — that science would gradually realize the importance of paranormal phenomena as an opportunity to expand its theories of the world, which he had thought was "our only chance to redefine human dignity in the world to come." His verdict: "I now believe differently."
- His closing claim: what is now in danger is "not simply our freedom" but "a certain concept of humanity"; neither science nor "some secret file in Washington" holds the key to this psychic crisis; "The solution lies where it has always been: within ourselves. We can reach it any time we want."

## Concepts
- **wonderful untruth** — Vallee's opening term for a recurring historical pattern: an untruth around which "magnificent energy crystallizes, and great deeds are done." He assigns the expectation of visitors from outer space that role in the present.
- **myth as "truer than truth"** — Creighton's definition, quoted by Vallee: people mistakenly believe that a myth is an untruth; "A myth is that which is TRUER THAN TRUTH." Vallee presents it as the definition that clarifies the confusion of competing approaches to the UFO problem.
- **truer than true** — Vallee's operational corollary: a belief that is not factually correct becomes effectively "truer than true" when enough people hold it — for long enough that certain things change irreversibly.
- **participation in the myth-making** — The process Vallee says is under way: a climate has been created in which a much larger number of people now take part in making the myth, with belief reinforced by successive waves of sightings and skepticism eroded.
- **another UMMO** — Vallee's shorthand, in the phrase "The stage is set for another UMMO," for a repeat of that engineered belief episode on a larger, more official stage. He invokes UMMO, AFFA and Mrs Keech here by name only, without re-describing them (they are treated in earlier chapters).
- **evidence that eludes classical analysis** — Vallee's characterization of the accumulating case material: it gives more and more evidence of the reality of the UFOs, yet "is so constructed as to elude classical analysis by scientists."
- **meta-logic** — The third of Vallee's three "perhaps" explanations for that elusiveness (after non-causal behavior and a differently flowing time): the phenomenon's logic may be a meta-logic rather than ours.
- **shifting mythological structure / the human learning curve** — Vallee's preferred reading of the twenty-year change in attitudes toward extraterrestrial life: not the achievement of radio astronomy and a few biologists, but "a shifting of our mythological structure, the human learning curve bending toward a new cosmic behavior."
- **irreversible learning** — The completion state of that curve. Vallee says that once it is achieved the UFO phenomenon may vanish entirely, or take "some suitable representation on a human scale."
- **"the angels may land downtown"** — Vallee's image for that post-learning, human-scale representation of the phenomenon.
- **the coverup theory** — The belief Vallee says he once held and repeatedly rationalized his disappointments into: that Blue Book was a front and that somebody, somewhere, was seriously gathering and studying the real evidence. He states he no longer holds it.
- **incoherent restlessness** — What Vallee says the documents actually reveal in place of quiet government research: restlessness in every country, closed-door meetings, scientists and military men swapping scary stories while the phenomena go unstudied.
- **the Invisible College** — In this closing unit the term appears only as the network whose members, together with other friends in Europe, gave Vallee access to the many documents of the kind he has quoted; the book defines the term earlier (Introduction).
- **the psychic crisis** — Vallee's name in the final paragraph for what is happening; he states that it is "no longer to science that we must turn" to understand its nature and find its key.
- **a certain concept of humanity** — What Vallee says is at stake beyond freedom itself in the closing paragraph.
- **the solution within ourselves** — The book's last claim, and the referent of its UK title: "The solution lies where it has always been: within ourselves. We can reach it any time we want."

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **Gordon Creighton's definition of myth (London, winter 1973)** — Vallee reports that while discussing Uri Geller's abilities with the British scholar Gordon Creighton, driving through the midst of London in the winter of 1973 — "a winter plagued by strikes and the energy crisis" — Creighton gave him a definition of myth: "People mistakenly believe," he said, "that a myth is an untruth. But myth is not that. A myth is that which is TRUER THAN TRUTH." Vallee says it clarified the confusion of many approaches to the contemporary problems of UFOs; it becomes the organizing idea of the Conclusion.
- **The Washington rumors** — Vallee reports that "some of the best informed sources of gossip in Washington" are convinced UFOs will be increasingly prominent in coming years, and that there are persistent rumors that highly placed officials in the U.S. government have long had evidence that another form of intelligence was contacting us. He names no source and cites no document, presenting the material explicitly as gossip and rumor; his own gloss is that "the stage is set for another UMMO."
- **The Bicentennial disclosure prediction** — An unnamed "former aerospace engineer turned UFO lecturer" believes that on the occasion of the Bicentennial the government will announce that there is life on Mars, and that a meeting between U.S. representatives and extraterrestrials is imminent. Vallee gives it as a specimen of "an interesting path" these people are going down. (No year is stated in the text.)
- **Puharich's predicted mass landing** — Vallee notes that Puharich "has already traveled [that path] with enthusiasm" and predicts a mass landing. His point is the changed reception, not the content: ten years ago such statements would not have been taken seriously, "but today they are eagerly listened to, evoking fear or passion in their audiences," and "tomorrow some higher officials may join the ranks of the believers."
- **UMMO, AFFA and Mrs Keech as respectable belief episodes** — Vallee cites the UMMO affair, the case of AFFA, and the predictions of Mrs Keech (identified only by the parenthetical "of *When Prophecy Fails*") in a single sentence, on the single point that they "have involved sincere people, holding responsible positions." No details of the three episodes are repeated here; the outcome he draws is that a climate has been created in which many more people participate in the myth-making.
- **Project Blue Book — three rationalizations he now disowns** — Vallee lays out his own history of self-persuasion. (1) Seeing "the blunders of Project Blue Book and its biased conclusions," he imagined the project was "only a front to keep civilian scientists from poking their inquisitive noses into a very serious matter that was regarded in high places as an important item." (2) Noticing that certain files had disappeared from the Blue Book archives and that the best photographic evidence was missing, he "rationalized my disappointment into the reassuring thought that somebody, somewhere, was gathering this evidence and studying it in earnest." (3) Seeing that Air Force bases around the world were sending copies of all their UFO information to government organizations, he took it as confirmation of the coverup theory — "and I did not think it was my role, as an inexperienced scientist, to challenge it." Outcome, in his words: "Today I am no longer young, and I have lost my unquestioning trust of official wisdom in matters of advanced research."
- **The documents obtained through the Invisible College** — Vallee reports that in recent years, thanks to several members of the Invisible College and to other friends in Europe, he has had the chance to examine "many documents like the ones I have quoted" (i.e., of the kind reproduced earlier in the book). Result: "Far from revealing government authorities engaged in quiet research, they give a picture of incoherent restlessness in every country. Meeting behind closed doors, scientists and military men swap scary stories, while the real phenomena go on, unstudied, unconcerned, UNIDENTIFIED!" This is the evidentiary basis he gives for abandoning the coverup theory.

## Thread
This is the book's closing move, and it is a reversal rather than a summary. The preceding chapter, "The Control System," had argued that UFO sightings are not visitations but the operation of a control system conditioning human consciousness; the Conclusion converts that mechanism into a statement about myth, using Creighton's "truer than truth" to make the ET interpretation's factual status irrelevant to its historical effect — the myth does the work whether or not the saucers are craft from another planet. Everything the book has assembled is then re-read as symptom rather than proof: the UMMO and AFFA files, Mrs Keech's prophecies, Puharich's and Geller's cosmic sources, the waves of sightings, the changed attitudes of scientists and media are all evidence of a mythological structure shifting, of a learning curve bending toward "a new cosmic behavior." The chapter also settles the book's posture toward officialdom and toward science with two explicit renunciations — no competent secret investigation exists (the documents show "incoherent restlessness in every country"), and science will not be the discipline that redeems the paranormal — which retroactively lowers the status of the coverup material earlier chapters spent so much space on: it documents confusion, not custody. Note that the renunciation is made load-bearing for the book's existence ("If I still believed it today I would not be publishing this book"), so the Conclusion is where Vallee's authorial motive is stated most plainly. Finally, it supplies the referent of the UK title: on this reading, "the psychic solution" is not a solution to the UFO puzzle but a solution located inside the human being, available "any time we want" — a placement of the answer that the US title, *The Invisible College*, does not advertise.
