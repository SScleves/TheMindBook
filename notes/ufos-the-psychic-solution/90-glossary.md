# Glossary

All definitions below are Jacques Vallee's own (or those he adopts and attributes), drawn from the chapter notes; none are "corrected" toward outside knowledge, and none are endorsed as fact. **Chapter legend:** pointers use the note chapter numbers — **0** = Introduction; **1** = The Psychic Component; **2** = The Triple Coverup; **3** = Memorandum for Archives; **4** = The Function of OEMII; **5** = The Confrontation; **6** = The Winged Disk; **7** = A Morphology of Miracles; **8** = Who Are You, Mr Geller?; **9** = The Control System; **10** = Conclusion.

- **7171** — The entity encountered in Vallee's own automatic-writing investigation, begun in 1972 with a subject whose psychic abilities he had personally tested; it claimed connection with UFOs and described itself as "only one of the infinite number of entities through which I may speak." Vallee refuses Puharich's conclusion and reads the dialogue as "an instance of communication with a level of consciousness, possibly (but not necessarily) nonhuman." Chapters: 3, 8. Related: AFFA, automatic writing, level of consciousness, borrowed content, hidden-target drawing tests.
- **The 1897 airship** — The nineteenth-century American wave whose "occupants" supplied culture-appropriate explanations of their own origin ("We are from Kansas"; "We are from ANYWHERE … but we'll be in Cuba tomorrow"); Vallee's historical exhibit for the proposition that the phenomenon has always "received (or provided) its own explanation within the framework of each culture," and, per his astronomer correspondent, "a teasing manifestation in form somewhat ahead of its time, technically." Chapters: 1, 8. Related: five propositions, animal abduction / Hamilton's cow, diversionary explanation.
- **Absurdity / absurd elements / absurdity as design** — The feeling produced by "the superimposition of psychic effects — psychokinesis, telepathy, travel to a different space-time frame — to the array of physical descriptions"; Vallee treats it not as grounds for dismissal but as the entry point to a general framework, then as an operating principle that "causes a rejection of the story by the upper layers of the target society and an absorption at a deep unconscious level of the symbols conveyed by the encounter." In the miracle material it reappears as the "mixture of seriousness and absurdity" (Fatima's prophecy, Bernadette's meaningless instructions) and as absurdity "carefully designed to misguide our probing minds." Chapters: 0, 1, 2, 5, 7, 9. Related: meta-logic, third coverup, ring of absurdity, image beyond the belief structure, revelation as isolation.
- **AFFA** — The entity contacted by automatic writing by Mrs Swan and then by the intelligence officer Vallee calls Commander Curtis, said to live on the planet Uranus and signing its answers "No. Signed AFFA"; minutes after answering "Go to the window," three officers reported a disk over Washington on July 6, 1959. Donald Hanlon later pointed out that AFFA exists as a word in the Enochian language meaning "empty" — so the officers "were in direct communication with . . . emptiness!" Chapters: 3, 4, 8, 10. Related: Memorandum for Archives, Mrs Swan's entities, Enochian language, OEEU, automatic writing, 7171.
- **Ahuramazda** — Sir E. A. Wallis Budge's identification of the god emerging from the winged disk on Vallee's Figure A: "Ahuramazda or some Assyrian god." Chapters: 6. Related: winged disk, "divine beings."
- **Aliens among us (rumors of)** — The consistent rumors Vallee says he has been exposed to over ten years of investigation, of contact between humans and alleged visitors residing on earth — some descriptions extremely detailed, some involving scientists as witnesses, some of the humans said eventually to have disappeared. He records them as playing a role "in the unfolding of the total myth" rather than as authenticated cases. Chapters: 9. Related: spectrum of contact experience, folklore rich in new images.
- **Angel hair** — The shiny white "petals" or "falling flowers" seen dropping from the sky at Fatima, which shrink as they approach and melt away on contact; Vallee identifies it as the phenomenon "so consistently reported after the passage of a UFO, and sometimes interpreted as an ionization effect." Chapters: 7. Related: morphology of miracles, Table 2.
- **The Angel of Peace** — The "youth of admirable beauty" who appeared three times during 1916 to Lucia and her two cousins, identifying himself with that phrase, teaching prayers, demanding sacrifices and finally giving them Communion; the culmination of the two-year sequence of sightings that preceded the apparitions of the Lady. Chapters: 7. Related: beam of light, angels and djinns, strangeness category 6.
- **Angel Uriel / the convex crystal** — The entity that appeared to John Dee in November 1581 in a dazzling light in his west-facing museum and handed him a convex crystal — "in fact a black stone, or piece of polished coal, according to an account published in Granger's *Biographical History*" — to be used whenever he wished to converse with "the beings of another sphere," who would "unveil to him all the secrets of futurity." Chapters: 3. Related: Enochian language, linguistic translation device, vanishing tapes.
- **Angels and djinns** — Beings about whom, Vallee observes, the official theologies are notably reticent ("Official Christian theology does not have much to say about the angels, just as official Muslim theology remains discreet about the djinns"), while the flying-disk symbol remains "consistently associated with the angels" in early Christian art. In many UFO stories of the olden days witnesses thought they had seen angels from God and never reported it; others thought they had seen devils — "The difference may be very small." Chapters: 6, 7. Related: the Sophia document, winged disk, "the angels may land downtown."
- **"The angels may land downtown"** — Vallee's image for what the phenomenon may become once the irreversible learning is achieved: it "may go away entirely," or "may assume some suitable representation on a human scale." Chapters: 10. Related: irreversible learning, the next form of religion.
- **Animal abduction / Hamilton's cow** — The motif Vallee finds "equally fascinating to the student of close-encounter UFO cases": animals carried toward the hovering winged disk on the Phoenician seals (Figures B and E), including a god holding a horned animal under each arm; his modern analogue is the April 1897 case in which a farmer named Hamilton and several others saw the "hideous" occupants of an "airship" lift one of his cows aboard, the butchered remains later dropped in a field. Chapters: 6. Related: Phoenician amulets, the 1897 airship.
- **Animal effects** — The reactions of animals at close encounters: at Delphos the dog did not move or bark and the sheep were "almost glued to the spot," and for two weeks afterwards the sheep bolted from the pen every evening at sunset while the dog nearly destroyed the screen door trying to get inside at nightfall; parallel cases in the Table 2 morphology report horses and dogs paralyzed and cows unable to move. Chapters: 1, 7. Related: UFO ring, paralysis of witnesses.
- **The Apocalypse counterculture / the Ptolemaeus thought experiment** — Aimé Michel's exercise: imagine an Alexandrine thinker "like Ptolemaeus," schooled in Archimedes, Euclid and Aristotle, reading the Apocalypse; he would merely shrug — "This counterculture was too absurd to retain the attention of a reader of Plato. A short time — a very short time — elapsed, the counterculture triumphed, and Plato was forgotten for a thousand years. Is this what is happening again?" Chapters: 6. Related: collapse of ancient civilizations, absurdity.
- **Archaic and futuristic designs** — Vallee's observation that modern UFOs carry archaic as well as futuristic markings — the Socorro object's insignia being the Arabic astrological sign for Venus — which he offers as support for a literal reading of the ancient symbolism. Chapters: 6. Related: Socorro insignia, symbolic manipulation of belief.
- **Astarte** — The Phoenician deity in whose sanctuaries, per the account of ritual Vallee summarizes, women surrendered their virginity. Chapters: 6. Related: Phoenician amulets.
- **Astronomical symbols (on the seals)** — The stars and crescent moons abundant on the Phoenician cylinder seals; Vallee cites them as the reason the disk is read as a cosmological symbol, and as grounds for asking whether it "appear[s] to come from a star." Chapters: 6. Related: winged disk, literal vs. symbolic interpretation.
- **Automatic writing** — The technique common to Mrs Swan, Commander Curtis, Vallee's 7171 subject, Geller and (in seance form) the tapping table: the sitter relaxes the arm completely, holds a pencil, poses questions, and an "unknown" or "outside force" takes control of the arm and writes the reply. Vallee insists it is nothing new — "as old as the Old Testament, which was, after all, written through communication with such a 'higher source'," whose contents "still rule over the lives and conduct of hundreds of millions of human beings." Chapters: 2, 3, 4. Related: AFFA, 7171, the Guardians, table-tapping, Ouija board, psychically received scriptures.
- **Beam of light / conical beam / the tranquilizing light** — "A major feature in all religious traditions": a beam from a point in the sky or a peculiarly shaped cloud, focused on a person, usually a sign of blessing, conveying information from a divine source. Vallee tracks the same motif through modern testimony — Dr X's rotating shaft of white light, Geller's beam from the sky, the Bouahmama Legionnaire's "intense conical beam of emerald-green light" that left him permanently tranquil ("It was like time running very slowly"), Monroe's probing beam from the north, and the child pinned to his bed by a blinding beam that came "right through the wall." Chapters: 1, 6, 7, 8. Related: spontaneous healing, consciousness outside the body, psychic technology.
- **"Believe in us but not too much"** — The self-limiting instruction Vallee reports from ufonauts, cited (with Schirmer's "you will not speak wisely about this night") as one of the things his level-of-control assumption explains. Chapters: 9. Related: "You will not speak wisely about this night," why there is no genuine contact.
- **The benevolent trap** — Vallee's warning that society is "developing a great thirst for contact with superior minds that will provide guidance for our poor harassed, hectic planet," and that in doing so "we may be ready to fall into a trap, perhaps a kind, benevolent trap." Chapters: 9. Related: thirst for superior minds, salvation from heaven, the next form of religion.
- **The Bicentennial disclosure prediction** — An unnamed "former aerospace engineer turned UFO lecturer" believes that on the occasion of the Bicentennial the government will announce that there is life on Mars and that a meeting between U.S. representatives and extraterrestrials is imminent; Vallee gives it as a specimen of "an interesting path" such people are going down, and of the changed reception such statements now get. Chapters: 10. Related: Washington rumors, participation in the myth-making.
- **Big Science** — Vallee's name for the institutional research machine that renders wonder tedious; he predicts the UFO mystery will become "as dull as any venture that is in the process of being rationalized away by the academic mill" once it falls into its hands, just as "the journey to the moon can be turned into the exasperating bore that modern technology has generated." Chapters: 2. Related: classical research response, Big Science's arrival.
- **Biogenetic bases** — The UMMO concept that drew the extreme interest of the Paris biologist at the June 1971 Spanish meeting, who announced he would return home and test the theory that krypton could be found in the human hypothalamus. Chapters: 4. Related: krypton soul link, WOA.
- **Borrowed content / "nothing beyond the participants"** — Vallee's operating rule for judging channelled material: "nothing that is within the power of any one of the participants is beyond the power of the alleged entity," and such messages "could tap the knowledge, conscious or unconscious, of the participants." His two demonstrations are the table at Victor Hugo's house tapping out a Hugo-grade poem and Geller producing tensors in Puharich's presence. Whether the entity claims a space origin or claims to be "Aunt Martha," it does "nothing more than borrow words and expressions that exist as thoughts, possibly unconsciously, in the minds of the persons who attend the meeting." Chapters: 3. Related: light-year confusion, ventriloquist hypothesis, hazardous to psychic health, how belief is born.
- **BUAE BIEE** — The UMMO documents' name for the telepathic impulses their adults use: one holds in consciousness the mental image of one's correspondent and sends impulses of approximately 0.14 milliseconds' duration, a special bilingual code permitting "double simultaneous conversations." Chapters: 4. Related: UMMO, telepathy.
- **The B.V.M. / the technology of the B.V.M.** — Vallee's deliberately deflationary phrase for the effects surrounding the "Blessed Virgin Mary" apparitions — light, cloud, paralysis, healing, bent branches — used as a refrain ("The technology of the B.V.M. was at work!"). His claim: "the technology which 'she' uses is indistinguishable from that of gods and goddesses of other tongues and garb; it is also indistinguishable from the technology surrounding the UFO phenomenon." Chapters: 7. Related: morphology of miracles, technology that transcends the physical, Tonantzin.
- **The capsules** — The small aluminum-looking cylinders reportedly found near the Santa Mónica landing site — tube 129.8 mm long, caliber 8.8 mm, central metallic disk 24 mm, rounded ends — one of which contained a piece of green plastic engraved with the UMMO symbol; nickel of 99% purity by INTA's analysis. Chapters: 4. Related: TEDLAR, INTA, UMMO insignia.
- **Century-long time frames / the media attention span** — Vallee's explanation of why the level of control is invisible: mythological change moves on "time frames of a century" and is "slow and sure," while mass media give "split-second images of transient noise (the noisier the better)" and a society whose attention span is "the interval between two TV commercials" can have no concept of events that began before one's grandfather was born and will end after one's grandson dies. Chapters: 9. Related: the level of control, mythology rules social reality.
- **A certain concept of humanity** — What Vallee says is at stake beyond freedom itself in the book's final paragraph: "It is not simply our freedom that is in danger now. It is a certain concept of humanity." Chapters: 10. Related: the psychic crisis, the solution within ourselves.
- **The charismatic movement** — One of the present-day settings Vallee names in which "the uncanny power of superhuman words" is still visible, alongside hundreds of occult groups and UFO-related writings such as those inspired by SPECTRA and AFFA. Chapters: 3. Related: uncanny power of superhuman words, how belief is born.
- **Chthonic / tellurian beings** — Professor Price-Williams's classification of the scorpion-men of the Phoenician seals, drawing on the Gilgamesh epic and the *Enuma Elish*: "These creatures would thus be tellurian beings, 'chthonic' as Jung would have said." Chapters: 6. Related: scorpion-men, Gilgamesh and the Enuma Elish.
- **The classical research response** — Vallee's forecast of what will happen when public awareness gets high enough: "millions of dollars to consultants and research institutes, thousands of questionnaires, field investigators with glass bottles, sociologists filling correlation matrices, medical personnel adjusting electrodes over the frontal lobes of ranchers" — judged in advance as "another wrinkle in the learning curve, another step in the conditioning." Chapters: 9. Related: Big Science, primary effects, schedule of reinforcement.
- **"Classified building, unclassified files"** — Vallee's description of the Blue Book archive after its transfer to the Air Force archives: "The files are not classified but the building is, with the interesting result that one needs a security clearance in order to see these 'unclassified' papers." Chapters: 2. Related: Project Blue Book, destroying the data, the coverup theory.
- **Close encounter** — The category of report at the center of Vallee's work, occurring at close range and disproportionately in sparsely populated areas; his computer catalogue of such cases was to hold "nearly two thousand" from all countries. Chapters: 1. Related: computer catalogue of close encounters, seven categories of strangeness, psychic component.
- **The cloud-and-cave pattern** — The recurring structure Vallee isolates across Fatima, Lourdes, Guadalupe and an Apocryphal nativity account: a bright or golden cloud associated with a cave or grotto, from which light and then an entity emerge. Chapters: 7. Related: Cova da Iria, morphology of miracles.
- **Cognitive dissonance / "doomsday believers"** — The sociological labels applied to groups like Mrs Keech's, which Vallee judges premature: the mechanism generating such movements "is not fully understood," and their potential social impact has not been made explicit. Chapters: 2. Related: *When Prophecy Fails*, the Guardians, how belief is born.
- **The collapse of ancient civilizations / scientific rationalism defeated by the irrational** — Vallee's chosen historical analogue: from the second century B.C. to the fall of Rome, Mediterranean intellectual elites raised in scientific rationalism were "confronted and eventually defeated by an irrational element similar to that contained in modern apparitions of unexplained phenomena" — an element "dramatized in their summary rejection by our own science." Chapters: 6. Related: the Apocalypse counterculture, universal fact hypothesis.
- **The collusion hypothesis** — The counter-explanation Vallee says his first telepathy test with Geller excluded: that Geller had contrived to learn the sealed target in advance. Since Vallee alone knew that two targets had been sent instead of one, advance knowledge "would [have produced] a single drawing." Chapters: 8. Related: trickery in mediumship, mental television screen, forcing of choices.
- **The computer catalogue of close encounters** — The content-analysis file Vallee reports compiling, to hold "the details of nearly two thousand cases of that type, from all countries," indicating "a formidable impact … being made on our collective psyche"; earlier, the computer analysis of occupant reports that yielded Magonia rather than a plausible extraterrestrial biology. Chapters: 0, 1. Related: Magonia, *Passport to Magonia*, monthly index of UFO activity.
- **The Condon report / the Condon Committee** — The University of Colorado study, headed by "a prestigious physicist close to retirement," which cost nearly half a million dollars and whose eight-hundred-page post-mortem declared that the study of UFOs "cannot be justified in the expectation that science will be advanced thereby." All members "had been selected because they had no previous knowledge of the subject"; the team split, the minority was fired after publishing an early internal memorandum, and the files were locked up, "later transferred to a private home, and were burned shortly thereafter." Its negative conclusions, prominently displayed in the Paris press, backfired in France. Chapters: 1, 2, 4. Related: Project Blue Book, destroying the data, first coverup.
- **The conditioning maze** — Vallee's closing image: his "strange urge" to stop behaving as a rat pressing levers, "even if I have to go hungry for a while," and to step outside the maze to see what makes it tick. Chapters: 9. Related: schedule of reinforcement, unattended clockwork, the rat and the lever.
- **The Confusion Technique** — Milton Erickson's hypnotic-induction method (via Watzlawick, Weakland and the co-author printed as "R. Rish"): deliberately answering an unasked question or otherwise redefining a social context unexpectedly, so that the resulting unresolved confusion leaves the subject "particularly ready and eager to hold on firmly to the next piece of concrete information" offered. Vallee asks whether the ufonaut who told a witness "You lie; it is four o'clock" was using an analogous technique. Chapters: 5. Related: reframing / second-order change, the fly bottle, thought transference via absurdity, meta-logic.
- **Consciousness breakthrough** — Vallee's claim that a scientific breakthrough alone is insufficient; what is needed is also "a global historical grasp of the beliefs — materialistic as well as idealistic — among which we have been groping for ten thousand years." Chapters: 7. Related: technology that transcends the physical, the arbitrary dichotomy.
- **Consciousness outside the body / out-of-the-body travel** — The possibility Vallee names as widening the range of hypotheses ("if consciousness can be manifested outside the body"), and the experience reported across his cases: the Aveyron witness "floating off" between 4 and 5 A.M. with his mind alert and body paralyzed; the engineer's claimed ability to "leave his body" and transfer consciousness into other objects or into space; Robert Monroe's systematic out-of-body research; and the correspondent's investigated witness who made several out-of-the-body trips, the longest from a military base in Japan to a friend's home in California, where "the friend saw him at the door of the room at the corresponding time." Chapters: 1, 5, 6, 8. Related: place in time, spontaneous levitation, beam of light.
- **Contact occurs under conditions controlled by the phenomenon** — The fifth of Vallee's five propositions: contact between percipients and the phenomenon occurs "under conditions controlled by the latter," not by the human party — a rule Geller confirms in his own case, saying that in all his meetings with "them" the initiative had been with "the other side." Chapters: 1, 8. Related: five propositions, percipient-controlled contact, why there is no genuine contact.
- **Contactee** — The publicity-seeking, message-bearing type against which Vallee's engineer is defined: "Contrary to most contactees, however, he sought no publicity, had no message to deliver to humanity, and did not claim to understand what had happened to him." Chapters: 0. Related: silent contactees / secret contactee, patterns of belief.
- **Control system** — Vallee's central hypothesis, stated in the Introduction and again as the book's thesis: UFOs as a mechanism regulating something rather than as vehicles or delusions — "We are not dealing with successive waves of visitations from space. We are dealing with a control system." What is regulated is human belief and "the relationship between our consciousness and physical reality," a control he claims has operated throughout history. To understand it "one must be ready to understand that it needs two opposite principles for its function." Chapters: 0, 8, 9. Related: thermostat analogy, control system for human consciousness, human belief as the controlled variable, reference level.
- **Control system for human consciousness** — The explicit hypothesis of chapter 9, with the conditioning of *Homo sapiens* as its effect; Vallee states he has *not* determined whether the system is natural and spontaneous (genetics, social psychology), artificial and "under the power of some superhuman will," or simply governed by laws not yet discovered. Chapters: 9. Related: control system, the level of control, schedule of reinforcement.
- **Convergence of events** — Vallee's preferred alternative to signal accounts of ESP: rather than information passing from brain to brain, several events "in our universe of events" converge on the same content. Usually two converge; the Geller number episode had three — the subject's 725 on the board, Geller's spoken 7-2-5, and Vallee's own 726 written beforehand and shown to Janine. Chapters: 8. Related: transmission hypothesis, single universe of events, interpenetrating universes.
- **Cosmic morality / non-interference** — UMMO's stated reason for staying hidden: "A cosmic morality forbids any paternalistic attitude on planetary social networks that must be individually created by each of them," plus the claim that any official presentation would produce "grave alterations, incalculable social perturbations," destroying the possibility of studying human society "in its current virgin conditions." Chapters: 4. Related: UMMO, warning against cultism, request not to inform governments.
- **The cosmic broadcast hypothesis (Bergier)** — Jacques Bergier's 1973 "amusing explanation": a galactic civilization broadcasting a cosmic education program at intervals, picked up imperfectly by gifted prophets on earth "and millions of other retarded planets" — which would explain the divergences between Koran, Bible and Book of Mormon, as sorcerers "playing at night with old radio sets in different parts of the jungle" would garble the Sorbonne. Chapters: 7. Related: psychically received scriptures, higher beings.
- **Cova da Iria** — The large hollow pasture near Fatima where the apparitions occurred; Vallee glosses it as "literally: the Cave of St Irene, an old sacred spot." Chapters: 7. Related: cloud-and-cave pattern, tree of the apparitions.
- **The coverup theory (and its renunciation)** — The belief Vallee says he once held and repeatedly rationalized his disappointments into: that Project Blue Book's blunders were "only a front," that the missing Blue Book files and photographs meant "somebody, somewhere, was gathering this evidence and studying it in earnest," and that worldwide Air Force reporting confirmed it. His verdict in the Conclusion: "Today I am no longer young, and I have lost my unquestioning trust of official wisdom in matters of advanced research" — and "If I still believed it today I would not be publishing this book." Chapters: 10. Related: incoherent restlessness, Project Blue Book, first coverup, "classified building, unclassified files."
- **The credibility gap** — The public's distrust of authorities on UFOs, measured by Vallee's report that in a survey of technically trained witnesses only one in twelve had bothered to call the Air Force. He treats this attitude as "an important component of the UFO phenomenon," not a side effect of it, because it lets the idea of a cosmic mystery linger in "the more shadowy areas of our imagination." Chapters: 2. Related: reaction as part of the phenomenon, first coverup, stigma of UFO research.
- **CRILL, ALOMAR, PONNAR and ANKAR** — The other entities in Mrs Swan's contact file alongside AFFA: CRILL from Jupiter, ALOMAR from Mercury, PONNAR also from Mercury, ANKAR from the constellation Centaurus. What impressed the investigators was that "the woman had been able to answer questions that appeared to be beyond her education and technical knowledge." Chapters: 3. Related: AFFA, OEEU, automatic writing.
- **Cultural conditioning of perception** — The thesis of Vallee's astronomer correspondent that "it is a cultural artifact that only rational phenomena are respectable," and that culture, education and training "lead us to see the world in a certain way and, apparently, lead us to comfortably ignore or reject those aspects of reality that don't fit" — supported by the kitten visual-cortex experiment and the perspective-blind natives reported in *Science*. Chapters: 8. Related: separate reality, stigma of UFO research.
- **Deliberately misleading contact** — The category Vallee allows within a system whose rule is that direct genuine contact "would ruin the experiment," and for which UMMO is his named example. Chapters: 9. Related: why there is no genuine contact, "the phenomenon must mislead us," UMMO.
- **Dematerialization / materialization** — The first of Vallee's five propositions holds that UFOs "are neither objects nor flying" because they can dematerialize "as some recent photographs show"; the Dr X disk dematerialized with a "bang," leaving a whitish glow blown away by the wind. In the psychic literature the same effects are "reminiscent of 'materializations' in the spiritualist literature," and Geller's claimed powers extend to partly or completely dematerializing metal — which, Vallee notes, has not been demonstrated under strict laboratory control. Chapters: 0, 1, 8. Related: psychokinesis, metal bending, violation of the laws of motion.
- **Descriptive phenomenology** — Vallee's chosen methodological framework, quoted from social scientist Cynthia Nelson: it "attempts to communicate the quality and structure of experiences, be they clear and symbolically defined . . . or diffuse and even not symbolized." In practice: instead of the physicist's question or the psychologist's, review what witnesses experience, observe what they do as a result, and correlate within a total framework. Chapters: 0. Related: "real in their consequences," Zeitoun apparitions, the arbitrary dichotomy.
- **Destroying the data / the destruction of scientific data** — The behavior that first drew Vallee into the Invisible College: French scientists met his own theodolite observations "by denying every observation, by blaming it on airplanes when the documentation was unassailable, and by destroying the data when it was demonstrated to them that no airplane could have behaved as the objects did." He holds that scientists' silence, whatever its motive, cannot justify "the deliberate destruction of scientific data." Chapters: 0, 2. Related: theodolite, Condon Committee, first coverup, stigma of UFO research.
- **Disk with extended legs / claws** — The winged disk's appendages on the Phoenician seals, which the sun-or-soul reading does not account for; Vallee's central speculation, posed as a question, is whether "the representation of a disk with extended claws may in fact seek to preserve the memory of a vision, or observation, of a flying craft capable of landing." Chapters: 6. Related: winged disk, literal vs. symbolic interpretation.
- **Diversionary explanation** — Vallee's suspicion that the popular "craft from another planet" reading is itself part of the phenomenon's camouflage, serving "a diversionary role in masking the real, infinitely more complex nature of the technology that gives rise to the sightings." Chapters: 1. Related: extraterrestrial hypothesis, image beyond the belief structure, the 1897 airship.
- **"Divine beings"** — The term Vallee reports the archaeological literature uses for the figures shown emerging from the upper part of the winged disks; they appear in four of the five seals, sometimes in elaborate headdresses, sometimes walking outside the disk. Chapters: 6. Related: winged disk, Ahuramazda, scorpion-men.
- **Enochian language** — The complete language revealed to John Dee and Edward Kelly by the entities, "still regarded by contemporary occultists as a tool of the greatest power"; Aleister Crowley expressed his greatest magical invocations in it, and they must be pronounced a certain way to be effective. The word AFFA in it means "empty." Chapters: 3. Related: AFFA, Angel Uriel, linguistic translation device.
- **The essence of prophecy / the prophetic element / prophetic dreams** — Vallee's phrase for the Aveyron witness's certainty that he will one day write a book he is presently incapable of writing — "THEY told me not to worry about that … When the time is right, I will know what to put on paper" — glossed "We are touching here the very essence of prophecy." The same element runs through Mrs Keech's cataclysm messages and through the correspondent whose dreams "invariably had a prophetic element" (the house fire, the Vanguard front page). Chapters: 1, 2, 8. Related: precognition, mass landings prediction, the Guardians.
- **Evidence that eludes classical analysis** — Vallee's late characterization of the accumulating case material: it gives more and more evidence of the reality of the UFOs, "but this evidence is so constructed as to elude classical analysis by scientists." His three speculative reasons, all offered as "perhaps": the UFOs may not behave according to our laws of causality; their time may flow differently from ours; their logic may be a meta-logic. Chapters: 10. Related: meta-logic, the phenomenon negates itself, space-time distortion.
- **The extraterrestrial hypothesis / the space visitors hypothesis** — The reading Vallee rejects throughout: he "cannot subscribe" to it, calls the flying-saucer enthusiasts' version "a naive concept," "too simpleminded to account for the diversity of the reported behavior of the occupants," and holds that it "fails to explain adequately the ancient symbolism," since the record is not of travelers who "spotted the earth and explored it casually on their way to another cosmic destination." Chapters: 0, 1, 6. Related: mass psychosis hypothesis, Magonia, place in time, terrestrial-origin hypothesis.
- **The Fairy-Faith / the "Little People"** — Evans-Wentz's Celtic folklore material — his 1909 French dissertation "Fairy-Faith in Celtic Countries," collected on foot in Brittany, Ireland, Wales and Scotland from older people who had seen the "Good People" and knew nothing of UFOs — which supplied Vallee with the historical analogues to UFO occupants used in *Passport to Magonia*. Chapters: 7. Related: Invisible Otherworld, Shining Beings / Devas, *Passport to Magonia*.
- **Faith-healing hypothesis (and its rejection)** — Charcot's explanation, raised and discounted by the three doctors who published the Pierre de Rudder case: they find it impossible that the nervous system could restore destroyed tissue "in violation of the known laws of natural healing of the body… To state this is to place oneself in opposition to the principles of medical science." Chapters: 7. Related: spontaneous healing, psychosomatic cures.
- **Falling-leaf motion** — The zigzagging descent of a disk, a standard UFO signature, which Vallee finds in the October 13, 1917 solar phenomenon at Fatima, where the disk "plunged downwards in zig-zag fashion toward the earth." Chapters: 7. Related: solar phenomenon of October 13, Table 2.
- **First coverup** — "The efforts that are made by men in a position of authority to discourage the reporting of a UFO incident," ranging "from the laughter of a local deputy sheriff to intimidation of pilots by their commanding officer"; a variant leads the public to believe reporting is unnecessary because the government already knows everything. Chapters: 2. Related: triple coverup, "Silence Group" / bureaucratic confusion, witness intimidation.

- **Mrs Keech** — The Midwestern woman who woke one winter morning with a tingling arm, picked up a pencil, and found her hand writing "in another handwriting" — the automatic-writing contact with the Guardians that produced a sect, a flood prophecy, a sociological infiltration, and the visit of two men, one of whom said "I am of this planet, but he is not." "Now the ring of absurdity was complete around Mrs Keech." Vallee calls her sect a prototype for a growing class of belief-groups. Chapters: 2, 3, 4, 10.
- **John Keel** — Ufologist, source (as Vallee reports it) of the term "silent contactees," and one of the researchers whose observations Vallee's publishing experiment let him verify. Chapters: 2, 5.

- **Martin Luther King** — Named in the UMMO condolence document among the "expeditionary group." Chapters: 4.
- **Francis Kirkham** — Source of the privately printed 1937 volume from which Vallee quotes Joseph Smith's "I had full confidence in obtaining a divine manifestation, as I had previously had one." Chapters: 7.
- **Philip Klass** — The skeptic whose explanation in *UFOs Explained* — that the Delphos white substance was produced by the urine of sheep feeding from a circular device — Vallee says the fibrous, vegetal structure of the material destroys. Chapters: 1.
- **Fernand Lagarde** — The French investigator who worked the Aveyron farm series thoroughly and published his summary in *Flying Saucer Review* ("This is a story with all the ring of truth about it"); one of "two of the best-informed researchers of paranormal phenomena in Europe" who drove the La Javie mountains with Vallee and Michel in August 1974. Chapters: 1, 4.

Chapters: 7.
- **Charles Mackay** — Author of *Extraordinary Popular Delusions and the Madness of Crowds*, quoted on Dee and Kelly — on which Vallee remarks, "One might wonder whether a future historian will not have the same thing to say about Dr Puharich and Uri Geller." Chapters: 3.
- **Miss Marsland** — Daughter of George Marsland and president of the Order of the Initiates of Tibet, receiving her teachings from a mysterious Paris source allegedly emanating from Tibet, written in Sanskrit and translated by "the high initiate in Paris." Chapters: 3.
- **George Marsland** — Founder of the American Bankers' Association, and father of the Order's president. Chapters: 3.
- **Manuel Pedro Marto** — The Fatima witness who stated under oath in the canonical enquiry that on August 13, 1917 — with the children in jail — he clearly saw a luminous globe spinning through the clouds. Chapters: 7.
- **Karl Marx** — Named in the UMMO condolence document among the "expeditionary group." Chapters: 4.

Matsumura** — The Japanese researcher who reported the *Sophia* document, a commentary on Greek Orthodox dogma in the Leningrad National Library describing the angel's cloud-topped eye projection, sound-receiving head device, and instruction-bearing mirror. Vallee could not verify the document or the translation. Chapters: 6.
- **Thomas McGlynn** — Fatima author, source of Lucia's statement, on close questioning, that "our Lady's feet rested lightly on the top of the leaves." Chapters: 7.
- **Mendes** — The prominent lawyer who told John Haffert in 1960: "What I saw at Fatima could not help but affect the interior life… I still remember it today as vividly as at the moment it happened." Chapters: 7.
- **Donald Menzel** — The astronomer whose televised demonstration — pouring benzine over a tankful of acetone to argue UFOs were mirages — prompted Vallee's aside to his wife: "Let me know next time it rains benzine, will you? We'll go out and watch the flying saucers!" Chapters: 2.
- **Joël Mesnard** — The French investigator whose *Flying Saucer Review* report "Tranquilizing Visitation at Bouahmama" (May 1973) supplies the Algerian Legionnaire case; he met the witness again in May 1970 and found him still in the same peaceful state. Chapters: 6.
- **Aimé Michel** — Science writer and, per Vallee, "a leading figure in the study of paranormal phenomena in France"; author of the *Flying Saucer Review* report on the Case of Dr X (whom he visited on November 8, 1968, six days after the sighting), source of the Ptolemaeus thought experiment on the Apocalypse counterculture, and one of Vallee's two companions on the August 1974 La Javie field trip. Chapters: 0, 1, 4, 6.
- **Professor Miklos** — The Romanian researcher whose letter offers three main sources of psi (matter's evolutionary ascent via brain–mind; an actually existing non-material "other world"; "the UFO's world that brings about psi-occurrences"), the matter–antimatter analogy for a psi–nonpsi interaction, and the endorsement of Vallee's "interpenetrating universes" and "'metalogical' preference." Chapters: 8.
- **Minkowski** — Named, with Eddington, as the author of advanced texts against which the UMMO IBOZOO UU document was said to compare favorably. Chapters: 4.
- **Molière** — The identity claimed by the first entity at the Victor Hugo séance, before it was replaced — on a question about death — by "The Shadow of the Sepulchre." Chapters: 3.
- **Montes de Oca** — Author of *More About Fatima*, source of the physical trace: "the topmost branches of the tree were bent in the form of a parasol and remained thus as if an invisible weight had come to rest upon them." Chapters: 7.
- **Robert Monroe** — The American businessman who systematically investigates out-of-body travel (*Journeys Out Of the Body*), whose September 1960 notes record being "bathed in and transfixed by a very powerful beam that seemed to come from the North," an intelligence "beyond my comprehension" searching every memory in his mind; and whose fight with two small beings, ended by the arrival of a robed man, Vallee offers as a "curious parallel" to Jose Antonio's vision. Chapters: 5, 6.
- **Moroni** — The personage who appeared at Joseph Smith's bedside on September 21, 1823 "standing in the air, for his feet did not touch the floor," identified himself as a messenger from the presence of God, described the buried gold plates and the two translating stones, and departed up "a conduit open right up into heaven." Chapters: 7.
- **Moses** — Named as a precedent for revelation as isolation: the prophet who, like Joseph Smith, had to flee, form a sect and move away. Chapters: 7.
- **Emmanuel Mounier** — Named in the UMMO condolence document among the "expeditionary group." Chapters: 4.
- **Antonio Muñoz** — The La Ponderosa restaurant employee, atop a ladder stringing colored bulbs, who was told a craft had landed nearby, thought the man mad, and then received a stream of shaken witnesses — a middle-aged couple who sketched the H-like symbol, and two young couples reporting a huge sphere of fire. Chapters: 4.
- **Charles Muses** — The mathematician (co-author with Arthur Young of *Consciousness and Reality*) whose image of a saucer hovering over the chasm — "Do you want a lift?" — Vallee answers with three questions about the stranger, the chasm, and our own resources. Chapters: 9.
- **John Musall** — Author of "Sunlight Saves," the 1974 song from which chapter 5's epigraph is taken. Chapters: 5.
- **Antoine Nancey** — The name at "Lista de Correos - Madrid" to which all correspondence about the UMMO capsules was to be addressed before June 28th; like Dagousset, untraceable in Madrid. Chapters: 4.
- **Cynthia Nelson** — The social scientist whose paper "Stress, Religious Experience and Mental Health: A Phenomenological View" (Third International Congress of Social Psychiatry, Zagreb, 1970) supplies Vallee's methodological charter — descriptive phenomenology, and "If men define situations as real they are real in their consequences" — via her treatment of the Zeitoun apparitions. Chapters: 0.
- **John Ballou Newbrough** — Who received the OAHSPE bible psychically about 1881 from Shining Beings he calls "Angels." Chapters: 7.
- **Nilsson** — Author of *Greek Piety*, footnoted with "Dodd" for the classical-era defeat of rationalism by the irrational. Chapters: 6.
- **Dr Brien O'Brien** — The physicist who headed the special study group that recommended the Air Force spend $250,000 a year to obtain "high-grade data" — a recommendation Vallee reads as evidence against a deliberate suppression policy. Chapters: 2.
- **Captain Orrego** — The Chilean naval officer whose 1948 Antarctic UFO photographs the Chilean Naval Mission acknowledged a request for, before reporting that Orrego had "recently" said he saw no UFOs there — "Therefore the photographs requested by you do not exist." Chapters: 2.
- **Vicente Ortuño** — One of the witnesses to the February 6, 1966 Aluche landing. Chapters: 4.
- **Antonio Pardo** — The producer of the second San José de Valderas photo series (August 26, 1967) and the source from whom the analyzed capsule reached Farriols; he was never interviewed in person by any investigator. "As someone was later to remark, the name Antonio Pardo can be translated as Anthony Brown." Chapters: 4.
- **Calvin Parker** — Aged 19, the younger of the two Pascagoula fishermen. Chapters: 2.
- **Joseph Pelletier** — Fatima author whose lists of physical phenomena (buzzing, decreased solar glow, whitish cloud, loud noise) and whose account of the annihilating "power" that "practically deprives them of the use of their bodily senses" Vallee says "could apply to the entire spectrum of close encounters with UFOs" — and whose description of the unrevealable experience Vallee calls one of the best definitions of his strangeness category 6. Chapters: 7.
- **Father Peyramalle** — The curé of Lourdes, especially angry at the story, who demanded the Lady make the rosebush bloom before the whole crowd; when Bernadette conveyed the demand, "the apparition simply smiled." Chapters: 7.
- **Ted Phillips** — The investigator who reached the Delphos site thirty-two days after the landing and found the ring still repelling water under melting snow: "the soil would not permit the water to pass through the surface. This was most remarkable"; he supplied Vallee with the sample of white substance sent to France. Chapters: 1.
- **Pius XI** — Named in the Fatima prophecy of July 13, 1917 ("another and worse [war] will begin during the reign of Pius XI"); Vallee's footnote: "Pius XI died in 1939." Chapters: 7.
- **Plato** — Whose readers, in Michel's thought experiment, dismissed the Apocalypse counterculture — after which "the counterculture triumphed, and Plato was forgotten for a thousand years." Chapters: 6.
- **PLTHAN** — The owner named in the inscription on Figure B of the Phoenician seals. Chapters: 6.
- **Dr Claude Poher** — Head of scientific studies for the French equivalent of NASA, co-author with Vallee of "Basic Patterns in UFO Observations" (AIAA Paper 75-42, January 1975), and one of the three French scientists heard alongside Galley's 1974 radio interviews. Chapters: 0, 2.
- **Price-Williams** — The UCLA professor whose gloss on the scorpion-men (Gilgamesh's guardians of the mountain of the sun; the *Enuma Elish*'s monster created by chaos) concludes "These creatures would thus be tellurian beings, 'chthonic' as Jung would have said"; also credited, with Fred Beckman, for the Skinner suggestion. Chapters: 6, 9.
- **Ptolemaeus** — The Alexandrine thinker of Aimé Michel's thought experiment, schooled in Archimedes, Euclid and Aristotle, who would merely shrug at the Apocalypse. Chapters: 6.
- **Dr Andrija Puharich** — The physician and parapsychologist who brought Geller to the United States and arranged his laboratory study, and who from November 30, 1971 recorded the SPECTRA/Hoova communications. Vallee regards him as "an intensely sincere man" while rejecting his conclusions outright — "I cannot accept the idea (proposed by Dr Puharich and others) that mankind is being psychically contacted by a benign intelligence from outer space" — and reads the SPECTRA material as borrowed from his and Geller's own minds. At the San Francisco panel he is "totally committed to the idea… that the only course for mankind is to place its destiny in 'their' hands." Chapters: 0, 2, 3, 4, 8, 9, 10.
- **Antonio Ribera** — Spanish investigator, co-author with Farriols of *Un Caso Perfecto* and of later articles on the Aluche landing. Chapters: 4.
- **Henry Riecken** — Co-author of *When Prophecy Fails*. Chapters: 2.
- **R. Rish** — The name printed in Vallee's text as third co-author, with Watzlawick and Weakland, of *Change: Principles of Problem Formation and Problem Resolution* (Norton, 1974) — the source of the Erickson Confusion Technique material. Chapters: 5.
- **Rivero** — The shopowner at No. 33 Calle de Sedano who, stepping out of his door on the evening of June 1, 1967, saw a "conical thing" giving off a fiery light rise rapidly over the trees. Chapters: 4.
- **Peter Rogerson** — The British researcher who wrote to Vallee that the childhood experiences of Cayce and Geller follow "the classical pattern of the evolution of the shaman," citing Eliade's *Shamanism* and Andrew Jackson Davis's staff of cures. Chapters: 7.
- **Pierre de Rudder** — The Belgian whose leg was crushed by a falling tree, leaving the two bones separated by over an inch through eight years of failed treatment, and who on April 7, 1875 walked unaided from the shrine at Oostacker; the cure was verified by his doctors, he worked normally until his death from pneumonia in 1898, and the exhumation and autopsy of May 24, 1899 produced photographs and a signed report Vallee says show "no explanation for the piece of healthy white bone, over one inch long, connecting the two sections." Chapters: 7.
- **Bertrand Russell** — On whose death UMMO sent a document of condolence to "Men of the Earth!", naming him and others as members of its embedded "expeditionary group." Chapters: 4.
- **The Ryan family (Rayme, Larry, Raymond and Earl)** — The Mississippi fishermen — twin brothers aged 42 and their sons, 17 and 16 — who on the night of November 6, 1973 saw a submerged object in the oyster-bed area southwest of Pascagoula; part of the pattern of three cases in two months, all involving an older and a younger fisherman. Chapters: 2.
- **Dr V. Sakleanu** — The Romanian psychic researcher, relayed by Miklos, who postulates that matter has substantial, energetical and informational aspects, so that "nonmaterial" should really be taken to mean "nonsubstantial." Chapters: 8.

- **Stanley Schachter** — Co-author of *When Prophecy Fails*. Chapters: 2.
- **Herb Schirmer** — The 22-year-old Ashland, Nebraska patrolman who on December 3, 1967 logged "Saw a flying saucer at the junction of highways 6 and 63. Believe it or not," went home with a headache, a buzzing noise and a red welt below the left ear, and under hypnosis recovered a full encounter — the winged-serpent emblem, the running tapes, "Are you the watchman over this place?", "TO A CERTAIN EXTENT THEY WANT TO PUZZLE PEOPLE," and the parting "YOU WILL NOT SPEAK WISELY ABOUT THIS NIGHT." Chapters: 2, 9.
- **Albert Schweitzer** — Named in the UMMO condolence document among the "expeditionary group." Chapters: 4.
- **Fernando Sesma Manzano** — The Spanish government employee and minor Madrid literary figure who founded the Sociedad de Amigos del Espacio in 1954, hoped for years to be contacted, and in early January 1965 received the telephone call from a strongly accented stranger — "I am under strict orders" — that began the flood of UMMO documents. Chapters: 4.
- **Dr Simon** — The doctor with whom Vallee had a chance to discuss the Hills' abduction case. Chapters: 9.
- **B. F. Skinner** — The behaviorist who has worked "for half a century" on modifying behavior through selective reinforcement, and whose *Patterns of Reinforcement* (with Ferster) supplies Vallee's central analogy: "When Skinner designs a machine that feeds a rat only when the right lever is depressed, this is extremely misleading for the rat! But if the rat doesn't do it he becomes extremely hungry." Chapters: 9.
- **Joseph Smith** — The Mormon prophet whose 1820 vision of "a pillar of light… above the brightness of the sun" and two Personages, and whose September 21, 1823 visitations by Moroni (repeated three times in one night, on the Autumn Equinox, leaving him so exhausted next day that he collapsed unconscious at a fence), Vallee treats as a light-and-entity contact of the standard type, keyed line by line into Table 2. Evans-Wentz calls his first vision a vision of the Shining Beings and his role that of a Terton; Vallee notes he "was lynched by a crowd in Illinois." Chapters: 7.
- **Bernadette Soubirous** — The 14-year-old of Lourdes who on February 11, 1858 heard "a great noise, like the sound of a storm" that disturbed neither trees nor water, saw a golden cloud come from the grotto and a Lady above a bush "that was moving as if it were windy," and found her arm paralyzed until the Lady crossed herself. Told to wash in a nonexistent spring, she dug in the sand "in a sort of stupor" and water came; told to eat the grass, she did, and the crowd laughed. Chapters: 7.
- **Paul Souday** — The journalist friend to whom Flammarion reported the Hugo séance in a manuscript letter, asking his advice, rather than publishing it. Chapters: 3.

You will never want for anything again." He walked out the door and had vanished; her health and money problems disappeared and she lived to 89. Chapters: 7.

- **Emanuel Swedenborg** — The eighteenth-century Swedish mystic whose warning is chapter 8's epigraph and its governing suspicion: "When Spirits begin to speak with a man, he must beware that he believe nothing that they say. Her account matches, phrase for phrase, the unnamed British woman's letter in chapter 6 whose fuller narrative adds the crouching, green-eyed, goat-legged figure she saw on the lawn that evening and took for a demon. - **Dr Van Hoestenberghe** — The specialist who recorded in January 1875 that de Rudder's leg bones were separated by three centimeters and the lower leg could be "twisted, with the heel in front and the toes in back"; who came reluctantly after the cure because he refused to believe in miracles and found his patient digging in the yard; and who in 1899 obtained permission to exhume the body, amputated both legs at the knee, and published the autopsy with Deschamps and Royer.
