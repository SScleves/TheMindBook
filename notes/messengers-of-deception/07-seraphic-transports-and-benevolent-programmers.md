# Seraphic Transports and Benevolent Programmers

## In one paragraph
Vallée follows the thread of the "Order of Melchizedek" through a century of channeled cosmologies, showing that the belief system now attaching itself to UFOs — superior beings who travel between worlds, run Earth's evolution, and recruit human agents — predates the saucer era and keeps resurfacing in ever more technological dress. He dissects the Book of Urantia (2,097 pages "mysteriously received" in Chicago, published 1955), whose Melchizedeks incarnate among mortals and whose "seraphic transports" depart Jerusem every three seconds — proto-UFOs embedded in a full cosmic bureaucracy; attends a San Francisco Urantian meeting; and traces the book's origin to Dr. William Sadler, a Chicago surgeon and professional debunker of mediums who wrote that nothing supernatural ever comes through automatic writing — yet confessed in an appendix to *The Mind at Mischief* (1929) that one sleeping subject, observed since 1911 over ~250 night sessions, channeled "alleged extraplanetary personalities" whose messages eighteen years of psychoanalysis and hypnosis could not trace to the man's own mind. Vallée then excavates the American branch of the Order of Melchizedek: hermit Hiram Butler's Esoteric Fraternity (Applegate, California, 1889), which demanded total suppression of orgasm — even of sexual thought — as the price of contact with the "Spirit-world," and whose books openly describe an occult plan to "overthrow nations" through mob psychology once 144,000 initiates gather. The sect is nearly extinct (two old brothers left in 1971), yet its themes flourish anew: transpersonal-psychology students initiated into the Melchizedek priesthood in a Silicon Valley hot tub; the STELLE community in Illinois under Melchizedek and billions of Masters from planet Klarian; and Jim Hurtak — associate of Puharich and Geller — who received the "Keys of Enoch" from a beam of light on Highway 152 and invites Vallée to join a psychic think tank working with "benevolent programmers" who reprogram planetary consciousness (as against "poachers" like the grotesque Reticulants), predicting open contact within eighteen months (nothing happened — "That, too, is typical"). Vallée declines: not because contact is impossible, but because the forces these systems release "were not forces of love, but of disorder and violence" — forces of change bending those who let themselves be used — and the real question is who is doing all this, and what their designs on us might be.

## Core claims
- Vallée claims today's most ardent believers in flying saucers and their occupants include the followers of the Book of Urantia — a channeled scripture whose "amazing word structure... appears to have all the earmarks of paranoia": the consistency of its statements, the richness of its material, and the fondness it displays for neologism "make it a monument to the unchecked imagination of man" — yet whose sections on religious history are "surprisingly clear and readable," in striking contrast to the "childish descriptions of the Spheres of the Beyond." That contrast, he argues, is "the dilemma of human faith." He grounds the claim in the San Francisco Urantian meeting he attended — ten attendees, several "regular students, advanced in the knowledge of Seraphic Planetary Government" — and came away certain "a number of real people truly believed in Melchizedek and his cosmic role."
- He claims the Urantia cosmology contains ready-made UFO material: Melchizedek Sons can "make himself visible to mortal beings" and even incarnate in mortal flesh; seraphic transports depart Jerusem every three seconds at 25 miles per second; Thought Adjusters from other worlds live within our brains and "influence our actions by subtle sabotage of our physical and chemical functions." He asks pointedly: "Are these our UFOs?"
- He notes (documented observation, with a barb) that Urantia's "Bright and Morning Star" — whose first executive associate is the Father Melchizedek — is, in the traditional sense, the lightbearer, Lucifer.
- He claims the Urantia case turns on Dr. William Sadler: a writer "of talent and considerable scholarship" who condemned quackery, astrology, trances, and automatic writing, explained cataleptic "trips to other worlds" as projections of "marginal consciousness," and declared that nothing from automatic writing "bears any evidence of supernatural origin" — and who nonetheless documented one exception he could not classify. Vallée presents Sadler's appendix as a model of "the scientific methods that can be applied to alleged communications from cosmic entities."
- He keeps evidence levels explicit: Sadler's account is a quoted third party — the subject (possibly a former stockbroker) was "utterly unconscious," unwakeable but never rigid, indifferent to the proceedings; over eighteen years no communication ever claimed to be a deceased human spirit (unlike spiritualism); "psychoanalysis, hypnotism, intensive comparison, fail to show" the messages originated in the subject's own mind; the material was internally consistent and contrary to the man's habits of thought. Sadler's verdict after 18 years: "I find myself at the present time just where I was when I started." Vallée adds the Book was received not in 1934 (the sect's story) but beginning closer to 1910–1911.
- He claims the same puzzlement applies to UFOs: "a phenomenon that leaves physical traces must be taken seriously, but what can we say of the people who claim to be in contact with superior intelligences emanating from these objects?" — and warns their claim that UFOs are directing mankind's evolution, once a fringe idea, "now has the potential to draw the attention of the media and to make a major impact," complete with threats of catastrophe if the "higher levels" are ignored.
- He claims the movement has escalated: "From the gentle belief in higher forms of life bent on helping mankind, we have gone to explore increasingly disturbing systems of philosophy and some new faiths that demand total obedience from their followers. The forces they released were not forces of love, but of disorder and violence. Yet they were forces of change, bending those who allowed themselves to be used" (asserted without argument).
- He claims Hiram Butler's Esoteric Fraternity shows the Melchizedek template before UFOs: Butler — a Pennsylvanian by birth who died in 1916, a self-educated sawmill worker who lost several fingers in an accident and then lived 14 years as a hermit before moving to California in 1889 with a dozen disciples to homestead 260 acres overlooking the American River near Applegate, Placer County — taught that contact with "the inhabitants of the Spirit-world" was earned through absolute sexual abstinence (First Degree: no orgasm; Second Degree: no sexual impulse "even in thought") — the opposite of Eastern mystics, who reabsorb desire rather than suppress it.
- He claims Butler's Order was "an esoteric group complete with initiations, passwords, and secret handshakes" whose purpose was "occult work with political ambitions": world control via "Mob Psychology" ("he who can play on the feelings of the masses can control them" — published 1926, when such control "was already becoming a well-understood tool"), the gathering of the 144,000 who "shall take the dominion under the whole heaven," and ultimate merger with the Elohim.
- He claims the similarities between this old fraternity and modern UFO contactee groups "are quite obvious" — the H.I.M. sect (of ch. 4) speaks of regeneration, transformation, and contact with a higher level "in the same terms as Hiram Butler" — and that "the puzzle is beginning to take shape."
- He claims the resonance point is generational and geographical irony: Butler's "eccentric, harmless writing" and Penn's "innocuous teachings" have found "a powerful resonance in the most unlikely place of all: the first post-industrial generation in the most advanced country on Earth" — hot-tub Melchizedek initiations within a few miles of Memorex, Lockheed, Amdahl, and Hewlett-Packard.
- He claims Hurtak's system recasts the whole tradition in computer jargon: "benevolent programmers" who allow planetary societies free will versus "poachers" who interfere with programs under way; the Brotherhood of Melchizedek handles "consciousness reprogramming"; UFOs "do not travel" but are "one aspect of higher evolutionary technology" used "to impress upon civilizations purely in the material range of technology that there is a higher energy model controlling the technology"; advanced extraterrestrials look human ("this is the basis of why it is [said] in the book of Genesis, that man was created into the image"); and mankind is "receiving a new program" that supersedes "the old programming of Armageddon."
- He claims Hurtak followed "the usual pattern: illumination, belief in contact, mental changes, and, finally, prophecy" — contact predicted within 18 months (statement prepared with Puharich and friends with access to "confidential and secret information"); eighteen months passed, nothing happened; "That, too, is typical."
- He claims his investigation has now identified "a complete panorama of organizations, cults, and groups that had in common the idea that a superior group of Manipulators was controlling the Earth," on the survey just assembled — Urantia's following, Butler's Fraternity, STELLE, and Hurtak's Sons of Light — with the Order of Melchizedek said to be supreme, intervening "through their agents in religion, the arts, and science."
- He claims his refusal to join was not based on scientific impossibility — "Such unprovable contact, for all I know, may be occurring every day. But contact with what? With genuine beings from space? With some dark part of the human mind, alive with delusions and strange fears?" — and reports becoming "aware of some pretty shady business behind the apparently harmless antics of the contactee groups."

## Concepts
- **the Book of Urantia** — 2,097-page channeled scripture "mysteriously received" in Chicago, printed 1955 by the Urantia Foundation; Urantia is Earth's cosmic name, "the six hundred sixth inhabited world in the local system of Satania... in the constellation of Norlatiadek," in the local universe of Nebadon.
- **the Melchizedeks** — a self-governing order of cosmic beings ("not once throughout all the super-universe of Orvonton have they ever betrayed their trust") devoted to the universe's educational system; they can become visible to mortals and incarnate; the biblical Melchizedek allegedly volunteered to incarnate to initiate Abraham lest "the light of consciousness" be extinguished.
- **seraphic transports** — Urantia's interworld vehicles: mechanical developments provide initial energy for escaping planetary gravity; departures from Jerusem every three seconds at ~25 standard miles per second, not attaining standard velocity until they are over 2,000 miles away from Jerusem; arrivals on the crystal field, "the so-called sea of glass." Vallée's candidate for proto-UFOs.
- **Thought Adjusters** — Urantian entities from other worlds living within human brains, influencing action "by subtle sabotage of our physical and chemical functions"; the topic of the San Francisco lecture Vallée attended.
- **autopsychism vs. the exception** — Sadler's general finding that all psychic phenomena he examined were self-generated ("projection of marginal consciousness without the awareness of the central consciousness"), against the one unclassifiable case behind Urantia.
- **the sleeping-subject channel** — Sadler's case: profound natural-seeming sleep, unwakeable, body never rigid, heart action never modified, respiration sometimes markedly interfered with; the subject a "clearing house for the coming and going of alleged extraplanetary personalities" — student visitors en route from one universe to another, never spirits of the dead.
- **cataleptic-trance trips** — Sadler's 1920 observation that trance subjects "imagine themselves taking trips to other worlds" and that their written accounts are "so unique and marvelous as to serve as the basis for founding new sects, cults, and religions" — Vallée's implicit bridge to abduction narratives; he wonders what Sadler would say of Jane Roberts' best-selling *Seth Material*.
- **the Order of Melchizedek (American branch)** — Hiram Butler's Esoteric Fraternity: revelations received during 14 hermit years, lectures in Boston (late 1880s), a 260-acre mystical center near Applegate, Placer County, California (1889); own publishing house, ~20 books and pamphlets still sold.
- **orgasm-suppression doctrine** — Penn's two great truths: an interior life through which one meets "the inhabitants of the Spirit-world," and the key — retention of "the seed," in which resides a power that awakens the soul to the realm of the "Intelligences who are the creators of man." First Degree renounces orgasm; Second Degree bans all sexual impulse, even in thought, shutting the neophyte's sympathies from "all who belong to generation."
- **mob psychology / the 144,000** — the Fraternity's occult-political program: "the power to overthrow nations" once the sons of God work as a unit; control of the feeling-driven masses; Revelation's promise that the sealed 144,000 "shall take the dominion under the whole heaven and shall reign for ever and ever."
- **Melchizedek priesthood revival** — the tradition's respectable face: priests in the Jewish, Roman Catholic, and Anglican traditions initiated as priests of Melchizedek (Genesis 14, "priest of God Most High" who initiated Abraham); also the higher of the two Mormon priesthoods — a fact the transpersonal-psychology newsletter missed but Hurtak knew.
- **STELLE** — a new Illinois community deriving its entire philosophy from Melchizedek, believed to preside over "the Archangelic Host of our Solar System," helped by billions of Masters from the planet Klarian.
- **the Keys of Enoch** — Hurtak's channeled scientific "codes," received via a beam of light during a 1973 roadside UFO encounter; manuscript title: "The Biocomputer Keys to our Consciousness Time Zone, Revealed to Me by Master Control Messengers Metatron and Opthanim," signed "Enoch," written "in flaming letters and Kabbalistic style" — as impenetrable to Vallée as Grace Pettipher's "magnetic aura."
- **benevolent programmers vs. poachers** — Hurtak's dualism: programmers allow planetary societies free will; poachers "come in and experiment with someone else's experiments" — named poacher Brotherhoods include the Asteriants, the Marinites, and the Reticulants ("of a grotesque appearance"). The test: true visitors bring "a cosmic law" and spiritual teaching, and the more advanced ones look human.
- **Councils of Light / spacetime overlap** — organizational boards where archangels or Ascended Masters organize "all of the codes"; beginning April 18–May 18, 1976, the Brotherhoods would define the blueprint to "literally re-educate and reorganize the human community to find its destiny in space, because it, in essence, had failed the program."
- **the Sons of Light of the Order of Melchizedek** — the think tank Hurtak was told to found, working with Brotherhoods described as "fields of intelligence going between star systems and the reprogramming of lower evolutionary systems"; 70 Brotherhoods comprise the Sons of Light, or White Brotherhood.
- **the contactee pattern** — Vallée's four-step sequence: illumination, belief in contact, mental changes, prophecy — with failed prophecy as the equally typical fifth step.
- **the Manipulators hypothesis** — the common denominator of every group surveyed: a superior group controls the Earth from a higher plane, intervening through agents in religion, the arts, and science; the chapter's pivot from cataloguing believers to asking who benefits.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **The Urantia cosmic hierarchy** — Paper 35's census ("between fifteen and twenty thousand Trinity Teacher Sons exclusive of 9,642 creature-Trinitized assistants of record"); the Melchizedek University near Salvington where all graduating evolutionary mortals will land, offering "Universe Administration" and "Comparative Creature Existence" alongside Energy, Matter, Organization, Communication, Records, and Ethics; Salem missionaries claimed to have penetrated Italy in the sixth century B.C., paving the way for Christianity — world religions as Melchizedek-messenger products.
- **The San Francisco Urantian meeting** — a Sunday afternoon, ten attendees in a small classroom; a tall woman in her late fifties in purple and mauve lecturing on Thought Adjusters beside a chart of Nebadon, Urantia, and the Isle of Paradise. Recorded dialogue: a student asks how the Higher Masters got on our plane ("They were received here, channeled..."); a girl asks if the channel was a Chicago taxi driver ("That's one more story!"); the instructor cites Dr. William Sadler — a fine surgeon who found most mediums victims of suggestion but two exceptions whose data was "definitely coming from a superhuman source." To Vallée's question: the Book was received in 1934 (actually ~1910–11, he notes), published 1955; the typist — a Federal bank examiner, "a very dynamic woman" who typed it three times — still living.
- **Sadler's credentials and investigation** — *The Physiology of Faith and Fear* (Chicago, 1920; Professor of Physiologic Therapy, University of Chicago Medical School; Director, Chicago Institute of Physiologic Therapeutics; by 1929 Senior Attending Surgeon at Columbus Hospital) attacks premature mental-healing literature, charms, relics, quackery, astrology, palmistry, crystal-gazing, trances, catalepsy. *The Mind at Mischief* (1929, "Tricks and Deceptions of the Subconscious and How to Cope with Them") concludes automatic writing never yields supernatural evidence — then appends the exception: contact begun summer 1911, ~250 night sessions attended, stenographer taking voluminous notes, publication withheld by a sacred promise during the subject's lifetime; eighteen years of psychoanalysis, hypnotism, and intensive comparison failed to reveal a psychic origin; material internally consistent, quality varying with "the personalities making the communications."
- **The Esoteric Fraternity at Applegate, 1971** — a journalist finds an old four-story, 18-room house (built from timber on the property, furniture homemade) at the end of a dirt road; two surviving members: William Corecco, 89, fourth president, and his brother Steven, who had recently buried the last female member. Corecco, rocking beside a pot-bellied stove: "The Bible says that when 144,000 persons establish the Order of Melchizedek, the Kingdom of God will be established on Earth. That was our hope... We've all lived on Earth hundreds of times before. The population of the world remains constant. Souls that leave at death reappear in the newborn."
- **Enoch Penn's book (first copyrighted 1926)** — states the Order's philosophy and four degrees of initiation (Figure 7.1); Vallée notes it "contains several of the themes that have since become popular in contactee literature."
- **Vallée's 1976 interview with the Fraternity's representative** — "If the Order exists now, it's probably in Heaven. It's not here; we just have the printed word." On Grace Pettipher: "I don't know her, but women can be redeemed too" — and "we have had many false prophets." On UFOs: Penn and Butler died before the UFO era, but "an angel can sometimes be a UFO as well as a craft being a UFO. And so, unless you develop yourself enough, you might be insulting some angel, calling him a UFO." Vallée's dry verdict: the California Order is "caught between its aspiration to gather 144,000 members and its predilection for avoiding orgasmic release. Someone, after all, would have to make all those babies."
- **The hot-tub initiation, March 12** — newsletter of the California Institute of Transpersonal Psychology (Menlo Park): eleven second-year students initiated into the Order of Melchizedek with Zalman Schachter's inspiration and support, first baptized by Zalman "in the hot tub at Richana's," receiving new names, after a week's work on the Ten Sephiroth and the Kabbalistic Tree of Life; Jan Gaston as congregation. Vallée's frame: this in the middle of Silicon Valley, near Memorex, Lockheed, Amdahl, Hewlett-Packard.
- **The Blavatsky footnote** — Vallée picked up the Melchizedek trail via Leadbeater and a contemporary of Madame Blavatsky; he relays (explicitly unverified) Jacques Bergier's claim that Richard Nixon sits on the Board of the Helena Petrovna Blavatsky Foundation.
- **The Hurtak/Puharich talk show, April 1, 1976 (San Francisco)** — Hurtak, friend of Puharich and figure in the Uri Geller affair, describes his post as visiting sinologist at the Center for Democratic Studies, Santa Barbara (defending it against "commie organization" charges as "a shot in the arm to get better government"); Puharich recounts a contact near Tel Aviv: Geller walked into a UFO at night and "came out carrying a glass object that had disappeared three days earlier"; Hurtak recounts his own: a light following his car down Highway 152, the vehicle landing 100–200 yards off, magnetic attraction, "a beam of light went through my body, and I received scientific codes which I was told to call the Keys of Enoch."
- **The restaurant meeting** — Hurtak presses Vallée to join the Sons of Light; expounds benevolent programmers, poachers (Asteriants, Marinites, Reticulants), Councils of Light, the 1976 spacetime overlap, UFOs as non-traveling higher-energy displays moving "several times the speed of light" via "magnetohydrodynamic energy, parallel-phase electromagnetic energy"; gives political reasons for going public: mankind "was going to go through the collective Christ experience of worshipping UFOs" and the information "would help mankind balance its political focus."
- **The failed prophecy** — Hurtak's closing statement, prepared with Puharich (described as a "sensitive" working for the American Navy) and friends with access to "confidential and secret information": contact by "highly evolved intelligent beings from other worlds" within 18 months, based on prophetic literature, claimed UFO contacts, recent forecasts, "and countless other materials." Eighteen months passed; nothing happened.
