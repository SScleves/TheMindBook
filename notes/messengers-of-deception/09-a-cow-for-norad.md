# A Cow for NORAD

## In one paragraph
Opening Part Three, Vallée turns to the cattle-mutilation wave of the mid-1970s — 700 mutilations in 15 western states in the 18 months before January 1977, more than 180 in Colorado in 1975 alone — and admits he cannot conclusively say whether they are real UFO effects, but argues they matter because they follow the same social pattern as UFO landings: high-strangeness reports by credible witnesses, spurious "evidence," ineffective authorities, press amplification, hoaxers, and academic "explanations" that witnesses throw in the garbage, all spreading "anguish and unformulated expectation" of something dreadful from the sky. Relying on original police reports, state veterinarians, and county coroners (and discarding every predator-possible case), he documents a consistent signature — night, silence, no determinable cause of death, drained blood, specific organs removed with surgical skill, no tracks even in mud and snow, and carcasses left conspicuously in the open ("It was like someone wanted us to find them") — that survives the sociologists' "delusionary spiral" and the Public Relations boys' predator theory: some carcasses show the Rokitansky autopsy technique, and "coyotes and delusionary spirals do not use the Rokitansky technique." He recalls that mutilation has always been a tool of political terror (the fabricated 1919 Munich Thule Society atrocity story that got two hundred leftists executed in reprisal), traces animal effects through older UFO cases (Conway 1953, Isola 1954, Barcelos 1962, the Fitzgerald sheep and steer disappearances, Snippy the Horse 1967) and the international spread (Puerto Rico 1975, Alberta 1976, the 1977 Vosges sheep slaughter that defeated French troops), and endorses the one interpretation that makes sense to him — Frederick W. Smith's: "Someone has been delivering a message to the American people, to the government, the intelligence community." The evidence for a message: carcasses apparently airlifted and dropped where discovery was certain (padlocked pastures, all four legs broken), "signed" mutilations (an udder emptied and packed with gallons of sand, a military-surplus scalpel left behind), and a geographic concentration in Elbert County, Colorado, amid the most sophisticated military installations on Earth — capped by an expert mutilation at the Cheyenne Mountain Zoo and a "standard" one beside NORAD's own guarded entrance gate. His closing inference: since the intelligence community would hide the truth only if it were causing the phenomenon itself or simply didn't know the answers — and a secret agent's first rule is to always appear to know everything — "the so-called UFO censorship may be more of a bluff than a cover-up," and dropping a mutilated cow at NORAD's main entrance "may be a clear indication that somebody is now calling that bluff."

## Core claims
- Vallée claims he cannot conclusively answer whether the mutilations are real and UFO-caused; he can only summarize the facts and show why they are relevant to the possible exploitation of UFO beliefs.
- He lays out the three positions people have taken: (1) natural deaths mutilated by predators, or a few isolated sensation-seekers; (2) an organization secretly conducting large-scale mutilation — a witchcraft cult or a secret government testing program; (3) UFOs and their occupants.
- He claims the data "seems to prove that many of the reported mutilations are real" because — every predator-possible case discarded — the original police reports, state veterinarians, and county coroners describe "surgical techniques that demand both good anatomical knowledge and procedural skill," which "restricts considerably the range of possible suspects"; a Montana official: research by officers, pathologists, veterinarians, and toxicologists "has failed to unravel the mystery."
- He warns the evidence may itself be planted: someone could be simulating UFO events to turn investigators away from the real cause of the mutilation — or the mutilations may be the "next step" in a process directly related to the UFOs.
- He claims mutilation, real or faked, has been a tool of political terror for both left and right throughout history — on the precedent of the fabricated Munich Thule Society atrocity story, which convinced the middle class the executions were a "Jewish conspiracy" and got more than two hundred leftists executed in reprisal; such acts evoke symbols of helplessness and fear, and the isolated countryside under the night sky is especially vulnerable to "ancestral terrors."
- He claims the mutilation problem follows "the same general principles" as UFO landings — credible witnesses, spurious site "evidence," ineffective authorities, press amplification, hoaxers, and academic "explanations" that impress the scientific community into not probing deeper — with the ultimate result of spreading anguish and expectation "of something dreadful that will come from the sky... something swift and pitiless that will reshape human life."
- He claims the mutilations share the UFO landing signature: at night and in silence; no determinable cause of death; blood drained; specific organs cut away; no tracks; and no effort to hide the carcasses — as if discovery were intended.
- He claims his files show peculiar effects on animals wherever UFOs are seen (horses lifted from the ground, dogs paralyzed with their masters), plus claims of animals taken away or left as mutilated carcasses.
- Against James Stewart's "collective delusion" explanation (delusionary spiral; insurance money under the vandalism clause; human killers; wild dogs), he grants all four are valid for a portion of cases "but they are emphatically contradicted by much of the evidence gathered by the police" — citing state-police and Texas Ranger captains who classified cases as deliberate and unexplainable.
- He claims authorities systematically tried to reassure and suppress: a Utah Department of Agriculture veterinarian was told he would be fired if he announced his conclusion that an autopsied animal had died abnormally; French authorities tried to keep the Vosges stories out of the newspapers.
- He claims that, as with many UFO encounters, "the mutilations carried a message" to military authorities — "See how helpless you are; see how obsolete your technology is!" — on the evidence assembled in "The Calling Cards": carcasses airlifted into padlocked pastures, "signed" mutilations (the sand-packed udder, the military-surplus scalpel), and the concentration beside Elbert County's military installations, capped by the NORAD-gate cow.
- He endorses Frederick W. Smith's interpretation as the only one that makes sense to him: "Someone has been delivering a message to the American people, to the government, the intelligence community" — an interpretation that "leaves far behind the 'delusionary spirals' of the social scientists, and the soothing 'predator theory' of the Public Relations boys."
- He claims the phenomenon's characteristics support the message thesis: death without struggle; no tracks or blood (though circular depressions and pod-like marks were sometimes reported); carcasses apparently airlifted and dropped where discovery was maximized; and intense blinding lights from the sky often associated with the events.
- He claims the mutilations' most remarkable feature is their "beautiful precision" because some carcasses show the Rokitansky autopsy technique (viscera removed as an integral unit); he concedes coyotes' razor-sharp claws can produce circular cuts around eaten parts, but a trained investigator can tell natural incident from deliberate mutilation — "Coyotes and delusionary spirals do not use the Rokitansky technique."
- He claims the Colorado concentration in Elbert County — beside Ent AFB, Fort Carson, Peterson Airfield, the Lowry bombing range, the Air Force Academy, Buckley, the Rocky Mountain Arsenal (nerve gas), Rocky Flats (H-bomb triggers), and NORAD's Cheyenne Mountain — supports the message reading, as do "signed" mutilations (sand-packed udder, organs left on top of the carcass, perfect squares and circles of hide, a military-surplus scalpel left behind).
- He claims the mutilation specter dealt "another deadly blow" to the idea of contact with friendly space brothers — freezing believers' blood, arming skeptics, and above all challenging the law-enforcement community (asserted without argument).
- He rejects the believers' cover-up theory (leaders hiding an alien invasion) and infers the censorship is a bluff from two premises he states: the intelligence community has "only two good reasons to hide the truth" — it is secretly causing the phenomenon (its own deception operations), or it simply doesn't know the answers — and a secret agent's first rule is to always appear to know everything; hence "the so-called UFO censorship may be more of a bluff than a cover-up."
- His closing inference: dropping a mutilated cow at NORAD's main entrance "may be a clear indication that somebody is now calling that bluff."

## Concepts
- **the three positions** — Vallée's map of opinion on mutilations: predators/sensation-seekers; a secret organization (cult or government program); or UFO occupants. The chapter tests all three against police evidence.
- **mutilation as political terror** — the historical frame: actual and faked mutilations have served left and right alike as terror tools, loading symbols of helplessness and fear into the public mind; the fabricated 1919 Munich Thule Society atrocity is the type case.
- **the pattern parallel** — mutilations replicate the UFO-landing social sequence (credible witnesses → worthless site evidence → failed authorities → press amplification → hoaxers → rejected academic "explanations"), ending in diffuse dread of "something dreadful that will come from the sky."
- **the mutilation signature** — night and silence; undetermined cause of death; drained blood; specific organs excised; no tracks or traces; carcasses displayed rather than hidden.
- **carcasses meant to be found** — the display quality of the phenomenon, from Investigator Hankes's Covelo remark to carcasses dropped where discovery was maximized; a core support for the message thesis.
- **delusionary spiral / collective delusion** — sociologist James Stewart's four-part explanation; for Vallée the same reflex "explanation" as for UFOs, valid for a portion of cases and emphatically contradicted by police evidence for the rest.
- **predator theory** — the "soothing" official explanation; refuted case-by-case by veterinarians, coroners, and investigators who could distinguish razor-clawed coyote work from deliberate surgery.
- **the Whiteface circles** — the Cochran County, Texas police-report cases: two ~30-foot circles (one burned clean into four-inch wheat), each with a bloodless, precisely mutilated animal at its center; local UFO reports of a round object "the color of the sun when it is going down."
- **the Rokitansky technique** — a standard autopsy procedure removing the viscera as an integral unit; its appearance on carcasses is Vallée's sharpest evidence of skilled human-grade surgery.
- **airlifted carcasses** — animals with all four legs broken, found outside intact fences, in padlocked pastures, in ponds, wedged between rocks, or at places inaccessible even to helicopters, with internal-pressure evidence of a fall.
- **"signed" mutilations** — flourishes that read as deliberate calling cards: an udder emptied to the skin and packed with several gallons of sand; organs cut out and left on top of the carcass; perfect squares or circles of hide; an ordinary military-surplus scalpel left behind.
- **the calling cards / message thesis** — Frederick W. Smith's interpretation, adopted by Vallée: the mutilations are a demonstrative message to the American people, government, and intelligence community — a taunt at radar-and-computer helplessness.
- **the military-installation concentration** — the Elbert County cluster amid Ent AFB, Fort Carson, Peterson, Lowry, the Air Force Academy, Buckley, the Rocky Mountain Arsenal, Rocky Flats, and Cheyenne Mountain; the setting that gives the message its addressee.
- **NORAD / Cheyenne Mountain** — the fortress carved from 45,000 cubic yards of granite, steel buildings on the biggest coil springs ever made, built to detect everything entering North American air space "so that even a needle couldn't get in undetected" — and the site, at its entrance gate, of a standard mutilation.
- **the red-sun legend** — the Zulu legend of "the Red Sun that rises straight up into the sky after devouring some of the tribe's cattle" (the Cherokee have a similar one); folklore precedent for the Fitzgerald sheep and steer disappearances.
- **bluff, not cover-up** — Vallée's reading of UFO censorship: since agencies would conceal only what they cause or what they cannot explain, and agents must always appear omniscient, the secrecy is a bluff — which the NORAD cow calls.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **The Munich fabrication, 1919** — right-wing newspapers accused the left of murdering and mutilating hostages who were members of the occult Thule Society, their sexual organs allegedly severed before they were shot. The story was a fabrication, but it convinced farmers and the middle class the executions were a "Jewish conspiracy"; more than two hundred leftists were executed in reprisal by May 5.
- **The wave in numbers** — 700 mutilations in 15 western states in the 18-month period before January 1977; more than 180 cases in Colorado alone in 1975. Mendocino Sheriff's Investigator Baron Hankes, after studying seven mutilated cattle in Covelo, California (November 1976–January 1977): "It was like someone wanted us to find them."
- **Four older cases** — (1) January 29, 1953, Conway, South Carolina: a farmer hears a commotion in his barn, sees a light-gray, internally lit, half-egg-shaped object about seven by four yards at treetop level and fires his gun at it; numerous cattle afterward found dead of unknown causes. (2) November 14, 1954, Isola, Italy: farmer Amerigo Lorenzini takes cover as a bright cigar-shaped craft lands; three dwarfs in metallic diving suits, speaking an unknown language, examine his caged rabbits; his rifle fails to fire and he feels too weak to hold it; the dwarfs take the rabbits and depart. (3) September 18, 1962, Barcelos, Brazil: three rubber-plantation workers watch a large, bright silvery disk emit sparks and hover near the river, then rise straight up at high speed; cattle disappear from the area. (4) Fall 1969, near Kansas City: two women see an object come close to the ground and hear the yells of an animal being slaughtered as it takes off.
- **The Fitzgerald incidents (mid-sixties, one year apart)** — Natal midlands, Africa: Anton Fitzgerald and farm manager Jock Marais see "an eerie reddish glow" on the farm runway ~200 yards away, the sheep standing in two one-third circles facing it — "like iron filings on a piece of paper around a magnet" (Fitzgerald in *Wings Over Africa*); the pinkish glow rises vertically without a sound; one old sheep is missing. Western Texas: flying with pilot Jake Rugel, Fitzgerald watches an object his companion took for a weather balloon climb straight up with a pink glow more intense at the center; Whiteface Hereford steers on Ted Leslie's ranch had stood in a semicircle in the paddock, and one older steer was missing. Reported to Project Blue Book.
- **Snippy the Horse, Colorado, September 1967** — the first case officially investigated in a UFO context; nothing came of it because the horse had been left too long before scientists arrived. There had been UFO reports in the specific area where the carcass was found.
- **Fort Shaw, Montana, late July 1975** — on Ashlot Bench a mutilated cow is found with teats cut off by a sharp instrument, the grass trampled down for nine feet around the carcass; the farmer had seen a blinking red light at the spot the previous night moving down the road; shortly after, a young boy sees a red light crossing a field inaccessible to vehicles (no vehicle traces found); two cows missing, never found. Captain Wolverton of Cascade County reports sheriff's records "filled with numerous reports of UFO sightings" — saucer-shaped craft, "a giant pear," a two-story-building-sized object with window-like lights.
- **Simms, Montana, December 8–9, 1975** — a rancher's wife and children watch a large white light with a red light on top land where a calf carcass (dead of natural causes) lay about a half mile from the house; ascending, it seems to roll on its side and fly off west at low level; it returns the next evening for about one minute and leaves eastward. The calf is found mutilated; three officers find the udder area removed, cut marks "consistent with those found in earlier cases that pathologists had verified were made with a very sharp instrument."
- **The Stewart rebuttal** — sociologist James Stewart (University of South Dakota, Vermillion) calls belief in mutilations "the result of collective delusion" (delusionary spiral; insurance motives; human killers; wild dogs). Counters: Captain Vern Byler (Criminal Division, Nebraska State Patrol) cites investigated evidence of deliberate mutilation not attributable to predators; Captain W. Wilson of the Texas Rangers says several studied cases were classified unexplainable — "only the tip of the iceberg."
- **The Vaughn, Montana drawing (Figure 9.1)** — a witness in a heavy-mutilation area draws two objects with glowing domes and revolving blue and white lights, a lightning bolt seen beneath them at intervals; observed at dusk for five minutes.
- **The Cochran County police report, March 10, 1975** — Sheriff Richards of Cochran County, Texas, responding to a Whiteface complainant (offense line: "Cattle Mutilation"), finds a perfectly round circle with a heifer at its center, head to the north — bottom jaw cut back, tongue gone, sex organs cut off, navel cut out in a round circle with the meat inside untouched, no blood on the ground or the cow. A quarter mile west, a second ~30-foot circle burned clean into four-inch wheat, with a dead steer. A Reese Air Force Base team finds no abnormal radioactivity; the only related circumstance is frequent recent UFO reports of a round object "about as wide as a two-lane highway... the color of the sun when it is going down."
- **The Utah gag order** — a veterinarian on the staff of the Department of Agriculture is told he will be fired if he announces his conclusion that an animal he autopsied died abnormally.
- **Puerto Rico, 1975** — mutilations occur simultaneously with UFO phenomena; per engineer S. R. Lamarche, the first strange deaths come in February 1975; by April 9 Assistant Secretary of Agriculture F. N. Rodriguez says the problem occupies his department full-time and Federal Meat Inspector I. Fernandez calls the cause of death unknown. Domestic animals, poultry, cows, sheep, and pigs killed, apparently by stabbing with a very sharp instrument; biophysicist Dr. Angel de la Sierra (University of Puerto Rico) investigates an incision "similar to what is done in experimental surgery."
- **The Franceschi encounter, Ponce, April 18, 1975** — a rooster is killed near the house of hospital worker Orlando Franceschi after he observes a dwarfish being float away from him; trying to hit it with a shovel, he suffers temporary paralysis. Independent witnesses had seen (and stoned) the being the same night; the next day two women watch "a vivid orange light hanging stationary above their garage" near Ponce rise silently after five minutes, a glow remaining over the whole area. Sequel: for days afterward Franceschi hears messages "inside his head" warning mankind of disaster unless people started saying more prayers.
- **The international spread** — Ontario 1975 and Quebec 1976; spring 1976, Wildwood, Alberta: two horses found in a snowbank, the uterus and left eye removed from each, the only marks resembling bird prints, the carcasses allegedly flown to Winnipeg for analysis; the same Texas research group reports cases from Brazil, Bolivia, Sweden, Australia, and Scotland.
- **The Vosges slaughter, France, 1977** — April: 34 sheep killed in a single night at Verrières and eight at Vomécourt; 61 sheep and one deer dead within a week. Hunters, forest rangers, and troops of the 43rd Regiment search around the clock; the unknown agent kills five more sheep in Rambervillers anyway. Police blame a wolf ("and a hungry little bastard it must have been"); in one night in early May it kills eight more sheep in Jessarupt, some inside barbed-wire fields. In late September two sheep are killed near Épinal — the only survivors of the earlier slaughter on Mrs. Claude Regit's property. A wolf killed in Lozère was 500 km south; a man with a knife and rifle arrested near Épinal in October 1977 could hardly explain the earlier slaughter. Figure 9.2 shows French press coverage of over $40,000 in cattle deaths in a few weeks.
- **The Colorado mobilization, September 1975** — Governor Richard D. Lamm flies to Pueblo on September 4 with Colorado Bureau of Investigation director John MacIvor to confer with the Cattlemen's Association executive board, calling the mutilations "one of the greatest outrages in the history of the western cattle industry" and adding, "It is no longer possible to blame predators." The same week "the largest force of law enforcement manpower ever assembled in the history of Colorado" is deployed and fails to identify a single suspect; sixty officers from Nebraska, Wyoming, and Colorado coordinate at Fort Morgan with no visible progress. CBI special investigator Carl Whiteside: removal of sex organs and other body parts was "definitely not made by predators."
- **The FBI declines, September 18, 1975** — Senator Floyd K. Haskell discloses he discussed the matter with FBI director Clarence Kelley and his chief assistant; the FBI concludes it has no jurisdiction because there is no evidence of interstate movement.
- **Precision cases** — Adams County (Idaho) Sheriff Jim Hileman: "I've been around livestock all my life, and I've never seen anything like this." September 13: a Hereford cow found across the Garfield County line in Mesa County missing sex organs, rectum, and an ear (per an officer named Hart); a steer found in open country cut open at the ribs with the heart excised; north of Cascade, Montana, Wolverton's unit examines a bull calf whose right hind leg was removed in a circular cut completely through the leg bone below the hip socket, extending toward the midsection and exposing the stomach, all sex organs removed, no footprint or other evidence; a Montana cow found in an undisturbed plowed field, loose furrows intact, three-quarters of a mile from the fence line — two teats and the rectal area removed, an incision under the front leg reaching the heart. The absence of tracks was remarkable because Montana mutilation sites were frequently soft, muddy, or snow-covered.
- **The Cheyenne Mountain Zoo buffalo, October 21, 1975** — a 1,500-pound female buffalo is mutilated at the zoo north of NORAD's mountain: an udder, an ear, and four square feet of hide removed, the vagina cut. Zoo director Dan Davis: "no doubt the animal was mutilated with some sort of sharp instrument handled by man." El Paso County Coroner Dr. Raoul W. Urich: the cutting was neat and clean, "of the type that would eliminate any type of predator," the hide removed without puncturing the tissue underneath — "It was better than I could do if I were trying. It was really an expert job."
- **The NORAD-gate cow, July 6, 1975** — a "standard" mutilation of a cow due to calve in two months, next to the NORAD entrance gate near the unmarked NORAD road (absent from most road maps), inside a military reservation posted every few feet with "VIOLATORS WILL BE PROSECUTED TO THE FULL EXTENT OF THE LAW." Sgt. Robert Stone of the Sheriff's Office "ruled out the possibility that the sex organs were removed by a coyote." The chapter's title case and the epigraph's subject.
- **The airdrop and signature evidence** — a large bull found with all four legs broken; animals outside intact fences; two cows mutilated in Park County found in a pasture with a padlocked gate, not where the owner left them — Sheriff Norman Howey: they "couldn't possibly have been where they were found unless they had been dropped from the air," the carcasses showing internal pressure from the fall; other animals in ponds, straddling ditches, wedged between large rocks, or lying bloodlessly by large trees at places inaccessible even to helicopters. Sheriff George Yarnell's (Elbert County) "most memorable case": an udder "neatly cleaned out, leaving only the skin, which was then tightly packed with several gallons of sand"; elsewhere, organs left atop carcasses, perfect squares or circles taken from hides, and a military-surplus scalpel left behind.
