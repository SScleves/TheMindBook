# The Puppets

## In one paragraph
Vallée closes Part Two by arguing that contactees and occult believers are not messengers but marionettes — "used as puppets" by hypothetical agents he names the Manipulators. He opens with San Francisco street interviews showing the public already primed to accept space beings among us, and with French rationalists who laugh at American cult gullibility while Urantia's largest organization and a branch of the Order of Melchizedek operate in France. His centerpiece contactee is Claude Vorilhon ("Raël"), a Clermont-Ferrand reporter whose December 13, 1973 encounter with a glowing child-like pilot — wearing a swastika-inside-Star-of-David insignia — yielded a "naive, absurd" gospel commanding mankind to eliminate elections, create a world government, a new monetary system, a single language, and abolish military service before the Elohim return; Vallée notes these are the same themes advocated by Mr. T. in his California mansion, and diagnoses the appeal as a simplistic faith in external intervention ("Hitler's 'final solution' for humanity was equally simple"). The phenomenon, he suggests, is a technology "like the television set, that manipulates the perceptions of the human mind" — and the Manipulators may be human. Major Murphy supplies the strategic frame: wartime German circular-aircraft and ELF/mind-control research, Doolittle's unexplained 1946 ghost-rocket mission to Sweden, and 35 years of documented U.S. mind-control projects show a capability exists "to create artificially both the UFO sightings and the effects reported by contactees"; the Manipulators should be sought on Earth, among infiltrated occult groups ("I never said they were wrong... I said they were infiltrated"). Rosicrucian Grand Master Raymond Bernard's 1967 encounters with "Maha" of the "High Council" — hypnotic contacts in Amsterdam and Vienna, staged rituals, instructions to publish books, and the claim that Atlantean knowledge came from another galaxy — read to Vallée as a man who has met a Manipulator in person and been placed under hypnotic control. The chapter's thesis in italics: the first group to harness the fear of cosmic forces and the emotions of UFO contact to a political purpose will wield "incredible spiritual blackmail." Vallée's verdict: the UFO problem is real, it is being manipulated for political ends, and the manipulators may be human beings with a plan for social control — cosmic mythology in such a plan "is not always necessary. But it certainly helps."

## Core claims
- Vallée claims (from the reporter's street interviews, Figure 8.1) that the public is "generally aware of the main features of the phenomenon, and that some people are prepared to accept uncritically the idea that space people are already among us."
- He claims French rationalism has a blind spot: researchers lump UFO cultists with parapsychologists, and Paris-Match (interviewing Claude Poher, 1978) could pose an "incredible" either/or — refuting the parapsychology hypothesis therefore proves objective reality — because "It never occurred to Paris-Match that a phenomenon could be real both in a physical sense and in a psychic sense."
- He claims French intellectuals' dismissal of cults as an American aberration is refuted by the facts that one of the largest Urantia organizations is based in France, as is a branch of the Order of Melchizedek — and by Monsieur Vorilhon.
- He claims Vorilhon's revelation "reads like a space opera written by a twelve-year-old," and that its political core — wipe out democracy, eliminate elections, install a world government, a new monetary system, and a single language, end military service everywhere, or the Elohim will destroy our scientific centers as they did Sodom and Gomorrah — repeats themes already met among the contactee sects ("the same theories are advocated by Mr. T. in his California mansion").
- He claims Vorilhon's own conclusion confesses the key to his appeal: the revelations bring well-being and inner peace because "everything is cleared up and appears simple" — and that this is "the grave danger of contactee beliefs: an unquestioned faith in external intervention that will bring simple answers to mankind. Hitler's 'final solution' for humanity was equally simple."
- He claims the familiar preconditions are: (1) science and education have made the "Good God with a long white beard" unbelievable, while (2) providing no real answers to where we come from and where we are going — leaving a vacuum faith-based illumination fills.
- He claims Vorilhon never explains his story's contradictions — why would the Elohim want democracy destroyed after choosing a prophet from "the country of democracy and freedom"? why use so human and terrestrial a symbol as a swastika inside a Star of David? — and that the answer lies in the phenomenon's own indications: human elements yet alien; physical in appearance yet behaving "like a projection from the unconscious."
- He claims (speculation, explicitly flagged) the phenomenon "represents a technology, like the television set, that manipulates the perceptions of the human mind"; Vorilhon may have had an initial experience and later hallucinated the dialogue — but "hallucination is a big word" and he does not mean nothing was real: "Whatever the technology, it is the effect that is interesting. The new man Vorilhon, like other contactees, is out to change the world."
- He names "the Manipulators" — the hypothetical agents who might cause the UFO contacts and engineer their effects — and runs through the candidate identities (aliens, psychic entities, automata of a nonhuman consciousness, holographic nightmares) before landing on the possibility "right under our nose": humans, "masters of deception so skillful that they plan to counterfeit an invasion from space."
- He claims (italicized thesis) that "Beyond the attention of academic science, below the dignity of official history, there are groups, cults, and sects that serve as 'leading indicators' of mass movements" — and the myth of contact is such a leading indicator. The recurring Atlantis pedigree among "space brothers" itself argues against a far-away star origin.
- He relays Major Murphy's claims (quoted third party, kept distinct from Vallée's own): UFOs might be "psychotronic devices"; by 1943 several countries worked on circular aircraft and the Germans on controlled electrical discharges; captured hardware went mostly to the Russians; modern UFOs began in Sweden in 1946; and much secret research by 1946 concerned mind control and ELF radiation effects on the human body.
- Murphy's scenario: a device obtained by war's end, ineffective as a weapon but emitting radiation causing paralysis and hallucinations as it flew, tested in Sweden in 1946 and the States in 1947, found useless as an aircraft "but very useful as a means of propaganda" — with the open questions: has some group understood what UFOs are and is it confusing the issue by simulating waves? "Or is the entire phenomenon under their control?"
- Murphy's careful three-part caution (which Vallée records as the chapter's analytic backbone): (1) there is a real UFO phenomenon with contactees "since time immemorial"; (2) there is also a capability to create artificially both the sightings and the contactee effects; (3) some group may have already understood — even solved — the whole puzzle, perhaps including "an elegant solution to multidimensional travel." He adds: "silent, disk-shaped flying machines can be built."
- Murphy claims the manipulation need not be retail: "I am not suggesting that secret agents are going around the world giving these thousands of people individual suggestions to see UFOs... A few well-placed stories, a well-planned program publicizing sensational incidents, will do marvels. The contactees are being manipulated. And I think we should not look in outer space for the Manipulators."
- Murphy claims the place to look is "Right here, on Earth," starting among occult groups — ideal cover precisely because intellectuals and "scientific investigators" fear looking silly there — and that contactee stories that seem foolish carry explosive ideas: political control from a "higher level," social unification, resistance to nuclear energy, transcending traditional religions, elimination of the current financial system; the conditioning pattern in Vallée's computer studies "may turn out to be aimed at long-term social changes."
- Murphy's research program: look for direct contacts between such groups and someone fitting the manipulator profile, and for other unexplained effects with the same modus operandi — strong symbolic content, no apparent sense, refused by academic science, subliminal public impact. "The UFOs are only one aspect of their activity, one tool in their arsenal." His parting line on the occult groups: "I never said they were wrong... I said they were infiltrated."
- Vallée claims (own observation) that many occult organizations serve as cover for international espionage on the historical record — "From John Dee and Casanova to Aleister Crowley, occultists have often doubled as spies" — and that the domain's confusion is "blatant and deliberate": its first purpose, to discourage superficial minds and give rationalists a reason to reject everything (asserted without argument), its second, to mislead the rest into blind alleys of temptation, on the example of gold-making, the Adepts' favorite sidetrack — only those uninterested in the literal gold find their way deeper into the labyrinth.
- He claims one thing is certain regardless of whether Atlantis existed: occult groups have preserved from ancient times excellent operating knowledge of hypnosis, suggestion, the laws of forms, and their use for ritual and behavior control — on the evidence that such techniques "occasionally play a role in publicly visible events": Sirhan Sirhan, an AMORC member who used the Order's self-hypnosis technique preparing his role, killed Robert Kennedy in Los Angeles, 1968.
- He claims Raymond Bernard's account of Maha is "the statement of a man who has met in person one of the 'Manipulators,' an important statement, even if the witness is a believer in the occult and a highly trained practitioner of a tradition that is obviously affecting his perceptions" — and that everything Bernard describes (sudden loss of consciousness on a two-syllable keyword, unheard ritual speech, frequent perceptual anomalies, unquestioning trust) "indicates hypnotic control," possibly posthypnotic suggestion. Bernard trusts these people "as completely as the contactees trust their 'space brothers.'"
- He claims the "High Council" probably does not exist as such — the name may have been picked for Bernard's benefit — and notes the investigative dead end: "there is no law against people who think they can run the world by psychic means."
- He claims Bernard's instruction to publish was the point: "a deliberate action on the part of his guides to get their symbols and their message out into the world," with staged rituals described in detail so that other books, movies, and conversations would carry the ideas farther.
- He claims Maha's Atlantis doctrine — knowledge from another galaxy brought by the founders of Atlantis, whose "pure race" persists, linked to unidentified beings who pay in pure gold — knots the occult thread to the contactee thread (the Canigou being who paid in gold, Helen's motor, the Hills' trick map, Vorilhon's "fake Gospel": "were they of the same origin as Maha?").
- He claims the USAF's attempts to understand UFOs were naive and misguided because it looked only for a direct military threat: "It never occurred to the USAF that the threat might be at a different level."
- Chapter thesis (italicized): "The group of people who will first manage to harness the fear of cosmic forces and the emotions surrounding UFO contact to a political purpose will be able to exert incredible spiritual blackmail." Such weapons are less flexible but less detectible than tanks, "a more lasting form of control over the lives of men," slow-acting because secrecy is essential.
- He claims the operation's results are already visible: the contactees and occult believers "have been used as puppets"; the public everywhere associates UFOs with wise space visitors; a majority of Americans believe in such visitors; "They have harnessed Hollywood"; the subject has been kept ridiculous among scientists; "There is in the White House a man who has seen a UFO and is impressed by what he saw"; sects worldwide use "a vague and confusing jargon that protects the unspeakable reality." His flat refusal: "I don't think we should expect salvation from the sky."
- His concluding position (inference, carefully hedged): "I believe there is a very real UFO problem. I have also come to suspect that it is being manipulated for political ends. And the data suggest that the manipulators may be human beings with a plan for social control." Such plans have succeeded before; a cosmic mythology "is not always necessary. But it certainly helps."

## Concepts
- **the Manipulators** — Vallée's name for "the hypothetical agents who might cause the UFO contacts and engineer their effects"; the chapter narrows the candidates from aliens/psychic entities/automata/holograms to the mundane and frightening possibility of skilled humans counterfeiting an invasion from space.
- **puppets** — the chapter's title image: contactees and occult believers as figures moved by unseen hands; "The contactees and the occult believers have been used as puppets."
- **leading indicators** — groups, cults, and sects beneath academic attention and official history that presage mass movements; the myth of contact is one.
- **real both physically and psychically** — the dual-reality position Paris-Match could not conceive; Vallée's standing frame for the phenomenon (physical in appearance, yet behaving like a projection from the unconscious).
- **contactee symbol combination** — contactee insignia recombine well-known designs into new patterns (Figure 8.2 caption): Vorilhon's swastika-inside-Star-of-David, echoing the Melchizedek pentacle's Star of David with spinning pattern; the symbol's stated meaning: "As above, so below, and everything runs in cycles" (cf. Waite on Melchizedek: "He raised the world below to the height of that which is above").
- **the Raelian political program** — eliminate elections and votes ("ill-adapted to the current evolution of mankind"; men as cells of the body Humanity, the brain decides), create a world government, new monetary system, and single unifying language, end military service in all countries — or the Elohim will destroy our scientific centers as at Sodom and Gomorrah.
- **simplistic salvation** — the danger of contactee belief: unquestioned faith in external intervention bringing simple answers; convincing precisely "because it rests on faith alone"; Vallée's chosen comparison is Hitler's equally simple "final solution."
- **perception-manipulating technology** — Vallée's suggestion that the phenomenon is a technology "like the television set, that manipulates the perceptions of the human mind"; what matters is the effect — the "new man" it produces, out to change the world.
- **psychotronic devices** — Murphy's alternative to spacecraft: machines acting on the mind/body (paralysis, hallucination via radiation), rooted in wartime German circular-aircraft and controlled-lightning research and postwar ELF/mind-control work.
- **artificial UFO simulation** — the demonstrated capability to create both sightings and contactee effects; the propaganda method needs no retail suggestion — "a few well-placed stories" suffice.
- **occult groups as cover / infiltration** — organizations ignored by intellectuals are ideal platforms for a clever manipulator; Murphy's formula: the occult groups are not wrong, they are infiltrated. Vallée adds the espionage record (Dee, Casanova, Crowley).
- **deliberate confusion** — the occult hall of mirrors' two purposes: discourage superficial minds (and give rationalists an excuse to dismiss all of it), and sidetrack the remainder with temptations like making gold; only those uninterested in literal gold get deeper into the labyrinth.
- **manipulators' modus operandi** — the search profile Murphy dictates: strong symbolic content, apparently senseless, refused by academic science, subliminally impactful; UFOs as only one tool in the arsenal.
- **keyword hypnosis** — the AMORC-adjacent control technique Bernard describes: a spoken two-syllable "keyword" drops him unconscious; "every man can be linked to others by his 'keyword,' forming infinite series of chains."
- **the High Council (the "A")** — Maha's claimed planetary body, above capitalism/communism ("the truth of one continent" vs. another), vigilant against human tragedy without visibly interfering; probably nonexistent as named — a construct for Bernard's benefit.
- **instructed publication** — the Manipulators' propagation method: Bernard "was instructed to write books about his encounters," seeding symbols and staged rituals into books, movies, and conversations that carry them farther.
- **Atlantis pedigree** — the recurring claim (AMORC, Maha, many contactees) of knowledge preserved from Atlantis — per Maha, brought from another galaxy by Atlantis's founders, whose "pure race" persists and is linked to gold-paying unidentified beings; for Vallée, a mark against literal star origins and for a common manufactured mythology.
- **the Kingdom of Graal** — what the occultists await: contact openly established "When the knights of the Earth meet the celestial knights of the macrocosm" — the cosmic messengers every religion has promised "since Abraham — who got the idea from Melchizedek!"
- **spiritual blackmail** — the chapter's central weapon-concept: political leverage from harnessed cosmic fear and contact emotion; less flexible but less detectible than tanks and aircraft, a more lasting control, dependent on secrecy.
- **ridicule as protection** — keeping the subject disreputable among scientists is itself part of the operation, alongside harnessing Hollywood.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **San Francisco street interviews (Figure 8.1, Chronicle Publishing Co., 1977)** — a reporter records ordinary citizens (a student, a forklift operator, a construction worker from San Leandro) on "UFO beings": one believes the Pascagoula fishermen because they passed shock/lie-detector treatment; one thinks visitors are "inspecting right now... picking out the good ones"; one saw two saucers darting in and out of clouds near Rochelle, Illinois, and says lots of people see space ships "but don't report them because they're afraid of being laughed at." Vallée's use: the public is prepped for uncritical acceptance.
- **The Paris-Match/Poher interview, 1978** — the magazine tells French space expert Claude Poher that refuting parapsychology (suggestion, provoked hallucination) means the phenomena "seem to have an objective reality" — Vallée's exhibit of the physical/psychic false dichotomy, alongside the French scientist who claimed "such crazy movements and cults would never have a chance in France."
- **Claude Vorilhon / "Raël," December 13, 1973, near Clermont-Ferrand** — a young reporter and racing enthusiast, hiking in foggy old-volcano country, sees a blinking red light; a bus-sized craft, conical on top, flashing white light at the apex, red light underneath, hovers two yards up. A stairway appears; a smiling child-like occupant with a glowing body emerges, swastika-inside-Star-of-David on craft and green suit. Dialogue: the visitor speaks "every language on Earth," has come "very often," and today comes to speak. Vorilhon was picked because France is "the land of freedom" where new ideas are received, because he is a free thinker but not antireligious, not a scientist (would "explain the thing simply"), not a professional writer (no convoluted sentences), and born in 1946, after the first atom bomb. Half a dozen morning meetings follow (bring the Bible); he is renamed "Raël" and receives commentaries on Scripture — Man created by scientists of an advanced space civilization, the gods split into camps of Jahweh and Satan, the Last Days of an Age. Published privately by "Message Editions," 1974, as *The Message Given by the Extraterrestrials*. He resolves "It is better to be an illuminated one who knows than an enlightened man who doesn't," answers doubters with promised apparitions in the sky, builds a mansion for the returning Elohim, appeals on French radio and TV, attracts followers, and "waits for the Angels."
- **Vorilhon's San Francisco lecture, December 1976** — about 150 people pay $3 each; by the time he details his trip to the Elohim's planet, a reporter counts five walkouts and "three elderly ladies... lapsed into soothing snoozes." They missed the bathtub episode: in *Beings From Outer Space Took Me to their Planet*, a robot-making apparatus materializes a "splendid-looking dark-haired girl" in a three-dimensional luminous cube (offered with modifiable face and shape), produces five more, and Vorilhon has "an unforgettable bath" with robots who "submitted to all my desires." Figure 8.3 (Chronicle, 1978) shows him lecturing in California for world government and a reformed monetary system on the Elohim's instructions.
- **The Major Murphy conversation** — Vallée drives his contactee-group files to Murphy's house. Murphy, still following government-funded parapsychology research in the U.S. and aware of Soviet advances, proposes "psychotronic devices": 1943 evidence of circular-aircraft programs in several countries; German work on controlled electrical discharges and "controlled lightning"; captured hardware mostly to the Russians; modern UFOs starting in Sweden 1946. Vallée raises General James Doolittle's 1946 mission to Sweden under cover of the Shell Corporation to investigate the "ghost rockets"; Murphy: "His conclusions have never been revealed" — a year before Kenneth Arnold, before Project Blue Book, "Dammit, the Air Force didn't even exist in 1946!" Murphy's timeline analogies: V2s in 1944 → Atlas in the '60s; foo-fighters in '43 → the green fireballs of New Mexico nine years later. Not Nazis specifically — "other groups have become involved": industrial concerns, psychotronic laboratories, with mastery of pharmacology (drugs to distort witness memories) and mind control (to suggest stories, plant fake observations). Vallée pushes back with Charles Fort's *Book of the Damned* (the "tricks" predate any secret weapon); Murphy answers with his three-layer caution and the infiltration thesis, and ends with practical advice: don't chase every reported UFO — "the really important observation" may happen in your own backyard — and "for God's sake, don't waste your time with the UFO groups."
- **The mind-control dossier** — Murphy produces New York Times clippings beginning in 1977 describing part of a 35-year U.S. project involving hypnosis, narcoanalysis, electronic brain stimulation, and the behavioral effects of every physical vibration known to science, from ultrasound to microwaves; and W. H. Bowart's *Operation Mind Control*: an Air Force veteran with amnesia who, like many UFO contactees, vaguely remembered "a good time" but no details; therapy recovered service on a military committee in Vietnam — a long table on a beach, Communist officers on one side, Americans on the other, nothing written down, the man trained for total recall — "then his entire memory of these assignments was erased like a magnetic cassette."
- **Raymond Bernard and AMORC** — Bernard, of Villeneuve-St.-Georges near Paris, heads the European branch of the Ancient and Mystical Order of the Rosy Cross (AMORC), which mails its "sacred knowledge" — traced to Atlantis via Egyptian priests — from lavish headquarters in San Jose, California, with members in all French-speaking countries including large parts of Africa; he reports to the worldwide "Imperator." Context Vallée supplies: occult groups preserve real operating techniques (hypnosis, suggestion, laws of forms, ritual behavior control), and in 1968 Sirhan — Robert Kennedy's killer in Los Angeles — was an AMORC member who "used the Order's technique for self-hypnosis while preparing his role in this tragedy." Bernard used the same technique to obtain visions supporting his leadership role.
- **The Maha contacts, 1967** — "The Manipulators struck in 1967": a "superior being" called Maha, with extraordinary eyes reflecting an entire universe, contacts Bernard; first meeting at the Carlton Hotel, Amsterdam — Bernard finds himself standing before Maha with no memory of rising, perceiving only the pale eyes demanding he abandon all control. A car ride past the Leidersplein to a splendid brick residence in a flower park; Maha declares himself of "the High Council" (the "A"), speaking in the same suggestive "mystic" style as Dr. Pettipher — discourse that "lulls the mind... until it is ready to receive the real message, the 'plant.'" His message: mankind is at the "right level"; the "new cycle" brings global competition but starts badly on a mistaken concept of money; the coming cycle will reverse the scale of values — new social good, abolition of borders, death of nationalism; and groups like AMORC have a specific role in "propaganda and public indoctrination."
- **The Vienna ritual, June 1967** — a man with a fine profile, dark eyes, and white eyebrows; a car with diplomatic-corps plates; an isolated walled house near a forest; a circular room floored in black and white marble triangles, twelve men in white robes. Bernard suddenly cannot hear the celebrants though their lips move; a mounting vibration ("not quite the om sound"); he loses consciousness and concludes he was "examined" — with, Vallée notes, no proof he was not given posthypnotic suggestions. Later the "master" of the house gives him the ritual's color key (red = the cosmic masters, regulators of human evolution), and a spoken two-syllable keyword again drops Bernard unconscious. He is instructed to write books making it all public; he learns the group awaits "the coming of the Kingdom of Graal" — when "the knights of the Earth meet the celestial knights of the macrocosm" — and that the Atlantean knowledge came from another galaxy, its "pure race" persisting, linked to unidentified gold-paying beings whose whereabouts Maha will not reveal even to Bernard.
- **The convergence roll-call** — Vallée's bridge into the "Spiritual Blackmail" section links prior chapters' figures to Maha: the being of the Canigou (long blond hair, hypnotic eyes, unphotographable by Jacques Bordas, paid in pure gold); the scientist who showed Helen the motor; the dwarfs who exhibited the trick map to Betty Hill and gave Vorilhon a fake Gospel — "were they of the same origin as Maha?"
