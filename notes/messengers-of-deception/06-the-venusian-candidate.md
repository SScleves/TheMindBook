# The Venusian Candidate

## In one paragraph
Under an Ellul epigraph on technique replacing the sacred, Vallée asks what the growing "contact" subculture means politically and socially. The believers' mix of philosophy, politics, and pseudoscience "reminds me of classical cases in psychopathology," yet "these people are not crazy" — they are keeping their balance in a world where official science no longer provides good answers, and once irrational beliefs about space visitors are born, irrational beliefs about new social forms on Earth follow. The chapter's centerpiece is his and Steve's Christmas-week visit to Mr. T., the jovial sixty-year-old owner of a decaying California-hills mansion who has founded a political party — with a Board of Governors, chapters on several continents, and two conventions held — to nominate a Presidential candidate on a "Space Platform" that the Space People have "O.K.'d... entirely": recognition of the Space Beings among us and gradual elimination of the money system. His resident scientist Marvin (a physics degree plus "a little graduate work") denies antimatter, blames particle accelerators for suicides near universities, defends suppressed perpetual-motion machines, locates gravity in a 0.3–4.3 mm electromagnetic band, and proposes crystal-activated saucer propulsion; Mr. T. explains that "the Brothers," who control this end of the universe with an all-powerful ray and who sent the Christ two thousand years ago, invisibly sparked the Constitution and the Magna Carta and stand ready to stop Russian missiles from Cuba — "our first line of defense is the Saucer People." What keeps such men from running a really powerful movement, Vallée reflects, is only that too few people believe contact is possible; under the right economic and social fears, "contact with space may become a significant social fact a long time before it is a scientific reality." The second half tracks how the subculture already reaches for real power: contactee groups seized on the UFO sighting made by young Jeff Carter and his father — now President of the United States — and one Italian group "channeled" a message from Adoniensis reminding Jimmy Carter that "this authority has been granted from above," a revival, Vallée argues, of the divine-right legitimacy fundamental to authoritarian government; Gerald Light's Muroc tales and von Däniken's sales show the same eagerness for help from above ("If divine intervention is obsolete for our rational minds, why not have extraterrestrial intervention!"). Letters from Ivan of the Order of Melchizedek and from the SCCF — a "messianic movement guided by Beings who are very evolved" whose programming exists "to traumatise positively and consciously the souls" — close the chapter as Vallée turns toward the most complex contactee language of all, the Urantia sect.

## Core claims
- Vallée claims a new subculture is growing around the idea of "contact" with other beings, and that its political and social significance — not its scientific validity — is the question that matters.
- He claims the believers are not crazy: their mixing of philosophy, politics, and pseudoscientific dreams resembles classical psychopathology, but they are people "trying to keep their balance in a world where 'official' science no longer provides good answers," their taken-for-granted reality shattered by "strange phenomena, designed and manipulated by forces they do not understand."
- He claims a causal sequence: "Once irrational beliefs about space visitors are born, irrational beliefs about new social forms on Earth are likely to follow" — on the evidence of Mr. T.'s group, the extreme form of that process: believers attempting to change the political system in anticipation of massive saucer landings.
- He claims (from observation) that contactees, "although they often seem to agree on the surface, are just as jealous and spiteful in private as academic scientists" — instanced by Marvin's reluctance to discuss Van Tassel.
- He claims Marvin and Mr. T. illustrate a growing mistrust of "big science" — an establishment laymen see as inaccessible, entrenched behind its textbooks — on the evidence of the interview just given: Marvin's antimatter denial, his accelerator-suicide claim, and his suppressed-perpetual-motion theory, plus Mr. T.'s "The petroleum industry also"; expressed in "extreme, fantastic terms, but tomorrow it may find more powerful social forms."
- He calls Marvin's antigravity-frequency-band theory (the 0.3–4.3 mm electromagnetic band, the lead-ball thought experiment) "perfectly absurd," though "we didn't contradict him" aloud (asserted without argument).
- He states he is himself "skeptical about 'impending disasters,' space invasions, and free-energy motors" and does not believe volcanic activity will destroy the Earth or that the Midwest will become an ocean (asserted without argument).
- He infers the chapter's central prediction from two premises he states: rational talk will not convince true believers, and what prevents these men from running a powerful movement is simply that too few people believe contact with Space People is possible; hence a combination of economic and social circumstances could let contactee groups capitalize on public fear — people would blame the scientists for not forecasting the disaster and hold the government in contempt, and "it might become very, very tempting to go out into the desert and wait for salvation from heaven" — therefore: "Contact with space may become a significant social fact a long time before it is a scientific reality."
- He claims the idea that leaders receive legitimacy "from higher levels, rather than from the people, is fundamental to authoritarian forms of government," citing the Kings of France and the Egyptian Pharaohs, established by divine right — and that the UFOs, by suggesting another reality, "seem to hold another form of power, transcending or restricting our individual ability to make decisions."
- He claims it is "but a small step" from the common contactee belief that UFOs watch over the Earth to the idea that they guide our political development — the step taken by Mr. T. (a party to "put a Contactee into the White House") and by the channel of Adoniensis (the President secretly "programmed").
- He claims that although these groups reach only a minuscule audience, "what they express and dramatize has been thought by many people who simply have not verbalized the idea" — on the evidence that the immense success of von Däniken's books shows people are eager to believe we are receiving help from above.
- He claims the danger in this process "cannot be underestimated" because "from the reality of UFO phenomena, it is easy to jump to the conclusion that our space brothers are coming back to help us, and that they are already influencing our leaders" — and that whether a few contactees' vision can change many lives will be answered by the UFO subculture's activities and dreams, which show that "as human technology expands, our range of awareness, rationality, and irrationality grow together to fill the new voids revealed by science, and heal the new anguish it leaves behind."
- He raises but does not resolve the question of whether the group behind the SCCF and the French contactees is "also behind some UFO incidents? Or are they simply another element of confusion?" — flagged as an open question, not a finding.
- He observes that beyond common attitudes and belief in transcendence, each contactee group has its own scriptures, its own channeling method, and its own technical jargon — the most complex being the Urantia sect's, the bridge to chapter 7.

## Concepts
- **the contact subculture** — the growing movement built around belief in communication with other beings; the chapter examines its political and social significance rather than its truth.
- **the Space Platform party** — Mr. T.'s political organization: presents "the Saucer point of view" in resolutions and a platform, holds conventions (Chicago hotel planned), nominates a Presidential candidate (any person over forty), has a Board of Governors and chapters "all over the Earth" (Australia, Africa, France) but, like the major parties, "no members."
- **the no-money system / New Age** — the platform's goal, shared with contactees Cyna and Ivan: eliminate the money system gradually, "in a manner that will not hurt our economy and still will satisfy the people, and the Space People as well."
- **endorsement from above** — the platform's claimed legitimacy: "The Space People have O.K.'d our platform, entirely"; the contact's promise: "one morning you'll wake up, and you and your Party will be in the White House."
- **the Integratron** — George Van Tassel's four-story structure near Yucca Valley, California, built entirely without nails or metallic fasteners to space-people specifications, "supposed to reverse the aging process" (Figure 6.1); emblem of the movement's strange stations and esoteric equipment.
- **big-science mistrust** — the laymen's view of science as an inaccessible establishment entrenched behind textbooks, suppressing inconvenient discoveries; Vallée's warning is that this mistrust may later find "more powerful social forms."
- **Marvin's counter-physics** — antimatter is "just some pipe dream" (accelerator particles are "artificially produced... meaningless"); accelerator radiation causes "subnormal" behavior and elevated suicide rates near universities; nuclear energy is "an abomination"; suppressed free-energy and proven perpetual-motion machines exist ("They passed every conceivable test"); the vested interests, the petroleum industry, and the government suppress the data; NASA is "just as rotten, as corrupt as anything else."
- **antigravity frequency band** — Marvin's claim that gravity is a frequency range in the electromagnetic spectrum between infrared and radar, wavelengths 0.3 to 4.3 millimeters; generate radiation there and "you'll get gravity effects"; much of Earth's gravity "we get from the Sun."
- **crystal propulsion** — Marvin's saucer-drive theory: activating certain crystals with sounds at certain frequencies generates the gravity-range frequencies at high intensity — echoing, Vallée notes, the crystals from his own Mojave expedition.
- **the Brothers** — in Mr. T.'s cosmology, the one group of saucers that "control this end of the universe": they brought the Christ to Earth two thousand years ago, keep him on a Mother Ship, and possess a ray "absolutely more powerful than anything in the universe" that can destroy "even several universes away" — "So we are absolutely under their control, right now."
- **the three-day blackout** — the modern contactees' one firm prophecy per Mr. T.: a three-day period in which the Earth is covered completely, with no Sun or stars visible; otherwise, "we've never made prophecy in our platform."
- **the Saucer People as first line of defense** — Mr. T.'s political strategy: the Space People don't make our laws but watch "around the corner" and have promised to step in if Russia threw missiles from Cuba; the organization's purpose is "to protect first of all the United States of America."
- **saucer-sparked liberty** — Mr. T.'s claim, "which will surely come as a shock to historians," that the Constitution, the Magna Carta, and "all of the free instruments for the freeing of man" were sparked invisibly but absolutely by the Saucer People — who "can prove that it is"; plus the unknown man who occasionally rises in the Halls of Congress, sways the audience, and disappears.
- **contact as social fact** — Vallée's thesis-phrase: belief in contact can reshape society long before (and regardless of whether) contact is scientifically established; the hinge between the funny interview and the serious warning.
- **legitimacy from above** — the authoritarian principle (divine right of kings, Pharaohs) that the contactee movement revives in extraterrestrial dress; contrasted with the modern pride in leaders who are "people like ourselves" whom we can force from office.
- **the Adoniensis message** — the channeled telepathic message to "Terrestrial Brother Jimmy Carter" published by an Italian group: his authority "has been granted from above," their "terrestrial operator" long knew of the process bringing him to "the supreme charge of a continent" (Nicolosi, 4 November 1976, 12:30).
- **the Order of Melchizedek (Ivan)** — Ivan's Paris-based order (he claims responsibility for Israel, Africa, Europe, India, and China); membership is "by direct divine inspiration, or something close to it"; saucers seen over Paris "as predicted in his leaflet"; alongside it Ivan founded the Front for Christian Liberation, Jesus Revolution, Jesus People Europe, the Charismatic Christian, Jew, and Arab Movement, and the Christian Socialist Party.
- **Study Center of Cosmic Fraternity (SCCF)** — Valverde, Italy group (symbol: the Adamski saucer over two stars shaking hands; motto "FROM HEAVEN TO EARTH"), source of the Carter message; self-described as "no characteristic of a human organization... a messianic movement guided by Beings who are very evolved coming from space," with secretariats all over the world.
- **positive traumatization / programming** — the SCCF's stated method: "a programmation that lends itself mainly to traumatise positively and consciously the souls particularly predisposed to receive the teachings of the Superior Beings" — for Vallée, "another organization which attempts to 'program' us."
- **contactee jargon** — each group's private scriptures, channeling method, and technical vocabulary; the differentiator beneath the surface agreement, maximal in the Urantia sect.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **The visit to Mr. T.** — Christmas week; Vallée and Steve drive a station wagon to a mansion in the California hills — palm trees, view of the city, but cracked walls, empty balconies, no flowers or lights. Mr. T. (introduced in the text as founder "Mr. X"), a jovial man of sixty in white shirt and suspenders, receives them under a tapestry at the foot of a formal staircase with "his colleague Marvin," the group's resident scientist. He describes the party: Space-People-approved platform, two major conventions held, a third planned for a downtown Chicago hotel (Steve's Conrad Hilton joke — 1968 riots — sails past him), open nomination ("just so they're forty years old") for "the coming election for President"; membership structure copied from the Republicans and Democrats (no members, a Board of Governors, chapters with their own officers in Australia, Africa, France). Currently "striking for money."
- **The Van Tassel Rejuvenator / Integratron** — Steve asks about the Arizona [sic; the structure is in the California desert near Yucca Valley] contactee George Van Tassel, building a device under space-being instructions with "a part missing"; Marvin won't engage — Vallée's evidence of private jealousy among contactees. Figure 6.1 shows the four-story, nail-free Integratron (photo courtesy of The Hefley Report, Psychic/UFO News).
- **Marvin's interview** — degree in physics, some graduate work, then a decision to "completely reexamine these laws"; denies antimatter ("I could give reasons why antimatter couldn't possibly exist"; the particles' infinitesimal lifetimes show "there is something fishy"); blames accelerator radiation for abnormal, "subnormal" behavior and a higher per-capita suicide rate near universities (Vallée privately notes he'd always assumed the mental condition of Berkeley/Stanford eccentrics "was a prerequisite rather than a result of their occupation"); asserts suppressed free-energy devices and physicist-proof perpetual-motion machines; gives the 0.3–4.3 mm gravity-band theory with a lead-ball thought-experiment (thermal agitation radiating an infinitesimal percentage in the gravity range); proposes crystal-activated propulsion; explains suppression economically — rewritten textbooks, billions in scrapped equipment, scientists "drawing nice fat salaries" who "hope it won't come out in their lifetime"; dismisses NASA as rotten and corrupt. Vallée and Steve deliberately do not contradict him.
- **Mr. T. on religion and power** — the prophecy problem ("I can't tell you the time or the hour that my Father will come again" treated as "a statement of fact"), the three-day blackout, the Brothers who brought the Christ (crucified, "kicked him in the pants") and keep him aboard a Mother Ship as "a Prince of this end of the universe," the universe-destroying ray, and the Space People's promised intervention against Cuban missiles; the Constitution/Magna Carta revelation; the vanishing orator in Congress ("That's happened many times").
- **The Carter sighting** — quoted interviews: young Jeff Carter remembers thinking it a falling star and telling his father to "make a wish" before the UFO moved and changed color ("now it's red... now it's green... My God..."), trembling without knowing why; the father describes an incandescent mass "as brilliant and as big as the Moon," no noise (though Jeff reported a metallic sound), ten minutes of open-mouthed watching, course changes south then east. Vallée's reveal: the sighting was in 1973 [as given in the text], the young man is Jeff Carter, and his father is President of the United States — a fact "not escaped [by] contactee groups around the world."
- **The Gerald Light claims** — Light claims he journeyed to Muroc Air Base in April 1954, allegedly with Franklin Allen of the Hearst papers, Edwin Nourse of the Brookings Institute, and Bishop McIntyre of Los Angeles; cleared into the restricted section for two days, he says he saw five types of extraterrestrial spacecraft "being studied and handled by our Air Force officials, with assistance and permission of the Etherians!"; the same Light writes that President Eisenhower was "spirited away" to Muroc one night during a Palm Springs visit, also April 1954, to meet the "aliens." Presented by Vallée strictly as claims by small groups reaching a minuscule audience.
- **Ivan's letter from Paris** — Melchizedek duties across five regions; Paris saucer sightings the previous December "as predicted in his leaflet: 'the reports are on file'"; new demonstrations for the Gulag Archipelago inmates and "for the animals" ("as far as the terrestrial side is concerned. Politics again."); his portfolio of five other organizations; membership by "direct divine inspiration"; his claim that the SCCF, his group, and correspondents like Vallée himself will "play a great role in the future."
- **The SCCF letters** — typed stationery from Valverde, Italy: the self-description as a non-human, non-sect messianic movement with worldwide secretariats, and the "programmation... to traumatise positively" passage; plus the newsletter message from "an extraterrestrial in mission on your planet" noting "the incapacity of your political, economic, and scientific organizations" and warlike expenditures, denying they limit themselves to observation while not yet at "the moment of our most solid intervention," and promising in capitals: "OUR HELP WILL BE REVEALED IN THE MOST APPROPRIATE TIME, WHEN YOU WILL ALL BE CAUGHT 'RED-HANDED.'" (Nicolosi, 13 January 1977, 12:25).
