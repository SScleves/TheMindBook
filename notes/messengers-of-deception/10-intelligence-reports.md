# Intelligence Reports

## In one paragraph
Opening under two military epigraphs — a U.S. State Department report of an Iranian F-4 scrambled after a UFO on September 19, 1976, and a Boca Chica security-squadron member's testimony that a Cuban MIG-21 exploded while intercepting a "bogey," after which the NSA seized all tapes and ordered the loss filed as equipment malfunction — Vallée dissects how officialdom actually processes the mutilation wave. His centerpiece is an October 1975 preliminary Intelligence Report prepared for the U.S. attorney in Minneapolis, nominally on "occult activities" but really on cattle and human mutilations and UFOs, built on the jailhouse "confessions" of convicts Albert K. Bankston and Dan Dugan: a Satanic organization called "the Occult," biker clubs, human sacrifice at Cozad, Nebraska, tranquilizer guns and PCP, an assassination list including Hubert Humphrey and Kim Novak. The tale dovetailed with real police files (occult murders near Aspen and Vail; every person named existed), pulled in dozens of agencies plus the FBI and ATF — and collapsed when both convicts escaped on successive days in late May 1975, exposing the story as a ploy for lenient treatment; by late 1975 investigators agreed cult involvement was the exception and "the real culprits were much more powerful." Vallée then walks the ranchers' rival theory — military helicopters, government drug experiments, the remembered Utah nerve-gas sheep deaths — noting they fired on Fort Carson helicopters, yet mutilations continued on stormy nights when nothing could fly. The government theory is not far-fetched, he argues, because counter-insurgency doctrine has used faked mythology before: staged vampirism against the Huks in the Philippines, "evil eye" corpse-mutilation in Vietnam, even a plan (per Church Committee testimony) to simulate the Second Coming off Cuba to topple Castro. Whatever the aircraft facts, mutilations belong to UFO folklore back to 1897, and the taken organs — eyes, ears, tongue, genitals, the organs of communication and reproduction — mark the culprits as "good surgeons, but also... good psychologists." A law-enforcement interview (the scalpel-through-rectum cow, the silently slaughtered pig under a blacked-out photocell light) defeats the predator explanation. The chapter closes with Vallée's darkest synthesis yet: infiltrated contactee groups spreading racial theories of "extraterrestrial blood," the same process traced from Adamski and Williamson to Parsons and Hubbard — "a very dangerous process right here on Earth, whatever the ultimate source of the UFO phenomenon happens to be" — and the possibility that the whole manipulation of UFO belief "has been a grotesque hoax" to bend social behavior. Who decided in what direction? Premature to answer, "But it is not premature to ask."

## Core claims
- Vallée presents (as quoted documents) two military interception incidents: the Iranian F-4 scramble from Shahrokin AFB on September 19, 1976 (State Department report), and the Cuban MIG-21 destroyed while attacking a bogey, after which, per a member of the 6947th Security Squadron at Boca Chica, orders came "within hours" to ship all tapes to the National Security Agency and log the incident as "aircraft loss due to equipment malfunction."
- He claims the October 1975 Minneapolis Intelligence Report matters no matter how false the "confessions" were, because it shows the public now files UFOs, mutilations, and fringe seers in the same mental category, reveals how thoroughly police informers have infiltrated these groups, and exposes "the extreme naïveté" with which investigations of this scope are handled.
- He notes the special agent's own opening statement in that report confirmed all the mutilation patterns already established in Chapter 9.
- He documents (from the report) the Bankston–Dugan story: a secret Satanic organization, "the Occult" [sic], responsible for the mutilations, using animal organs in rituals, involving motorcycle clubs, human sacrifice, and an assassination list of prominent liberals.
- He claims the story succeeded because it dovetailed with real data — Texas Cattlemen's Association and Texas Ranger interviews describing an identical occult structure; four 1974 Colorado murders with occult elements; every named person real, most interested in witchcraft — but that all this "could easily have become known to Dugan and Bankston through their contacts in the underworld"; the story was "a mixture of fiction and reality, but many people were ready to swallow it."
- He claims ufologists' standard dismissal — "It's all been solved as a simple cult activity" — could not survive his three 1976 questions: which cult (at least three "Satanic" organizations in northern California alone), why no arrests, why the mutilations continued. Police normally crack such cases fast (the Queens "warlock" skull case: five arrests, November 1977).
- He reports the special agent's recantation: some men Dugan placed at mutilation sites were in jail at the time; the convicts' back-to-back escapes (Bankston May 31, 1975 from his Minnesota county jail; Dugan the next day in Texas) revealed the confessions as tall tales for lenient treatment. By late 1975 investigators' consensus was that cult involvement was the exception, the real culprits "much more powerful": "The Occult Theory was inadequate."
- He claims the ranchers' military theory split into two schools — a ritual-performing sect inside the military, or massive government drug experiments — fed by memory of the "nerve gas" deaths of thousands of sheep in Utah, long denied by military authorities, and of government research on epidemics, mind control, and drugs on unsuspecting victims; ranchers fired on low-flying Fort Carson helicopters, and several returned to base with bullets in their sides.
- He notes a Fort Carson pilot-officer arrested for drug traffic was suspected of night-flying animals to witch covens — "tantalizing allegations... never proven" — and that no one explained how a single group could cause hundreds of incidents in several states; hilltop pickup vigils with CB radios changed nothing, and mutilations continued even on stormy nights when helicopters could not fly.
- He claims government involvement is not far-fetched because ranch families included men returned from Vietnam and the Philippines who had themselves used mythology in counter-insurgency operations to achieve political change — precedents he cites: the Philippine fake-vampirism operation against the Huks, Vietnam Special Forces gouging out enemy eyes to exploit the "evil eye" myth, and Church Committee testimony about a plan to simulate the Second Coming of Christ with submarine-launched flares off Cuba; "mutilation, the simulation of paraphysical phenomena, and exploitation of local beliefs are indeed familiar tools in the arsenal of some government agencies."
- He claims the helicopter theory cannot be completely disproved: silent aircraft now exist whose maneuvers resemble UFO behavior, and equipped with lights (as Vietnam-type helicopters are) they "would closely duplicate what UFO witnesses have reported."
- He claims mutilations are woven into ufological folklore regardless of the aircraft question: the 1897 cow-butchering report (later denied by descendants of those involved), Snippy the Horse, animal and poultry thefts by ufonauts in France in 1954, the human dissections linked to the UMMO affair in Spain, and gory incidents across half the American states and Puerto Rico.
- He claims the UFO phenomenon's attached symbols are "the primary images of life: blood, death, sex, time, space, and sky" — material on which Jung "could expand vastly" (asserted without argument).
- He infers the culprits are "good surgeons, but also... good psychologists" because the organs taken — eyes, ears, tongue, genitals — are precisely the organs of communication and reproduction.
- From his northern-plains law-officer interviews he claims the surgical sophistication (organs removed through the rectum by cutting ligaments; an eye extracted through an incision above the socket) rules out predators, and the photocell-blackout pig case defies any conventional staging.
- He claims a dangerous social process is visible now: contactee groups infiltrated by "shadowy figures from various factions," leaders voicing new political and social ideas, and racial theories appearing with increasing frequency — that some people are "of extraterrestrial blood" and therefore "superior" to the rest of mankind — a process he has traced around Williamson, Adamski and associates, some Rosicrucian groups abroad, the Order of Melchizedek, Jack Parsons (a founder of JPL), and Ron Hubbard (founder of Scientology).
- His summary inference: "we are not dealing with the preliminary stages of a friendly landing from outer space" but with "a very dangerous process right here on Earth, whatever the ultimate source of the UFO phenomenon happens to be."
- He explicitly limits his evidence: "I confess I do not have any proof that a single group of men is responsible"; he cannot tell whether the UMMO human mutilations in Spain and the cattle mutilations of the western United States share an author ("Things are not usually so simple, as Major Murphy had said"), nor whether the groups behind contactee sects also discourage scientists from examining UFO evidence; what he called "manipulation" may be "simple exploitation."
- He closes with the working hypothesis of Part Three: the manipulation of UFO belief may be "a grotesque hoax, perpetrated on thousands of unsuspecting witnesses, in order to use the minds and emotions of the contactees as a means for influencing social beliefs and behavior" — leaving open the question of who chose the direction.

## Concepts
- **the Minneapolis Intelligence Report (October 1975)** — a preliminary report for the U.S. attorney in Minneapolis titled "Intelligence information regarding occult activities throughout the United States," actually centered on cattle and human mutilations and their relationship to UFOs; the chapter's case study in how officialdom absorbs and mishandles the phenomenon.
- **"the Occult"** — the secret Satanic organization of Bankston and Dugan's jailhouse story (Vallée keeps the report's "[sic]"): mutilators using animal organs in rituals, with motorcycle-club involvement, human sacrifice, and "definite political and racial overtones."
- **the confession hoax** — the mechanism by which the report was built: convicts trading elaborate "revelations" for jail transfers and confidence, then escaping when leniency failed; a mixture of fiction and reality that dozens of agencies swallowed.
- **dovetailing** — why the hoax worked: the story matched data already in investigators' files (occult murders, real names, witchcraft interests), all of it plausibly available through underworld channels; corroboration by file-matching proves nothing about mutilation involvement.
- **the Occult Theory's inadequacy** — the late-1975 investigator consensus: cult involvement is the exception, not the rule, and "the real culprits were much more powerful"; the ufologists' "solved as a simple cult activity" is the answer that survives only if you ask no questions.
- **the helicopter / government-experiment theory** — the ranchers' two schools: a ritual sect inside the military, or mass drug testing on animals; motivated by "unmarked helicopters" at sites and by memories of the Utah sheep deaths and covert human experiments; wounded by the stormy-night counter-evidence, but not disprovable given silent aircraft.
- **counter-insurgency use of mythology** — the documented precedent making the government theory thinkable: staged vampirism in the Philippines, "evil eye" mutilation in Vietnam, the planned simulated Second Coming off Cuba; proof that agencies deliberately manufacture paranormal belief to achieve political change.
- **mutilation as UFO folklore** — the lineage from the 1897 cow-butchering story through Snippy the Horse, the 1954 French ufonaut thefts, and the UMMO human dissections to the current wave; the belief-content of mutilations exists independently of who or what performs them.
- **primary images of life** — blood, death, sex, time, space, sky: the symbol set attached to the UFO phenomenon, echoed in fringe religion and occultism; Vallée's Jungian observation.
- **organs of communication and reproduction** — the pattern in what is taken (eyes, ears, tongue, genitals); the basis for calling the culprits "good psychologists" as well as good surgeons — the selection itself is a message.
- **the photocell-blackout case** — the officer's unsolved pig mutilation: lights killed for twenty minutes (replicable only by continuously spotlighting the photocell), a pig slaughtered noiselessly 200 yards from a family at dinner; the case that defeats both predator and simple-hoax explanations.
- **extraterrestrial-blood racial theories** — the newly frequent contactee doctrine that some humans are of alien descent and "superior"; for Vallée the clearest sign that the process is political and dangerous, continuous with the fascist-adjacent lineage of the early contactees.
- **manipulation vs. exploitation** — Vallée's 1978 hedge: civilian UFO groups, contactee organizations, occult sects, and government branches are "engaged in a strange sort of game"; what looks like orchestration by one hidden group may be many parties opportunistically exploiting the same belief.
- **the grotesque-hoax hypothesis** — the chapter's terminal formulation: UFO belief manipulated to use contactees' minds and emotions as instruments for influencing social beliefs and behavior; stated as a possibility to be investigated, not a conclusion.

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **The Iranian interception, September 19, 1976** (chapter epigraph, U.S. State Department report) — after reassuring citizens it was "only stars," an official sees a star-like object, bigger and brighter, and scrambles an F-4 from Shahrokin Air Force Base to investigate.
- **The Cuban MIG-21 loss** (chapter epigraph, testimony from a member of the 6947th Security Squadron, Boca Chica Naval Air Station, which monitored Cuban military communications) — Cuban installations report a bogey approaching from the northeast; two MIG-21s scramble; Air Defense HQ orders the wing leader to arm weapons and destroy the object; seconds later the wingman screams that the leader's aircraft has exploded. Within hours, orders arrive to ship all tapes and intelligence to the NSA and file the incident as aircraft loss due to equipment malfunction.
- **The Bankston letters** — prison inmate Albert K. Bankston writes to authorities claiming to know the mutilators: a Satanic cult he wants to help arrest, but he fears reprisals and asks transfer to a Minnesota jail. He names motorcycle clubs, human sacrifice, and an assassination list of prominent liberals including Senator Hubert Humphrey and actress Kim Novak; his source is a former Hell's Angel met in jail who talked of "the Occult."
- **The story of Dan Dugan** — on Bankston's word, the U.S. Attorney's office (through Judge Lord) issues a writ; Dugan is transferred March 16, 1975 to Dakota County Jail, Hastings, Minnesota, and eagerly interrogated. His tale: recruited into "the Occult" through Texas drug contacts; witnessed small-animal mutilations; in 1969 observed a human sacrifice at Cozad, Nebraska — four teenagers shot with tranquilizer guns, given massive PCP injections, dead about two hours later, mutilated, and used in "atrocities too horrible to mention." Cultists killed animals with tranquilizer pellets, took a "thrill" in leaving no tracks, and "often talked about how the authorities would sooner or later begin blaming the whole thing on UFOs, which is what came to pass." He names names, including a Bell Helicopter employee already suspected of narcotics traffic, burglary, and fencing — of interest because of frequent "unmarked helicopter" reports at mutilation sites.
- **The corroboration that wasn't** — Texas S.W. Cattlemen's Association investigators and a Texas Ranger Captain interview a young couple whose description of Texas occult structure matches Bankston and Dugan's "almost identical[ly]"; Colorado state investigators report four 1974 murders with occult elements (two near Aspen, two near Vail); every person Dugan named existed and had police records, most with witchcraft interests. Vallée's caveat: all knowable through the underworld.
- **The collapse** — Vallée, told in 1976 the mutilations were "solved as a simple cult activity," asks which cult, why no arrests, why the killings continue; getting no answers, he contacts the report's author. The special agent no longer believes his own report: some named men were in jail when Dugan's story placed them at sites. On the last day of May 1975 Bankston escapes his small Minnesota county jail; the next day Dugan escapes his Texas jail; both are recaptured. Consensus: tall tales to win jailers' confidence and leniency, executed as an escape plan when that failed.
- **The Queens "warlock" case (footnote)** — contrast showing police normally solve occult cases fast: the Queens DA's office investigates a self-styled warlock selling human skulls to pagan seers; November 1977, five arrests, bail set at $25,000 by a judge who called cemetery desecration a crime that "defied description among civilized people."
- **The Fort Carson shootings** — ranchers seeing helicopter-like objects at night begin firing on machines leaving or returning to Fort Carson at low altitude; several come back with bullets in their sides. A Fort Carson pilot-officer is arrested for drug traffic and suspected (never proven) of night-flying animals to local witch covens. Ranchers keep hilltop vigils in pickups linked by CB radio; the mutilations continue, even on stormy nights when weather grounds all helicopters.
- **Counter-insurgency precedents** — Philippines, anti-Huk campaign: troops instructed to fake vampirism — enemy dead strung upside-down from tree limbs, jugulars pierced with small incisions, found drained of blood with apparent "teeth-marks," presumed victims of immortal enemies. Vietnam: Special Forces gouge out enemy soldiers' eyes and leave them on the corpses' backs to exploit the "evil eye" myth. Church Committee testimony: a plan to simulate the Second Coming of Christ using flares launched from a submarine off Cuba, to help overthrow Castro.
- **The Puerto Rico correlation map (Figure 10.2)** — investigator R. Lamarche's map of unusual phenomena in Puerto Rico (Flying Saucer Review, June 1975, p. 9) showing an apparent correlation between UFO sightings and mutilation cases.
- **Figure captions as data** — farmer Reuben Olson with a calf mutilated near Kiowa, Colorado, August 1975 (Colorado Bureau of Investigation agent Carl Whiteside: catching the culprits "was like chasing a ghost"); a Holstein calf found October 28, 1975 near Middleton, Idaho, sexual organs, rectum, and tongue removed per local law enforcement.
- **The northern-plains officer interview** — first-hand detail from a law-enforcement investigator: a cow's sex organs removed not from outside but by a scalpel introduced through the rectum, cutting the ligaments — "That takes someone with good knowledge of anatomy"; eyes taken out through an incision above the socket, which no predator does; and the unsolved photocell case — a farmer at dinner planning to fix his failed automatic light system finds, twenty minutes later, a pig dead and mutilated 200 yards from the house among the other pigs, no sound, no disturbance; the officer verifies with colleagues that a patrol-car spotlight aimed at the photocell can switch the system off, but cannot imagine anyone holding a light on it for twenty minutes while noiselessly mutilating a pig. The officer, since taken off such cases: "I'm sorry we got involved in this problem in the first place."
- **The contactee-fascist lineage recapped** — the infiltration-and-radicalization process Vallée has shown at work around early contactees Williamson and Adamski and their associates, behind some Rosicrucian groups abroad and the Order of Melchizedek in the U.S., with the same links traceable around Jack Parsons (founder of JPL) and Ron Hubbard (founder of Scientology) — the bridge into Chapter 11's stratagem theories.
