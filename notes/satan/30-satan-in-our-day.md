# Ch 30 — Satan in Our Day

## In one paragraph
The volume's last essay, written by the Benedictine Dom Alois Mager, Dean of the Faculty of Theology at Salzburg, immediately before his sudden death from overwork on 26th December 1946 — his name is printed under a dagger, and the piece is prefaced by an editorial note from Père Bruno de Jésus-Marie. Mager's thesis is that our period differs from the preceding one in that it *proves experimentally* what was formerly only apprehended theoretically: modern man, having taken himself as the theme of his own spiritual evolution, has stopped understanding himself as a *res cogitans* and descended into "the lowest strata of instinct and appetitive power" — precisely the double instinct (preservation of the individual and of the species) most damaged by original sin — where he comes into "immediate contact with the satanic" and, if he cannot conquer it, must succumb. Against true mysticism, which enters the underworld of demons to vanquish it, Mager sets a "satanic mysticism" that enters in order to legitimise and deify that underworld and to become its medium; he calls two witnesses to prove it, modern literature and National Socialism. Literature (Bernanos, Gide via Du Bos, above all Dostoievski as read by Karl Pfleger) discovered demonism as an individual and literary phenomenon; National Socialism made it general, seizing a whole society and erecting the threefold concupiscence of original sin into the highest values of culture — the apotheosis of the concupiscence of the eyes (*lebensraum*), of the flesh (schools, Hitler Youth camps, the *Ordensburgen*, the S.S. barracks) and of the pride of life (*Herrenvolk*) — with Adolf Hitler as "the medium of Satan". Applying Christ's own criterion in John viii. 44, Mager identifies the two infallible signs of the satanic as lying and murder, documents both from Nazi sources and from a casualty ledger reprinted from *Le Chaos Européen*, and argues that satanic power is in fact impotence: he who can only annihilate annihilates himself, and the devil boasts, insults and curses precisely because he is a coward. The essay — and the book — ends by warning that extirpating National Socialism would not by itself remove "the satanic element" from our period, since its spirit of conscious neo-paganism "slips in everywhere", and that one power alone can drive the devil back into the pit: redemption through the Cross, sealed with the exorcism formula "Ecce crucem Domini, fugite partes adversae."

## Core claims
- Mager claims the present period differs from its predecessor in kind: "it is proving experimentally what before was only apprehended theoretically." Man himself is the theme of the present spiritual evolution, and man is now to be understood "in himself, independently of the supernatural world."
- He claims that until now man understood himself only as a being of consciousness and knowing, "a *res cogitans*"; today he descends into the lowest strata of instinct and appetitive power, to the two fundamental forces which preserve the individual and the species.
- He claims this double instinct of preservation was, more than any other, seriously damaged by the consequences of original sin — so that a man who, through some profoundly felt experience, comes down to the last depths of original corruption "finds himself in immediate contact with the satanic, and if he is not able to conquer it, he is bound to succumb." This, he says, is precisely what characterises the events of our day.
- He claims there exists a *satanic mysticism* exactly parallel and opposite to true mysticism: true mysticism resists and conquers the underworld of demons for the sake of salvation; satanic mysticism penetrates the same underworld "not to vanquish it, but to legitimise it, deify it and assume the role of medium for it."
- His method is explicitly evidentiary: "To prove the truth of this I shall call two witnesses: modern literature and National Socialism."
- He claims the French and Russian novelists — not the philosophers — were the discoverers here: Nietzsche had uncovered the satanic depths before them, but it was "the men of letters, the masters of living psychology", whose shrewd prevision foretold what has become an immediate reality to the world of today.
- He claims that in theological language the consequences of original sin "are not in themselves demonic: they are human: but they are *points of entry* for demons"; the doors open when man consciously and experimentally lets himself be guided by the triple result of original sin in thought, will and action — and the man who chooses that slavery "gives himself up to the action of Satan and himself becomes, gradually, a demon."
- He claims demonism in literature remained an individual and quasi-literary affair, whereas in National Socialism it "seized hold of a whole society" with the deliberate intention of swallowing first the nation and finally the whole world, becoming a general phenomenon and a form of life on which a new organisation of the world and humanity was to be based.
- He claims the motive force of National Socialism "proceeds directly from the triple consequences of original sin", and that its highest ideal — the aim above all others — was to fulfil positively, as the highest values of human culture, the appetites of the threefold concupiscence; those who opposed this ideal "had simply to be exterminated."
- He claims never in history have the lust of the eyes, the lust of the flesh and the pride of life "been presented so cleverly and with such conviction, as being the very opposite of their true essence", and labels the three Nazi programmes in italics as the apotheosis of each concupiscence in turn.
- He claims Satan used a human medium to overthrow the norms of law and morals still generally recognised despite the general process of de-Christianisation, and that "there is no definition more concise, more precise, or better adapted to the nature of Hitler than this — the medium of Satan."
- He claims all mediums without exception are morally entirely mediocre in character and personality, and this holds especially of the devil's medium: "No one who is not deluded could see in Hitler a great personality either from the point of view of character or of morality."
- He claims demonic power is always a power founded on delusion: in the imagination — "the element where the devil works" — gigantic dimensions pierce through, but under cold reflection they shrink to "a pathetic caricature."
- He claims Christ Himself supplied the one infallible criterion for distinguishing the satanic (John viii. 44): "These are the two signs which characterise the satanic: lying and murder" — and that lying and murder are the very essence of National Socialism, "never before... committed so entirely for their own sake, with such cold premeditation, in so passionless a reflex, and with such complete fanaticism, as being the motive power of the life of a whole nation."
- He claims lying is categorically distinct from error: "lying is not error: it is more. It is the conscious reversal of truth." Since the human spirit is created for truth, voluntary lying acts against its own metaphysical nature — and "only spiritual beings, which is what demons are, can live essentially in the perversity of the lie." Wherever lying has become the principle of life, "one may be sure that the forces of Satan are directly at work."
- He claims that in past revolutions blood-baths came from "outbursts of human passion", whereas under National Socialism "murder was a principle, a perfectly normal method used at every turn" and the supreme manifestation of power.
- He claims lying and murder are two forms of one aim: "Lying annihilates the life of the spirit, murder annihilates the life of the body. It is the true satanic aim — always to annihilate" — and notes that no words recur so often in the speeches of Hitler and the leading Nazis as "destruction" and "annihilation".
- He claims this exposes the central paradox of the satanic: "he who can only destroy and annihilate, destroys and annihilates himself"; the devil's power is factitious because he is in himself powerless, and to hide that powerlessness he raises "a smoke-screen of loud noises, boasting, grandiloquent gestures, factitious successes, insults and curses." Boasting, insult and cursing are "the infallible sign of demoniacal powerlessness."
- He claims — writing in 1946 — that the judgment of history has already delivered an unalterable verdict that one man alone, Adolf Hitler with his closest initiates, was responsible for the war.
- Closing claim: extirpating National Socialism root and branch is the most urgent task of the German people and of other nations, but would *not* by itself eliminate "the satanic element" from our period, because its spirit — "the spirit of conscious neo-paganism, which raises the three consequences of original sin to an ideal of life" — slips in everywhere under other forms and degrees. Only one power can drive the devil back into the pit: redemption through Christ as it operates in Christianity and the Church, i.e. salvation through the Cross as the triumph over the threefold concupiscence.

## Concepts
- **experimental proof of the theoretical** — Mager's periodising thesis: what an earlier age grasped only in theory, this age is demonstrating by experiment, in lived history. The essay's whole method (two "witnesses") follows from it.
- **man as the theme of the age** — The present spiritual evolution takes man himself as its subject matter, and therefore seeks to understand him "in himself, independently of the supernatural world."
- ***res cogitans*** — The older self-understanding Mager says modern man has abandoned: man as a being of consciousness and knowing only.
- **descent into instinct** — The new movement: man descends "into the lowest strata of instinct and appetitive power, down to the very roots of human existence."
- **double instinct of preservation** — The two fundamental forces at those roots: preservation of the individual and preservation of the species. Mager holds this pair was, more than anything else, damaged by original sin, which is why the descent leads straight to the satanic.
- **immediate contact with the satanic** — What a man reaches when profoundly felt experience brings him "down to the last depths of original corruption": a contact he must either conquer or succumb to.
- **true mysticism / satanic mysticism** — Mager's governing antithesis. True mysticism resists and overcomes the underworld of demons for the sake of salvation; satanic mysticism penetrates the same underworld "not to vanquish it, but to legitimise it, deify it and assume the role of medium for it."
- **demonism** — The "new inner reality" uncovered by the French and Russian novelists: the state in which a man, having abused his liberty, becomes himself a demon; distinguished from the merely human consequences of original sin.
- **points of entry for demons** — Mager's theological reformulation of Pfleger: the consequences of original sin are human, not demonic in themselves, but they are doors, and they are thrown open when a man deliberately lets himself be guided by the triple concupiscence in thought, will and action.
- **slavery of triple concupiscence** — The fettering of liberty that follows: it remains possible to become and remain free of it, and equally possible not to; the second choice makes a man "gradually, a demon."
- **triple / threefold concupiscence** — The three consequences of original sin named throughout in the biblical triad: the concupiscence (lust) of the eyes, the concupiscence of the flesh, and the pride of life. The whole indictment of National Socialism and the closing appeal to the Cross are built on this triad.
- **apotheosis of concupiscence** — Mager's three italicised verdicts on Nazi programmes: the goods-of-this-world/*lebensraum* doctrine is *the apotheosis of the concupiscence of the eyes*; the enforced sexual amorality is *the apotheosis of the concupiscence of the flesh*; the *Herrenvolk* doctrine is *the apotheosis of the pride of life*.
- **demonism as general phenomenon** — The step from literature to politics: in a novel demonism stays an individual, literary affair; in National Socialism it takes hold of a whole society and becomes "a form of life and activity" on which a new organisation of the world was to be based.
- **medium of Satan** — Mager's definition of Hitler: not a great man in any moral or characterological sense, but the instrument through which Satan overthrew the recognised norms of law and morals. He generalises that mediums are without exception morally mediocre.
- **demonic power as delusion** — "Demonic power is always a power founded on delusion." The devil's element is the imagination, where dimensions look gigantic; cold reflection reduces them to "a pathetic caricature."
- **lying and murder** — The two infallible signs of the satanic, taken from Christ's words in John viii. 44 (the devil "was a murderer from the beginning", "a liar, and the father thereof"). The criterion is then applied to National Socialism.
- ***mendacium incarnatum*** — "The lie incarnate": the phrase Mager reports Pius XI applied to National Socialism, which he judges could not describe it more exactly.
- **the lie as conscious reversal of truth** — Lying is not the same as error: error is narrowness or blindness, even fanatically defended; lying is deliberate inversion, and so an act against the metaphysical nature of a spirit created for truth. Only demons can live *essentially* in that perversity.
- **"drying-up" of the Church** — The Nazi policy Mager quotes Hitler describing privately: not open combat with the Church (which would make martyrs) but attrition until it withers — followed in fact by formal elimination of the Church and Christianity.
- **annihilation as the satanic aim** — Lying annihilates the life of the spirit, murder the life of the body; the recurrence of "destruction" and "annihilation" in Nazi rhetoric is for Mager diagnostic.
- **impotence of the satanic** — The essay's most compressed idea: satanic power is factitious, "power which is impotence, impotence which pretends to be power"; the destroyer destroys himself, and the devil, being powerless, is a coward who compensates with noise, boasting and cursing.
- **conscious neo-paganism** — The surviving form of the spirit of National Socialism after the regime's fall: any outlook that "raises the three consequences of original sin to an ideal of life"; wherever it appears, "the doors are suddenly thrown open to the satanic."
- **redemption through the Cross** — The single power Mager grants against the devil: salvation lies solely in the Cross, i.e. in triumphing over the three consequences of original sin. "Only thus can the last domination of hell be finally annihilated."
- ***Ecce crucem Domini, fugite partes adversae*** — The Latin exorcism tag that is the last line of the essay and of the entire volume ("Behold the Cross of the Lord, flee ye adverse powers" — Mager gives no translation).

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **The editorial preface (Bruno de Jésus-Marie)** — Père Bruno introduces "these vehement pages" as written by his friend Dom Alois Mager, O.S.B., Dean of the Faculty of Theology in Salzburg, just before his sudden death from overwork on 26th December 1946, aged sixty-three. Mager was a faithful contributor to *Etudes Carmélitaines* and — after Agostino Gemelli, President of the Pontifical Academy of Science — the man responsible for the creation of the International Congresses of Religious Psychology. In 1939 he published, under an assumed name, *Custos quid de nocte?*, which completes *Le Risque Chrétien*. He "suffered intensely" over the opposition of National Socialism to the Catholic impetus in his own country, and "from his window he used to utter the words of exorcism over the Obersalzberg" — an image the essay's closing exorcism formula answers directly. [OCR note: the opening line is garbled to "Tursz vehement pages"; "apposition" here almost certainly renders *opposition*.]
- **The pilgrimage and the Emmerich prophecy** — Bruno reports that "midway through the Hitlerian tragedy" he went with Mager on a pilgrimage to "the holy woman" who a hundred and twenty-five years earlier told of a vision she said would have had the power to kill her had she tried to bring it back before her eyes: "In the midst of hell was a chasm of darkness: Lucifer was thrown into it loaded with chains, and black vapours poured [round] him. All this took place according to certain divine commands. I learnt that Lucifer was to be unleashed for a time — fifty or sixty years before the year 2000 after Christ, if I am not mistaken." The German original is printed in the text; the footnote sources it to *Das bittere Leiden unsers Herrn Jesu Christi nach den Betrachtungen der gottseligen Anna Katharina Emmerich*, dated February 1824 (Sulzbach, 1893). This vision — placing Satan's release in the 1940s — is the frame the editor sets around the essay. [OCR note: the day of the February 1824 date and the page reference ("3:19") are garbled beyond safe recovery.]
- **Bernanos** — Cited as an instance of French novelistic demonism: *Sous le Soleil de Satan* and *Le Journal d'un Curé de Campagne*.
- **Du Bos on Gide and Nietzsche** — Charles Du Bos, *Dialogue avec André Gide* (Paris, 1929), traced the lines of "demonism" in Gide and Nietzsche, and did not forget Dostoievski, "whose *Letters from the Underworld* shows demonism in its naked form." Mager reports that Dostoievski's expositions are so realistic that Du Bos "believes that he was in direct co-operation with Satan."
- **Karl Pfleger on Dostoievski's demons** — From *Die Geister, die um Christus ringen*, pp. 208–21, Mager quotes: "The demonic figures produced by Dostoievski — Raskolnikov, Svidrigailov, Kirlov, Verchovenski, Ivan, Dmitri, Smerdiakov and the father of the Karamazov brothers — are not mere creatures of the imagination: they are born of what he himself had experienced inwardly." Mager adds his own reading: no writer before Dostoievski showed such realism in portraying demonism "in the infra-human, the supra-human and the infra-supra-human"; these "demons with human faces think unreally", are "pure visionaries", and lose all contact with "living life" through analytical reason or fleshly voluptuousness; they seem powerful and weighty, "but they are so only in destruction", because their work comes from men already destroyed to the depth of their souls. [OCR note: "Kirlov" as printed; the character-list spelling is the translation's.]
- **Pfleger on liberty** — A second quotation (pp. 208–9) which Mager says shows Pfleger has "a strong inkling, though he is not consciously aware, of the origins of demonism in Dostoievski": "The lower regions are nothing else than the anthropological secret of liberty and trial in liberty. They are not in themselves satanic, but demons emerge from them. Man who is from birth destined to liberty becomes a demon if he abuses liberty."
- **The concupiscence of the eyes: *lebensraum*** — For the National Socialists "all happiness, all salvation both private and public, consisted solely and exclusively in the goods of this world"; if more *lebensraum* was felt necessary, every means of taking it was "not only permitted but encouraged: in fact, regarded as an absolutely binding duty", the right resting "on the very existence of the race."
- **The concupiscence of the flesh: the institutions of amorality** — The instinct of preservation of individual and species was "declared to be the highest norm of morality", and "a radical sexual amorality was extolled in the schools, in the Hitler Youth camps, in the '*Ordensburgen*' and in the S.S. barracks."
- **The pride of life: the *Herrenvolk*** — Nothing was so despicable and hateful to National Socialism as "Christian humanity", regarded as self-degradation, a detestable weakness, "the cause of all failures"; pride of spirit was made the highest ideal of individual education, and the nation's unity was to be shown in the consciousness of superior qualities entitling it to count itself "a race of the élite, *Herrenvolk*", with the right and duty by its very existence to appoint itself ruler and governor of the whole world.
- **General Jodl at Nuremberg** — Mager's supporting testimony on Hitler's stature: as General Jodl said of him at the Nuremberg trial, "He was a great man, but an infernal great man."
- **The Luke iv temptation applied to Hitler** — "It is as if Lucifer had once approached Hitler with the promise recorded by St. Luke", followed by Luke iv. 5–7 in full: the devil showing all the kingdoms of the world in a moment of time, "To thee will I give all this power and the glory of them. For to me they are delivered... If thou therefore wilt adore before me, all shall be thine."
- **The John viii. 44 criterion** — Quoted in full as the criterion given by Christ Himself: "You are of your father the devil... He was a murderer from the beginning and he stood not in the truth, because truth is not in him. When he speaketh a lie, he speaketh of his own, for he is a liar, and the father thereof."
- **Pius XI's phrase** — "If it is true that Pius XI called National Socialism the '*mendacium incarnatum*', 'the lie incarnate', he could not have described it more exactly." (Mager marks the attribution as conditional.)
- **The election posters** — In the last days before the election of the *Reichpraesident*, posters were hung in many Catholic places reading: "Catholics, vote for the believing Catholic, Adolf Hitler."
- **Hitler's public promise of March 1933 vs. his private plan** — Publicly, on 21st March 1933 [OCR: "aist March 1933"]: "The rights of our churches will remain unchanged. Nothing in their relation to the State will be altered. . . . The Government of the Reich sees in Christianity an impregnable basis for the work of reconstruction. It will cultivate and develop friendly relations with the Holy See." Privately, to his accomplices: "I know the right way to treat these people, to get them to give way. They will bend or break, and if they are not fools they will bend. It is impossible to fight the Church; one would only make martyrs. It must be dried up. I, too, once had that fence around my soul, but I broke it, stick by stick." Mager's point: the pair of statements *is* the proof of lying as a principle.
- **National Socialism as a rival religion** — *Bayerische Lehrerzeitung* (1935, Nos. 36 and 37, p. 577), quoted triumphantly: "National Socialism is the highest form of religion. There has never been a higher." And the contemporary formula: "In the centuries to come it will be said by those who look back: 'Christ was great, Adolf Hitler was greater'" (quoted in *Münchener Katholische-Kirchenzeitung* (1946), No. 35, p. 27 f.).
- **The Theodor Haecker testimony (editor's footnote)** — Attached to the point above, marked "[Editor's note.]": lines by Theodor Haecker published by Robert d'Harcourt in "Résistants d'Allemagne" (*Etudes*, March 1948) — "They are not content with using religion to serve their aims of domination. They want to destroy it and replace it. *Salus ex Germanis*: the bearer of German light and salvation is called to replace Christ." The editor recalls visiting Haecker in Munich in the first days of November 1937: taken up to his little working cell at the top of the house, Haecker held both his hands and said, with tears flowing down his face, "We are slaves here," then insisted his visitor come on 9th November to watch "the Wagnerian procession of National Socialism" — "You must see it, it is a new religion." "I came and I saw that what he said was true." (Cf. *Etudes Carmélitaines*, April 1938; *L'Esprit et la Vie*, p. 125.) [OCR note: the date is printed "gth November", read here as 9th November; the year is not repeated but the visit is dated to November 1937.]
- **The casualty ledger from *Le Chaos Européen*** — Mager reports the *Neue Zürcher Nachrichten* quoting "horrifying statistics" from a book entitled *Le Chaos Européen*: 16,000,000 killed in battle; 29,600,000 wounded and crippled; 3,000,000 civilians killed by bombs; 5,500,000 gassed, burnt or murdered; 24,500,000 victims of bombardments; 15,000,000 evacuated and deported; 11,000,000 in concentration camps — "And this is only a provisional balance sheet." He offers the murder of "those who did not deserve to live" as proof that murder was a principle rather than a passion.
- **Hitler's cowardice (eyewitness claim)** — "I myself know from an absolutely authentic source, from eyewitnesses, how cowardly Hitler was in decisive moments. He left this world like a coward — if indeed he did leave it and is not now skulking like an utter coward in some remote corner of the earth." Written in December 1946, the essay treats Hitler's death as not fully settled.
- **Canon Neuhausler, *The Cross and the Swastika*** — Canon Neuhausler of Munich Cathedral, who spent several years in a concentration camp, published in 1946 a large book (Munich, ed. Katholische Kirche Bayerns) on National Socialism's struggle against the Catholic Church and the Church's resistance. Its first part, "Anti-Christ Unchained", summarises: "There is a close connection between Satan and National Socialism. / Satanic was the hatred of National Socialism against Christianity and all that was sacred. / Satanic were the methods of warfare and the propaganda of National Socialism. / Satanic were the brutality and cruelty of National Socialism. / Satanic finally were the disintegration and the fall of National Socialism." Mager adds one line of his own: "Satanic were the murders and the murderers of National Socialism."
- **Editorial footnote: contemporary satanists (Crowley, Price, Kosok)** — A long footnote hung on the Jodl quotation, opening "We have not space to write of all the satanists and pseudo-satanists of our times", and — since it cites events of 1947–48, after Mager's death — evidently supplied by the editors. (i) On 2nd December 1947 the British press announced the death of "'Sir' Alastair Crowley", who once answered a question about his identity with "Before Hitler was, I AM"; between 1920 and 1922 he founded two reviews in Berlin, *Gnosis* and *Luzifer*; dying at seventy, he cursed the doctor who refused him morphia because he distributed it to young people — "Since you are the cause of my dying without morphia you will die immediately after me" — and "his prediction came true." The *Daily Express* of 2nd April 1948 reported that the funeral of "the black magician, Crowley" provoked the Brighton Municipal Council into registering a protest, Councillor J. C. Sherrott stating that a whole ritual of black magic had been performed over the grave: Crowley's disciples intoned diabolical incantations, Crowley's own "Hymn to Pan", Carducci's "Hymn to Satan" and the "Collects for the Gnostic Mass" composed by Crowley for his satanic temple in London. (ii) The British press of 30th March 1948 [OCR: "goth March"] carried obituaries of Harry Price, student of psychic problems and specialist in demonology, founder and permanent secretary of the Council for Psychical Investigation at London University; in a report made under the aegis of London University Price had declared that all over London there were men and women of good social standing and excellent education who worshipped the devil and regularly paid him homage, and that black magic, witchcraft and the invocation of the devil — "three forms of supposedly 'mediaeval' superstition" — are practised in London today "on a scale and with a licence unknown in the Middle Ages". (iii) Mr. A. Frank Duquesne draws attention to a report by Professor Paul Kosok of the University of Long Island, published in the annals of the American Museum of Natural History, on a 1946 exploration in Peru: over five hundred square kilometres of sandy desert, a double series of drawings — some the signs of the Zodiac, others birds and plants, but above all polycephalous serpents — and in the middle of the drawing of the Serpent an immense pit containing the skeletons of men and animals who had obviously been sacrificed; the whole is thought to be about 2,000 years old.

## Key quotes

- "Just as true mysticism consists in resistance to and victory over the underworld of demons for the sake of salvation, so, too, there is a satanic mysticism which also penetrates into this underworld, not to vanquish it, but to legitimise it, deify it and assume the role of medium for it." — the governing antithesis.
- "To prove the truth of this I shall call two witnesses: modern literature and National Socialism." — Mager's statement of method.
- "The consequences of original sin are not in themselves demonic: they are human: but they are points of entry for demons." — his theological gloss on Pfleger.
- "The man who chooses the second possibility gives himself up to the action of Satan and himself becomes, gradually, a demon." — demonism as a gradual, chosen condition.
- "*This is the apotheosis of the concupiscence of the eyes.*" / "*This is the apotheosis of the concupiscence of the flesh.*" / "*This is the apotheosis of the pride of life.*" — the three italicised verdicts structuring the indictment of National Socialism.
- "There is no definition more concise, more precise, or better adapted to the nature of Hitler than this — the medium of Satan." — the essay's definition of Hitler.
- "Demonic power is always a power founded on delusion." — followed by the imagination/cold-reflection contrast.
- "These are the two signs which characterise the satanic: lying and murder." — the criterion drawn from John viii. 44.
- "But lying is not error: it is more. It is the conscious reversal of truth." — the distinction on which the charge of the satanic rests.
- "Lying annihilates the life of the spirit, murder annihilates the life of the body. It is the true satanic aim — always to annihilate." — the unification of the two signs.
- "Because he is in himself powerless, the devil is a coward." / "We find here the same contradiction: power which is impotence, impotence which pretends to be power." — the essay's thesis on satanic impotence.

- "'Ecce crucem Domini, fugite partes adversae.'" — the last words of the essay and of the book.

## Thread
This is the closing essay of the entire anthology — the last item of Part V (Deicide), printed pages 497–506, signed under a dagger by an author already dead when the volume appeared — and it is built to function as a conclusion rather than as one more monograph. Where the preceding twenty-nine studies approach Satan through dogma (Part I), through non-Christian religions (Part II), through possession, exorcism and witchcraft dossiers (Part III), and through art and literature (Part IV), Mager collects the threads and asks what all of it amounts to *now*: his argument that our period "proves experimentally what before was only apprehended theoretically" is precisely the claim that the book's historical and doctrinal material has ceased to be historical. His two witnesses recapitulate the volume in miniature — literature (Part IV's terrain, with Dostoievski again central) supplies the individual case, National Socialism the collective one — and the escalation from the literary to the political is what makes Part V "Deicide" rather than merely "The Modern World": Lenz-Médoc's essay traces the intellectual road to the "death of God", Mager shows the society built on the far side of it. Charles Moeller's Introduction sets this up explicitly, naming "the article of Dom Alois Mager on Satan in our own time" alongside the Balzac, Milton, Gogol and Dostoievski studies as the place where "the devil triumphs in the establishing of a society under the sign of 'the death of God'." Structurally, the anthology's arc closes as a liturgy would: Père Bruno's preface opens with a nineteenth-century visionary's prophecy that Lucifer would be unleashed "fifty or sixty years before the year 2000" and with the image of Mager pronouncing exorcisms from his window over the Obersalzberg; the book's final line is the exorcism itself, *Ecce crucem Domini, fugite partes adversae*. The last theological word of the volume is deliberately not a demonology but a soteriology — the devil is powerless, and the Cross is the one power that drives him back into the pit — so that a 900-page inventory of Satan ends by refusing him the last word.

## Connections
- **Ch 29, "The 'Death of God'" (Paulus Lenz-Médoc)** — the immediately preceding essay and Mager's direct partner in Part V; Lenz-Médoc's "demoniacal" in modern German philosophy (rationalism and vitalism as the devil's favourite haunts) is the intellectual genealogy for which Mager supplies the political outcome, and both essays converge on National Socialism.
- **Ch 27, "The Devil in Gogol and Dostoievski" (Jacques Madaule) and Ch 28, "The Devil in Contemporary Literature" (Claude-Edmonde Magny)** — Mager's first witness overlaps their subject matter; his Dostoievski (via Pfleger and Du Bos) and his Bernanos should be read against their fuller treatments. Ch 26 ("The Turning-Point of Romanticism", Paul Zumthor) supplies the earlier stage of the same literary lineage.
- **Ch 00, Introduction (Charles Moeller)** — names Mager's essay by title and gives the volume's own summary of it ("the devil triumphs in the establishing of a society under the sign of 'the death of God'"); Moeller's "sin of the *anima*" and "supernatural in reverse" are the categories Mager operationalises as lying and murder.
- **Ch 01–05 (Part I, Satan's existence and nature)** — Mager's claims that the devil works in the imagination, that his power is factitious, and that he cannot compel but only open doors, presuppose the Thomistic limits on diabolical action set out there; his "medium of Satan" is a strong instance of influence short of possession.
- **Ch 13, "Exorcism and Diabolical Manifestation" (F. X. Maquart)** — the volume's technical treatment of exorcism; Mager's closing formula and the editorial image of exorcism pronounced over the Obersalzberg extend the rite from a possessed individual to a political landscape.
- **Ch 17, "The Confession of Boullan" and Ch 21, "The Sixteenth Century and Satanism"** — the anthology's dossiers on deliberate satanism; the editorial footnote here (Crowley, Harry Price's London report) is the volume's only glance at *twentieth-century* organised satanism, and points backwards to them.
- **Ch 12, "Demoniacs in the Gospel"** — Mager's criterion is a single Gospel verse (John viii. 44) applied to political history; the contrast in method with the exegetical treatment of the Gospel demoniacs is instructive.
- *Cross-book, speculative/thematic only (not Mager's own references):* the reading of a totalitarian society as a religion whose lie becomes "the principle of life" invites comparison with the discussion of manipulation, deception and coercive control in dark-psychology; and Mager's "man descends into the lowest strata of instinct... independently of the supernatural world" is a mirror-image of the receiver/consciousness-first framings in my-big-toe and the-holographic-universe — same diagnosis of modern materialism, opposite prescription.
