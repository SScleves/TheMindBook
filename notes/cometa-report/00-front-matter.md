# Front Matter — Forewords, Committee, and Introduction

## In one paragraph
The front matter of the COMETA report performs three jobs at once: it certifies the document's respectability, names the people who stand behind it, and states the method. André Lebeau, former chairman of CNES, opens with an epistemological argument — some phenomena become scientific facts only when collective, simultaneous observation substitutes for the reproducibility of an experiment (his precedent is the Laigle meteorite shower) — and concludes that the "strong irrational environment" around UFOs is a reason to apply scientific precepts to them, not to avoid them; the report's value, he says, is that it "contributes toward stripping the phenomenon of UFOs of its irrational layer." General Bernard Norlain, former director of IHEDN, narrates the committee's institutional birth (Gen. Denis Letty's March 1995 visit to his office, the referral to the IHEDN Auditors Association, the earlier AA report of some twenty years before) and insists this "is not a purely academic study": concrete problems are raised "that call for a response in terms of action." Letty himself, as chairman, states the committee's premise — the accumulation of well-documented sightings by credible witnesses "forces us to consider from now on all of the hypotheses… and the extraterrestrial hypothesis, in particular" — thanks his contributors and lists the COMETA members, a roster of generals, an admiral, engineers, and a police chief. The unsigned Introduction then supplies the lineage (the 1976 IHEDN committee under General Blanchard → GEPAN → SEPRA at CNES), fixes the terminology (UFO in preference to the "more scientific" UAP), and lays out the report's three-part architecture, ending on the demand that the strategic, political, and religious consequences of a possible confirmation of the extraterrestrial hypothesis be considered — "the bizarre connotation of which it is advisable to eliminate here and now."

## Core claims
- **Lebeau's claim:** phenomena treated as popular mythology can cross into science; the report's precedent is that stones falling from the sky were "long considered in our country to be the stuff of fables" until a meteorite shower over the town of Laigle "permitted a collective and indisputable observation," at which point it "entered into the domain of science."
- **Lebeau's claim:** the criterion of a scientific fact is reproducibility of experiment; where a phenomenon is not open to experimentation (astronomy, mostly geophysics), repeated observation is the only basis, and "collective and simultaneous observation plays the same role as the reproducibility of experiments."
- **Lebeau's claim:** the collective-observation criterion does not rescue UFOs — "this is not true when the event is not only rare but discrete as well, and when there is a very small amount of evidence at each occurrence, which opens the door to various suspicions," and "unidentified flying objects, or UFOs, fall into this category."
- **Lebeau's claim:** UFOs are mixed in with a background noise that is itself hard to identify but "not at all mysterious" — human activity, especially since the beginning of the space age, has generated atmospheric phenomena whose origin observers cannot immediately ascertain.
- **Lebeau's claim:** unexplained manifestations "both in the atmosphere and occasionally on the surface of the earth" inevitably raise a fundamental question — "are we alone in the universe? Could some of these phenomena be the work of extraterrestrial beings?" — which gives the UFO issue a sociological, media-related, and even religious dimension "in a domain that is not that of science and scientific methods," and it is precisely this dimension that "elicits reactions of rejection in the scientific community."
- **Lebeau's claim (the inversion at the heart of his foreword):** the existence of a strong irrational environment is "another reason to apply the precepts of this method to the issue of UFOs," not a reason to abstain.
- **Lebeau's claim:** whether *those who created the irrational layer* believe in extraterrestrial visitors "concealed in a variety of phenomena that are surprising in appearance but commonplace with respect to their cause" is, "when all is said and done," "of no real importance"; a scientist's belief matters only as motivation — "his belief is not important to the results of his research nor does it have any effect on those results if he is meticulous."
- **Lebeau on what the report rests on:** COMETA "has tried its luck at this in the report that it is presenting, supported, namely, by the work performed by GEPAN, which later became SEPRA" — the GEPAN→SEPRA lineage is thus invoked in the foreword as well as in the Introduction.
- **Lebeau's assessment of the report itself:** the large space given to sightings, testimonies, and to the analysis of cases that *have* been explained "shows the major role played here by the establishment of facts"; but the document also contains "a reflection on the hypothesis of extraterrestrial intelligence and of the importance that it could have if the work came together to confirm it."
- **Norlain's claim:** the study is not academic — "Concrete problems are raised, and not just for civilian and military pilots, that call for a response in terms of action," and the makeup of COMETA was designed to take these into account.
- **Norlain's claim:** the fields of knowledge affected by the UFO phenomenon "are very diverse," and Letty found experts "within the AA, but on the outside as well," whose efforts he coordinated; almost all COMETA members have or had "important responsibilities in defense, industry, teaching, research or various central administrations"; the roster of civilian and military degrees is "very impressive," covering officers, engineers, and specialists in the physical sciences, life sciences, and social sciences.
- **Norlain's claim:** "The first report of the AA favored the creation within CNES of the only civilian government agency known in the world dedicated to the study of UFOs." (Norlain separately mentions an AA preliminary report on the subject published in its bulletin "some twenty years earlier"; he does not state in so many words that the two are the same document.)
- **Norlain's recommendation:** that COMETA's recommendations, "which are inspired by good sense," be examined and implemented by the French authorities, and that "this new report, which is much more in-depth" than the AA's first, "give new impetus to our national efforts and to indispensable international cooperation."
- **Letty's claim:** the accumulation of well-documented sightings made by credible witnesses "forces us to consider from now on all of the hypotheses regarding the origin of unidentified flying objects, or UFOs, and the extraterrestrial hypothesis, in particular."
- **Letty's claim:** UFOs "are now a part of our media environment," as films, television broadcasts, books, and advertisements amply demonstrate.
- **Letty's framing of the committee's status:** "no characterized threat has been perceived to date in France," yet former IHEDN auditors judged it necessary to take stock; they formed, with qualified experts of varied backgrounds, "a private in-depth fact-finding committee, which was christened COMETA," later transformed into a COMETA association that Letty chairs. (Note the tension with the blurb that heads the report, which describes the document as detailing "the results of a study by the Institute of Higher Studies for National Defence" — COMETA is presented in the text itself as private, built around *former* IHEDN auditors.)
- **Introduction's claim:** twenty years after the 1976 committee "it seemed useful to us to take stock once again of the knowledge regarding these sightings, which are becoming of greater and greater interest to a large public that is often convinced of the extraterrestrial origin of UFOs" — "just look at the number of films or television broadcasts on this subject."
- **Introduction's claim:** the phenomenon persists and "the number of sightings, which are completely unexplained despite the abundance and quality of data from them, is growing throughout the world."
- **Introduction's claim:** ground sightings such as Trans-en-Provence (1981) have been the subject of in-depth studies "proving that something did in fact land on the ground and parked there"; pilot testimonies are "often corroborated by radar recordings, as was the case recently in France."
- **Introduction's claim:** in view of the lack of irrefutable proof regarding origin, "the need for understanding persists."
- **Introduction's claim:** scientists worldwide are proposing "partial explanations that are based on known laws of physics," and some of these (propulsion systems, nonlethal weapons, etc.) "could become realities in the short, medium and long term."
- **Introduction's announced plan:** the first part of the report is devoted "to several particularly remarkable French and foreign cases"; the second, "after having recalled the current organization of the research on these phenomena in France and abroad," will "evaluate the work being done by scientists worldwide who are interested in UFOs"; the last part examines the measures the phenomenon calls for.
- **Introduction's announced scope:** it will "review the principal global explanations proposed, focusing on those that are in keeping with the current scientific data, which range from secret weapons to extraterrestrial manifestations" — the extraterrestrial hypothesis is placed at one end of a spectrum whose other end is terrestrial secret weapons.
- **Introduction's claim:** the UFO phenomenon "involves defense in the broad sense" and calls for three classes of measure — informing pilots, the public, and decision-makers; developing SEPRA and promoting supplemental scientific monitoring or research; and considering the strategic, political, and religious consequences of a possible confirmation of the extraterrestrial hypothesis.
- **Terminological ruling:** the report will use "UFO (Unidentified Flying Object) generally instead of the more scientific term UAP (Unidentified Aerospace Phenomenon)" — explicitly "for the sake of convenience with respect to language."

## Concepts
- **irrational layer** — Lebeau's term for the belief-and-suspicion film that has accreted around the UFO question; the report's usefulness is that "it contributes toward stripping the phenomenon of UFOs of its irrational layer." Whether the people who created that layer sincerely believe in extraterrestrial visitors is, for him, "of no real importance."
- **scientific fact** — The preliminary problem Lebeau poses for any phenomenon of this type: "does a scientific fact exist?" Where experimentation is possible, reproducibility "is the touchstone and furnishes the fact that must then be interpreted."
- **collective and simultaneous observation** — Lebeau's substitute criterion where experiment is impossible: when a fact, albeit rare, "is collectively and indisputably visible, it is easy to elevate it to the status of scientific object" (eclipses, comets, novas). It "plays the same role as the reproducibility of experiments."
- **rare and discrete events** — The category into which Lebeau places UFOs: events that are not only rare but discrete, with very little evidence at each occurrence, "which opens the door to various suspicions."
- **background noise** — The mass of atmospheric phenomena generated by human activity, especially since the beginning of the space age, whose origin observers cannot immediately identify; UFOs proper (those attributable to neither a human source nor an identified natural mechanism) are "mixed in with" it. The noise is difficult to identify but "not at all mysterious."
- **UFO / UAP** — The Introduction's terminology ruling: UFO (Unidentified Flying Object) will be used generally, "for the sake of convenience with respect to language," in place of the "more scientific" UAP (Unidentified Aerospace Phenomenon).
- **the extraterrestrial hypothesis** — Not asserted in the front matter but installed as the hypothesis that must now be considered among all others (Letty), whose possible confirmation would carry strategic, political, and religious consequences (Introduction), and whose "bizarre connotation" the report proposes "to eliminate here and now."
- **COMETA** — Glossed by Norlain as "Committee for In-Depth Studies"; described by Letty as a private in-depth fact-finding committee of former IHEDN auditors plus outside experts, later constituted as an association governed by the French Law of July 1, 1901, chaired by Letty.
- **IHEDN Auditors Association (AA)** — The alumni body of the Institut des Hautes Études de Défense Nationale; Norlain referred Letty to its management, which gave the project its support. Member entries in the roster are tagged "AA (xx)" or "AR (xx)", explained in a footnote: "AA or AR xx: auditor of national or regional promotion no. xx."
- **GEPAN** — Groupe d'Étude des Phénomènes Aérospatiaux Non Identifiés [Unidentified Aerospace Phenomena Study Group], created following the recommendations of the 1976 IHEDN committee; precursor to SEPRA.
- **SEPRA** — Service d'Expertise des Phénomènes de Rentrée Atmosphérique [Atmospheric Reentry Phenomena Consulting Department], an agency of CNES, "which is in charge of this file" — i.e., the successor body holding the French UFO dossier. The Introduction's recommendations include "developing the actions of SEPRA."
- **defense in the broad sense** — The Introduction's framing of the stakes: the phenomenon "involves defense in the broad sense and calls for a certain number of measures." (The concession that "no characterized threat has been perceived to date in France" is Letty's, in the preceding piece, not the Introduction's.)
- **partial explanations based on known laws of physics** — The report's stated posture toward the scientific literature it will survey in Part 2: not global proof, but partial physical explanations, of which "some of these (propulsion systems, nonlethal weapons, etc.) could become realities in the short, medium and long term."
- **counterexamples of explained phenomena** — A structural device announced by the table of contents (Chapter 5) and endorsed by Lebeau: the analysis of cases that *have* been explained is part of what "shows the major role played here by the establishment of facts."

## Experiments, examples & case studies
- **The Laigle meteorite shower (Lebeau's precedent case)** — Falling stones were "long considered in our country to be the stuff of fables," until a meteorite shower over the town of Laigle "permitted a collective and indisputable observation," at which point the phenomenon "entered into the domain of science." Lebeau gives no date here. This is his model for how a mythologized phenomenon is admitted to science: not by experiment, but by unimpeachable collective witness.
- **NASA and "primitive life on Mars" (Lebeau's cautionary counter-case)** — "One century later NASA, no doubt hastily, elevated these stones to proof of the existence of primitive life on Mars." The same class of object that had to fight for scientific status was then, in Lebeau's judgment, over-read in the opposite direction. It is the front matter's one explicit rebuke of scientific overreach, balancing his rebuke of scientific avoidance.
- **Eclipses, comets, and novas** — Recognized since ancient times even though their interpretation "long contained — and sometimes still contains — a religious dimension." Lebeau's illustration that a phenomenon can be a legitimate scientific object while its interpretation remains partly religious; the parallel to the UFO issue's religious dimension is left for the reader to draw.
- **The March 1995 meeting at IHEDN (the committee's origin)** — General Letty visited Norlain in March 1995 at his office at IHEDN to explain "his project for creating a new fact-finding committee on UFOs." Norlain assured him of his interest and referred him to the management of the IHEDN Auditors Association (AA), which gave the project its support. Norlain notes that some twenty years earlier the AA had produced and published a preliminary report on the subject in its bulletin, so an update was due.
- **The February 1995 École de l'Air conference** — One month before that meeting, Letty had organized, within the framework of the École de l'Air [Air Force Academy] Alumni Association, a conference on unidentified aerospace phenomena. Before a large public: former pilots "spontaneously related their encounters with UFOs"; "the person in charge of studying these phenomena at the CNES then presented his papers"; and "a well-known astronomer described a scientifically acceptable version of the extraterrestrial hypothesis." Norlain cites this as evidence that Letty was "the perfect one to spearhead this task." (Neither the CNES official nor the astronomer is named at this point.)
- **The AA's earlier report and the birth of the CNES agency** — Norlain records that the AA's first report "favored the creation within CNES of the only civilian government agency known in the world dedicated to the study of UFOs" — the claim of French institutional uniqueness that the report will lean on throughout.
- **The 1976 IHEDN committee under General Blanchard** — In 1976 a committee of the Institut des Hautes Études de Défense Nationale chaired by General Blanchard of the Gendarmerie Nationale "opened the unidentified flying objects file." Its objective: to make proposals for organizing research and the collection of data on these phenomena. The Introduction judges the goal achieved, "because the recommendations of this committee were followed in the creation of the Groupe d'Étude des Phénomènes Aérospatiaux Non Identifiés (GEPAN)… the precursor to the current Service d'Expertise des Phénomènes de Rentrée Atmosphérique (SEPRA)… an agency of the Centre National d'Études Spatiales (CNES), which is in charge of this file." COMETA presents itself, twenty years on, as taking stock of that same file.
- **Trans-en-Provence (1981)** — Cited in the Introduction as the exemplary ground case: "some sightings, like the Trans-en-Provence sighting in 1981, have been the subject of in-depth studies proving that something did in fact land on the ground and parked there." No further detail is given here; the case is deferred to the body of the report.
- **Air France flight AF 3532 (January 28, 1994)** — Letty thanks "the commander of the Air Force Air Operations Command Center for its participation during the investigation into flight AF 3532 on January 28, 1994." *(OCR: the source prints the day as "2&"; read here as 28, but the character is a reconstruction, not a certainty.)* Letty gives no detail about the flight or about what the investigation found. The report does not itself link this acknowledgment to the Introduction's claim that pilot testimony is "often corroborated by radar recordings, as was the case recently in France," though the Air Operations Command Center's involvement points that way.
- **The people Letty thanks first** — General Bernard Norlain, former director of IHEDN, and "Mr. André Lebeau, former chairman of the Centre National d'Études Spatiales, without whom COMETA would not have been born." The two foreword-writers are thus credited by the chairman as the committee's condition of existence.

## Key quotes
- "It is not looked on highly in certain scientific circles to be preoccupied with phenomena that are deemed to come under the heading of popular mythology or that are, at any rate, outside the realm of science." — Lebeau's opening sentence, naming the professional cost of the subject.
- "Such was the case with [the theory of] stones falling from the sky, which was long considered in our country to be the stuff of fables." — the Laigle precedent; brackets are the translator's.
- "Phenomena of this type pose a preliminary problem for the scientific approach: does a scientific fact exist?" — Lebeau's governing question.
- "Thus collective and simultaneous observation plays the same role as the reproducibility of experiments." — his substitute criterion where experiment is impossible.
- "In any case, UFOs, the origin of which cannot be attributed to either a human source or a natural mechanism that has been identified by science, are mixed in with a background noise the origin of which, although difficult to identify, is not at all mysterious." — Lebeau's definition-by-subtraction of the residue worth studying.
- "…the very existence of a strong irrational environment is another reason to apply the precepts of this method to the issue of UFOs." — the inversion that licenses the whole report.
- "What a scientist believes is important in the conducting of his research because this is what motivates and drives him. But his belief is not important to the results of his research nor does it have any effect on those results if he is meticulous." — Lebeau's closing defense against the charge of believer-bias.
- "This is not a purely academic study. Concrete problems are raised, and not just for civilian and military pilots, that call for a response in terms of action." — Norlain, setting the report's operational register.
- "I express the wish that the recommendations of COMETA, which are inspired by good sense, will be examined and implemented by the authorities of our country." — Norlain's appeal to the state.
- "IHEDN will then have well served the nation and, perhaps, humanity." — Norlain's closing line.
- "The accumulation of well-documented sightings made by credible witnesses forces us to consider from now on all of the hypotheses regarding the origin of unidentified flying objects, or UFOs, and the extraterrestrial hypothesis, in particular." — Letty's opening; the report's premise in one sentence.
- "Although no characterized threat has been perceived to date in France, it seemed necessary to the former auditors of the Institut des Hautes Études de Défense Nationale (IHEDN) to take stock of the subject." — Letty on why a non-threat still warrants a defense study.
- "…considering the strategic, political, and religious consequences of a possible confirmation of the extraterrestrial hypothesis, the bizarre connotation of which it is advisable to eliminate here and now." — the Introduction's third task, and the report's most forward-leaning sentence.

## Thread
This unit is the report's credentialing apparatus and its statement of method, and the two are inseparable: the argument the body will make only carries weight if the reader accepts that generals, an admiral, weapons engineers, a police chief, and a former head of the French space agency are the ones making it. Lebeau supplies the epistemology (collective observation as a stand-in for reproducibility; the irrational surround as a reason to investigate rather than abstain), Norlain supplies the institutional pedigree and the demand for action, Letty supplies the roster and the decisive move — that all hypotheses, the extraterrestrial one included, must now be on the table — and the Introduction supplies the plan: facts first (Part 1), state of knowledge second (Part 2), defense implications last (Part 3). Three commitments established here govern everything that follows: that the report will build from cases rather than from theory (including cases that were successfully explained away), that it will treat the phenomenon as a defense matter "in the broad sense" despite conceding no characterized threat exists in France, and that it will strip the "bizarre connotation" from the extraterrestrial hypothesis so that its strategic, political, and religious consequences can be discussed by serious people. The unit ends at the page break where Part 1's preamble and Chapter 1 begin.

- **Chapters 6–7** expand the GEPAN → SEPRA lineage sketched in the Introduction and pick up Norlain's claim about "the only civilian government agency known in the world dedicated to the study of UFOs"; Lebeau's Laigle precedent also returns in **Chapter 7**, in the account of GEPAN's method; **Chapter 9** answers his call for "indispensable international cooperation."
- **Chapter 8 (Hypotheses, Modeling Attempts)** is where the Introduction's "partial explanations that are based on known laws of physics" and the range of global explanations "from secret weapons to extraterrestrial manifestations" are worked out.

- **Appendices** carry the load the front matter deliberately keeps out of the argument proper: radar detection in France, astronomers' sightings, life in the universe, colonization of space, the Roswell affair and disinformation, a chronology of the phenomenon, and psychological/sociological/political reflections.
