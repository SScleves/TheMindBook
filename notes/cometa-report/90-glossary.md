## A–Z

- **Aberration of collective memory** — The claim that a population's collective record can diverge radically from what happened, "despite the tangible proof subsequently furnished to the catechumen." Instances: the New Hebrides cargo cult, the erasure of Bonaparte's Egyptian expedition from local annals, and modern moon-landing denial. Ch. 13. Related: catechumen, founding myth.
- **Absence of radar contact** — The recurring negative datum of the French military cases: the controlling station has no corresponding contact on its scope (Mirage IV's A1 and A2; the T-33's green sphere). The report treats the missing echo, where one would be mandatory, as evidence rather than as a gap. Ch. 1. Related: proof by elimination, radar/visual, primary radar.
- **Absence of sonic boom / sonic-boom focusing** — The physical argument from silence: a combat aircraft banking supersonically focuses its shock wave into a very loud ground boom, noticeable at night — "But no sound was heard in the region." Recurs as the objects' general signature (supersonic speed with no bang) and as a puzzle MHD is offered to solve. Chs. 1, 2, 3, 6–8, 10. Related: MHD propulsion, relative consistency.
- **Active stealth technologies** — COMETA's own deflationary parenthesis, inserted mid-narrative in the RB-47 account: sudden disappearance from a radar screen "is less surprising nowadays; it calls to mind the active stealth technologies currently in development and even in operation." Ch. 2. Related: stealth skins, "stealth" craft.
- **Aeronautic case** — Chapter 2's unit of analysis, defined by observation platform rather than object: "those cases that have been sighted from aircraft are considered to be aeronautic cases." Ch. 2. Related: aeronautical case, sightings from the ground.
- **Aeronautical case (Ch. 11 sense)** — A UFO encounter bearing on aviation by sighting, radar track, interference, shadowing, or aggressive conduct; the category is fixed by the aviation context, since its first type expressly includes sightings by *ground* personnel. COMETA counts "several hundred confirmed." Ch. 11. Related: five types of implication.
- **Aeronautic world as an evaluability criterion** — The committee's stated reason for opening its evidence with pilots: because these testimonies "pertain to the aeronautic world," they "can be evaluated better than other aerial phenomena." Repeated verbatim for the ground witnesses of Ch. 3 ("Here again…"). Chs. 1, 3. Related: credible and well-documented case.
- **AFR 200-2** — Air Force Regulation 200-2, described by the report as prohibiting public disclosure of information relating to sightings of unidentified objects; one of the two named instances of the American "repressive arsenal." Ch. 10. Related: JANAP 146, repressive arsenal.
- **"Airmiss" procedure** — The civil aviation rule for flight incidents that could involve safety, which the captain must follow on landing and which routinely triggers a DGAC investigation; applied to an unidentified object in the AF 3532 case. Chs. 1, 6–8. Related: DGAC, two reporting chains.
- **"Airships" (1880–1900)** — Appendix 6's pre-modern wave: "fairly voluminous, fusiform vessels equipped with powerful searchlights, often emitting engine sounds, and, in some cases, even seeming to have propellers," seen by tens of thousands, resembling dirigibles that factories would not build for another twenty years. The report argues the dirigible explanation cannot bear the technology timeline. App. 6. Related: foo fighters, ghost rockets.
- **"Almost certain physical reality"** — The Conclusion's calibrated central verdict on twenty years of French State studies: they "demonstrate the almost certain physical reality of completely unknown flying objects with remarkable flight performances and noiselessness, apparently operated by intelligent [beings]." The qualifier "almost" is carried into every downstream recommendation. Conclusion. Related: strong presumptions, apparently operated by intelligent beings.
- **Amaranth bush / the "Amaranth" case** — The plant that names the Nancy case of 21 October 1982: the tips of its leaves had completely dried up, suggesting exposure to intense electrical fields. One of the two standout GEPAN/SEPRA trace cases (with Trans-en-Provence), detailed in GEPAN technical memoranda nos. 16 and 17. Chs. 4, 6–8. Related: vegetation trauma, electrical field thresholds.
- **Animal reaction as a witness** — The report's use of livestock panic as corroboration at Antananarivo: the town's zebus and the herds 150 km away were both overcome, "a surprising detail, since normally these animals do not show any agitation when Air France planes pass by." Ch. 3.
- **Anonymous deposition** — The evidentiary form of the Tours testimony: extracts from a written deposition by a pilot who wished to preserve his anonymity and who later revealed his name (Colonel Claude Bosc). Ch. 1. Related: written depositions.
- **Anthropocentric humanism** — The name the report attributes to "various American philosophers" for the framework in which man is master of the earth and its cosmic vicinity, "the best nature can produce in this small corner of the galaxy"; admitting scientifically superior outside intelligences "would cause the collapse of the framework of comfortable thoughts" of that outlook. App. 7. Related: elimination of the gods, UFO paradox.
- **Anti-UFO SDI** — The report's worked example of media manipulation: lobbies or pressure groups pushing politicians to create an anti-UFO Strategic Defense Initiative, making the media "the unwitting spokesmen of a disinformation campaign or a destabilization attempt." Ch. 14. Related: manipulation.
- **Apparent lack of inertia** — Maltsev's formulation for the Russian case of 21 March 1990: an S-shaped maneuver in both planes, axial rotation, hovering, then flight at two to three times combat-aircraft speed, all silently — "in one fashion or another they had overcome gravity." Chs. 2, 9. Related: motion without transition, relative consistency.
- **"Apparently aggressive conduct"** — Ch. 11's careful formulation for maneuvers that read as hostile without a claim of hostile intent; instanced by the Mirage IV, the student pilot at Tours, and Tehran. Ch. 11. Related: intimidation maneuvers, appropriate demonstrations.
- **"Apparently operated by intelligent [beings]"** — The Conclusion's minimal intentionality claim: the objects behave as though under intelligent control. The bracketed word is the translator's supplement. Conclusion. Related: natural or artificial intelligence, Twining conclusions.
- **"Appropriate demonstrations"** — Ch. 10's category for manifestations read as deliberate signalling attached to a possible visitor concern about nuclear war: overflights of nuclear missile bases, intimidation maneuvers against aircraft (Luxeuil, Tehran), and paralysis / engine cut-out / lights-out effects (San Carlos de Bariloche). Ch. 10. Related: gradual acclimatization.
- **"Area of defence significance"** — The sole criterion by which the British MOD unit Sec(AS)2a judges the UFO reports it receives: "Its unique mission is to determine whether the reports are of interest for defense purposes." Ch. 9. Related: Sec(AS)2a, presumed hidden department.
- **Artificial planets / O'Neill cylinders** — Rotating habitats "30 km in length by 6 km in diameter, in rotation to create an artificial gravity and able to shelter millions of people in an earth-type biosphere," credited to "American physicist O'Neill" and sited in the asteroid belt; proposed as an alternative to permanent Martian colonies. Chs. 6–8, App. 4. Related: islands in space, ship-worlds, asteroid-belt staging base.
- **"Ascientific" hypotheses** — The report's category for unfalsifiable explanations — manipulation by a secret group, unknown beings, spirits, the devil, or our own fantasies, plus parapsychology, collective hallucination, and time-travelling human descendants. Dismissed with: they "cannot be proven; their main drawback is that they aren't much good to us." The Conclusion restates the test: such hypotheses "can neither be confirmed nor invalidated. They are therefore not scientific." Chs. 6–8, Conclusion. Related: non-scientific hypotheses.
- **"Assailant" (A1, A2)** — The report's own designation for the object(s) that closed on the Mirage IV on 7 March 1977; it labels the first glow A1 and the identical second A2, leaving open "whether or not they were the same craft." Ch. 1.
- **Asteroid-belt staging base** — The use COMETA makes of the Ribes–Monnet scenario: a civilization from elsewhere could equally have colonized *our* asteroid belt and used it as a staging base to Earth; Ch. 10 extends this to stations or colonies in the belt plus lunar relay stations, and Ch. 13 turns it round, imagining our own belt bases under threat. Chs. 6–8, 10, 12, 13, App. 4. Related: Ribes–Monnet scenario, islands in space.
- **Astronomers' objection** — The recurring anti-UFO argument Ribes sets out to answer in Appendix 2: astronomers, "who should have a ringside seat, do not relate any such sightings." App. 2. Related: dome problem, guaranteed anonymity, comparable sighting rate.
- **Authenticity — "only negative conclusions are definitive"** — Louange's methodological rule for image analysis: authenticity is "completely relative," because one can prove a document false but at best a document "can stand up to analyses at any given moment." Ch. 9 (sidebar). Related: two-step analysis, fortuitous witnesses.
- **Background noise** — Lebeau's term for the mass of atmospheric phenomena generated by human activity, especially since the space age, whose origin observers cannot immediately identify. UFOs proper — attributable to neither a human source nor an identified natural mechanism — are "mixed in with" it; the noise is hard to identify but "not at all mysterious." Front matter. Related: scientific fact, rare and discrete events.
- **Bionic androids / remote-controlled androids** — The crew type for the shorter interstellar voyages Ch. 13 postulates, "which would follow the instructions received from a parent station or from earth"; in phase four they reappear as an expendable advance party and as a calibration instrument for gauging indigenous reaction. Ch. 13. Related: ship-world, four-phase contact program.
- **Biosphere 2** — The American closed-ecosystem experiment: a privately funded 1.3-hectare greenhouse maintaining plants, animals and eight people in closed circuit with an outside power supply. Reported results: total water recycling, imperfect air recycling (oxygen added after fifteen months), slightly inadequate food. The report calls it unjustly criticized and says "it is a descendent of Biosphere 2 who could represent the future autonomous moon base." App. 4. Related: closed-circuit ecosystem.
- **Bolender letter (October 1969)** — Ch. 9's central documentary find, surfaced by the 1974 FOIA amendment: Air Force Brigadier General Bolender's statement that the imminent end of Blue Book "would not put an end to military reports concerning UFOs that constituted a threat to national security," which were outside the Blue Book system and would continue under JANAP 146 and Air Force Manual 55-11. Ch. 9. Related: "threat to national security" channel, Project Blue Book.
- **"Calculated indiscretions" / "calculated disclosures"** — The report's paired phrase for graduated, trauma-free revelation, seen from both ends. In App. 4 it is what *we* would do on reaching a civilization we had been observing: "commit 'calculated indiscretions' that would accustom the population, little by little, to the idea that there could indeed be extraplanetary visits." In App. 7.3.3 it returns as "more or less calculated disclosures" — one of the few things that might yet move U.S. leaders off denial. The report does not say whether the echo is deliberate. Apps. 4, 7. Related: gradual acclimatization, discrete study.
- **Categories A / B / C / D** — GEPAN/SEPRA's post-study classification of every case by degree of identification: **A** completely identified; **B** probably identifiable but not with certainty, for lack of evidence; **C** unidentifiable for lack of data; **D** unidentifiable *despite* the abundance and quality of the data. Defined in Chs. 6–8 and nowhere else in the report, though later chapters use the vocabulary without redefining it. Chs. 6–8. Related: UAP D, CE3, radar/visual.
- **Catechumen** — The report's unusual term for a population being instructed: used first of the cargo-cult believers to whom tangible proof was "subsequently furnished," and last of the advanced civilization the retreating visitors would study ("the still unknown techniques of our catechumens" — obscure in this translation, reproduced as printed). Ch. 13. Related: aberration of collective memory.
- **CE3 (close encounters of the third kind)** — The label generally applied to the strangest and most mysterious UAP D cases, per the classification proposed by Professor A. Hynek, astronomer and USAF consultant, within the Blue Book Project. Defined only in Chs. 6–8. Chs. 6–8. Related: Categories A–D, close encounter.
- **CIRVIS report** — "Communications Instructions for Reporting Vital Intelligence Sightings": a secret urgent radio report to the Air Defense Command, mandatory when the Air Force sights an unidentified aerial object. The "Utah" radar center prepared one during the RB-47 encounter. Ch. 2. Related: JANAP 146.
- **Close encounter** — Ch. 4's operative category: the phenomenon observed at very short range at or near the ground, leaving physical traces, physiological effects on the witness, or both. The chapter never defines the term; it enacts it through Valensole, Cussac, Trans-en-Provence and "Amaranth." Ch. 4. Related: CE3, physical traces.
- **Closed-circuit ecosystem** — A recreated ecosystem in which "the essential raw material needs (including air, water, and food) could be extracted on site or recycled" — the precondition for large-scale settlement, since the current method requires almost everything to be brought from Earth by costly launch. Russian experiments date from 1961. App. 4. Related: Biosphere 2, minimal lunar station.
- **Collective and simultaneous observation** — Lebeau's substitute criterion where experiment is impossible: when a rare fact "is collectively and indisputably visible, it is easy to elevate it to the status of scientific object" (eclipses, comets, novas). It "plays the same role as the reproducibility of experiments." Front matter. Related: scientific fact, meteorite precedent.
- **COMETA** — Glossed by Norlain as "Committee for In-Depth Studies"; described by Letty as a private in-depth fact-finding committee of former IHEDN auditors plus outside experts, later constituted as an association governed by the French Law of July 1, 1901. Note the tension with the blurb heading the report, which calls the document a study by IHEDN itself. In the Conclusion the committee signs collectively, naming no individual at all. Front matter, Conclusion. Related: IHEDN Auditors Association.
- **Condon Commission / Condon report** — The university commission tasked with evaluating USAF research on UFOs; its summary and conclusions, drafted by the physicist Condon, held that UFO study had little chance of advancing science. Its 1969 report nonetheless classed both Lakenheath and the RB-47 objects "unidentified," and — 22 years on — first revealed the existence of Twining's 1947 secret report. Ch. 10 calls its conclusions "strange," "just one case in point" of American disinformation. Chs. 2, 9, 10. Related: AIAA critique, "unidentified" classification, Twining.
- **AIAA critique** — The American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics' objection that Condon's summary conflicted with analyses inside the report's own body; its counter-recommendation was "moderate, but continuous scientific work on UFOs." Ch. 9.
- **Contact typology** — Ch. 13's three-axis classification of possible contact modes, presented as the decision to be made at phase two: "furtive and covert contacts, visible and overt contacts, continuous or intermittent contacts." Ch. 13. Related: four-phase contact program.
- **Contingency anticipation** — Ch. 12's closing recommendation, to "already be anticipating the measures to be taken and the decisions to be made" should indubitable physical or radio contact occur; it becomes recommendation 6 of the Conclusion and is the assignment Ch. 13 carries out. Chs. 12, 13, Conclusion. Related: four contingencies, spectacular and indisputable manifestation.
- **Control and Detection Center (CDC)** — The French military radar installation type; CDCs can record and reconstruct radar situations on site, enabling investigation after the fact, and hold the primary radars capable of seeing a transponder-less object. Ch. 11, App. 1. Related: primary radar, STRIDA.
- **Corona effect** — The companion electrical discharge phenomenon invoked alongside the power arc in the Dombes case; the report names it without explaining it. Ch. 5. Related: power arc effect.
- **Cosmic fear** — The class of risk Ch. 10 attaches to the phenomenon in the media, psychological, cultural and religious registers: panic fear, world wars, psychoses created by sects or lobbies. Ch. 10. Related: cosmic vigilance, panic.
- **Cosmic vigilance** — The remedy proposed against cosmic fear: watchfulness by the political, scientific and intellectual elite "calculated to prevent any shocking surprise, erroneous interpretation, and malicious or unhealthy manipulation." Ch. 10. Related: critical vigilance.
- **Cosmic-timescale argument / the 0.1% argument** — Ch. 8's reasoning that since other worlds' conscious life cannot have developed at exactly our rate or epoch, "even a minuscule deviation of 0.1%" in initial data would place such a civilization several million years ahead of or behind us — so rough parity is very improbable, and any neighbour ahead of us is likely thousands of years or more ahead, at a level that "would certainly exceed our forecasting capabilities in every domain." Chs. 6–8. Related: humility of technical forecasting.
- **Cosmos 1900** — The Soviet satellite carrying a nuclear power generator whose fall was expected on 29 September 1988, and the reason Geiger counters and a radiological cordon met a red ball beside the Paris–Lille freeway. CNES, consulted, reported it was over the Indian Ocean at that moment. Ch. 5. Related: atmospheric reentry signatures.
- **Counterexample** — Ch. 5's genre and title: a case presented precisely because it *was* explained, as the complement to the unexplained cases of Chs. 1–4. The word appears only in the chapter title; the text never defines it. Ch. 5. Related: minority of unexplained cases, short investigation.
- **Counterinvestigation** — A second, independent investigation of an already-recorded case. The 1978 Cussac counterinvestigation, run "as an example, at the request of the GEPAN scientific council," was staffed by GEPAN investigators plus qualified outside advisors, one a former examining magistrate; its value lay in *new* elements — secondary witnesses — rather than in the original accounts. Ch. 4. Related: secondary witnesses, on-site reconstruction.
- **Course of action to take** — Ch. 11's compressed aircrew doctrine in one line: "observe, note the maximum amount of details, take photographs if possible, make a report, allowing the visitors the initiative of possibly making contact, and avoid premature publicity in the media." Ch. 11. Related: reflex responses, objective observation, discretion vis-à-vis the public.
- **Crease-resistant material** — The recurring described Roswell artifact: "a thin sheet that looks like metal with very great resistance and that is so elastic that after it has been crumpled up into a ball, it spontaneously returns to its initial shape without the least sign of a residual fold." App. 5. Related: simplified disinformation, affidavits.
- **Credible and well-documented case** — The report's working standard of a "remarkable" case, glossed in exactly those words; Part 1's examples are offered as samples of "the hundreds of remarkable, that is to say credible and well-documented, cases observed around the world in recent decades." Chs. 1–5. Related: aeronautic world as an evaluability criterion.
- **Crew sedition** — Ch. 13's scenario of expedition members deciding "on their own authority to live on one of the worlds discovered and, ultimately, mix with the indigenous populations, going against orders received … not to intervene or interfere in local affairs" — contact policy defeated from inside the mission. Ch. 13. Related: prophylactic ban on mixing.
- **Critical vigilance** — The minimum posture Ch. 10 demands regardless of the origin question: "Whether or not UFOs are extraterrestrial in origin, the UFO phenomenon is already with us and, at any rate, requires critical vigilance on our part." The hedge that makes the policy recommendations independent of the ET question. Ch. 10. Related: cosmic vigilance, defense in the broad sense.
- **Deep-time chronology (Appendix 6)** — The report's argument that "human beings in all ages have sighted phenomena in the sky that they considered, rightly or wrongly, to be abnormal," running back from Kenneth Arnold (24 June 1947) through the 1946 ghost rockets, the wartime foo fighters, the 1880–1900 airships, Goethe's 1768 sighting, Nuremberg 1561 and Basel 1566, the Detchani frescoes, Agobard under Charlemagne, and the Latin and Greek chroniclers. The appendix concedes its own epistemic problem: "our epoch naturally has a tendency to doubt the accuracy of ancient testimonies." App. 6.
- **Defense in the broad sense** — The Introduction's framing of the stakes and Ch. 12's opening frame: the phenomenon "involves defense in the broad sense and calls for a certain number of measures" — breadth that generates a scientific and technical proposal list, not only an operational one. Note Letty's concession that "no characterized threat has been perceived to date in France." Front matter, Ch. 12. Related: critical vigilance.
- **Deliberate crash / "authenticator" question** — App. 5's open question about Roswell: the crash appears to have occurred on 4 July (Independence Day) around 2330 hours, and "the date and time symbolize American power," so "was it truly an accident or was it a deliberate crash constituting a message and/or the authenticator?" (The appendix's own chronology dates the base's press release 8 July and does not reconcile the two.) App. 5.
- **Detchani frescoes** — The Yugoslav monastery, built between 1327 and 1335, "decorated with frescoes that represent angels enclosed in sorts of vessels flying in the sky"; named in Ch. 13 as a preindustrial record and catalogued in App. 6. Chs. 13, App. 6. Related: Nuremberg 1561 / Basel 1566.
- **DIA assessment criteria** — The six conditions the Defense Intelligence Agency's attached note listed as making the Tehran case "a classic that meets all the necessary conditions for a legitimate study of the UFO phenomenon": multiple witnesses in different locations; strong witness credibility; radar confirmation of the visual sightings; similar electromagnetic effects reported by three different crews; physiological effects on crew members; an extraordinary degree of maneuverability. Chs. 2, 9. Related: freedom of information act.
- **Discrete study** — The mode of investigation App. 4 says an ethically-minded advanced expedition would adopt toward a less developed civilization: no open intervention, "high-speed and silent craft to move through the planet's atmosphere (MHD propulsion offers interesting prospects in this respect), and nonlethal weapons to avoid the consequences of an untimely encounter (the paralyzing effect of pulsed microwaves is under study in several countries)." App. 4. Related: fatal culture shock, calculated indiscretions.
- **Discretion vis-à-vis the public** — The obligation Ch. 11 places on a witness to hold back from the press: "It is essential to allow scientists [time] to make use of the information before letting the media trigger the curiosity of the general public, which could result in the disappearance of important evidence." Ch. 11. Related: media implications.
- **Disinformation, simplified vs. enhanced** — App. 5's two-technique, two-audience theory of how a real phenomenon can remain officially non-existent. **Simplified**: truncation and mundane reinterpretation — cutting testimony down "so as to give credence to the Mogul balloon hypothesis," or relegating inconvenient affidavits to an appendix; practiced by the Air Force "from the onset" (per Hynek's own account) and intensified after the CIA's Robertson Committee of December 1952. **Enhanced**: inflation with spectacular but questionable material until the subject collapses into ridicule; "probably implemented very early on" (Adamski, 1952) and extensive only since the late-1970s resurgence, with the Bennewicz/Kirtland episode as its "point of departure." The formula: "Simplified disinformation is effective on those who do not want to accept the possibility of the extraterrestrial hypothesis. Enhanced disinformation is aimed at others." App. 5. Related: weapon of the ridiculous, active and passive disinformation, spill-over effect (of ufological excess).
- **Disinformation attempts (as a global hypothesis)** — One of the rival explanations Ch. 8 triages and rejects: a direct product of cold-war thinking, and "even less satisfying than the preceding ones because it runs up against the objections to each of those." Chs. 6–8. Related: secret weapons of a superpower.
- **Disinformation (as an object of study)** — Ch. 12's proposal that "the different attempts at disinformation made by certain foreign governments should be analyzed discretely [i.e. discreetly], but in depth," motivated by those governments' interest in monopolizing possible futuristic aircraft and weapons technologies; carried into the Conclusion as the warning to DICOD/SIRPA about "disinformation processes."
